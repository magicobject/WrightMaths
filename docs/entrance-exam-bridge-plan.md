# Bridge to university entrance exams: plan (draft 1, 27 Nov 2026)

A possible fourth book: from "good at A-level" to "ready for the hardest university admissions tests". This document asks **whether** to build it as much as how.

**Status.** Proposal only. Facts about the tests (who runs them, formats, syllabuses) are from general knowledge and **change often**; every one must be checked against the test's own pages before anything is written or published.

## 1. The gap

A-level questions are mostly structured: the method is signposted. Admissions tests ask for the same content used in unfamiliar ways, with no signposts, under time pressure. Students in schools without a tradition of these tests often meet that style for the first time in the exam. This book is about **problem-solving habits on A-level content**, not new content.

## 2. Tests in scope (to verify)

| Test | Used by (indicative) | Style |
|---|---|---|
| **STEP** (Sixth Term Examination Papers) | Cambridge, Warwick and others, as part of offers | Long, open problems; papers built on A-level and Further Maths |
| **MAT** (Mathematics Admissions Test) | Oxford, Imperial and others | Multiple choice plus longer questions, A-level Year 1 content mainly |
| **TMUA** (Test of Mathematics for University Admission) | Cambridge, LSE, Warwick, Durham and others | Multiple choice; mathematical thinking and logic |

Who runs each test, its dates, its syllabus and which universities use it have all changed recently. Check before relying on any of the above. Past papers belong to their owners: **we write our own problems** and link to the official past papers; we never copy them.

## 3. What the book teaches

Organised by **habit**, not topic, each with problems on A-level content:

1. Read the question: what is given, what is wanted, what kind of answer.
2. Try small cases and spot the pattern.
3. Draw it: a sketch decides many questions.
4. Work backwards.
5. Use symmetry and invariants.
6. Change the variable or the viewpoint.
7. Estimate and bound: "is it bigger than 1?".
8. Logic and proof: necessary vs sufficient, "if" vs "only if", spotting a flawed argument (TMUA-style).
9. Write it up: a STEP answer that earns full marks.
10. Exam craft: choosing questions, timing, partial credit.

Then three **test tracks** (MAT, TMUA, STEP) with original practice papers in each test's style and a "which test do I need?" page.

## 4. How it differs from the other books

- Questions are fewer and longer. The "Help, I'm stuck!" ladder becomes the main teaching tool: four or five rungs from a nudge to a full plan, then a model write-up.
- Answers are often proofs or explanations, so self-marking is by **mark scheme checklist** rather than an answer box (multiple-choice items still auto-check).
- Each problem names the A-level topics it uses, linked into the A-level and Further Pure books.

## 5. Should we build it?

For: a real, under-served gap (state-school applicants with no specialist support); builds on what exists; strong reason to visit the site.
Against: free high-quality material already exists from the universities and charities; writing genuinely good original problems is slow and the accuracy gate is harder; the tests' formats change.
Recommendation: build a **small pilot** (habits 1–3 plus one TMUA-style practice set), measure interest (without analytics: ask schools and tutors), then decide. A `market-researcher` pass on existing free resources should come first.

## 6. Gates

Maths accuracy (two checkers per problem, given the difficulty), axe, no third-party requests, no data leaves the device. Any wording that names a test or university is checked against that body's own pages and approved by Greg before publishing.
