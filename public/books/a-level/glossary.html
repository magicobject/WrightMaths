    Glossary · Years 1 and 2

    The language of A-level pure maths

    4 terms in plain English, for Year 1 and Year 2 together. Each has an example, a warning about common slips where there is one, the reverse side of the idea, and where it is taught in this book.

    A-level Maths Book / Glossary

    How to use it: terms are in alphabetical order. Each entry shows Year 1 or Year 2. Choose an area of maths (such as Algebra and functions) to see one area, search for a word, or jump to a letter.

    Jump to: D I Q

    D

    derivative Differentiation Year 1

    The gradient function of a curve: a formula that gives the gradient of the curve at any point.

    Example: If y=x2, the derivative is dydx=2x. At x=3 the gradient is 6.

    Watch out: The derivative is a function, not a single number. A gradient at one point is a value of the derivative.

    Flip it: Integration reverses differentiation.

    See also: integral

    discriminant Algebra and functions Year 1

    For a quadratic with coefficients a, b and c, the number b squared minus 4ac. Its sign tells you how many real roots there are.

    Example: For x2+5x+4, the discriminant is 52−4×1×4=9. It is positive, so there are two different real roots.

    Watch out: The discriminant is not the answer to the equation. It only tells you how many roots there are.

    See also: quadratic

    I

    integral Integration Year 1

    The result of reversing differentiation, or the area under a curve between two values of x.

    Example: The integral of 2x with respect to x is x2+c.

    Watch out: Do not forget the constant of integration +c when there are no limits.

    Flip it: Differentiation reverses integration.

    See also: derivative

    Q

    quadratic Algebra and functions Year 1

    An expression or equation where the highest power of the unknown is 2, such as x squared plus 5x plus 4.

    Examples:x2+5x+4 is a quadratic expression.x2+5x+4=0 is a quadratic equation.

    Watch out: A quadratic must have an x squared term. 5x+4 is not a quadratic.

    Flip it: Solving a quadratic gives its roots. Starting from the roots, you can build the quadratic.

    See also: discriminant
