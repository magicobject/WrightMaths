Percentages

Percent means “out of 100”. Learn to switch between percentages, fractions and decimals. Find a percentage of an amount, increase and decrease with one multiplier, and then run it all backwards.

Where you will use this: sale prices, VAT on a builder's quote, a recipe scaled up, a game's win rate, and the interest on savings.

1. Percent means out of 100

A Percentage is a Fraction with 100 on the bottom. The word comes from per cent, which means “for each hundred”. So 35% means 35 out of every 100.

35% = 35 over 100 = 0.35

Key idea: the % sign is just a way of writing “divided by 100”. To go from a percentage to a decimal, divide by 100. To go back, multiply by 100.

Try it: write 8% as a Decimal. (Divide by 100.)

A few to know by heart: 50% = 1 over 2, 25% = 1 over 4, 75% = 3 over 4, 10% = 1 over 10, 20% = 1 over 5, 1% = 1 over 100.

Switching between forms

  • Percentage to decimal: divide by 100. 62% = 0.62.
  • Percentage to fraction: put it over 100, then simplify. 62% = 62 over 100 = 31 over 50.
  • Decimal to percentage: multiply by 100. 0.6 = 60%.
  • Fraction to percentage: make the bottom 100 using an equivalent fraction, or divide the top by the bottom and multiply by 100.

Worked example: fraction to percentage

Write 3 over 8 as a percentage.

  1. 8 does not go into 100 a whole number of times, so divide: 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.
  2. Multiply by 100: 0.375 × 100 = 37.5.
  3. 3 over 8 = 37.5%.

Now you finish it

Write 7 over 20 as a percentage.

  1. 20 × 5 = 100, so multiply the top and the bottom by ___.
  2. 7 × 5 = ___.
  3. So 7 over 20 = ___ out of 100, which is ___%.
Check my answer

20 × 5 = 100 and 7 × 5 = 35, so 7 over 20 = 35 over 100 = 35%.

2. A percentage of an amount

There are two good ways. Use the first when you have no calculator, and the second when you do.

Without a calculator: build it from 10% and 1%

10% is one tenth, so divide by 10. 1% is one hundredth, so divide by 100. Halve 10% to get 5%. Add pieces together to make any percentage.

Key idea: percentages add up. 35% = 10% + 10% + 10% + 5%. Work out the 10% once, then build the rest from it.

With a calculator: use a multiplier

Change the percentage to a decimal by dividing by 100. Then multiply. That decimal, the number you multiply by, is called a Multiplier. 35% of 240 is 0.35 × 240.

Worked example: no calculator

Work out 35% of £240.

  1. 10% of 240 = 240 ÷ 10 = 24.
  2. 30% = 3 × 24 = 72.
  3. 5% is half of 10%, so 5% = 12.
  4. 35% = 72 + 12 = £84.

Worked example: with a calculator

Work out 35% of £240 again.

  1. 35% = 35 ÷ 100 = 0.35.
  2. 0.35 × 240 = 84.
  3. The two methods agree: £84.

Now you finish it

Work out 15% of £60 without a calculator.

  1. 10% of 60 = ___.
  2. 5% of 60 = half of that = ___.
  3. 15% = 10% + 5% = ___ + ___ = £___.
Check my answer

10% = 6 and 5% = 3, so 15% = 6 + 3 = £9.

3. One amount as a percentage of another

To say “18 out of 45” as a percentage, make a fraction with the part on top and the whole on the bottom. Then change it to a percentage.

percentage = (part ÷ whole) × 100

Key idea: the whole always goes on the bottom, and both amounts must be in the same units. 60 cm out of 3 m needs a change to 60 cm out of 300 cm first.

Try it: what percentage of 20 is 5? (Write 5 over 20, then think quarters.)

Worked example

What percentage of 45 is 18?

  1. Write the fraction: part over whole, 18 over 45.
  2. 18 ÷ 45 = 0.4.
  3. 0.4 × 100 = 40. So 18 is 40% of 45.
  4. Check: 10% of 45 is 4.5, and 4 × 4.5 = 18. ✓

Now you finish it

Tess sold 21 of her 60 raffle tickets. What percentage did she sell?

  1. Write the fraction: ___ over ___.
  2. Divide: 21 ÷ 60 = ___.
  3. Multiply by 100 to get ___%.
Check my answer

21 over 60 = 0.35, and 0.35 × 100 = 35%.

4. Increase and decrease with a multiplier

To increase 80 by 15%, you could find 15% and add it on. A faster way does it in one step. After a 15% increase you have 100% + 15% = 115% of the starting amount. As a decimal that is 1.15, so you multiply by 1.15.

A 15% decrease leaves 100% − 15% = 85%, so you multiply by 0.85.

increase by r%: multiplier = 1 + r/100    decrease by r%: multiplier = 1 − r/100

  • Increase by 5%: × 1.05. Increase by 20%: × 1.2. Increase by 100%: × 2.
  • Decrease by 10%: × 0.9. Decrease by 30%: × 0.7. Decrease by 50%: × 0.5.

Key idea: a percentage change is one multiplication. The multiplier is the percentage you END with, written as a decimal.

Try it: what multiplier increases an amount by 25%? (It is 100% + 25% = 125%.)

Worked example: increase

Increase £80 by 15%.

  1. Multiplier: 100% + 15% = 115% = 1.15.
  2. 1.15 × 80 = 92.
  3. The new price is £92.

Worked example: decrease

Decrease £240 by 35%.

  1. Multiplier: 100% − 35% = 65% = 0.65.
  2. 0.65 × 240 = 156.
  3. The new price is £156.

Now you finish it

Decrease 60 by 20%.

  1. Multiplier: 100% − 20% = ___% = ___.
  2. ___ × 60 = ___.
Check my answer

100% − 20% = 80% = 0.8, and 0.8 × 60 = 48.

Where you meet this

VAT is a tax added to the price of most things. The usual UK rate is 20%, so a price without VAT times 1.2 gives the price with VAT. A sale of “25% off” is a multiplier of 0.75.

Simple interest

When you save money, the bank pays you interest. With Simple interest, the interest is always a percentage of the starting amount, so it is the same every year. Work out one year's interest, then multiply by the number of years.

Worked example: simple interest

Mia saves £600 at 3% simple interest a year. How much interest does she get in 4 years?

  1. 3% of 600 = 0.03 × 600 = £18 each year.
  2. 4 years: 4 × 18 = £72.

Next topic: when the interest is added to the savings each year, the next year's interest is bigger. That is repeated percentage change, and it has its own page after this one.

Flip it: run it backwards

Everything so far goes forwards: you know the start and the percentage, and you find the end. Now go the other way.

Forwards

£80 increased by 15% = ?

Multiply by 1.15. Find the new amount.

Backwards

? increased by 15% = £92

Divide by 1.15. Find the old amount. This is a Reverse percentage.

Key idea: after a percentage change, the new amount is a percentage of the ORIGINAL. Undo it by dividing by the multiplier. Never take the percentage off the new amount: it was not taken from there.

Worked example: reverse a discount

After a 20% discount a coat costs £48. What was the original price?

  1. A 20% discount leaves 80%, so 80% of the original = 48.
  2. 80% = 48, so 1% = 48 ÷ 80 = 0.6.
  3. The original is 100% = 0.6 × 100 = £60.
  4. Same thing in one go: 48 ÷ 0.8 = 60.
  5. Check forwards: 0.8 × 60 = 48. ✓

The trap: adding 20% back onto £48 gives £57.60, which is wrong. The 20% was taken from the original, not from £48.

Worked example: take the VAT off

A kettle costs £30 with 20% VAT included. What is the price without VAT?

  1. The price with VAT is 120% of the price without, so 120% = 30.
  2. 30 ÷ 1.2 = 25.
  3. Without VAT it costs £25.

Finding the percentage change

The other reverse: you know the start and the end, and you find the Percentage change. Always divide by the original (the starting amount).

percentage change = (change ÷ original) × 100

Worked example

A price goes from £80 to £92. What is the percentage increase?

  1. Change = 92 − 80 = 12.
  2. 12 ÷ 80 = 0.15.
  3. 0.15 × 100 = 15%. It is a 15% increase.

Spot the mistake

Finding an error in someone else's working helps you learn. For each one, decide what went wrong.

Tom says: a coat costs £48 after 20% off, so the original price was £48 + 20% = £57.60. What went wrong?

  • He found 20% of the sale price, not of the original.
  • He should have taken 20% off the sale price instead of adding 20% to it.
  • Nothing is wrong. Adding the 20% back is how you undo a 20% discount.
  1. Test his answer. Take 20% off £57.60. Do you get £48?
  2. The discount was taken from the original price. What percentage of the original is £48?

20% off £57.60 is 0.8 × 57.60 = £46.08, not £48. The right way is 48 ÷ 0.8 = £60.

Priya says: if a price goes up by 10% and then comes down by 10%, you are back where you started. What is wrong with that?

  • Nothing is wrong. A rise of 10% and a fall of 10% always cancel out exactly.
  • The fall is 10% of the new, bigger price, so you end up lower.
  • The fall should be 11%, because 10% of £110 is not as big as 10% of £100.
  1. Pick a starting price, such as £100, and do it step by step.
  2. For the second step, what is 10% of the new price?

£100 up 10% = £110. Down 10% = 0.9 × 110 = £99. The second 10% is of £110, not £100.

  1. 0.04 means 4 hundredths.
  2. Decimal to percentage: multiply by 100.
  • That is 0.4 as a percentage. Multiplying 0.04 by 100 moves the point two places.
  • That is the number you started with. Multiply by 100.

0.04 × 100 = 4, so 0.04 = 4%.

Without a calculator, work out 17.5% of £80. Answer in pounds.

  1. Build 17.5% from 10%, 5% and 2.5%.
  2. Find 10% first. 5% is half of that, and 2.5% is half of 5%.
  • That is ten times too small. Check your 10%: it should be 80 ÷ 10.
  • That is the percentage, not the amount. Take 17.5% of 80.

10% = 8, 5% = 4 and 2.5% = 2. 8 + 4 + 2 = £14.

A train fare is £36. It rises by 5%. What is the new fare? Give your answer in pounds, like 4.50.

  1. Which multiplier increases by 5%?
  2. Multiply 36 by it.
  • That is the rise only. The new fare includes the old fare as well.
  • That takes 5% off. The fare rises.

1.05 × 36 = 37.8, so £37.80.

A TV costs £340 in a 15% sale. What was the price before the sale? Answer in pounds.

  1. After 15% off, you pay 85% of the old price.
  2. Divide by the multiplier.
  • You added 15% of £340. The 15% was taken from the original price.
  • That takes another 15% off. Work backwards: divide by 0.85.

100% − 15% = 85% = 0.85. 340 ÷ 0.85 = £400.

In a music class, 18 of the 24 students play guitar. What percentage play guitar? Give your answer like 12%.

  1. Put the guitar players over the whole class.
  2. Simplify 18 over 24 if you can, then change it to a percentage.
  • You divided the class by the guitar players. The whole class goes on the bottom.
  • That is the decimal. Multiply by 100.

18 over 24 = 3 over 4 = 0.75, and 0.75 × 100 = 75%.

A recipe uses 240 g of butter. For a big batch you need 150% of that amount. How many grams of butter is that?

  1. 150% is more than 100%, so the answer is bigger than 240 g.
  2. Change 150% to a decimal and multiply.
  • 150% is 1.5, not 0.15.
  • That is only the extra 50%. You need the 240 g as well.

150% = 1.5. 1.5 × 240 = 360 g. (100% is 240 g and 50% is 120 g.)

Use these like a real paper. The marks tell you how much to write. A calculator is fine here.

Write your working on paper, then type the answer.

  • A laptop costs £450 before VAT.
  • VAT is added at 20%.
  • In a sale, the price with VAT is reduced by 10%.

[3 marks] Work out the sale price. Answer in pounds.

  1. Do it in two steps. First find the price with VAT.
  2. The sale then takes 10% off that new price, not off £450.
  3. Which multiplier takes 10% off?
  • That is the price with VAT, before the sale. The 10% off still has to come off.
  • That takes 10% off £450 and misses the VAT. VAT first, then the sale.
  • You took 10% of £450 away from £540. The sale is 10% of £540.

450 × 1.2 = 540 (price with VAT). Then 540 × 0.9 (10% off the price with VAT) = 486. The sale price is £486.

  • M1 for 450 × 1.2 = 540
  • M1 for 540 × 0.9
  • A1 for 486
  • Tia buys a bike in a 15% sale.
  • She pays £255.

(a) [3 marks] Work out the original price of the bike. Answer in pounds.

  1. After 15% off, Tia pays what percentage of the original?
  2. So 255 is that percentage of the original. Undo it.
  • You added 15% of £255. The 15% was taken from the original price.
  • That takes another 15% off £255. Divide by the multiplier instead.

100% − 15% = 85%, so the multiplier is 0.85. Then 255 ÷ 0.85 = £300.

  • M1 for the multiplier 0.85
  • M1 for 255 ÷ 0.85
  • A1 for £300

(b) [2 marks] The shop paid £240 for the bike. What is the shop’s percentage profit on that cost when it sells the bike to Tia for £255? Give your answer like 12.5%.

  1. Profit = selling price minus cost.
  2. Percentage profit is the profit divided by the cost (the original amount), times 100.
  • You divided by £255. Profit is compared with what the shop paid, £240.
  • That is the profit in pounds. Divide by the cost and multiply by 100.

Profit = 255 − 240 = 15, and 15 ÷ 240 = 0.0625, and 0.0625 × 100 = 6.25%.

  • M1 for profit = 255 − 240 = 15, and 15 ÷ 240
  • A1 for 6.25%

[2 marks] A price of £100 goes up by 20% and then goes down by 20%. Work out the final price. Answer in pounds.

  1. Do the increase first and write down the new price.
  2. The decrease is worked out on that new price.
  • That would be true only if the 20% fall were 20% of £100. It is 20% of the new price.
  • That is the price after the rise. The fall has to come off it.

100 × 1.2 = 120, then 120 × 0.8 = 96. The final price is £96. The 20% fall is taken from the bigger £120, so it takes off more (£24) than the rise added (£20).

  • M1 for 100 × 1.2 = 120
  • A1 for 120 × 0.8 = 96

Why it works

  • A percentage is a fraction of 100. That is why you can divide by 100, make fractions over 100, and add percentages together: 10% + 5% = 15% for the same reason 10 over 100 + 5 over 100 = 15 over 100.
  • One multiplier does the whole job. Adding 15% to an amount means 100% + 15% = 115%, so it is the same as multiplying by 1.15. You skip the extra adding step.
  • Reverse percentages divide. If × 1.2 made the new price, then ÷ 1.2 gets back to the old price. Each operation has an opposite.
  • Percentage change divides by the original. The question is “what fraction of where I started?”, so the starting amount goes on the bottom.

Common traps

  • Forgetting to divide by 100 (30% is 0.3, not 30).
  • Giving the amount of the change when the question asks for the new amount, or the other way round.
  • Taking a percentage off the new amount when you are working backwards. Divide by the multiplier.
  • Dividing by the new amount instead of the original in a percentage change.
  • Mixing units, such as cm and m, in “one amount as a percentage of another”.

Going further

A price rises by 25%. By what percentage must the new price fall to get back to the start? Give your answer like 12.5%. (Try starting with £100.)

  1. £100 rises 25% to what?
  2. You need to take off the £25 you added. What percentage of the new price is that?
  • The fall is a percentage of the new, bigger price, so it needs to be smaller than 25%.
  • That is the new price. Compare the £25 you must take off with it.

£100 becomes £125. To return to £100 you take off £25. 25 over 125 = 0.2, so the fall is 20%. In multipliers: 1.25 × 0.8 = 1.

Without a calculator, find 4% of 75. Here is a trick: 4% of 75 is the same as 75% of 4.

  1. 75% is three quarters. How would you find three quarters of 4?
  2. Check it the long way: 1% of 75 is 0.75.
  • You forgot to divide by 100. Use the trick: 75% of 4.
  • Check your decimal point. A quarter of 4 is 1, and you need three of them.

75% of 4 = 3 over 4 × 4 = 3. The long way: 1% of 75 = 0.75, so 4% = 4 × 0.75 = 3.

Why is a% of b always the same as b% of a? For example, 8% of 25 = 25% of 8 = 2.

  • Both are a × b ÷ 100, and multiplying works in either order
  • It is only true when a = b
  • Percentages always cancel out. It is a coincidence of the numbers chosen.
  1. Write a% of b as a fraction of b: a over 100, times b.
  2. Now write b% of a the same way. What do you notice about the two products?

a% of b = a over 100 × b = a × b ÷ 100. b% of a = b over 100 × a = b × a ÷ 100. Multiplication can be done in either order, so they are equal.

A radio costs £96 with 20% VAT included. How many pounds of the £96 is VAT?

  1. Be careful: 20% of £96 is not the VAT, because the 20% was added to a smaller price.
  2. First find the price without VAT. Then the VAT is the difference.
  • 20% of £96 is not the VAT. VAT is 20% of the price without VAT.
  • That is the price without VAT. The question asks for the VAT.

96 ÷ 1.2 = 80 is the price without VAT. VAT = 96 − 80 = £16. Check: 20% of 80 = 16. ✓

  2. Adding 20% means multiplying by 1.2.
  3. Work out 84.50 × 1.2.
  • That is only the VAT. Add it on to the price.
  • You added 20 pounds. VAT is 20% of the price, not 20 pounds.

A merchant gives a plumber 15% trade discount on a basin listed at £132. What does the plumber pay, in pounds? Type it like 42.75.

  2. Taking off 15% means multiplying by 0.85 (that is 100% − 15%).

  • That is the discount. Take it off the list price.

A plumber sells a shower valve for £126.50 after adding a 15% markup to what she paid. What did she pay, in pounds? Type it like 42.75.

  1. Is £126.50 the price she paid, or the price after the markup?
  2. The selling price is 115% of what she paid, which is × 1.15.
  3. To undo a × 1.15, divide by 1.15.
  • You took 15% off the selling price. The selling price is 115% of the cost, so divide by 1.15.
  • That is 15% of the selling price. Divide the selling price by 1.15 instead.

The selling price is 115% of what she paid, so what she paid = 126.50 ÷ 1.15 = 110. She paid £110. (Check: 110 × 1.15 = 126.50 ✓.)

[3 marks] A boiler service costs £95 plus VAT at 20%. A landlord gets 10% off the price before VAT. How much does the landlord pay in total, in pounds? Type it like 42.75.

  1. Which comes first, the discount or the VAT?
  2. The discount is × 0.9 and the VAT is × 1.2.
  3. Work out 95 × 0.9, then multiply the answer by 1.2.

The supply to a socket is 230 V. Along a long cable the voltage drops by 5 V. What is the voltage drop as a percentage of 230 V? Give your answer to 2 decimal places, as a number without the % sign.

  1. Is the question asking for the drop as a part of the 230 V supply, or the voltage left at the end?
  2. Percentage = part ÷ whole × 100. Which number is the part and which is the whole?
  3. Work out 5 ÷ 230, then multiply by 100.
  • You divided 230 by 5. Put the part (5 V) over the whole (230 V).
  • That is the decimal. Multiply by 100 to turn it into a percentage.

The part is 5 V and the whole is 230 V. 5 ÷ 230 = 0.0217..., and × 100 gives 2.17... So the voltage drop is 2.17% (2 d.p.).
