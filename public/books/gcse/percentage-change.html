    Repeated percentage change

    When something grows or shrinks by the same percentage again and again, you multiply by the same multiplier again and again. That is compound interest and depreciation, and it runs backwards too.

    Where you will use this: savings and loans, what a van or laptop is worth after a few years, a game or town that keeps growing, and the cost of materials that rise every year.

    Words you need

    Tap a teal word anywhere on the page to see this again.

    Compound interest
    Interest that is added to the account each year, so later interest is worked out on the interest as well.
    Multiplier
    A single number you multiply a quantity by to make a percentage change in one step.
    Depreciation
    When something loses value over time. If it loses the same percentage of its value each year, this is compound depreciation.
    Percentage change
    How much an amount has changed, written as a percentage of the starting amount: change divided by original, times 100.

    Increase £200 by 5%. Answer in pounds.

    Quick recap of the layer below (skip if you are happy)

    What multiplier increases an amount by 12%? Give it as a decimal.

    1. After the increase you have 100% + 12% of the amount.
    2. Write that percentage as a decimal.
    • That finds 12% of the amount only. To increase, keep the whole amount as well.
    • That is a 12% decrease.

    100% + 12% = 112% = 1.12.

    After a 20% discount a coat costs £48. What was the original price? Answer in pounds.

    1. After 20% off, you pay what percentage of the original?
    2. Divide the sale price by that multiplier.
    • You added 20% of £48. The 20% came off the original price, so divide by 0.8.
    • That takes another 20% off. Work backwards.

    100% − 20% = 80% = 0.8. 48 ÷ 0.8 = £60.

    A price rises from £40 to £46. What is the percentage increase? Give your answer like 12%.

    1. Work out the change first: new minus old.
    2. Divide the change by the original and multiply by 100.
    • You divided by the new price, £46. Divide by the original, £40.
    • That is the change in pounds. Divide it by the original and multiply by 100.

    Change = 46 − 40 = 6. 6 ÷ 40 = 0.15, and 0.15 × 100 = 15%.

    1. Compound interest and repeated growth Higher

    On the last page, simple interest paid the same amount every year because it was always worked out on the starting amount. Compound interest is different. The interest is added to the account each year, so next year's interest is worked out on the bigger amount.

    In year 1 you multiply by the Multiplier, say 1.04. In year 2 you multiply by 1.04 again. After 3 years you have multiplied by 1.04 three times, which is 1.043.

    final amount = start × multipliern

    Key idea: n changes of the same percentage means multipliern. It does NOT mean multiplying the percentage by n. Three rises of 4% are not a 12% rise: it is 1.043 = 1.124864, a rise of 12.4864%.

    Try it: what is the overall multiplier for 5% growth each year for 2 years? (Write it as 1.052.)

    On a calculator, type the power with the ^ or xy key: 500 × 1.04 ^ 3. Only round at the very end, to the nearest penny for money.

    Worked example

    Work out the value of £500 invested at 4% compound interest for 3 years.

    1. Multiplier: 100% + 4% = 104% = 1.04.
    2. Three years: 500 × 1.043 = 562.432.
    3. To the nearest penny: £562.43.
    4. Compare simple interest: 3 × £20 = £60, so £560. Compound interest gives £2.43 more, because years 2 and 3 earn interest on interest.

    Zara invests £500 at 4% compound interest for 2 years. How much interest does she earn altogether? Give your answer in pounds, like 4.50.

    2. Interest is the final value minus what she started with.

    A town of 12000 people grows by 2% each year. How many people live there after 5 years? Give your answer to the nearest whole number.

    2. Use it 5 times. People are whole, so round at the very end.

    • That is only one year of growth. You need five years.

    A game starts with 5000 players. The number of players grows by 10% every week. How many players are there after 3 weeks?

    1. What is the multiplier for a 10% rise?
    2. Multiply by it once for each week.

    • That is only one week.

    £2000 is saved at 5% for 2 years. How many pounds more interest does compound interest give than simple interest? Give your answer in pounds.

    1. Simple interest: 5% of £2000 is the same each year. Work out the total for 2 years.
    2. Compound interest: 2000 × 1.052 gives the total. Subtract 2000 to get the interest, then compare.
    • That is the compound interest on its own. The question asks how much more it is than simple interest.
    • That is the simple interest. Find the difference from the compound interest.

    Simple: 2 × £100 = £200. Compound: 2000 × 1.1025 = 2205, so interest £205. Difference: 205 − 200 = £5.

    2. Percentage change and combining changes Higher

    Two kinds of question share this section. First, the Percentage change between a starting value and a final value:

    percentage change = (change ÷ original) × 100

    Second, when different percentage changes follow each other, multiply their multipliers to get one overall multiplier for all the changes together. Then read the overall percentage change from it.

    Key idea: a multiplier above 1 is an increase and below 1 is a decrease. 1.32 is a 32% increase. 0.85 is a 15% decrease. To find the percentage change, work out how far the multiplier is from 1 and write that as a percentage.

    Try it: what percentage change is a multiplier of 0.9? (It leaves 90%, so 10% is lost.)

    Worked example: two different changes

    A price rises by 20% and then falls by 10%. What is the overall percentage change?

    1. Multipliers: 1.2 for the rise and 0.9 for the fall.
    2. Overall: 1.2 × 0.9 = 1.08.
    3. 1.08 is 108% of the start, so the overall change is an 8% increase.
    4. Check with £100: £120, then 0.9 × 120 = £108. ✓

    Now you finish it

    A price rises by 50% and then falls by 50%. What is the overall change?

    1. Multipliers: 1.5 and ___.
    2. Overall multiplier: 1.5 × ___ = ___.
    3. That is ___% of the start, so the price has ___ (rise or fall) by ___%.
    Check my answer

    1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75. That is 75% of the start, so the price has fallen by 25%. The fall is taken from the bigger amount, so it does not cancel the rise.

    A coat is reduced from £400 to £340. What is the percentage decrease? Give your answer like 12%.

    The population of a village goes from 2400 to 3000. What is the percentage increase? Give your answer like 12%.

    2. Divide by the original population.

    • That is the change in people. Divide by the original and multiply by 100.

    A rent rises by 10% and then, a year later, by another 20%. What is the overall percentage increase? Give your answer like 12%.

    1. Write the multiplier for each rise.
    2. Multiply the two multipliers, then read off the percentage.
    • You added 10% and 20%. The 20% is of the bigger, already-increased rent, so multiply the multipliers.
    • That is the overall multiplier. The question asks for the percentage increase.

    1.1 × 1.2 = 1.32, which is 132% of the start. That is a 32% increase.

    A price rises by 20% and then falls by 20%. What is the overall change?

    • No change at all
    • A 4% decrease
    • A 4% increase
    • A 40% decrease
    1. Write the multiplier for each change.
    2. Multiply them. Is the answer above or below 1?

    Show that a price that rises by 10% each year for 2 years has risen by 21% overall. Type the overall percentage increase you find, like 12%.

    1. What is the multiplier for a 10% rise?
    2. Two years means multiplying by it twice. Then take away 1.
    • That adds 10% + 10%. The second 10% is of the larger amount, so it is bigger.
    • That is the overall multiplier. The question asks for the percentage increase.

    1.12 = 1.21, which is 121% of the start. That is a 21% increase. (Two lots of 10% would only make 20%.)

    A price rises by 4% and then falls by 2%. What is the overall percentage change, to 2 decimal places? Give your answer like 1.25%.

    1. Multipliers: 1.04 for the rise and what multiplier for the fall?
    2. Multiply them on a calculator, then work out how far the result is from 1.
    • That is 4% − 2%. The fall is of the bigger, increased price, so the overall change is a little smaller.
    • That is the overall multiplier. Take 1 away and write it as a percentage.

    1.04 × 0.98 = 1.0192. That is 101.92% of the start, an increase of 1.92%.

    3. Depreciation: repeated decrease Higher

    Many things lose value over time. A van, a laptop or a guitar is worth less each year. If it loses the same percentage each year, that is Depreciation. It works exactly like compound interest, but the multiplier is below 1.

    Key idea: a loss of 10% a year does NOT mean a loss of 20% after 2 years. Each year you lose 10% of what is left, which is a little less each time. Use 0.9n.

    Try it: what is the multiplier for a 15% fall each year? (100% − 15% = 85%.)

    Worked example

    A car is worth £12000. It loses 15% of its value each year. What is it worth after 3 years?

    1. Multiplier: 100% − 15% = 85% = 0.85.
    2. 12000 × 0.853 = 12000 × 0.614125 = 7369.5.
    3. The car is worth £7369.50.
    4. Sense check: adding three losses of 15% would give 45% off. 45% off £12000 leaves £6600, and the real value is higher because each year’s loss is smaller. ✓

    Now you finish it

    A machine worth £8000 depreciates by 10% a year. What is it worth after 2 years?

    1. Multiplier: 100% − 10% = ___.
    2. 8000 × (___)2 = £___.
    Check my answer

    8000 × 0.92 = 8000 × 0.81 = £6480.

    Check your knowledge: depreciation Higher

    A £1000 scooter loses 10% of its value each year. What is it worth after 2 years? Answer in pounds.

    1. Look at the picture. What happens at each arrow?
    2. Multiply 1000 by 0.9 twice, or by 0.92.
    • That takes off 10% of £1000 twice. In year 2 the 10% is of a smaller amount.
    • That is only one year. Multiply by 0.9 again.

    0.92 = 0.81. 1000 × 0.81 = £810.

    What multiplier shows a loss of 8% each year? Give it as a decimal.

    1. After the loss, what percentage of the value is left?
    2. Write that percentage as a decimal.
    • That is the 8% that is lost. The multiplier is what is left.
    • That is a growth of 8%. For a loss, take away from 100%.

    100% − 8% = 92% = 0.92.

    A van is worth £5000. It loses 20% of its value each year. What is it worth after 2 years? Answer in pounds.

    2. Use it twice.
    • That takes 20% of £5000 off twice (40%). The second 20% is of a smaller amount.

    A laptop is worth £900. It loses 30% of its value in year 1, and then 10% of its value in year 2. What is it worth after 2 years? Answer in pounds.

    2. Multiply the start by the year 1 multiplier and then by the year 2 multiplier.

    A guitar worth £450 depreciates by 6% each year. What is it worth after 4 years? Give your answer in pounds to the nearest penny, like 123.45.

    2. Raise it to the power 4 and multiply by 450. Round only at the end.
    • That takes 6% of £450 off four times (24%). Each year the 6% is of a smaller amount, so use 0.944.
    • That is only one year of depreciation.

    A £1000 tablet loses 10% of its value each year. After how many whole years is it first worth less than £700?

    1. Work out the value after 1 year, 2 years, 3 years, and so on, by multiplying by 0.9 each time.
    2. Stop at the first year when the value is under £700.
    • After 3 years it is worth £729, which is still more than £700.
    • It is under £700 a year earlier than that.

    After 1 year: £900. 2 years: £810. 3 years: £729. 4 years: £656.10. So it is first under £700 after 4 years.

    A car loses 10% of its value each year. After 2 years, how much of its original value has it lost?

    • 10%
    • 20%
    • 19%
    1. Use £100. What is it worth after year 1, then after year 2?
    2. How much has been lost from the £100?

    £100 becomes £90 and then £81. It has lost £19, which is 19%. (0.92 = 0.81.)

    Flip it: working back to the start Higher

    So far you knew the start and found the end. Now go the other way: find the start, or find the yearly percentage rate.

    Forwards

    £1000 grows 10% a year for 2 years = ?

    Multiply by 1.12. Find the final value.

    Backwards

    ? grows 10% a year for 2 years = £1210

    Divide by 1.12. Find the starting value. This is a Reverse percentage.

    Key idea: to undo n changes, divide by multipliern. To find the rate when you know the start, the end and the number of years n, find the multiplier first: it is the n-th root of (end ÷ start).

    Worked example: find the start

    After 2 years of 10% compound growth an account holds £1210. How much was paid in?

    1. Multiplier for 2 years: 1.12 = 1.21.
    2. Final = start × 1.21, so start = 1210 ÷ 1.21.
    3. 1210 ÷ 1.21 = £1000.
    4. Check forwards: 1000 × 1.21 = 1210. ✓

    Now you finish it

    After 2 years of 20% depreciation a van is worth £5760. What was it worth new?

    1. Multiplier for 2 years: 0.82 = ___.
    2. New value = 5760 ÷ ___.
    Check my answer

    0.82 = 0.64 and 5760 ÷ 0.64 = £9000. Check: 0.64 × 9000 = 5760.

    Worked example: find the rate

    £2000 grows to £2205 in 2 years at the same compound rate each year. What is the yearly rate?

    1. Total multiplier: 2205 ÷ 2000 = 1.1025.
    2. This is the yearly multiplier squared (2 years), so take the square root: √1.1025 = 1.05.
    3. 1.05 means 100% + 5%. The rate is 5% a year.
    4. For 3 years you would take the cube root.

    Spot the mistake

    Finding an error in someone else's working helps you learn. For each one, decide what went wrong.

    Zoe says: £2000 at 5% compound interest for 3 years is 2000 × 1.15 = £2300. What went wrong?

    • She added 5% three times instead of multiplying.
    • 5% should be written as 1.5, not 1.05.
    • Nothing is wrong. Three lots of 5% make 15%, so 1.15 is correct.
    1. Work out 1.05 × 1.05 × 1.05. Is it 1.15?
    2. The multiplier for 5% is 1.05. Is it used once or three times?

    1.053 = 1.157625, not 1.15. 2000 × 1.157625 = £2315.25. Compound growth multiplies each year; it does not add 5% to the same starting amount.

    Leo says: a van loses 20% of its value in each of two years, so it has lost 40% of its value. What went wrong?

    • He added the 20%s, but the second is of a smaller amount.
    • The van really lost more than 40% of its value.
    • He should have squared 0.2 instead of adding the percentages.
    1. Start with £100 and do the two years one at a time.
    2. In year 2, what is 20% of the new, smaller amount?

    £100 → £80 → £64. The van has lost £36, which is 36%. In multipliers: 0.82 = 0.64, so 64% is left.

    • M1 for 6000 × 1.0253
    • A1 for £6461.34

    (b) [1 mark] How much interest has Dev earned in the 3 years? Give your answer in pounds, like 123.45.

    1. Use your answer to part (a).
    2. Interest is the final value minus the starting value.
    • That is the final value. Take away the £6000 Dev started with.

    6461.34 − 6000 = £461.34.

    • A car costs £18000 when new.
    • It loses 20% of its value in the first year.
    • It then loses 12% of its value in each following year.

    [4 marks] Work out the value of the car after 3 years. Give your answer in pounds, like 123.45.

    1. Year 1 and the later years use different multipliers. Write both.
    2. There is one year at the first multiplier and two years at the second.
    3. Write 18000 × (first multiplier) × (second multiplier)2.
    • You took 20% + 12% + 12% = 44% off 18000 in one go. Each year is a percentage of the value at that time.
    • That is the value after 2 years. There is one more year to go.

    Year 1 multiplier 0.8; later years 0.88. 18000 × 0.8 = 14400. Then 14400 × 0.882 = £11151.36.

    • M1 for the multipliers: 0.8 for year 1 and 0.88 for later years
    • M1 for 18000 × 0.8 = 14400
    • M1 for 14400 × 0.882
    • A1 for £11151.36

    [3 marks] A price rises by 5% and then falls by 10%. It is now £113.40. Work out the original price. Answer in pounds.

    1. Write the multiplier for each change.
    2. Multiply them to get one overall multiplier. Is it bigger or smaller than 1?
    3. Divide £113.40 by the overall multiplier.
    • That works forwards from £113.40. You need to go backwards: divide.
    • You added 10% onto £113.40. Divide by the overall multiplier instead.

    The multipliers are 1.05 and 0.9, so overall 1.05 × 0.9 = 0.945. Then 113.40 ÷ 0.945 = £120. Check: 120 × 1.05 = 126 and 126 × 0.9 = 113.40. ✓

    • M1 for the overall multiplier 1.05 × 0.9 = 0.945
    • M1 for 113.40 ÷ 0.945
    • A1 for £120

    Why it works

    • Each change multiplies the amount you have NOW. After year 1 you have a new, different amount, and year 2's percentage is taken from that. That is why changes multiply instead of adding.
    • Repeated multiplying is a power. 1.04 × 1.04 × 1.04 is 1.043, the same way 2 × 2 × 2 is 23.
    • Going back divides. Dividing by 1.04 undoes one multiplication by 1.04. Dividing by 1.043 undoes three.
    • Finding a rate uses a root. If m2 = 1.1025 then m = √1.1025. A root is the opposite of a power.

    Common traps

    • Adding the percentages (10% twice is 20%) instead of multiplying the multipliers (21%).
    • Multiplying the percentage by the number of years, which is simple interest.
    • Rounding in the middle of a calculation. Round at the end.
    • Taking a percentage off the final amount when working back. Divide by the multiplier.
    • Dividing by the new value instead of the original in a percentage change.

    Going further Higher

    A price rises by 10%. By what percentage must it then fall to return to the original? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, like 12.5%.

    1. Call the original price 1. After the rise it is 1.1. What multiplier takes 1.1 back to 1?
    2. That multiplier is 1 ÷ 1.1. Work it out and see how far below 1 it is.
    • The fall is taken from the bigger, increased price, so it must be smaller than 10%.
    • Right idea. The question asks for 1 decimal place.

    1 ÷ 1.1 = 0.90909…, so the multiplier is about 0.909. That is a fall of about 9.1%. Check: 1.1 × 0.909… = 1.

    Account A pays 6% simple interest a year on £1000. Account B pays 5% compound interest a year on £1000. After how many whole years is account B first worth more than account A?

    1. Account A grows by the same amount each year: £60. Write its value after each year.
    2. Account B is 1000 × 1.05n. Compare the two, year by year.
    • After 6 years A has £1360 and B has about £1340, so A is still ahead.
    • B is ahead a year earlier than that.

    After 8 years: A = £1480 and B = 1000 × 1.058 = £1477.46. A is still ahead. After 9 years: A = £1540 and B = £1551.33. So B first wins after 9 years. Compound growth starts slower but grows faster.

    A price p rises by x% and then falls by x%. Which expression gives the final price?

    • p × (1 + x/100) × (1 − x/100)
    • p, because a rise and a fall of the same percentage cancel exactly
    • p × (1 + x/100)2, because the same change happens twice
    • p × (1 + x/100) − x/100, taking the fall off at the end
    1. A rise of x% has multiplier 1 + x/100. What is the multiplier for a fall of x%?
    2. Do the two changes one after the other, so multiply.

    Rise: × (1 + x/100). Fall: × (1 − x/100). Together: p × (1 + x/100) × (1 − x/100). This equals p × (1 − x2/10000), which is a little less than p whenever x is above 0.

    After a price rises by x% and then falls by x%, the overall multiplier is 1 − x2/10000. Use x = 30. What is the overall multiplier? Give it as a decimal.

    1. Work out x2 first: 30 × 30.
    2. Divide it by 10000 and take the result away from 1.
    • That is x2/10000. The multiplier is 1 minus that.
    • That is a plain 30% fall. A rise first changes it. Use 1 − x2/10000.

    302 = 900 and 900 ÷ 10000 = 0.09. So the multiplier is 1 − 0.09 = 0.91. Check: 1.3 × 0.7 = 0.91. ✓ The price ends 9% lower.
