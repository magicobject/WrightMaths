Solving equations

An equation is a puzzle with a missing number. Learn to undo it step by step, then flip it round and build your own puzzles.

GCSE Maths Book / Solving equations

Before you start: what this stands on

Maths is built like a pyramid, each topic standing on the ones below. This topic needs:

  • Algebra basics Algebra
  • Negative numbers Number

The "Last time" questions below are a quick check of that base. If they feel shaky, build the base first.

Warm-up: what do you already know?

Have a go before you read anything. Wrong answers are welcome: they show where to look.

I think of a number, double it, then add 3. My answer is 17. What was my number?

  1. Work backwards from 17. What was the last thing I did?
  2. Undo it: take 3 away. Then undo the doubling.
  • That is 17 − 3. I also doubled my number at the start, so there is one more step to undo.
  • You added 3 instead of taking it away. Working backwards means doing the opposite.

17 − 3 = 14, then 14 ÷ 2 = 7. You just solved 2x + 3 = 17.

Last time: the base this stands on

Work out −3 + 5.

  1. Start at −3 on a number line and move 5 to the right.

−3 + 5 = 2.

Work out 2 × (−4).

  1. Positive times negative is negative.
  • The sign is wrong: a positive times a negative gives a negative.

2 × (−4) = −8.

Work out 18 ÷ (−3).

  1. Positive divided by negative is negative.
  • The size is right but not the sign. Positive divided by negative is negative.

18 ÷ 3 = 6, and with one negative the answer is −6.

Work out 5 − (−2).

  1. Subtracting a negative is the same as adding the positive.
  • That is 5 − 2. Taking away a negative makes the answer bigger.

5 − (−2) = 5 + 2 = 7.

1. An equation is a balance

An Equation says two things are equal, like a set of balanced scales. The Unknown (variable) is a letter, usually x, standing for the number you want to find. To Solve the equation is to find the value that keeps it true.

x + 7 = 12

The best picture is a traditional kitchen scale: a pan on each side that only balances when both hold the same amount. The unknown is the mystery weight. Putting the right value for the unknown in is what makes both sides of the = the same.

Key idea: whatever you do to one side you must do to the other, so the kitchen scale stays balanced. To find x, use the inverse operation: the one that undoes what was done to x.

+ undoes −
− undoes +
× undoes ÷
÷ undoes ×

Worked example

Solve x + 7 = 12.

  1. 7 has been added to x. The inverse is to take away 7.
  2. Do it to both sides: x + 7 − 7 = 12 − 7.
  3. x = 5.
  4. Check: 5 + 7 = 12 ✓

Check your knowledge: one-step equations

Solve x + 7 = 12. Give x.

  1. What has been done to x?
  2. Do the opposite to both sides.
  • You added 7. The inverse of "+ 7" is "− 7".

x = 12 − 7 = 5.

Solve x − 4 = 9.

  1. The inverse of "− 4" is "+ 4".
  • You subtracted 4 again. Undo a subtraction by adding.

x = 9 + 4 = 13.

Solve 3x = 21.

  1. 3x means 3 × x. What undoes multiplying by 3?
  • You subtracted 3. 3x is multiplication, so divide.
  • You multiplied. Undo a multiplication by dividing.

x = 21 ÷ 3 = 7.

Solve x over 4 = 5.

  1. x over 4 means x ÷ 4. What undoes dividing by 4?
  • You divided again. Undo a division by multiplying.

x = 5 × 4 = 20. Check: 20 ÷ 4 = 5 ✓

2. Two steps

Most equations have two things done to x. Undo them in the reverse order to the way they were done. Think of getting dressed: socks, then shoes. To undo it you take off the shoes first.

Key idea: in 3x + 5 = 20, x was first multiplied by 3, then 5 was added. So undo the "+ 5" first, then the "× 3".

Worked example

Solve 3x + 5 = 20.

  1. Take 5 from both sides: 3x = 15.
  2. Divide both sides by 3: x = 5.
  3. Check: 3 × 5 + 5 = 20 ✓

Now you finish it

Solve x over 2 + 3 = 8.

  1. Take 3 from both sides: x over 2 = 5.
  2. Multiply both sides by 2: x = ___.

x = 10. Check: 10 ÷ 2 + 3 = 8 ✓

Check your knowledge: two-step equations

Solve 4x + 1 = 21.

  1. Undo the "+ 1" first.
  2. You get 4x = 20. Now undo the "× 4".
  • That is 4x. One more step: divide by 4.
  • You divided before taking away the 1. Undo the add or subtract first.

4x = 20, so x = 5. Check: 4 × 5 + 1 = 21 ✓

Solve 7x − 3 = 18.

  1. Undo the "− 3" by adding 3 to both sides.
  • You subtracted 3 again. To undo a minus, add.
  • That is 7x. Divide by 7 to finish.

7x = 21, so x = 3.

Solve x over 3 + 2 = 6.

  1. Take 2 from both sides first.
  2. Then undo the "÷ 3".
  • That is x over 3. One more step: multiply by 3.

x over 3 = 4, so x = 12. Check: 12 ÷ 3 + 2 = 6 ✓

Solve 12 − 2x = 4.

  1. Be careful: x has a minus in front of it. Take 12 from both sides to get −2x = −8.
  2. Or, add 2x to both sides to make the x positive: 12 = 4 + 2x.
  • Check the signs: −2x = −8 means x = (−8) ÷ (−2) = +4.
  • You took 4 from 12. Try again, or put x back in to check.

12 − 2x = 4 gives −2x = −8, so x = 4. Check: 12 − 2 × 4 = 4 ✓

Solve 3x + 10 = 4. The answer is negative.

  1. Take 10 from both sides: 3x = 4 − 10.
  • The size is right, but 4 − 10 is −6, so x is negative.

3x = −6, so x = −2. Check: 3 × (−2) + 10 = 4 ✓

3. Brackets and letters on both sides

With brackets you have two options: divide both sides by the number outside, or Expand (multiply out) the bracket first. If x appears on both sides, collect the x terms on one side, then the numbers on the other.

Worked example: brackets

Solve 3(x + 2) = 21.

  1. Divide both sides by 3: x + 2 = 7.
  2. Take 2 from both sides: x = 5.
  3. Check: 3 × (5 + 2) = 21 ✓

Worked example: x on both sides

Solve 5x + 3 = 2x + 15.

  1. Take 2x from both sides, so the x terms are on the left: 3x + 3 = 15.
  2. Take 3 from both sides: 3x = 12.
  3. Divide by 3: x = 4.
  4. Check: left 5 × 4 + 3 = 23; right 2 × 4 + 15 = 23 ✓

Key idea: the bigger x-term is the safer one to keep, so you end up with a positive number of x.

Check your knowledge: brackets and both sides

Solve 2(x + 3) = 14.

  1. Divide both sides by 2.
  • You took 3 away but forgot the 2 multiplies the whole bracket. Divide by 2 first.
  • That is x + 3. One more step.

x + 3 = 7, so x = 4.

Solve 4(x − 1) = 20.

  1. Divide both sides by 4, then deal with the − 1.
  • You subtracted 1 from 5. To undo "− 1", add 1.
  • That is x − 1. One more step.

x − 1 = 5, so x = 6.

Solve 7x − 4 = 3x + 16.

  1. Take 3x from both sides.
  2. Now you have 4x − 4 = 16. Add 4, then divide.
  • That is 4x. One more step: divide by 4.
  • Check the signs when moving the numbers: 4x − 4 = 16 means 4x = 20.

4x = 20, so x = 5. Check: left 7 × 5 − 4 = 31, right 3 × 5 + 16 = 31 ✓

Solve 2(3x − 1) = 4x + 8.

  1. Expand the bracket: 6x − 2.
  2. Collect: 6x − 4x = 8 + 2.
  • Check the signs: 6x − 2 = 4x + 8 gives 2x = 8 + 2 = 10. Adding 2 to both sides, not taking it away.
  • You forgot to multiply the −1 by 2 when expanding. 2(3x − 1) = 6x − 2, not 6x − 1.

6x − 2 = 4x + 8, so 2x = 10, x = 5. Check: 2(15 − 1) = 28 and 20 + 8 = 28 ✓

Flip it: build the equation

Solving goes from an equation to a number. Flip it: go from a number puzzle or a real situation to an equation. Being able to do both is what turns algebra from symbols into a tool.

Forwards

Solve 3x − 4 = 11

From the equation to x.

Backwards

"I think of a number, multiply by 3, take away 4, and get 11."

From the words to the equation.

Key idea: a number puzzle is an equation told in words. Call the number x and write each step in order: 3x − 4 = 11. To solve it, undo the steps in reverse: 11 + 4 = 15, then 15 ÷ 3 = 5.

Another reversal: checking. Put your answer back into the equation. If both sides match, you are right. If they do not, you will find the slip.

Check your knowledge: flip it

Is x = 3 a solution of 2x + 5 = 11?

  • Yes: 2 × 3 + 5 = 11
  • No: it gives 10
  • No: it gives 12
  1. Replace x with 3 and work out the left-hand side.

2 × 3 + 5 = 11, which equals the right-hand side, so x = 3 is a solution.

Which equation has the solution x = 6?

  • 2x − 3 = 9
  • 2x + 3 = 9
  • 3x − 6 = 9
  1. Put x = 6 into each one and see which gives the right-hand side.

2 × 6 − 3 = 9 ✓. The other two give 15 and 12 on the left.

I think of a number, multiply by 4, then subtract 6. The answer is 22. What is my number?

  1. Call the number x and write the equation: 4x − 6 = 22.
  2. Undo in reverse: add 6, then divide by 4.
  • You subtracted 6, but the puzzle subtracted 6 from 4x, so undo it by adding: 22 + 6 = 28, then divide by 4.

4x − 6 = 22 ⇒ 4x = 28 ⇒ x = 7. Check: 4 × 7 − 6 = 22 ✓

A pen costs £x. Four pens and a £2 ruler cost £10 altogether. Form an equation and find x (in pounds).

  1. Four pens cost 4x. Add the ruler: 4x + 2 = 10.
  • Check by putting it back: 4 × 3 + 2 = 14, not 10.
  • That is 4x. The question asks for the price of one pen.

4x + 2 = 10, so 4x = 8 and x = 2. A pen costs £2.

Tom is x years old. His sister is 3 years older. Their ages add up to 25. How old is Tom?

  1. Sister is x + 3. The sum is x + (x + 3) = 25.
  • You split 25 equally, but the ages are not equal: sister is 3 years older.
  • That is the sister's age. The question asks for Tom.

2x + 3 = 25, so 2x = 22 and x = 11. Tom is 11, his sister is 14 and 11 + 14 = 25 ✓

Spot the mistake

Ali says: "3x + 5 = 20, so 3x = 25." What went wrong?

  • He added 5 to both sides. To undo "+ 5" he should have subtracted 5.
  • He should have divided by 3 first
  • You cannot solve this kind of equation
  1. Check Ali's line by putting x = 25 ÷ 3 back in: it will not give 20.

The correct step is 3x = 20 − 5 = 15, so x = 5.

Bea says: "2(x + 3) = 10, so 2x + 3 = 10." What went wrong?

  • She multiplied only the x by 2. The 2 multiplies everything in the bracket, giving 2x + 6.
  • She should have subtracted 10 first
  • Nothing: her line is right
  1. Expand 2(x + 3). Does every term inside get multiplied by 2?

2x + 6 = 10, so 2x = 4 and x = 2.

Check your knowledge: mixed review

All kinds of equation shuffled together. Decide what has been done to x, then undo it.

Solve 5x − 3 = 22.

  1. Add 3 to both sides, then divide by 5.
  • That is 22 − 3. To undo "− 3" you add 3.
  • That is 5x. One more step: divide by 5.

5x = 25, so x = 5.

Solve 3(2x + 1) = 27.

  1. Divide both sides by 3 first.
  • Check: 2x + 1 = 9 means 2x = 8, not 10.

2x + 1 = 9, so 2x = 8 and x = 4. Check: 3(8 + 1) = 27 ✓

Solve x over 5 − 2 = 1.

  1. Add 2 to both sides, then multiply by 5.
  • That is x over 5. One more step: multiply by 5.

x over 5 = 3, so x = 15.

Solve 9x − 4 = 5x + 12.

  1. Take 5x from both sides: 4x − 4 = 12.
  • That is 4x. One more step: divide by 4.

4x = 16, so x = 4. Check: 36 − 4 = 32 and 20 + 12 = 32 ✓

The sum of three consecutive whole numbers is 48. What is the smallest of them?

  1. Call the smallest n. The others are n + 1 and n + 2.
  2. n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 48.
  • That is the middle number (48 ÷ 3). The smallest is one less.

3n + 3 = 48, so n = 15. The numbers are 15, 16, 17: 15 + 16 + 17 = 48 ✓

Exam style

A triangle has sides of length x cm, (2x + 1) cm and (3x − 2) cm. Its perimeter is 29 cm.

(a) [2 marks] Write down a simplified expression for the perimeter in terms of x. (Type it like 6x-1.)

  1. Add the three sides: x + (2x + 1) + (3x − 2).
  2. Collect the x terms (x + 2x + 3x) and then the numbers (+1 − 2).
  • Check the numbers: +1 and −2 make −1.
  • +1 and −2 make −1, not −3.

x + 2x + 3x = 6x and 1 − 2 = −1, so the perimeter is 6x − 1.

(b) [2 marks] Use the perimeter to form an equation and find x.

  1. 6x − 1 = 29.
  • That is 6x. One more step: divide both sides by 6.
  • That is 28 ÷ 6. First add 1 to both sides: 6x = 30.

6x = 30, so x = 5.

(c) [1 mark] What is the length of the longest side, in cm?

  1. Put x = 5 into each side to find all three.
  • That is the shortest side. Work out all three first.
  • That is the middle side, 2x + 1. The longest is 3x − 2.

The sides are 5, 11 and 13 cm. Check: 5 + 11 + 13 = 29 ✓

Why it works

  • Same on both sides. If A = B, then A + 5 = B + 5 and 3A = 3B. The equals sign is a promise, and doing the same to both sides keeps it.
  • Reverse order. The steps were done in one order, so the undoing happens in the opposite order. Shoes off before socks.
  • Checking is a reverse. Substituting your answer runs the equation forwards and tests the solution.

Common traps

  • Doing the operation to only one side.
  • Using the same operation instead of the inverse (adding when you should take away).
  • Dividing before undoing the plus or minus.
  • Forgetting that a bracket multiplies everything inside.
  • Losing a minus sign, especially with "12 − 2x".

Going further

Solve x + 1 over 3 = x − 3 over 2.

  1. Multiply both sides by 6 (the LCM of 3 and 2) to clear the fractions: 2(x + 1) = 3(x − 3).
  2. Expand: 2x + 2 = 3x − 9.

2x + 2 = 3x − 9 gives 11 = x. Check: 12 over 3 = 4 and 8 over 2 = 4 ✓

Solve 5 − 2x = 3x − 10.

  1. Add 2x to both sides so x is positive: 5 = 5x − 10.
  • Check the signs: 5 + 10 = 15, so 5x = 15.

5 = 5x − 10 ⇒ 15 = 5x ⇒ x = 3. Check: 5 − 6 = −1 and 9 − 10 = −1 ✓
