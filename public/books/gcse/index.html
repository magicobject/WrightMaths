Aiming high

Already confident? Go straight to the hard parts

Every topic page has a “Know the basics?” bar at the top. It jumps straight to exam style and going further, and can fold away the recaps and check sets. For real-paper practice, try the Grade 7–9 stretch papers: single questions worth 3 to 6 marks, labelled calculator or non-calculator, with a mark scheme you tick off yourself.

Not in this book yet

This book does not yet cover all of the Higher specification. Still to come, beyond the grey topics on the pyramid: surds in depth, functions and transformations of graphs, circle theorems, vectors, the sine and cosine rules, similarity and congruence, bounds, iteration, algebraic fractions and proof, histograms and cumulative frequency, and conditional probability. Use your own exam board’s specification alongside this book until they are added.

How it works

Five habits built into every page

Flip it

Maths comes in pairs, like multiply and divide. Learn one side, then flip it and run it backwards.

Words in teal

Tap a teal word to see what it means. The glossary has examples and common traps.

Give me a clue

Every question has hints, one at a time. Still stuck? Explain the problem to the duck. Saying it out loud often shows you the answer. You always do the last step yourself.

Check your knowledge

Quick questions after every chunk. Pulling it out of your head makes it stick. Want a mix? Try Mixed practice. Stuck on a question word? See Question words.

Build the base

Each topic says what it builds on. A topic feels hard? The gap is usually underneath. The “Before you start” box shows where to look.

Private by design

No accounts, no tracking, no adverts. Your progress and reading settings are saved only in this browser, on this device, and the book has no server collecting your answers. Anyone using this browser can see them. The guide for parents and carers has the details.

The method

How to get the most from each topic

  1. Try it first. Spend a few minutes before you look at any help. Being stuck is normal; it is where the learning happens.
  2. Use one hint at a time. After each hint, go back and try again.
  3. Read the key idea, then try once more before you read the full solution.
  4. Compare line by line with the worked solution and mark anything you missed.
  5. Read "Why it works" and the common traps. They help you understand the method, not just copy it.
  6. Come back in a week and do the Check your knowledge questions again. Returning after a gap is one of the best ways to make an idea last.
  7. Explain it. Can you tell a friend the key idea in two sentences? Then you have understood it.

Calculators: unless a question says otherwise, work without one first and then check with one.
