Shapes and angles

Angle facts, triangles, polygons and parallel lines: how to find a missing angle and give a reason for every step.

GCSE Maths Book / Shapes and angles

Before you start: what this stands on

Maths is built like a pyramid, each topic standing on the ones below. This topic needs:

  • Add, subtract, multiply, divide Number

The "Last time" questions below are a quick check of that base. If they feel shaky, build the base first.

Words you need

Tap a teal word anywhere on the page to see this again.

Angle
A measure of turn between two lines that meet at a point. It is measured in degrees: a full turn is 360°.
Acute angle
An angle that is less than 90°.
Obtuse angle
An angle that is more than 90° and less than 180°.
Reflex angle
An angle that is more than 180° and less than 360°.
Parallel
Lines that stay the same distance apart and never meet. They are marked with matching arrows.
Polygon
A closed flat shape with straight sides. A shape with n sides has interior angles that add up to (n − 2) × 180°.

Warm-up: what do you already know?

Quick warm-up. No marks, and nobody is watching.

Two angles sit side by side on a straight line. One is 110°. How big is the other, in degrees?

  1. A straight line is 180°.
  • You added 110 and 180. The two angles add up to 180.
  • The other angle is smaller: 180 − 110.

180 − 110 = 70°.

Quick recap (no marks)

Work out 180 − 74.

  1. 180 − 70 = 110, then take 4 more.
  • You borrowed wrongly. 180 − 70 = 110, then take 4 more.

180 − 74 = 106.

Work out 360 ÷ 5.

  1. 360 ÷ 10 = 36, and 5 is half of 10, so double it.

360 ÷ 5 = 72.

Work out 3 × 60.

  1. Three sixties.

3 × 60 = 180.

1. Measuring turns

An Angle measures a turn. We measure it in degrees. A full turn is 360°. There are four names to know:

acute: less than 90°
right angle: exactly 90°
obtuse: between 90° and 180°
reflex: between 180° and 360°

Key idea: 180° is a straight line. 360° is a full turn. Say the type of an angle first. That gives you an estimate to check your answer against.

Try it: is 100° acute, obtuse or reflex?

Check your knowledge: types of angle

An angle of 135° is…

  • acute
  • right
  • obtuse
  • reflex
  1. Is it bigger than 90° and smaller than 180°?

135° is between 90° and 180°, so it is obtuse.

Which of these is a reflex angle?

  • 45°
  • 120°
  • 270°
  1. Reflex means more than 180°.

270° is between 180° and 360°.

How many degrees are there in a full turn?

  1. A whole turn all the way round.
  • That is a half turn (a straight line).

A full turn is 360°.

How many degrees are there in a right angle?

  1. A quarter of a full turn.
  • That is a straight line (two right angles).

360 ÷ 4 = 90°.

2. Angle facts

Four facts handle most simple angle questions:

  • Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.
  • Angles around a point add up to 360°.
  • Vertically opposite angles (the “X” shape) are equal.
  • A right angle is 90°.

Key idea: to find a missing angle, pick the fact that fits. Write the total (180 or 360). Then subtract the angles you know. Give the reason too, for example “angles on a straight line add up to 180°”. The reason earns marks in exams.

Try it: angles on a straight line are 50° and x. Find x.

Worked example

Angles around a point are 90°, 120° and 100°, and x. Find x.

  1. Angles around a point add up to 360°.
  2. 90 + 120 + 100 = 310.
  3. x = 360 − 310 = 50°.

Check: 90 + 120 + 100 + 50 = 360. ✓

Check your knowledge: angle facts

Angles on a straight line are 65° and x. Find x.

  1. They add up to 180°.
  • You added to 360. A straight line is 180°.
  • You subtracted from 90. A straight line is 180°.

180 − 65 = 115°.

Angles around a point are 90°, 120°, 100° and x. Find x.

  1. They add up to 360°.
  • You used 180. Around a point the total is 360°.
  • That is the sum of the angles you know.

360 − 310 = 50°.

Two straight lines cross. One of the angles is 48°. How big is the angle directly opposite it?

  1. Vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • That is the angle next to it (180 − 48). The opposite angle is equal.

Vertically opposite angles are equal, so 48°.

Two angles on a straight line are x and 2x. Find x.

  1. Together: x + 2x = 3x = 180.
  • That treats both angles as x. The second is double.
  • That is 2x. The question asks for x.

3x = 180, so x = 60° (and the other angle is 120°).

A right angle is split into two angles. One is 35°. Find the other.

  1. They add to 90°.
  • That uses 180. A right angle is 90°.

90 − 35 = 55°.

3. Triangles

The three angles inside any triangle add up to 180°.

An isosceles triangle has two equal sides. The two angles opposite the equal sides (the base angles) are equal.

An equilateral triangle has all sides equal. All its angles are 60°.

Key idea: a triangle totals 180. A quadrilateral totals 360. A full turn totals 360. Pick the total that fits the shape, then subtract.

Try it: two angles of a triangle are 80° and 40°. Find the third.

Worked example: isosceles

An isosceles triangle has an angle of 40° at the top between the two equal sides. Find a base angle.

  1. The angles add up to 180°.
  2. The two base angles are equal, so share what is left: 180 − 40 = 140.
  3. 140 ÷ 2 = 70° each.

Check your knowledge: triangles

Two angles of a triangle are 50° and 70°. Find the third angle.

  1. The three add to 180°.
  • That adds the two you know. Subtract from 180.
  • That is 180 − 50. Subtract both angles.

50 + 70 = 120, so 180 − 120 = 60°.

An isosceles triangle has a 40° angle between its two equal sides. Find each base angle.

  1. Share 180 − 40 between the two equal angles.
  • That is both base angles together. Halve it. Share it between the two.

(180 − 40) ÷ 2 = 70°.

A right-angled triangle has one other angle of 35°. Find the third angle.

  1. The three angles add to 180°, and one is 90°.
  • That is 180 − 35. Remember the right angle too.

180 − 90 − 35 = 55°.

What is each angle of an equilateral triangle?

  1. The three angles are equal and add to 180°.
  • That is a right angle. 3 × 90 = 270, not 180.

180 ÷ 3 = 60°.

The angles of a triangle are x, 2x and 3x. Find x.

  1. x + 2x + 3x = 6x = 180.
  • That is 2x. The question asks for x.
  • That is the total, not x.

6x = 180, so x = 30°. (The angles are 30°, 60° and 90°.)

4. Quadrilaterals and polygons

The four angles inside any quadrilateral add up to 360°. A polygon is a flat shape with straight sides. Pentagons and hexagons are polygons.

Polygon facts

  • An inside angle is an interior angle.
  • Cut any shape into triangles from one corner.
  • A triangle is 180°.
  • Count the triangles and multiply by 180.
  • A shape with n sides gives n − 2 triangles.
  • So the angles add up to (n − 2) × 180°.

Regular polygons

  • In a regular polygon all sides are equal.
  • All the angles are equal too.
  • Each interior angle is the total ÷ n.

Now walk round the shape and turn at each corner. You turn 360° in total. In a regular shape every turn is equal, so each turn is 360 ÷ number of sides.

A turn at a corner is an exterior angle. Interior angle + exterior angle = 180°. They sit on a straight line.

Key idea: the exterior angles of a polygon always add up to 360°. The interior angles add up to (n − 2) × 180°.

Try it: how many triangles can you cut a hexagon into from one corner?

Worked example

Find each interior angle of a regular hexagon.

  1. A hexagon has 6 sides: (6 − 2) × 180 = 720°.
  2. Regular, so each angle is 720 ÷ 6 = 120°.

Check with the turn at each corner: 360 ÷ 6 = 60, and 180 − 60 = 120. ✓

Check your knowledge: polygons

Three angles of a quadrilateral are 90°, 110° and 70°. Find the fourth.

  1. The four angles add to 360°.
  • You left out one angle. Subtract all three from 360.
  • That adds the three you know. Subtract from 360.

360 − 270 = 90°.

Work out the sum of the interior angles of a pentagon.

  1. A pentagon has 5 sides: (5 − 2) × 180.
  • That is 5 × 180. Use n − 2 = 3 triangles.

3 × 180 = 540°.

Work out the sum of the interior angles of a hexagon.

  1. (6 − 2) × 180.
  • That is 6 × 180. Use n − 2 = 4.

4 × 180 = 720°.

Find each exterior angle (turn) of a regular octagon.

  1. An octagon has 8 sides. The turns add to 360°.
  • That is the interior angle. The question asks for the turn.

360 ÷ 8 = 45°.

Find each interior angle of a regular hexagon.

  1. 720 ÷ 6, or 180 − 60.
  • That is the turn. The interior angle is 180 − 60.

(6 − 2) × 180 ÷ 6 = 120°.

5. Parallel lines

Lines that never meet are parallel. They stay the same distance apart. Mark them with arrows.

A third line crosses the pair. It makes angles that are linked:

  • Corresponding angles (an F shape) are equal.
  • Alternate angles (a Z shape) are equal.
  • Co-interior angles (a C shape) add up to 180°.

See the three shapes on their own:

Key idea: when you use one of these facts, name it: “alternate angles are equal”. The name is the reason. The reason earns the mark. Vertically opposite angles and angles on a straight line can help you reach the angle you need.

Try it: parallel lines. One angle is 60°. What is its alternate angle?

Check your knowledge: parallel lines

Two parallel lines are crossed by a third. One angle is 70°. What is the alternate angle?

  • 70°
  • 110°
  • 20°
  1. Alternate angles make a Z shape and are equal.

70°. Alternate angles are equal.

Parallel lines: one angle is 70°. Find the co-interior angle.

  1. Co-interior angles add to 180°.
  • That is the alternate angle. Co-interior angles add to 180°.
  • That is 360 − 70. Use 180, not 360.

180 − 70 = 110°.

Parallel lines: one angle is 70°. Find the corresponding angle.

  1. Corresponding angles make an F shape and are equal.
  • That is the co-interior angle. Corresponding angles are equal.

70°. Corresponding angles are equal.

Which pair of angles adds up to 180° when the lines are parallel?

  • Alternate
  • Corresponding
  • Co-interior
  1. Think of the C shape.

Co-interior angles add to 180°.

Flip it: find the angle, or build the shape

So far you have gone one way. Now run it backwards. Same idea, other direction.

Forwards

A regular polygon has 10 sides. Find the exterior angle: 360 ÷ 10 = 36°.

Sides to angle.

Backwards

A regular polygon has exterior angles of 36°. How many sides? 360 ÷ 36 = 10.

Angle to sides.

Key idea: angle facts work both ways. Use them to find a missing angle. Or use angles to check that a shape can exist.

Another reverse: three angles make a triangle only if they add up to exactly 180°.

Check your knowledge: flip it

A regular polygon has exterior angles of 36°. How many sides does it have?

  1. Exterior angles add to 360°. How many 36s fit?
  • That is the turn. Divide 360 by it to count the sides.
  • That is 360 − 36. Divide 360 by 36.

360 ÷ 36 = 10 sides.

Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 150°. How many sides does it have?

  1. Find the exterior angle first: 180 − 150.
  2. Then 360 ÷ that.
  • That is the turn. Divide 360 by it for the sides.

Exterior angle = 30°. 360 ÷ 30 = 12 sides.

Could a triangle have angles 50°, 60° and 80°?

  • Yes
  • No: they add up to 190°
  1. Add the three angles. Do they make 180°?

50 + 60 + 80 = 190, not 180, so no triangle has these angles.

Could a quadrilateral have angles 100°, 80°, 90° and 90°?

  • Yes: they add up to 360°
  • No
  1. Add the four angles. A quadrilateral needs 360°.

100 + 80 + 90 + 90 = 360, so it could be.

A triangle has angles 3x, 4x and 5x. Find the largest angle.

  1. 12x = 180, so x = 15.
  • That is x, not the largest angle (5x).
  • That is 4x. The largest is 5x.

x = 15°, so the angles are 45°, 60° and 75°. The largest is 75°.

Spot the mistake

Ravi says: “The angles in a triangle are 60°, 70° and 40°, which add up to 170°, so it must be fine.” What went wrong?

  • Triangle angles must add up to exactly 180°.
  • Nothing is wrong: 170° is close enough.
  • They should add up to 360° instead.
  1. Add them: 60 + 70 + 40.

60 + 70 + 40 = 170°. That is 10° short of 180°, so it is not a triangle.

Tia says: “A regular pentagon has interior angles of 5 × 180 = 900°, so each is 180°.” What went wrong?

  • She used n, not n − 2. 5 × 180 is too big.
  • Nothing is wrong: 180° is right.
  • She should divide by 4, not by 5.
  1. A pentagon splits into 3 triangles.

The total is (5 − 2) × 180 = 540°. Each angle is 540 ÷ 5 = 108°.

Uma says: “Co-interior angles are equal.” What went wrong?

  • Co-interior angles add up to 180°. They are not equal.
  • Nothing is wrong: all these angles are equal.
  • Co-interior angles add up to 360°.
  1. Which shape is co-interior? C: they add to 180°.

Equal angles are alternate or corresponding. Co-interior angles add up to 180°.

Check your knowledge: mixed review

An isosceles triangle has a top angle of 48°. Find a base angle.

  1. (180 − 48) ÷ 2.
  • That is both base angles together.

132 ÷ 2 = 66°.

Find each interior angle of a regular pentagon.

  1. 540 ÷ 5.
  • That is the turn (360 ÷ 5). The interior angle is 180 − 72.

(5 − 2) × 180 ÷ 5 = 108°.

Angles on a straight line are 2x and x + 30. Find x.

  1. 2x + x + 30 = 180.
  2. 3x = 150.
  • You may have left out the 30. 3x = 150.

3x + 30 = 180, so 3x = 150 and x = 50.

Parallel lines: two co-interior angles are x and 2x. Find x.

  1. Co-interior angles add to 180°.
  • That treats both angles as x. The second is double.

3x = 180, so x = 60°.

What is the sum of the interior angles of a 15-sided shape?

  1. (15 − 2) × 180.
  • That is 15 × 180. Use n − 2.

13 × 180 = 2,340°.

Exam style

Give a reason for each step. The reason earns marks.

Not sure what a question word means? Question words explained.

Write your working on paper, then type the answer.

(a) [3 marks] Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC. Angle A = 48°. Work out angle B.

  1. Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.
  2. 180 − 48 = 132, then halve.
  • That is B plus C. They are equal, so halve it.
  • That is angle A.

Angles in a triangle add to 180°: 180 − 48 = 132. Base angles are equal: 132 ÷ 2 = 66°.

(b) [2 marks] Work out the size of one interior angle of a regular pentagon.

  1. Sum = (5 − 2) × 180.
  • That is the turn (exterior angle), not the interior angle.

3 × 180 = 540 and 540 ÷ 5 = 108°.

(c) [2 marks] Two parallel lines are crossed by a third. One angle is 65°. Find the co-interior angle to it.

  1. Co-interior angles add to 180°.
  • That is the alternate angle. Co-interior angles add to 180.

180 − 65 = 115° (co-interior angles add up to 180°).

Going further

Work out the sum of the interior angles of a 12-sided polygon.

  1. (n − 2) × 180 with n = 12.
  • Use n − 2, not n.

10 × 180 = 1,800°.

Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 140°. How many sides does it have?

  1. Exterior = 180 − 140 = 40.
  2. 360 ÷ 40.
  • That is the turn. Divide 360 by it.

360 ÷ 40 = 9 sides (a nonagon).

Why it works

  • Every angle fact is a total. Straight line 180°, point 360°, triangle 180°, quadrilateral 360°. Missing angle = total minus the angles you know.
  • Polygons split into triangles. That is why the angles add up to (n − 2) × 180°.
  • Parallel lines copy angles. Corresponding and alternate angles match; co-interior angles make a straight line together.

Common traps

  • Using 180 when the total is 360 (or the other way round).
  • Using n instead of n − 2 for polygon angle sums.
  • Mixing up alternate (equal) and co-interior (sum to 180°).
  • Forgetting that the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.
  • Giving an angle with no reason in an exam answer.
