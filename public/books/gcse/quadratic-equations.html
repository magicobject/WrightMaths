Quadratic equations

A quadratic equation has an x² in it and usually two answers. Learn to expand and factorise brackets, then use them to solve.

Where you will use this: finding the size of a floor, garden or panel when you only know its area, the path of a jump or a thrown ball, and any job where a measurement is squared.

Words you need

Tap a teal word anywhere on the page to see this again.

Quadratic
An expression or equation where the highest power of the unknown is 2.
Expand (multiply out)
Remove brackets by multiplying every term inside by what is outside. With two brackets, multiply every term in one by every term in the other.
Factorise
Write an expression as a product of factors, with brackets. It is expanding in reverse.
Root (solution) of an equation
A value of the unknown that makes the equation true. A quadratic can have two roots. On its graph, the roots are where the curve crosses the x-axis.

1. Expanding double brackets

A Quadratic has an x² in it and no higher power. Before we solve them, we need to build them. To Expand (multiply out) two brackets, multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second.

Typing tip: write x squared as x^2, like x^2+5x+6. Where a question wants two answers, it will tell you how to type them.

Start with numbers

You can already multiply 13 × 12 by splitting each number into tens and units. A grid keeps the four pieces tidy.

The same grid with x

Now let one side be x. A rectangle that is (x + 3) long and (x + 2) wide has an Area we can add up piece by piece.

(x + 3)(x + 2) = x² + 3x + 2x + 6 = x² + 5x + 6

Key idea: the two middle pieces are Like terms, so they join: 3x + 2x = 5x. In (x + a)(x + b) the middle number is a + b (the numbers added) and the last number is a × b (the numbers multiplied).

Try it: use that idea to expand (x + 1)(x + 4) in your head.

Worked example

Expand and simplify (x + 4)(x + 3).

  1. Multiply every pair: x × x = x², x × 3 = 3x, 4 × x = 4x, 4 × 3 = 12.
  2. Join the like terms: 3x + 4x = 7x.
  3. (x + 4)(x + 3) = x² + 7x + 12. Quick check with the pattern: 4 + 3 = 7 and 4 × 3 = 12.

Now you finish it

Expand and simplify (x + 5)(x + 2).

  1. The four pieces: x², 2x, 5x and ___.
  2. Join the like terms: 2x + 5x = ___.
  3. So the answer is x² + ___x + ___.
Check my answer

5 × 2 = 10 and 2x + 5x = 7x, so (x + 5)(x + 2) = x² + 7x + 10.

Check your knowledge: expanding

2. Minus signs, squares and a special case

Negative numbers work in the grid too. The rules are the ones you know: a minus times a plus is a minus, and a minus times a minus is a plus.

Worked example

Expand and simplify (x − 2)(x + 5).

  1. Four pieces: x × x = x², x × 5 = 5x, (−2) × x = −2x, (−2) × 5 = −10.
  2. Join like terms: 5x − 2x = 3x.
  3. (x − 2)(x + 5) = x² + 3x − 10. Pattern check: −2 + 5 = 3 and −2 × 5 = −10.

Now you finish it

Expand and simplify (x − 4)(x − 3).

  1. The four pieces: x², −3x, −4x and ___.
  2. Join like terms: −3x − 4x = ___.
  3. So the answer is x² ___ x ___.
Check my answer

(−4) × (−3) = +12 and −3x − 4x = −7x, so x² − 7x + 12.

Squaring a bracket

(x + 3)² means (x + 3)(x + 3). It is not x² + 9. Use the grid: x² + 3x + 3x + 9 = x² + 6x + 9. The two middle pieces are the bit people forget.

A special case: the difference of two squares

Try (x + 5)(x − 5). The pieces are x², −5x, +5x and −25. The two middle pieces cancel, so (x + 5)(x − 5) = x² − 25. This is the Difference of two squares: one square minus another square.

Key idea: (x + a)(x − a) = x² − a². The middle term disappears because +ax and −ax cancel.

Check your knowledge: signs and squares

Expand and simplify (x − 1)(x + 6). Type it like x^2+3x-2.

  1. Take care: the −1 is negative, so some pieces are negative.
  2. The pieces are x², 6x, −x and −6.
  3. Join the two x pieces: 6x − x.
  • The last piece is (−1) × 6 = −6. A minus times a plus is a minus.
  • The x pieces are 6x and −x, which make 5x, not 7x.

x² + 6x − x − 6 = x² + 5x − 6.

Expand and simplify (x − 5)(x − 2). Type it like x^2-3x+2.

  1. Both numbers are negative.
  2. The pieces are x², −2x, −5x and the product of −5 and −2.
  3. A minus times a minus is a plus.
  • (−5) × (−2) = +10, because a minus times a minus is a plus.
  • The two middle pieces are both negative, so they make −7x.

x² − 2x − 5x + 10 = x² − 7x + 10.

Expand and simplify (x + 4)(x − 4). Type it like x^2-9.

  1. Write all four pieces.
  2. Look at the two middle pieces. What happens when you add them?
  • The last piece is 4 × (−4) = −16.
  • The two middle pieces are +4x and −4x. They cancel to nothing.

x² − 4x + 4x − 16 = x² − 16.

Expand and simplify (x + 5)². Type it like x^2+6x+9.

  1. Squaring a bracket means writing it twice, side by side.
  2. Expand (x + 5)(x + 5) with a grid.
  • You squared each term but missed the two middle pieces, 5x and 5x.
  • There are two 5x pieces. They join to 10x.

(x + 5)(x + 5) = x² + 5x + 5x + 25 = x² + 10x + 25.

Which of these is the same as x² − 49?

  • (x − 7)(x − 7)
  • (x + 7)(x − 7) + 49
  • (x + 7)(x − 7)
  1. A difference of two squares has no middle term.
  2. 49 is 7².

(x + 7)(x − 7) = x² − 7x + 7x − 49 = x² − 49.

Expand and simplify (x + 3)(x − 3) + 9. Type it like x^2+1.

  1. Expand the brackets first.
  2. This is a difference of two squares. What does it make?
  3. Then add the 9.
  • You forgot the +9 at the end.

(x + 3)(x − 3) = x² − 9. Then x² − 9 + 9 = x².

3. Factorising x² + bx + c

To Factorise is to put the brackets back. Expanding goes forwards. Factorising goes backwards. You already know the pattern: in (x + a)(x + b) the numbers multiply to the last term and add to the middle term.

Key idea: to factorise x² + bx + c, find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b. Then write (x + first)(x + second).

Worked example

Factorise x² + 7x + 12.

  1. Pairs that multiply to 12: 1 and 12, 2 and 6, 3 and 4.
  2. Which pair adds to 7? 3 + 4 = 7.
  3. So x² + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4).
  4. Check by expanding: x² + 4x + 3x + 12 = x² + 7x + 12. ✓

When the numbers are negative

Look at the signs. If the last number is positive, the two numbers have the same sign as the middle number. If the last number is negative, the two numbers have different signs.

Worked example

Factorise x² + 2x − 15.

  1. The last number is −15, so the signs differ. Pairs that multiply to −15: 1 and −15, −1 and 15, 3 and −5, −3 and 5.
  2. Which pair adds to +2? −3 + 5 = 2.
  3. So x² + 2x − 15 = (x − 3)(x + 5).
  4. Check: x² + 5x − 3x − 15 = x² + 2x − 15. ✓

Now you finish it

Factorise x² − 8x + 15.

  1. The last number is positive and the middle is negative, so both numbers are negative.
  2. Negative pairs that multiply to 15: −1 and −15, −3 and ___.
  3. Which pair adds to −8? Write the brackets: (x ___)(x ___).
Check my answer

−3 + (−5) = −8 and (−3) × (−5) = 15, so x² − 8x + 15 = (x − 3)(x − 5).

Difference of two squares, backwards

If there is no middle term and the last number is a square taken away, use the special case: x² − 49 = (x + 7)(x − 7). Two numbers that multiply to −49 and add to 0 are 7 and −7.

Check your knowledge: factorising

  1. Which pairs of numbers multiply to give 6?
  2. Which of those pairs adds to 5?
  3. Write the two numbers after x in the brackets.
  • 1 × 6 = 6 but 1 + 6 = 7. You need the pair that adds to 5.
  • Those multiply to 6 but add to −5. The middle term is +5x.

2 × 3 = 6 and 2 + 3 = 5, so x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3).

Factorise x² − 7x + 12. Type it like (x-1)(x-4).

  1. The middle number is negative and the last is positive. What does that tell you about both numbers?
  2. Find two negative numbers that multiply to 12.
  3. Which pair adds to −7?
  • Those would give +7x. The middle term is −7x, so both numbers are negative.

(−3) × (−4) = 12 and (−3) + (−4) = −7, so x² − 7x + 12 = (x − 3)(x − 4).

Factorise x² + x − 12. Type it like (x-1)(x+4).

  1. The last number is negative, so the two numbers have different signs.
  2. Pairs that multiply to −12 include 3 and −4, −3 and 4, 2 and −6, −2 and 6.
  3. Which pair adds to +1?
  • That multiplies to −12 but adds to −1. Swap the signs.

(−3) × 4 = −12 and (−3) + 4 = 1, so x² + x − 12 = (x − 3)(x + 4).

  1. There is no x term. Is 81 a square number?
  2. It is a difference of two squares.
  • That is x² − 18x + 81. A difference of two squares has one bracket plus and one minus.

81 = 9², so x² − 81 = (x − 9)(x + 9).

  • Its width is (x + 2) m.

  • 5 and −2

4. Solving by factorising, and why there are two answers

To Solve a quadratic equation, we need a trick. Here it is, as a number puzzle first. Two numbers multiply to make 0. One of them is 5. What is the other? It must be 0, because 5 times anything else is not 0.

Key idea (the zero idea): if two things multiply to 0, then at least one of them is 0. So if (x − 2)(x + 3) = 0, then x − 2 = 0 or x + 3 = 0.

Each bracket can be the zero one. That gives two solutions, called the roots of the equation: x = 2 or x = −3. Check both: (2 − 2)(2 + 3) = 0 × 5 = 0 and (−3 − 2)(−3 + 3) = (−5) × 0 = 0.

This picture shows it. A quadratic makes a U-shaped curve called a Parabola. Solving the equation means asking where the curve is at height 0, and a U-shape can cross the x-axis twice.

The steps

  1. Rearrange so that one side is 0.
  2. Factorise the other side.
  3. Set each bracket equal to 0.
  4. Solve each little equation. That gives your two answers.
  5. Check them by putting each back in.

Worked example

Solve x² + 5x + 6 = 0.

  1. One side is already 0. Factorise: (x + 2)(x + 3) = 0.
  2. Either x + 2 = 0 or x + 3 = 0.
  3. So x = −2 or x = −3.
  4. Check x = −2: 4 − 10 + 6 = 0. ✓ Check x = −3: 9 − 15 + 6 = 0. ✓

Worked example: rearrange first

Solve x² = 3x + 10.

  1. Get 0 on one side: take 3x and 10 from both sides. x² − 3x − 10 = 0.
  2. Factorise: (x − 5)(x + 2) = 0.
  3. x − 5 = 0 gives x = 5. x + 2 = 0 gives x = −2.

Now you finish it

Solve x² − 7x + 10 = 0.

  1. Two numbers that multiply to 10 and add to −7: ___ and ___.
  2. Brackets: (x ___)(x ___) = 0.
  3. Set each bracket to 0 and solve.
Check my answer

(x − 2)(x − 5) = 0, so x = 2 or x = 5. Check: 4 − 14 + 10 = 0 and 25 − 35 + 10 = 0.

Two things to watch

If there is no number term, such as x² + 3x = 0, take out the common factor: x(x + 3) = 0. So x = 0 or x = −3. Do not divide by x: that loses the answer x = 0.

The zero idea only works when one side is 0. If (x − 2)(x + 3) = 6, you cannot say each bracket is 6. Rearrange so it equals 0 first.

Check your knowledge: the zero idea

  2. Try 7 × 0 and 7 × 1.
  • 1 × 7 = 7. You need the number that gives 0.
  • 7 × 7 = 49. Which number times 7 gives 0?

Anything times 0 is 0, so the other number is 0. This is the zero idea.

5. When x² equals a number

Some quadratics have no x term at all, such as x² = 25. The Square root undoes the squaring. But there are two numbers whose square is 25: 5 × 5 = 25 and (−5) × (−5) = 25.

Key idea: if x² = k (and k is positive), then x = √k or x = −√k. We write x = ±√k, which is read “plus or minus the square root of k”. A square is never negative, so x² = −9 has no solutions.

It matches factorising. x² − 25 = 0 is (x − 5)(x + 5) = 0, which gives x = 5 or x = −5. That is the difference of two squares again.

If k is not a square number, the roots do not come out whole. Use your calculator and round as asked: x² = 20 gives x = ±4.47… = ±4.5 to 1 decimal place.

Worked example

Solve 2x² = 50.

  1. Get x² on its own: divide both sides by 2. x² = 25.
  2. Square root, with both signs: x = 5 or x = −5.

Now you finish it

Solve 3x² = 48.

  1. Divide both sides by 3: x² = ___.
  2. x = ±√___ = ±___.
Check my answer

x² = 16, so x = 4 or x = −4. Check: 3 × 16 = 48.

In a real problem, think about what makes sense. A length cannot be negative, so for the side of a square only the positive answer is used.

6. Checking your solutions

A solution is right if it makes the equation true. Substitute it back in and see if you get 0. This takes seconds and catches sign slips.

Worked example

Is x = −2 a solution of x² − 3x − 10 = 0?

  1. Put −2 in for x: (−2)² − 3 × (−2) − 10.
  2. = 4 + 6 − 10 = 0. ✓ Yes, it is a solution.

Also ask whether the answer makes sense. If x is a length or a time, a negative solution may have to be thrown out.

7. Forming a quadratic from an area

Quadratics turn up when two lengths both contain x and you multiply them. Often the problem says something like “a rectangle has area 40”. You build the equation, then solve it.

  1. Draw it and label the sides with x.
  2. Write area = length × width and set it equal to the given area.
  3. Expand, then rearrange so that one side is 0.
  4. Factorise and solve.
  5. Throw out any answer that does not make sense (a negative length).

Worked example

A rectangle has width x cm and length (x + 3) cm. Its area is 40 cm². Find x.

  1. x(x + 3) = 40, so x² + 3x = 40.
  2. Rearrange: x² + 3x − 40 = 0.
  3. Factorise: (x + 8)(x − 5) = 0, so x = −8 or x = 5.
  4. A width cannot be negative, so x = 5 cm.
  5. Check: the rectangle is 5 by 8, and 5 × 8 = 40. ✓

Why it works

  • The grid is an area. (x + 3)(x + 2) is the area of a rectangle, and its four pieces are the four products. Nothing is left out.
  • The zero idea. Zero times anything is zero, and nothing else works. That is why a product equal to 0 forces one factor to be 0, and why we rearrange to 0 first.
  • Two solutions. Each bracket can be the zero one. On the graph, a U-shape can cross the x-axis twice.
  • Plus or minus. A positive number and its negative have the same square, so x² = k has two answers when k is positive.
  • The signs flip. The bracket (x − 3) is zero when x = +3, so the sign in the bracket is the opposite of the solution.

Common traps

  • Writing (x + 3)² = x² + 9 and missing the middle term.
  • Using the sign in the bracket as the solution, instead of its opposite.
  • Giving only one solution.
  • Using the zero idea when the right-hand side is not 0.
  • Dividing both sides by x and losing the solution x = 0.
  • Forgetting the negative root when you square-root both sides.
  • Keeping a negative length when the question is about a real shape.

The quadratic formula

Many quadratics do not factorise neatly. For any equation ax² + bx + c = 0 (with a not 0), the Quadratic formula always works:

x = −b ± √(b² − 4ac) over 2a

Write down a, b and c first, with their signs. Careful: if b is negative, then −b is positive. Work out the part under the root, b² − 4ac, which is called the Discriminant. If it is a perfect square the answers are neat. If it is not, the answers are not whole numbers, so round as the question asks. If it is negative there are no solutions.

  4. x = 5.65 or x = 0.35 (to 2 d.p.). Not whole, because 28 is not a square number.
