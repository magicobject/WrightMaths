    Algebraic fractions

    An algebraic fraction is a fraction with letters in it. It follows the same rules as the fractions you already know: cancel, multiply, divide, add and subtract.

    Where you will use this: two taps or pipes filling one tank, two workers sharing a job, and two resistors in parallel in an electrical circuit. In each one, the rates add as fractions.

    Algebraic fraction
    A fraction with algebra (a letter) on the top, the bottom, or both. It follows the same rules as a number fraction.
    Factorise
    Write an expression as a product of factors, with brackets. It is expanding in reverse.
    Common denominator
    A denominator that two or more fractions share. You need one before you can add or subtract fractions.
    Difference of two squares
    An expression with one square taken away from another, such as x² − 49. It factorises into two brackets that are the same except for the sign.

    How to type algebraic fractions on this page: write the top, a slash, then the bottom. Put brackets round the top or the bottom if it has more than one term. For example (x+1)/(x-2), 3/x or (2x+3)/6. Type x squared as x^2. Where there are two answers, type them like 2,3.

    Write 6 over 9 in its simplest form. Type it like 3/4.

    2. Divide the top and the bottom by the same number.

    Work out 1 over 3 + 1 over 4. Type it like 5/8.

    1. You need a common denominator: a number that 3 and 4 both go into.
    2. Use 12. Write each fraction with 12 on the bottom.
    • That looks like adding the tops and the bottoms. Make the bottoms the same first.

    1 over 3 = 4 over 12 and 1 over 4 = 3 over 12. Together: 7 over 12.

    Factorise x² + 5x + 6. Type it like (x+1)(x+4).

    1. Find two numbers that multiply to make 6 and add to make 5.
    2. The numbers are 2 and 3. Which brackets do they go in?
    • 1 and 6 multiply to 6 but they add to 7, not 5.
    • That is not fully factorised. Look for two brackets.

    2 × 3 = 6 and 2 + 3 = 5, so x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3).

    Factorise x² − 9. Type it like (x+1)(x-1).

    1. 9 is a square number: 3 × 3.
    2. It is a difference of two squares: a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b).
    • (x − 3)(x − 3) gives − 6x in the middle. A difference of two squares has one + and one −.

    x² − 9 = x² − 3² = (x + 3)(x − 3).

    1. What is an algebraic fraction?

    An Algebraic fraction is a Fraction with algebra on the top, the bottom, or both. Here are three: 3 over x, 2x over 5 and x + 1 over x − 2.

    The bottom of a fraction can never be zero. So x + 1 over x − 2 only makes sense when x is not 2. We usually just agree that x is not a number that makes the bottom zero.

    You simplify them the same way as number fractions: find something that divides into both the top and the bottom, and cancel it. To cancel 6 from 12 over 18 you divide both by 6 and get 2 over 3. In the same way:

    6x over 9 = 2x over 3 and 12x² over 8x = 3x over 2

    Key idea: you can only cancel a factor (something multiplied), never a term (something added). In x + 5 over x the x on top is added to 5, so it cannot be cancelled. In 5x over x the x is multiplied, so it can.

    Worked example

    Simplify 12x² over 8x.

    1. Numbers first: 12 and 8 both divide by 4. 12 over 8 = 3 over 2.
    2. Letters: x² ÷ x = x (x² is x × x, so one x cancels).
    3. So 12x² over 8x = 3x over 2.

    Now you finish it

    Simplify 10x over 15.

    1. 10 and 15 both divide by ___.
    2. After dividing: ___x over ___.
    Check my answer

    10 and 15 both divide by 5, so 10x over 15 = 2x over 3.

    Check your knowledge: cancelling

    Type fractions like 2x/3. Use brackets for a bottom with more than one part, like 1/(2x).

    Simplify 6x over 9.

    1. Look at the numbers first. What divides into 6 and 9?
    2. Divide both 6 and 9 by 3. The x stays on the top.
    • That looks like dividing the bottom by 3 but not the top. Divide both by the same number.
    • The x is still there: it is on the top, and nothing on the bottom cancels it.

    6x over 9 = 2x over 3.

    Simplify 12x² over 8x.

    1. Do the numbers first, then the letters.
    2. x² ÷ x leaves one x.
    • The number part is right. But x² ÷ x = x, not x².
    • x² ÷ x leaves x on the top. Do not lose it.

    12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 8 ÷ 4 = 2. x² ÷ x = x. So 12x² over 8x = 3x over 2.

    Simplify 3x² over 6x³.

    1. Numbers first: what divides into 3 and 6?
    2. x² on the top and x³ on the bottom: cancel two x's from each. How many are left on the bottom?
    • Check which side has more x's. There are more x's on the bottom, so the leftover x stays on the bottom.
    • That means 1 over 2 × x. The bottom is 2x, so put it in brackets: 1/(2x).

    3 over 6 = 1 over 2. x² ÷ x³ = 1 over x. So 3x² over 6x³ = 1 over 2x. Type it as 1/(2x).

    Which of these fractions can be simplified by cancelling?

    • x + 5 over 5
    • 5x over 10
    • x + 2 over x + 3
    1. A factor is multiplied. Look for something that multiplies the whole top and the whole bottom.
    2. Is each number or letter you want to cancel added or multiplied?

    5x over 10 = x over 2. In the other two, the part that looks the same is added to something else, so it is a term, not a factor.

    A rectangular panel has an area of 12x² cm² and a width of 4x cm. Write its length as a simplified expression, in cm.

    1. Length = area ÷ width.
    2. Write it as 12x² over 4x and simplify.
    • The numbers give 3, and x² ÷ x = x, not x².
    • That looks like subtracting 4 from 12. To find a length from an area, divide.

    Length = 12x² over 4x = 3x cm.

    2. Simplifying by factorising

    When the top or the bottom has more than one term, you cannot cancel straight away. First Factorise the top and the bottom. Then cancel any brackets that match.

    3x + 6 over x + 2 = 3(x + 2) over x + 2 = 3

    Key idea: factorise, then cancel whole brackets. The tools you already have: take out a common factor, quadratics like x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3), and the Difference of two squares: x² − 9 = (x + 3)(x − 3).

    Worked example

    Simplify x² + 5x + 6 over x² + 3x.

    1. Top: x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3).
    2. Bottom: x² + 3x = x(x + 3).
    3. The bracket (x + 3) is on the top and the bottom. Cancel it.
    4. The answer is x + 2 over x. Notice you cannot cancel the x here: on the top it is added to 2.

    Worked example: difference of two squares

    Simplify x² − 16 over x² + x − 12.

    1. Top: x² − 16 = (x − 4)(x + 4).
    2. Bottom: x² + x − 12 = (x + 4)(x − 3), because 4 × (− 3) = − 12 and 4 + (− 3) = 1.
    3. Cancel (x + 4). The answer is x − 4 over x − 3.

    Now you finish it

    Simplify x² − 9 over x² + 3x.

    1. Top: x² − 9 = (x + 3)(x − 3). Bottom: x² + 3x = x(___).
    2. Cancel the matching bracket. What is left?
    Check my answer

    Bottom: x(x + 3). Cancel (x + 3): x − 3 over x.

    Check your knowledge: simplifying by factorising

    Type answers like (x+1)/(x-2), with brackets round any top or bottom with more than one term.

    Simplify 3x + 6 over 3.

    1. Take out a common factor from the top.
    2. 3x + 6 = 3(x + 2).
    • That looks like dividing only the 3x by 3. The 6 on the top needs dividing by 3 too.
    • Divide every term on the top by 3.

    3x + 6 over 3 = 3(x + 2) over 3 = x + 2.

    Simplify 5x + 10 over x + 2.

    1. Factorise the top.
    2. 5x + 10 = 5(x + 2). What matches the bottom?
    • Cancel the bracket (x + 2), not just the x. It matches the bottom exactly.
    • The bottom is (x + 2), a bracket. It cancels as a whole, not as a 2.

    5x + 10 over x + 2 = 5(x + 2) over x + 2 = 5.

    Simplify x² + 4x over x.

    1. Factorise the top: what is common to x² and 4x?
    2. x² + 4x = x(x + 4).
    • Divide every term on the top by x. 4x ÷ x is 4, with no x left.
    • x² ÷ x is x, and 4x ÷ x is 4.

    x² + 4x over x = x(x + 4) over x = x + 4.

    Simplify x² − 25 over x + 5.

    1. The top is a difference of two squares.
    2. x² − 25 = (x + 5)(x − 5).
    • Check the signs: (x + 5)(x − 5). The bracket that does not match the bottom is (x − 5).

    x² − 25 over x + 5 = (x + 5)(x − 5) over x + 5 = x − 5.

    Simplify x² + 7x + 10 over x + 2.

    1. Factorise the top: two numbers that multiply to make 10 and add to make 7.
    2. The numbers are 2 and 5.
    • The brackets are (x + 2)(x + 5). Only the (x + 2) cancels.

    x² + 7x + 10 = (x + 2)(x + 5), so x² + 7x + 10 over x + 2 = x + 5.

    Simplify x² − 9 over x² + x − 6.

    1. Factorise the top (difference of two squares) and the bottom.
    2. Bottom: two numbers that multiply to make − 6 and add to make 1.
    3. The bottom is (x + 3)(x − 2).
    • Check the signs. The top is (x + 3)(x − 3) and the bottom is (x + 3)(x − 2).
    • Cancel only the matching bracket (x + 3). The other bracket stays on the bottom.

    x² − 9 = (x + 3)(x − 3) and x² + x − 6 = (x + 3)(x − 2). Cancel (x + 3): x − 3 over x − 2.

    Which is x² + 6x + 8 over x² + 2x in its simplest form?

    • x + 4 over x
    • x + 4
    • 6x + 8 over 2x
    1. Factorise the top and the bottom.
    2. Top: (x + 2)(x + 4). Bottom: x(x + 2).

    Top = (x + 2)(x + 4), bottom = x(x + 2). Cancel (x + 2): x + 4 over x.

    Simplify x² − 4 over 2x − 4.

    1. Factorise the top and the bottom.
    2. Top: (x + 2)(x − 2). Bottom: take out a 2.
    • The bottom is 2(x − 2). After cancelling (x − 2) the 2 is left on the bottom.

    x² − 4 = (x + 2)(x − 2) and 2x − 4 = 2(x − 2). Cancel (x − 2): x + 2 over 2.

    A rectangular lawn has an area of (x² + 5x + 6) m² and a width of (x + 2) m. Find its length, as an expression in x, in m.

    1. Length = area ÷ width.
    2. Factorise x² + 5x + 6 first.
    • That is the width. Divide the area by it, and see what is left.

    Length = x² + 5x + 6 over x + 2 = (x + 2)(x + 3) over x + 2 = x + 3 m.

    3. Multiplying and dividing

    Multiplying fractions: multiply the tops and multiply the bottoms. Then cancel. It is quicker to cancel before you multiply.

    x over 3 × 6 over x² = 6x over 3x² = 2 over x

    Dividing: flip the second fraction (swap its top and bottom) and multiply. The flipped fraction is the Reciprocal.

    x + 1 over x − 2 ÷ x + 1 over 4 = x + 1 over x − 2 × 4 over x + 1 = 4 over x − 2

    Key idea: multiply tops, multiply bottoms, and cancel matching factors. To divide, flip the second fraction first. If a bracket is on the top and the bottom, it cancels.

    Worked example: multiply

    Work out x over 5 × 10 over x².

    1. Multiply the tops: x × 10 = 10x. Multiply the bottoms: 5 × x² = 5x².
    2. 10x over 5x²: the numbers give 10 over 5 = 2 and x ÷ x² = 1 over x.
    3. The answer is 2 over x.

    Worked example: divide

    Work out x² over 8 ÷ x over 2.

    1. Flip the second fraction and multiply: x² over 8 × 2 over x.
    2. Multiply: 2x² over 8x. Numbers: 2 over 8 = 1 over 4. Letters: x² ÷ x = x.
    3. The answer is x over 4.

    Now you finish it

    Work out x over 4 ÷ x over 8.

    1. Flip the second fraction and multiply: x over 4 × 8 over ___.
    2. 8x over 4x = ___.
    Check my answer

    x over 4 × 8 over x = 8x over 4x = 2.

    Check your knowledge: multiplying and dividing

    Type answers like x/2 or 2/x.

    Work out x over 5 × 10 over x².

    1. Multiply the tops and the bottoms.
    2. Then cancel numbers and letters.
    • x² is on the bottom, so one x is left on the bottom, not on the top.
    • You can still cancel the numbers (10 and 5) and one x.

    x over 5 × 10 over x² = 10x over 5x² = 2 over x.

    Work out x + 2 over 3 × 6 over x + 2.

    1. Multiply the tops and the bottoms.
    2. The bracket (x + 2) is on the top and the bottom.
    • You cannot get an x out of this. The brackets cancel completely.

    x + 2 over 3 × 6 over x + 2 = 6(x + 2) over 3(x + 2) = 6 over 3 = 2.

    Work out x over 4 ÷ x over 8.

    1. Flip the second fraction, then multiply.
    2. x on the top and x on the bottom cancel.
    • That looks like flipping the wrong fraction. Divide means: flip the second one.
    • Flip the second fraction and multiply: x over 4 × 8 over x.

    x over 4 ÷ x over 8 = x over 4 × 8 over x = 8x over 4x = 2.

    Work out x² over 6 ÷ x over 3.

    1. Flip the second fraction and multiply.
    2. x² over 6 × 3 over x.
    • x² ÷ x is x, not x².
    • That looks like flipping the first fraction. Flip the second, then multiply.

    x² over 6 × 3 over x = 3x² over 6x = x over 2.

    Work out x + 3 over x − 1 × x − 1 over 2.

    1. Multiply the tops and the bottoms. Look for a matching bracket.
    2. (x − 1) is on the top and the bottom.

    (x + 3)(x − 1) over (x − 1) × 2 = x + 3 over 2.

    [3 marks] Simplify fully x² + 3x over x + 1 ÷ x + 3 over x² − 1.

    1. Flip the second fraction and multiply.
    2. Factorise x² + 3x and x² − 1.
    3. Cancel the matching brackets.

    x² + 3x over x + 1 × x² − 1 over x + 3 = x(x + 3) over x + 1 × (x + 1)(x − 1) over x + 3. Cancel (x + 3) and (x + 1): x(x − 1).

    • M1 for flipping the second fraction and multiplying
    • M1 for factorising to x(x + 3) and (x + 1)(x − 1)
    • A1 for x(x − 1)

    What is the first step in working out a over b ÷ c over d?

    • Flip the second and multiply
    • Flip the first fraction and multiply the two together
    • Divide the tops, then divide the bottoms separately
    1. Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal.
    2. Which fraction do you flip: the first or the second?

    To divide by c over d, multiply by its reciprocal d over c. So a over b ÷ c over d = a over b × d over c.

    4. Adding and subtracting

    When the bottoms are the same, add the tops: 3 over x + 2 over x = 5 over x.

    When the bottoms are different, use a Common denominator. Write each fraction with the same bottom, then add the tops.

    1 over x + 1 over 3 = 3 over 3x + x over 3x = 3 + x over 3x

    Key idea: find a bottom that both bottoms go into, multiply each top by the same thing as its bottom, then add or subtract the tops. When you subtract, put the whole top in a bracket so the minus sign reaches every term.

    Worked example: add

    Write x + 1 over 2 + x + 2 over 3 as a single fraction.

    1. The common denominator is 6.
    2. x + 1 over 2 = 3(x + 1) over 6 and x + 2 over 3 = 2(x + 2) over 6.
    3. Add the tops: 3x + 3 + 2x + 4 = 5x + 7.
    4. The answer is 5x + 7 over 6.

    Worked example: subtract

    Write x + 1 over 2 − x − 1 over 3 as a single fraction.

    1. The common denominator is 6: 3(x + 1) over 6 − 2(x − 1) over 6.
    2. Subtract the tops, with brackets: 3(x + 1) − 2(x − 1) = 3x + 3 − 2x + 2 = x + 5.
    3. The answer is x + 5 over 6. The minus sign changes both signs inside (x − 1): − 2x and + 2.

    Now you finish it

    Write 1 over x + 1 over 5 as a single fraction.

    1. The common denominator is 5x.
    2. 1 over x = 5 over 5x and 1 over 5 = ___ over 5x.
    Check my answer

    1 over x + 1 over 5 = 5 over 5x + x over 5x = 5 + x over 5x.

    Check your knowledge: adding and subtracting

    Type answers like (x+1)/6 or 5/x.

    Write 3 over x + 2 over x as a single fraction.

    1. The bottoms are the same.
    2. Add the tops and keep the bottom.
    • When the bottoms are the same, keep the bottom as it is. Do not add the bottoms.

    3 over x + 2 over x = 5 over x.

    Write x over 5 + 2x over 5 as a single fraction.

    1. The bottoms are the same.
    2. x + 2x = ?
    • Do not add the bottoms. The bottom stays 5.

    x over 5 + 2x over 5 = 3x over 5.

    Write 1 over x + 1 over 4 as a single fraction.

    1. The common denominator is 4x.
    2. 1 over x = 4 over 4x. What is 1 over 4 with 4x on the bottom?
    • You cannot add the tops and add the bottoms. Use a common denominator, 4x.

    1 over x + 1 over 4 = 4 over 4x + x over 4x = x + 4 over 4x.

    Write x + 1 over 2 + x + 2 over 3 as a single fraction.

    1. The common denominator is 6.
    2. Multiply the first top by 3 and the second top by 2.
    • That looks like adding the tops and the bottoms. Use the common denominator 6.
    • Multiply each whole top: 2(x + 2) = 2x + 4, not 2x + 2.

    3(x + 1) over 6 + 2(x + 2) over 6 = 3x + 3 + 2x + 4 over 6 = 5x + 7 over 6.

    Write x + 1 over 2 − x − 1 over 3 as a single fraction.

    1. The common denominator is 6.
    2. The minus sign must reach both terms of the second top: use a bracket.
    • Take care with the minus sign: − 2(x − 1) = − 2x + 2, not − 2x − 2.
    • Take care with the signs: − 2(x − 1) = − 2x + 2.

    3(x + 1) − 2(x − 1) over 6 = 3x + 3 − 2x + 2 over 6 = x + 5 over 6.

    Write 1 over 2 + x + 3 over x − 1 over x as a single fraction. (Tip: combine the two terms with x on the bottom first.)

    1. x + 3 over x and 1 over x have the same bottom, so combine them first.
    2. x + 3 − 1 over x = x + 2 over x. Then add 1 over 2, with common denominator 2x.
    • The second fraction needs its whole top multiplied by 2: 2(x + 2) = 2x + 4.

    x + 3 over x − 1 over x = x + 2 over x. Then 1 over 2 + x + 2 over x = x over 2x + 2(x + 2) over 2x = x + 2x + 4 over 2x = 3x + 4 over 2x.

    Bigger bottoms Higher

    Sometimes the bottoms are brackets. The common denominator is the two brackets multiplied together. If a bottom can be factorised, do that first: it may share a bracket with the other bottom.

    1 over x + 1 + 1 over x + 2 = x + 2 over (x + 1)(x + 2) + x + 1 over (x + 1)(x + 2) = 2x + 3 over (x + 1)(x + 2)

    Key idea: leave the bottom factorised, and expand only the top. Then check whether the top factorises and cancels with the bottom.

    Worked example: a bottom that factorises

    Write x over x² − 4 + 1 over x + 2 as a single fraction.

    1. Factorise the first bottom: x² − 4 = (x − 2)(x + 2). The second bottom (x + 2) is already part of it.
    2. The common denominator is (x − 2)(x + 2). Write 1 over x + 2 = x − 2 over (x + 2)(x − 2).
    3. Add the tops: x + (x − 2) = 2x − 2.
    4. The answer is 2x − 2 over (x − 2)(x + 2), or 2(x − 1) over (x − 2)(x + 2).

    Now you finish it

    Write 1 over x + 1 + 1 over x + 3 as a single fraction.

    1. Common denominator: (x + 1)(x + 3).
    2. Tops: (x + 3) + (x + 1) = ___.
    Check my answer

    Tops: x + 3 + x + 1 = 2x + 4. Answer: 2x + 4 over (x + 1)(x + 3).

    Check your knowledge: bigger bottoms Higher

    Type answers like (2x+3)/((x+1)(x+2)). Brackets round the whole top and the whole bottom.

    Write 1 over x + 1 + 1 over x + 2 as a single fraction.

    1. The common denominator is (x + 1)(x + 2).
    2. Top: (x + 2) + (x + 1).
    • That looks like adding the bottoms. Use a common denominator, (x + 1)(x + 2), and add the tops.

    1 over x + 1 + 1 over x + 2 = x + 2 + x + 1 over (x + 1)(x + 2) = 2x + 3 over (x + 1)(x + 2).

    Write 2 over x − 1 − 1 over x + 1 as a single fraction.

    1. The common denominator is (x − 1)(x + 1).
    2. Top: 2(x + 1) − (x − 1). Use a bracket for the minus.
    • Check the minus sign: − (x − 1) = − x + 1.

    2(x + 1) − (x − 1) over (x − 1)(x + 1) = 2x + 2 − x + 1 over (x − 1)(x + 1) = x + 3 over (x − 1)(x + 1).

    Write x over x² − 4 + 1 over x + 2 as a single fraction. (Factorise the first bottom.)

    1. x² − 4 is a difference of two squares.
    2. The common denominator is (x − 2)(x + 2).
    • That looks like adding the tops without changing the second one. Its top needs multiplying by (x − 2).

    x² − 4 = (x − 2)(x + 2). The second fraction becomes x − 2 over (x + 2)(x − 2). Add the tops: x + x − 2 = 2x − 2. So the answer is 2x − 2 over (x − 2)(x + 2).

    5. Solving equations with fractions

    To solve an equation with fractions, get rid of the fractions. Multiply every term on both sides by the common denominator. Then solve as usual.

    x over 3 + x over 4 = 7 → multiply by 12 → 4x + 3x = 84 → x = 12

    Key idea: multiply every term by the common denominator. If x ends up on the bottom, you may get an x² term. Then it is a Quadratic: move everything to one side and factorise.

    Worked example: linear

    Solve 3 over x = 6 over x + 2.

    1. Multiply both sides by x(x + 2): 3(x + 2) = 6x.
    2. Expand: 3x + 6 = 6x. So 6 = 3x and x = 2.
    3. Check: 3 over 2 and 6 over 4 are both 1.5. ✓

    Worked example: leading to a quadratic

    Solve x + 6 over x = 5.

    1. Multiply every term by x: x² + 6 = 5x.
    2. Move everything to one side: x² − 5x + 6 = 0.
    3. Factorise: (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0, so x = 2 or x = 3.
    4. Check x = 2: 2 + 3 = 5. ✓ Check x = 3: 3 + 2 = 5. ✓

    Now you finish it

    Solve x over 2 + x over 3 = 10.

    1. Multiply every term by 6: 3x + ___x = ___.
    2. So ___x = 60 and x = ___.
    Check my answer

    6 × x over 2 = 3x, 6 × x over 3 = 2x, 6 × 10 = 60. So 5x = 60 and x = 12.

    Check your knowledge: solving

    Where there are two answers, type them like 2,3.

    Solve x over 3 + x over 4 = 7.

    1. Multiply every term by 12.
    2. You get 4x + 3x = 84.
    • That looks like the 7 was not multiplied by 12 as well. Every term must be multiplied.
    • That is 7x. You still need to divide by 7.

    12 × x over 3 = 4x, 12 × x over 4 = 3x and 12 × 7 = 84. So 7x = 84 and x = 12.

    Solve x + 1 over 2 = x + 4 over 5.

    1. Multiply both sides by 10, or cross-multiply.
    2. 5(x + 1) = 2(x + 4).
    • That is 3x. You still need to divide by 3.

    5(x + 1) = 2(x + 4), so 5x + 5 = 2x + 8, so 3x = 3 and x = 1.

    Solve 6 over x = 3.

    1. Multiply both sides by x.
    2. 6 = 3x.
    • That looks like multiplying 6 by 3. Multiply both sides by x, then divide by 3.

    6 = 3x, so x = 2. Check: 6 over 2 = 3.

    Solve 2 over x + 1 = 1 over x − 2.

    1. Multiply both sides by (x + 1)(x − 2).
    2. 2(x − 2) = x + 1.
    • Check the expanding: 2x − 4 = x + 1, so x = 5.
    • Check the expanding on the left: 2(x − 2) = 2x − 4.

    2(x − 2) = 1(x + 1), so 2x − 4 = x + 1, so x = 5. Check: both sides equal 1 over 3. ✓

    Higher Solve x + 6 over x = 5. Type the two answers like 1,7.

    1. Multiply every term by x.
    2. You get a quadratic: x² − 5x + 6 = 0. Factorise it.
    • You have to solve the quadratic. Multiply by x, then factorise.
    • 1 and 6 add to 7. You need two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 5.

    x² + 6 = 5x, so x² − 5x + 6 = 0. (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0, so x = 2 or x = 3.

    Higher Solve x = 4 over x. Type the two answers like 3,-3.

    1. Multiply both sides by x.
    2. You get x² = 4. What numbers square to make 4?
    • Two numbers square to make 4. One is 2. What is the other?
    • That is x². Take the square root.

    x² = 4, so x = 2 or x = − 2. Check: 4 over 2 = 2 and 4 over − 2 = − 2. ✓

    6. Two pipes, one tank

    A job is done at a rate. If a pipe fills a tank in 3 hours, then in 1 hour it fills 1 over 3 of the tank. If a second pipe fills the same tank in 6 hours, it fills 1 over 6 of the tank in 1 hour.

    Working together, they add their rates: 1 over 3 + 1 over 6 = 1 over 2 of the tank in 1 hour. So together they take 2 hours.

    Key idea: the rate of a job that takes t hours is 1 over t per hour. Rates add. The time together is the reciprocal of the combined rate.

    Worked example

    Pipe A fills a tank in 4 hours. Pipe B fills it in 12 hours. How long do they take together?

    1. Rate of A: 1 over 4 of the tank per hour. Rate of B: 1 over 12 per hour.
    2. Together: 1 over 4 + 1 over 12 = 3 over 12 + 1 over 12 = 4 over 12 = 1 over 3 per hour.
    3. A rate of 1 over 3 per hour means 3 hours for the whole tank.

    Now you finish it

    Painter A paints a room in 5 hours. Painter B takes 20 hours. How long do they take together?

    1. Rates: 1 over 5 and 1 over 20.
    2. Together: 4 over 20 + 1 over 20 = ___ over 20.
    Check my answer

    4 over 20 + 1 over 20 = 5 over 20 = 1 over 4, so together they take 4 hours.

    Check your knowledge: combined rates

    Pipe A fills a tank in 4 hours. Pipe B fills it in 12 hours. How many hours do they take together?

    1. Write each pipe as a fraction of the tank per hour.
    2. 1 over 4 + 1 over 12.
    • That is 4 + 12 divided by 2. Rates add, not times.
    • That looks like adding the times. Add the rates (the fractions per hour) instead.

    1 over 4 + 1 over 12 = 3 over 12 + 1 over 12 = 4 over 12 = 1 over 3 of the tank per hour. So it takes 3 hours.

    Painter A paints a room in 5 hours. Painter B paints it in 20 hours. How many hours do they take working together?

    1. Write each rate as a fraction of the room per hour.
    2. 1 over 5 + 1 over 20.
    • That is the average of the two times. Rates add, so add 1 over 5 and 1 over 20.

    1 over 5 + 1 over 20 = 4 over 20 + 1 over 20 = 5 over 20 = 1 over 4 of the room per hour. So it takes 4 hours.

    Pipe A fills a tank in x hours. Pipe B fills it in (x + 3) hours. Write their combined rate, in tanks per hour, as a single fraction.

    1. The rate of A is 1 over x per hour. What is the rate of B?
    2. Add 1 over x + 1 over x + 3 using the common denominator x(x + 3).
    • Do not add the bottoms. Use a common denominator and add the tops.

    1 over x + 1 over x + 3 = x + 3 over x(x + 3) + x over x(x + 3) = 2x + 3 over x(x + 3) tanks per hour.

    Higher Pipe A fills a tank in x hours. Pipe B fills it in (x + 5) hours. Together they fill it in 6 hours, so 1 over x + 1 over x + 5 = 1 over 6. Solve this to find x, the time for pipe A on its own, in hours.

    1. Multiply every term by 6x(x + 5).
    2. You get 6(x + 5) + 6x = x(x + 5). Collect it into a quadratic.
    3. x² − 7x − 30 = 0 (x is a time, so it must be positive).
    • A time cannot be negative. The quadratic gives two answers; choose the positive one.
    • That is the time for pipe B (x + 5). The question asks for pipe A.

    6(x + 5) + 6x = x(x + 5), so 12x + 30 = x² + 5x, so x² − 7x − 30 = 0. (x − 10)(x + 3) = 0, so x = 10 or x = − 3. A time cannot be negative, so x = 10 hours. Check: 1 over 10 + 1 over 15 = 5 over 30 = 1 over 6. ✓

    7. Proof with algebraic fractions Higher

    To prove a statement with fractions, start on one side and work step by step until you reach the other side. Do not work on both sides at once. Write what you did at each step, and finish by saying it matches.

    Key idea: in a “show that” question the answer is given to you. The marks are for the working that gets there. Use a common denominator, expand the top carefully, and finish by saying “as required”.

    Worked example

    Show that 1 over n − 1 over n + 1 = 1 over n(n + 1).

    1. Common denominator: n(n + 1). 1 over n = n + 1 over n(n + 1) and 1 over n + 1 = n over n(n + 1).
    2. Subtract the tops: (n + 1) − n = 1.
    3. So 1 over n − 1 over n + 1 = 1 over n(n + 1), as required.

    Check your knowledge: proof Higher

    Write your working on paper, then tick the solution when you are happy.

    [3 marks] Show that x² − 1 over x + 1 + 1 = x.

    1. Factorise the top first.
    2. x² − 1 = (x − 1)(x + 1). Cancel with the bottom.

    x² − 1 over x + 1 = (x − 1)(x + 1) over x + 1 = x − 1. Then x − 1 + 1 = x, as required.

    • M1 for factorising x² − 1 as (x − 1)(x + 1)
    • M1 for cancelling (x + 1) to get x − 1
    • C1 for x − 1 + 1 = x stated

    [3 marks] Show that 1 over x − 1 − 1 over x = 1 over x(x − 1).

    1. Use the common denominator x(x − 1).
    2. Subtract the tops: x − (x − 1).

    Common denominator x(x − 1): x over x(x − 1) − x − 1 over x(x − 1) = x − (x − 1) over x(x − 1) = 1 over x(x − 1), as required.

    • M1 for the common denominator x(x − 1)
    • M1 for the top x − (x − 1)
    • C1 for 1 and a conclusion stated

    The sum of the reciprocals of the whole numbers n and n + 1 is 2n + 1 over n(n + 1). Use this to work out the sum of the reciprocals of 3 and 4. Type it like 5/8.

    1. Put n = 3 into 2n + 1 over n(n + 1).
    2. Check it by adding 1 over 3 + 1 over 4.
    • The bottom is n(n + 1), which is 3 × 4 = 12.

    With n = 3: 2 × 3 + 1 over 3 × 4 = 7 over 12. Check: 1 over 3 + 1 over 4 = 4 over 12 + 3 over 12 = 7 over 12.

    Flip it: run it backwards

    So far you have combined fractions into one, and simplified them by factorising. Now go the other way: take one fraction apart, or write a simple expression as a fraction.

    Forwards

    2 + 1 over x = ?

    Common denominator x: 2x over x + 1 over x = 2x + 1 over x.

    Backwards

    2x + 1 over x = ?

    Split the top: 2x over x + 1 over x = 2 + 1 over x.

    • To combine: give every part the same denominator, then add the tops.
    • To split: divide each term on the top by the bottom, a + b over c = a over c + b over c. (You can split the top, never the bottom.)
    • To write x + 3 as a fraction with bottom (x + 2): multiply the top by (x + 2) as well, (x + 3)(x + 2) over x + 2.

    Key idea: splitting and combining undo each other. Check one by doing the other.

    Worked example

    Write 3 + 2 over x as a single fraction.

    1. Write 3 with a bottom of x: 3x over x.
    2. Add: 3x over x + 2 over x = 3x + 2 over x.

    Now you finish it

    Write 5x + 2 over x as 5 + a fraction.

    1. Split the top: 5x over x + 2 over x.
    2. 5x over x = ___.
    Check my answer

    5x over x = 5, so 5x + 2 over x = 5 + 2 over x.

    Check your knowledge: flip it

    Type answers like (3x+1)/x or 4+3/x.

    Write 2 + 1 over x as a single fraction.

    1. Write 2 with a bottom of x.
    2. What top over x is equal to 2?
    • That looks like adding the numbers. The 2 is not 2 over x: it is 2x over x.

    2 + 1 over x = 2x over x + 1 over x = 2x + 1 over x.

    Write 3 + 2 over x as a single fraction.

    1. Write 3 with a bottom of x.
    2. What top over x is equal to 3?
    • That looks like adding the numbers. The 3 is 3x over x.

    3 + 2 over x = 3x over x + 2 over x = 3x + 2 over x.

    Write 5x + 2 over x as a number plus a fraction. Type it like 4+3/x.

    1. Split the top into two fractions, each over x.
    2. Divide each part of the top by x.
    • You cannot cancel the x from 2 or from 5x in a sum. Split the top: 5x ÷ x = 5 and 2 stays over x.
    • The bottom is x, so divide each part of the top by x.

    5x + 2 over x = 5x over x + 2 over x = 5 + 2 over x.

    Write 1 over 2 + 1 over x as a single fraction.

    1. The common denominator is 2x.
    2. 1 over 2 = x over 2x.
    • Do not add the bottoms. Use a common denominator, 2x.

    1 over 2 + 1 over x = x over 2x + 2 over 2x = x + 2 over 2x.

    Write x + 3 as a fraction with a bottom of (x + 2). Do not simplify it.

    1. Multiply the top by (x + 2) as well as the bottom.
    2. Multiply the top by the same bracket as the bottom.
    • That changes the value. If you put (x + 2) on the bottom, multiply the top by (x + 2) too.

    x + 3 = (x + 3)(x + 2) over x + 2 = x² + 5x + 6 over x + 2. Check by simplifying: it cancels back to x + 3.

    Which is the same as 6x + 9 over 3?

    • 2x + 3
    • 6x + 3
    • 2x + 9
    1. Divide each term on the top by 3.
    2. What is 6x ÷ 3? What is 9 ÷ 3?

    6x + 9 over 3 = 6x over 3 + 9 over 3 = 2x + 3.

    Spot the mistake

    Finding an error in someone else's working helps you learn. For each one, decide what went wrong.

    Dee says: x + 5 over x = 5, because the x on the top cancels with the x on the bottom. What went wrong?

    • The x on top is only added
    • Nothing is wrong, because both parts are x
    • The answer should be 5x, not 5
    1. Try a number: put x = 5 into x + 5 over x.
    2. You can only cancel something that is multiplied on the whole top.

    Only a factor can cancel. In x + 5 the x is added to 5. With x = 5, x + 5 over x is 10 over 5 = 2, not 5.

    Eli says: 1 over x + 1 over 3 = 2 over x + 3. What went wrong?

    • He added tops and bottoms
    • He should have multiplied the tops
    • Nothing is wrong. That is how to add fractions.
    1. Try x = 3 in both sides.
    2. To add fractions you need a common denominator.

    You cannot add fractions by adding the tops and the bottoms. Use the common denominator 3x: 1 over x + 1 over 3 = 3 + x over 3x.

    Fay says: x² − 9 over x − 3 = x − 3, because x² − 9 = (x − 3)². What went wrong?

    • The brackets are wrong
    • Nothing is wrong. The answer is right.
    • The 9 should be 3, because 3 × 3 = 9
    1. Expand (x − 3)(x − 3). Do you get x² − 9?
    2. x² − 9 is a difference of two squares.

    (x − 3)² = x² − 6x + 9, not x² − 9. The right factorisation of x² − 9 is (x + 3)(x − 3), so the answer is x + 3.

    Gus says: x over 2 − x − 1 over 3 = 3x − 2x − 1 over 6 = x − 1 over 6. What went wrong?

    • The minus sign hit only one term
    • The common denominator should be 5
    • He should have added, not subtracted
    1. The minus sign applies to the whole of (x − 1).
    2. What is − 2 × (x − 1)?

    The minus sign must reach both terms: − 2(x − 1) = − 2x + 2, not − 2x − 1. So the top is 3x − 2x + 2 = x + 2, and the answer is x + 2 over 6.

    Check your knowledge: mixed review

    Mixed up on purpose, like a real paper. Work out which idea fits.

    Simplify 2x + 6 over x + 3.

    1. Take out a common factor from the top.
    2. 2x + 6 = 2(x + 3).
    • The bracket (x + 3) cancels as a whole. Nothing else is left but the 2.

    2x + 6 over x + 3 = 2(x + 3) over x + 3 = 2.

    Write x + 1 over 3 + x − 1 over 3 as a single fraction in its simplest form.

    1. The bottoms are the same, so add the tops.
    2. The +1 and − 1 on the top cancel.
    • Check the second top: x − 1. The +1 and − 1 cancel.
    • Do not add the bottoms. Keep the bottom as 3.

    (x + 1) + (x − 1) over 3 = 2x over 3.

    Solve 5 over x = 2. Give your answer as a decimal.

    1. Multiply both sides by x.
    2. 5 = 2x.
    • That looks like multiplying 5 by 2. Multiply both sides by x, then divide by 2.

    5 = 2x, so x = 2.5.

    Work out x over 2 × 6 over x².

    1. Multiply the tops and the bottoms.
    2. Cancel the numbers and one x.
    • x² is on the bottom, so the leftover x is on the bottom.
    • That is right but not simplified: 6 ÷ 2 = 3.

    x over 2 × 6 over x² = 6x over 2x² = 3 over x.

    Simplify x² + 5x + 6 over x + 2.

    1. Factorise the top.
    2. x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3).
    • The bracket that cancels is (x + 2). What is left is (x + 3).

    (x + 2)(x + 3) over x + 2 = x + 3.

    Exam style

    Use these like a real paper. The marks tell you how much to write. A calculator is fine here.

    Not sure what a question word means? Question words explained.

    Write your working on paper, then type the answer.

    [3 marks] Simplify fully x² − 16 over x² + x − 12. Type it like (x+1)/(x-2).

    1. Factorise the top and the bottom.
    2. Top: difference of two squares. Bottom: two numbers that multiply to make − 12 and add to make 1.
    • Check which bracket cancels. The top is (x − 4)(x + 4), and the bottom is (x + 4)(x − 3).
    • The bottom is (x + 4)(x − 3). Check the signs: 4 and − 3 add to 1.

    x² − 16 = (x − 4)(x + 4). x² + x − 12 = (x + 4)(x − 3). Cancel (x + 4): x − 4 over x − 3.

    • M1 for (x − 4)(x + 4)
    • M1 for (x + 4)(x − 3)
    • A1 for x − 4 over x − 3

    [3 marks] Write 1 over x + 2 + 2 over x − 1 as a single fraction. Type it like (2x+1)/((x+1)(x-3)).

    1. The common denominator is (x + 2)(x − 1).
    2. Top: (x − 1) + 2(x + 2).
    • Check the first top. It is (x − 1), so the numbers combine as 4 − 1 = 3.
    • Do not add the tops and bottoms separately. Use a common denominator.

    (x − 1) + 2(x + 2) over (x + 2)(x − 1) = x − 1 + 2x + 4 over (x + 2)(x − 1) = 3x + 3 over (x + 2)(x − 1).

    • M1 for the common denominator (x + 2)(x − 1)
    • M1 for the top (x − 1) + 2(x + 2)
    • A1 for 3x + 3 over (x + 2)(x − 1)

    [4 marks] Solve x − 3 = 10 over x. Type the two answers like 3,-1.

    1. Multiply every term by x.
    2. You get a quadratic. Move everything to one side and factorise.
    3. x² − 3x − 10 = 0.
    • Multiplying every term by x gives a quadratic, so there are two answers to find.
    • You have to solve the quadratic. Multiply by x first.

    x(x − 3) = 10, so x² − 3x − 10 = 0. (x − 5)(x + 2) = 0, so x = 5 or x = − 2.

    • M1 for multiplying every term by x
    • M1 for x² − 3x − 10 = 0
    • M1 for factorising (x − 5)(x + 2)
    • A1 for 5 and − 2

    [3 marks] Show that 1 over n − 1 over n + 2 = 2 over n(n + 2).

    1. Use a common denominator: n(n + 2).
    2. The top of the combined fraction is (n + 2) − n.

    Common denominator n(n + 2): n + 2 over n(n + 2) − n over n(n + 2) = n + 2 − n over n(n + 2) = 2 over n(n + 2), as required.

    • M1 for the common denominator n(n + 2)
    • M1 for the top (n + 2) − n
    • C1 for 2 on the top and a conclusion

    Higher [4 marks] Ben can paint a fence in x hours. Cara can paint it in (x + 6) hours. Working together they take 4 hours. So 1 over x + 1 over x + 6 = 1 over 4. Work out x.

    1. Multiply every term by 4x(x + 6).
    2. You get 4(x + 6) + 4x = x(x + 6). Collect it into a quadratic.
    3. x² − 2x − 24 = 0. Factorise, and remember x is a time.
    • That is the time for Cara (x + 6). The question asks for Ben.
    • A time cannot be negative. Choose the positive answer.

    4(x + 6) + 4x = x(x + 6), so 8x + 24 = x² + 6x, so x² − 2x − 24 = 0. (x − 6)(x + 4) = 0, so x = 6 or x = − 4. A time must be positive, so x = 6 hours.

    • M1 for multiplying through by 4x(x + 6)
    • M1 for x² − 2x − 24 = 0
    • M1 for factorising (x − 6)(x + 4)
    • A1 for x = 6 (reject − 4)

    Higher [3 marks] Simplify fully x² − 4 over x + 3 ÷ x + 2 over x² − 9. Type it like (x+1)(x-4).

    1. Flip the second fraction and multiply.
    2. Factorise x² − 4 and x² − 9. Cancel matching brackets.

    (x + 2)(x − 2) over x + 3 × (x + 3)(x − 3) over x + 2. Cancel (x + 2) and (x + 3): (x − 2)(x − 3).

    • M1 for flipping and multiplying
    • M1 for factorising to (x + 2)(x − 2) and (x + 3)(x − 3)
    • A1 for (x − 2)(x − 3)

    Why it works

    • Cancelling is dividing by 1. (x + 3) over (x + 3) = 1 for any x where the bottom is not zero. That is why a matching bracket can go, but only if it multiplies the whole top and the whole bottom.
    • Letters are numbers. Put x = 5 into x + 5 over x and you get 2, not 5. Checking with a number is a quick way to catch a wrong cancelling.
    • A common denominator changes how a fraction looks, not its value. Multiplying the top and the bottom by the same thing is multiplying by 1.
    • Dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal. Dividing by 1 over 2 asks “how many halves fit?”, and that is multiplying by 2.
    • Rates add. If A does 1 over 4 of a job an hour and B does 1 over 12, together they do 1 over 3 an hour. It is the rates (fractions per hour) that add, never the times.

    Common traps

    • Cancelling a term instead of a factor: x + 5 over x is not 5.
    • Adding fractions by adding the tops and the bottoms.
    • Forgetting to put the second top in a bracket when subtracting.
    • Flipping the first fraction instead of the second when dividing.
    • Multiplying only some terms by the common denominator when solving an equation.
    • Writing the bottom without brackets: 1/2x means 1 over 2 × x, but 1/(2x) is 1 over 2x.

    Going further

    Solve x + 1 over x = 2. Type the value of x.

    1. Multiply every term by x.
    2. x² − 2x + 1 = 0 is a perfect square.
    • x cannot be 0, because 1 over x would not exist. Solve the quadratic.

    x² + 1 = 2x, so x² − 2x + 1 = 0, so (x − 1)² = 0. There is only one answer: x = 1. Check: 1 + 1 = 2.

    Show that 1 over n − 1 over n + 1 = 1 over n(n + 1). Then use the result to explain why 1 over 1 × 2 + 1 over 2 × 3 + 1 over 3 × 4 = 3 over 4.

    1. Use the common denominator n(n + 1).
    2. Write each of the three fractions as a difference like 1 over 1 − 1 over 2. What happens when you add them?

    1 over n − 1 over n + 1 = n + 1 − n over n(n + 1) = 1 over n(n + 1). So 1 over 1 × 2 = 1 over 1 − 1 over 2, 1 over 2 × 3 = 1 over 2 − 1 over 3 and 1 over 3 × 4 = 1 over 3 − 1 over 4. Adding: the middle parts cancel, leaving 1 over 1 − 1 over 4 = 3 over 4.

    Use the same idea to work out 1 over 1 × 2 + 1 over 2 × 3 + 1 over 3 × 4 + 1 over 4 × 5. Type it like 3/4.

    1. Each part is 1 over n − 1 over n + 1.
    2. Most of the parts cancel. What is left at the two ends?
    • That is the sum of the first three. There are four fractions here.
    • You are left with 1 over 1 at the start as well as 1 over 5 at the end.

    The sum is (1 over 1 − 1 over 2) + (1 over 2 − 1 over 3) + (1 over 3 − 1 over 4) + (1 over 4 − 1 over 5) = 1 over 1 − 1 over 5 = 4 over 5.

    Check your knowledge: on the job

    Real jobs from the building trades: electrics, plumbing and carpentry. Prices are typical, so check them with your own supplier.

    An electrician wires two resistors in parallel, one of 6 ohms and one of 12 ohms. The total resistance R in ohms follows 1 over R = 1 over 6 + 1 over 12. Work out R, in ohms.

    1. Add the two fractions first.
    2. 1 over 6 + 1 over 12 = ___ over 12. Then turn the answer upside down to get R.
    • That adds the resistances. In parallel you add the reciprocals, 1 over 6 + 1 over 12.
    • That is 1 over R. Turn it upside down to get R.

    1 over 6 + 1 over 12 = 2 over 12 + 1 over 12 = 3 over 12 = 1 over 4. So 1 over R = 1 over 4 and R = 4 ohms.

    Two resistors of 10 ohms and 15 ohms are wired in parallel. Use 1 over R = 1 over 10 + 1 over 15 to find the total resistance R, in ohms.

    1. Use the common denominator 30.
    2. 1 over 10 = 3 over 30. What is 1 over 15 over 30?
    • That adds the resistances. In parallel you add the reciprocals.
    • That is 1 over R. Turn it upside down to get R.

    1 over 10 + 1 over 15 = 3 over 30 + 2 over 30 = 5 over 30 = 1 over 6. So R = 6 ohms.

    A plumber fills a water tank using two pipes. Pipe A alone takes 12 hours and pipe B alone takes 6 hours. How many hours do they take together?

    1. Write each pipe as a fraction of the tank per hour.
    2. 1 over 12 + 1 over 6.
    • That is the average of the two times. Rates add, so add the fractions 1 over 12 and 1 over 6.
    • That looks like adding the times. Add the rates (fractions per hour) instead.

    1 over 12 + 1 over 6 = 1 over 12 + 2 over 12 = 3 over 12 = 1 over 4 per hour. So they take 4 hours.

    [4 marks] A carpenter and an apprentice fit a kitchen. The carpenter would take 6 days alone and the apprentice would take 3 days alone. Each is paid £200 a day. How much does the labour cost when they work together? Give your answer in pounds, like 120.

    1. Find the rate of each as a fraction of the job per day.
    2. 1 over 6 + 1 over 3. How many days is that for the whole job?
    3. Two people for that many days, at £200 a day each.
    • That is one person for 2 days. There are two people.
    • That is the carpenter working alone for 6 days. The question asks for them working together.

    1 over 6 + 1 over 3 = 1 over 6 + 2 over 6 = 3 over 6 = 1 over 2 of the job per day, so 2 days. Cost = 2 people × 2 days × £200 = £800.

    • M1 for 1 over 6 + 1 over 3
    • M1 for 1 over 2 per day, so 2 days
    • M1 for 2 × 2 × 200
    • A1 for £800

    [3 marks] An electrician has two resistors in parallel. One is x ohms. The other is 2x ohms. The total resistance is 4 ohms. So 1 over x + 1 over 2x = 1 over 4. Work out x.

    1. Combine 1 over x + 1 over 2x first. The common denominator is 2x.
    2. 2 over 2x + 1 over 2x = 3 over 2x.
    3. Then solve 3 over 2x = 1 over 4.
    • That is the total resistance. The question asks for x.
    • That is 2x. The question asks for x.

    1 over x + 1 over 2x = 2 over 2x + 1 over 2x = 3 over 2x. 3 over 2x = 1 over 4, so 12 = 2x and x = 6 ohms.

    • M1 for 2 over 2x + 1 over 2x = 3 over 2x
    • M1 for 3 over 2x = 1 over 4 and 12 = 2x
    • A1 for 6

    Higher [4 marks] A plumber fills a cistern with two pipes. Pipe A takes x hours alone. Pipe B takes (x + 3) hours alone. Together they take 2 hours. Find x, the time for pipe A alone, in hours.

    1. Write the equation: 1 over x + 1 over x + 3 = 1 over 2.
    2. Multiply every term by 2x(x + 3) to get a quadratic.
    3. x² − x − 6 = 0. Factorise it, and choose the sensible answer.
    • That is the time for pipe B (x + 3). The question asks for pipe A.
    • A time cannot be negative. Choose the positive answer.

    1 over x + 1 over x + 3 = 1 over 2. Multiply by 2x(x + 3): 2(x + 3) + 2x = x(x + 3), so x² − x − 6 = 0. (x − 3)(x + 2) = 0. A time is positive, so x = 3 hours.

    • M1 for the equation 1 over x + 1 over x + 3 = 1 over 2
    • M1 for 2(x + 3) + 2x = x(x + 3)
    • M1 for x² − x − 6 = 0 factorised as (x − 3)(x + 2)
    • A1 for x = 3
