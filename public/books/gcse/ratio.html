Ratio and proportion · Foundation and Higher

Ratio

A ratio compares amounts, so you can share things out fairly, scale a recipe or a plan up and down, and spot the better deal.

Where you will use this: mixing concrete or paint (1 part cement to 4 parts sand), scaling a recipe, reading a map or a builder's plan, and checking which pack in the shop is cheaper.

1. What a ratio is

A Ratio compares amounts of the same kind. We write it with a colon. A mix of 2 parts cement to 6 parts sand is the ratio 2 : 6, read “2 to 6”.

cement : sand = 2 : 6

Key idea: a ratio says “for every”. For every 2 scoops of cement there are 6 scoops of sand. The order matters: cement : sand = 2 : 6 is not the same as sand : cement = 6 : 2.

Simplifying and equivalent ratios

Equivalent ratios describe the same mix with different numbers. Multiply or divide both sides by the same number: 2 : 6 = 1 : 3 = 4 : 12. A ratio is in its simplest form when no whole number bigger than 1 divides both sides. Dividing by the HCF does it in one step. It is the same idea as simplifying a fraction.

Worked example: simplify

A bag has 12 red and 8 blue counters. Write red : blue in its simplest form.

  1. Write the ratio in order: 12 : 8.
  2. The HCF of 12 and 8 is 4.
  3. 12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 8 ÷ 4 = 2.
  4. red : blue = 3 : 2. Nothing else divides both 3 and 2.

Try it: simplify 10 : 15.

Same units first

A ratio only works when both sides use the same unit. For 40 cm to 2 m, change 2 m into 200 cm first. Then 40 : 200 = 1 : 5. The units then cancel and are not written in the ratio.

Now you finish it

Simplify 300 g : 1.5 kg.

  1. Change to the same unit: 1.5 kg = 1500 g.
  2. The ratio is 300 : 1500.
  3. Divide both by 300: ___ : ___.
Check my answer

300 ÷ 300 = 1 and 1500 ÷ 300 = 5, so the ratio is 1 : 5.

A ratio can also be written as 1 : n. To turn 4 : 10 into 1 : n, divide both sides by 4 to get 1 : 2.5. That is fine, because n does not have to be a whole number.

2. Sharing in a ratio

To share an amount in a ratio, think of parts. In the ratio 2 : 3 there are 2 + 3 = 5 equal parts. A bar model shows it.

Key idea: add the parts, divide the total by the number of parts to get one part, then multiply to get each share. Check that the shares add back to the total.

Worked example

Share £60 in the ratio 2 : 3.

  1. Add the parts: 2 + 3 = 5 parts.
  2. One part: £60 ÷ 5 = £12.
  3. First share: 2 × £12 = £24. Second share: 3 × £12 = £36.
  4. Check: £24 + £36 = £60 ✓.

Now you finish it

Share £48 in the ratio 5 : 3.

  1. Add the parts: 5 + 3 = 8 parts.
  2. One part: £48 ÷ 8 = ___.
  3. The shares are 5 × ___ and 3 × ___.
Check my answer

One part is £6, so the shares are £30 and £18. Check: 30 + 18 = 48 ✓.

Sharing works with three or more parts too. For 1 : 2 : 3 there are 6 parts, so divide by 6.

Check your knowledge: sharing in a ratio

Share £40 in the ratio 1 : 3. How much is the smaller share, in pounds?

  1. How many equal parts are there altogether?
  2. Divide the total by the number of parts to find one part.
  • That is the larger share. The question asks for the smaller one.
  • You divided by 3. Add the parts first: there are 4 parts.

1 + 3 = 4 parts. One part is £40 ÷ 4 = £10. The smaller share is 1 × 10 = £10. (The larger is £30.)

Mia and Tom share a £72 prize in the ratio 4 : 5. How much does Tom get, in pounds?

4 + 5 = 9 parts. One part is £72 ÷ 9 = £8. Tom gets 5 × 8 = £40. (Mia gets £32, and 32 + 40 = 72 ✓.)

A bricklayer mixes cement, sand and gravel in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3 to make 90 kg of mix. How many kilograms of sand are in it?

  2. Find the mass of one part.
  3. Sand is 2 of the parts.

  • That is the gravel (3 parts). Sand is 2 parts.

1 + 2 + 3 = 6 parts. One part is 90 ÷ 6 = 15 kg. Sand is 2 × 15 = 30 kg. (Cement 15 kg, gravel 45 kg: 15 + 30 + 45 = 90 ✓.)

A band shares £350 from a gig between the guitarist, the drummer and the singer in the ratio 2 : 2 : 3. How much does the singer get, in pounds?

  1. Add the three numbers to get the number of parts.
  2. Find the value of one part.
  3. The singer gets 3 parts.

  • You divided by 3. Divide by the total number of parts, 2 + 2 + 3 = 7.

2 + 2 + 3 = 7 parts. One part is £350 ÷ 7 = £50. The singer gets 3 × 50 = £150. (The others get £100 each, and 100 + 100 + 150 = 350 ✓.)

  • 10 and 20
  • 12 and 18
  • 15 and 15
  1. Add the parts, then divide 30 by that number.
  2. Check each option: do the two shares add to 30, and are they in the ratio 2 : 3?

2 + 3 = 5 parts, so one part is 30 ÷ 5 = 6. The shares are 2 × 6 = 12 and 3 × 6 = 18.

A plumber mixes cleaning concentrate with water in the ratio 1 : 4 to make 35 litres of solution. How many litres of concentrate does she need?

  1. Add the parts: how many are there?
  2. Share the 35 litres equally between the parts.
  3. Concentrate is the 1 in the ratio, so it is one part.
  • That is the water (4 parts). The concentrate is 1 part.
  • You divided by 4. Divide by the total number of parts, 1 + 4 = 5.

3. Ratio and fractions

A ratio and a fraction are two views of the same split. If the ratio of boys to girls is 3 : 5, there are 3 + 5 = 8 parts altogether. So boys are 3 over 8 of the class and girls are 5 over 8.

Key idea: in a ratio a : b, the first share is the fraction a over a + b of the total. The bottom of the fraction is the total parts, not the other number in the ratio.

Worked example: ratio to fraction

Cats to dogs at a shelter is 2 : 5. What fraction of the animals are dogs?

  1. Total parts: 2 + 5 = 7.
  2. Dogs are 5 of the 7 parts.
  3. So 5 over 7 of the animals are dogs.

Worked example: fraction to ratio

3 over 8 of a team's shots are on target. Write on target : off target as a ratio.

  1. On target is 3 parts out of 8, so off target is 8 − 3 = 5 parts.
  2. The ratio is 3 : 5.

Try it: in the ratio 1 : 4, what fraction is the first share?

Check your knowledge: ratio and fractions

The ratio of cats to dogs on a street is 3 : 7. What fraction of the pets are dogs? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/10.

  1. Add the parts to find the total number of parts.
  2. Dogs are 7 of those parts.
  • You compared dogs with cats. A fraction compares dogs with all the pets.
  • That is the fraction of cats. The question asks about dogs.

3 + 7 = 10 parts, and dogs are 7 of them, so 7 over 10 of the pets are dogs.

3 over 8 of a rugby team's kicks are successful. Write the ratio of successful to unsuccessful kicks in its simplest form, like 3:4.

  1. If 3 out of 8 parts are successful, how many parts are not?
  2. Write successful first, then unsuccessful, with a colon between them.
  • That compares successful with all the kicks. Unsuccessful is 8 − 3 = 5 parts.

8 − 3 = 5 parts unsuccessful. successful : unsuccessful = 3:5.

Three friends share money in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3. What fraction does the biggest share get?

  • 1 over 3
  • 3 over 1
  • 1 over 2
  1. Add up all the parts first.
  2. The biggest share is 3 parts. Write it over the total.

1 + 2 + 3 = 6 parts. The biggest share is 3 over 6 = 1 over 2 of the total.

In a paint mix the ratio of red to white is 2 : 5. What fraction of the mix is red? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/10.

  1. How many parts is the mix in total?
  2. Red is 2 of them.
  • That compares red with white. Compare red with the whole mix: 2 + 5 = 7 parts.
  • That is the fraction of white. The question asks for red.

2 + 5 = 7 parts, and red is 2 of them, so 2 over 7 of the mix is red.

4. Scales, recipes and changing units

A scale such as 1 : 50 000 is a ratio. Every 1 cm on the map is 50 000 cm in real life. A model or a plan works the same way. The scale is a Scale factor: multiply to go from the drawing to the real thing, divide to go the other way.

Units you need

  • Length: 10 mm = 1 cm, 100 cm = 1 m, 1000 m = 1 km.
  • Volume: 1000 ml = 1 litre.
  • Money: 100p = £1.

Worked example: a map

A map has the scale 1 : 25 000. A path is 4 cm long on the map. How long is it in real life, in km?

  1. Multiply by the scale: 4 × 25 000 = 100 000 cm.
  2. Change cm to m: 100 000 ÷ 100 = 1000 m.
  3. Change m to km: 1000 ÷ 1000 = 1 km.

Sense check: 1 cm on this map is 250 m, so 4 cm is 1000 m. ✓

Now you finish it

A plan has the scale 1 : 200. A wall is 6 cm long on the plan. How long is the real wall, in metres?

  1. Multiply by the scale: 6 × 200 = ___ cm.
  2. Divide by 100 to change cm to m: ___ m.
Check my answer

6 × 200 = 1200 cm, and 1200 ÷ 100 = 12 m.

Recipes

To scale a recipe, find the factor: new number of portions ÷ old number of portions. Multiply every amount by it. Exchange rates work the same way. If £1 buys €1.15, then £80 buys 80 × 1.15 euros.

Check your knowledge: scales, recipes and units

  2. That gives centimetres. How many centimetres are in a metre?
  • That is in centimetres. The question asks for metres: divide by 100.
  • You divided instead of multiplying. The real thing is bigger than the drawing.

5 × 100 = 500 cm = 5 m.

  1. Multiply 3 by 50 000. The answer is in centimetres.
  2. Change centimetres to metres, then metres to kilometres.
  • That is in metres. The question asks for kilometres: divide by 1000.
  • That is in centimetres. Change it to kilometres.

3 × 50 000 = 150 000 cm. 150 000 ÷ 100 = 1500 m, and 1500 ÷ 1000 = 1.5 km.

  1. Change 15.2 m into centimetres first.
  2. The model is smaller than the real thing, so divide by 76.
  • That is the real length in centimetres. The model is 76 times smaller: divide by 76.
  • That is in metres. The question asks for centimetres.

15.2 m = 1520 cm. 1520 ÷ 76 = 20 cm.

  2. Multiply by 15.

On a 1 : 20 plan, a kitchen worktop is 15 cm long. How long is the real worktop, in metres?

  1. Multiply the plan length by 20.
  2. Then change centimetres to metres.
  • That is in centimetres. The question asks for metres.
  • You divided by 20. The real worktop is bigger than the plan.

15 × 20 = 300 cm = 3 m.

A plumber has a 2.4 m length of pipe. She cuts it into pieces that are each 30 cm long. How many pieces can she cut?

  1. The units must match. Change 2.4 m into centimetres.
  2. How many 30 cm pieces fit into that length?
  • You divided 2.4 by 30 without changing the units. 2.4 m is 240 cm.
  • You changed 2.4 m to 2400. A metre is 100 cm, not 1000 cm.

2.4 m = 240 cm. 240 ÷ 30 = 8 pieces.

The exchange rate is £1 = €1.15. How many euros do you get for £80?

  1. Each pound buys 1.15 euros.
  2. Multiply the number of pounds by 1.15.
  • You divided by 1.15. You are changing pounds into euros, so multiply.

80 × 1.15 = 92, so you get €92.

5. Direct proportion and the unitary method

Two amounts are in direct proportion when doubling one doubles the other, and halving one halves the other. Five tiles cost five times as much as one tile. The ratio of cost to number of tiles never changes.

The Unitary method finds the value of one first. Then you multiply to get what you want.

Key idea: one first, then scale. Divide to find one, multiply to find many. A double number line shows both steps.

Worked example

5 tiles cost £12.50. How much do 8 tiles cost?

  1. Find one tile: £12.50 ÷ 5 = £2.50.
  2. Multiply by 8: 8 × £2.50 = £20.
  3. Sense check: 8 tiles is more than 5 tiles, so the cost should be more than £12.50. ✓

Now you finish it

7 batteries cost £10.50. How much do 12 batteries cost?

  1. One battery: £10.50 ÷ 7 = £___.
  2. Twelve batteries: 12 × ___ = £___.
Check my answer

One battery costs £1.50. 12 × 1.50 = £18.

Is it direct proportion?

Not everything is. A taxi with a £3 call-out fee plus £2 a mile is not in direct proportion: 0 miles still costs £3, and 4 miles (£11) is not double 2 miles (£7). In direct proportion, 0 of one means 0 of the other.

Check your knowledge: proportion

4 pens cost £3.20. How much do 7 pens cost, in pounds? Type it like 5.60 or 5.6.

  1. Find the cost of one pen first.
  2. Then multiply by 7.
  • That is the cost of one pen. You need seven.
  • That is 8 pens, double 4. You need 7 pens.

£3.20 ÷ 4 = £0.80 for one pen. 7 × 0.80 = £5.60, so 5.6 in pounds.

A train travels 150 miles in 2 hours at a steady speed. How far does it go in 3.5 hours?

  1. How far does the train go in one hour?
  2. Multiply by 3.5.
  • That is the distance in one hour. You need 3.5 hours.
  • You multiplied 150 by 3.5. The 150 miles took 2 hours, not 1 hour.

150 ÷ 2 = 75 miles in one hour. 75 × 3.5 = 262.5 miles.

In a game you earn 120 points every 5 minutes, at a steady rate. How many points do you earn in 12 minutes?

  1. Find the points for one minute.
  2. Then scale up to 12 minutes.
  • That is the points for one minute. You need 12 minutes.
  • You multiplied 120 by 12. The 120 points took 5 minutes, not 1 minute.

120 ÷ 5 = 24 points a minute. 24 × 12 = 288 points.

6. Best buy

To find the Best buy, compare like with like. Work out the price of the same amount for each deal (price per 100 g, per litre, per item). Or work out how much you get for £1. The lowest price per unit is the best value.

Worked example

Pack A is 500 g for £1.60. Pack B is 750 g for £2.25. Which is the better buy?

  1. Price per 100 g for A: £1.60 ÷ 5 = 32p.
  2. Price per 100 g for B: £2.25 ÷ 7.5 = 30p.
  3. 30p is less than 32p, so pack B is the better buy.

The bigger pack is not always cheaper per unit. Always work it out.

Now you finish it

Compare 2 litres for £3.10 with 3 litres for £4.35.

  1. Price per litre for the first: £3.10 ÷ 2 = £___.
  2. Price per litre for the second: £4.35 ÷ 3 = £___.
  3. The lower price per litre is the better buy.
Check my answer

First: £1.55 a litre. Second: £1.45 a litre. The 3 litre bottle is the better buy.

Check your knowledge: best buy

  2. Which operation shares an amount equally: add, subtract, multiply or divide?
  • You multiplied. Dividing the price by the number of pencils gives the price of one.

88 ÷ 4 = 22p.

Which pack is the best value?

  • Pack A: 250 grams for £1.00
  • Pack B: 400 g for £1.48
  • Pack C: 1 kilogram for £4.20
  1. Find the price per 100 g for each pack.
  2. The best value is the lowest price per 100 g. Remember 1 kg is 1000 g.

Per 100 g: A is 100p ÷ 2.5 = 40p. B is 148p ÷ 4 = 37p. C is 420p ÷ 10 = 42p. Pack B is cheapest per 100 g.

A 2.5 litre tin of paint costs £18. A 5 litre tin costs £32.50. How much cheaper per litre, in pounds, is the larger tin? Type it like 0.55.

  1. Find the price per litre of each tin.
  2. Then take one away from the other.
  • That is the saving on 5 litres of the small tin (2 × 18 = 36 against 32.50). The question asks per litre.

2.5 litres: £18 ÷ 2.5 = £7.20 per litre. 5 litres: £32.50 ÷ 5 = £6.50 per litre. The difference is 7.20 − 6.50 = £0.7 (70p).

A multipack of 6 cans costs £2.70. A pack of 10 cans costs £4.80. What is the price per can, in pence, of the cheaper deal?

  1. Change both prices into pence.
  2. Work out the price of one can for each pack.
  3. Choose the lower one.
  • That is the price per can for the 10 pack, which is the dearer deal.

6 cans: 270p ÷ 6 = 45p each. 10 cans: 480p ÷ 10 = 48p each. The cheaper deal is 45p a can.

Flip it: run it backwards

Sharing goes from a total to the shares. Now go the other way: from one share to the total, or from the shares to the ratio.

Forwards

Share £60 in the ratio 2 : 3.

Total ÷ parts = one part. Then multiply.

Backwards

The smaller share is £24 in the ratio 2 : 3. What is the total?

Share ÷ its parts = one part. Then × all the parts.

Key idea: a known share tells you the value of one part. If 2 parts are £24, one part is £12. Then the total is 5 parts: 5 × 12 = £60.

Worked example: from one share

Two sisters share money in the ratio 3 : 5. The younger gets £24. How much does the older get, and what is the total?

  1. Younger is 3 parts = £24, so one part is £24 ÷ 3 = £8.
  2. Older is 5 parts: 5 × £8 = £40.
  3. Total: 8 parts, 8 × £8 = £64.

Worked example: from the difference

Two shares are in the ratio 2 : 5. The difference between them is £36. Find the two shares.

  1. The difference is 5 − 2 = 3 parts, and those 3 parts are £36.
  2. One part is £36 ÷ 3 = £12.
  3. The shares are 2 × 12 = £24 and 5 × 12 = £60.

Check: 60 − 24 = 36 ✓ and 24 : 60 = 2 : 5 ✓.

Try it: the shares are £24 and £40. What is the ratio in its simplest form?

  • That is the difference between them, not the larger share.

The difference is 7 − 3 = 4 parts, so 4 parts are £48 and one part is £12. The larger share is 7 × 12 = £84. (The smaller is £36.)

Two sisters share money in the ratio 5 : 3. The older sister gets £18 more than the younger. How much money did they share in total, in pounds?

  1. The older sister has 5 parts and the younger has 3. The difference is how many parts?
  2. That difference is £18. Find one part, then the total parts.
  • That is how much more the older sister gets. The question asks for the total.
  • That is the older sister's share (5 parts). Add the younger sister's share too.

The difference is 5 − 3 = 2 parts = £18, so one part is £9. Total: 8 parts × 9 = £72. (Older £45, younger £27.)

Two shares are in the ratio 2 : 5. The larger share is 35. What is the total?

  • 70
  • 14
  • 49
  1. 35 is the larger share. How many parts is that?
  2. Find one part, then the total number of parts.

5 parts are 35, so one part is 7. The total is 2 + 5 = 7 parts: 7 × 7 = 49.

Zoe has 24 stickers and Ben has 40. Write the ratio of Zoe's stickers to Ben's in its simplest form, like 2:3.

  1. Write 24 : 40 in the order the question gives.
  2. Divide both sides by the same number: what is the biggest one that works?
  • Right values, but it can be simplified. Divide both by 8.
  • Right values. 12 and 20 still divide by 4.
  • That is Ben to Zoe. Zoe comes first.

The HCF of 24 and 40 is 8. 24 ÷ 8 = 3 and 40 ÷ 8 = 5, so the ratio is 3:5.

Spot the mistake

Finding an error in someone else's working helps you learn. For each one, decide what went wrong.

Dev says: To share £35 in the ratio 2 : 5, I do 35 ÷ 2 = 17.5 and 35 ÷ 5 = 7. What went wrong?

  • He divided by each number in the ratio. He should divide by 2 + 5 = 7.
  • He should have multiplied £35 by 2 and by 5, and then added the two answers together.
  • He found the total of the ratio wrongly. It should be 2 × 5 = 10, so he should divide by 10.
  1. Do his two answers add to 35?
  2. 17.5 + 7 is 24.5, not 35. So the method cannot be right.

2 + 5 = 7 parts. One part is 35 ÷ 7 = 5, so the shares are 2 × 5 = £10 and 5 × 5 = £25. Check: 10 + 25 = 35 ✓.

Ana says: 3 : 5 is the same as 6 : 8, because I added 3 to both sides. What went wrong?

  • She added 3 to both sides. She should multiply both sides by the same number.
  • Nothing is wrong: 3 : 5 and 6 : 8 are the same, she just used a different multiplier on each side.
  • She should have multiplied both sides by 2 (not added 3), to get 6 : 10, an equivalent ratio.
  1. What would the equivalent ratio with 6 on the left look like?
  2. Equivalent ratios have the same multiplier on both sides.

3 × 2 = 6, so 5 × 2 = 10. The ratio 3 : 5 is the same as 6 : 10, not 6 : 8.

Lee says: The ratio 1 : 4 means 1 over 4 of the total is the first share. What went wrong?

  • The total is 5 parts, so the first share is 1 over 5.
  • The first share is 4 over 1 of the total, because the ratio has a 4 in it.
  • The fraction should be 1 over 4 + 4 over 4, the two parts of the ratio added up.
  1. How many equal parts are there altogether in 1 : 4?
  2. The bottom of the fraction is the total parts.

1 + 4 = 5 parts, so the first share is 1 over 5 of the total. 1 over 4 would be the ratio 1 : 3.

Check your knowledge: mixed review

Mixed up on purpose, like a real paper. Work out which method fits.

Simplify 45 : 60 fully, like 2:3.

  1. Find the biggest number that divides both 45 and 60.
  2. Try 15. Divide the left side and the right side by it.
  • Right value. 9 and 12 still divide by 3.
  • Right value. 15 and 20 still divide by 5.

The HCF of 45 and 60 is 15. 45 ÷ 15 = 3 and 60 ÷ 15 = 4, so 3:4.

Share 90 in the ratio 4 : 5. What is the larger share?

  1. Add the parts.
  2. One part is 90 divided by the number of parts.
  • That is the smaller share. The question asks for the larger one.

4 + 5 = 9 parts, so one part is 10. The larger share is 5 × 10 = 50.

3 over 8 of a class are girls. Write the ratio of girls to boys in its simplest form, like 2:3.

  1. If 3 parts out of 8 are girls, how many parts are boys?
  2. Girls come first in the ratio.
  • That compares girls with the whole class. Boys are 5 parts.
  • That is boys to girls. Girls come first.

Girls are 3 parts out of 8, so boys are 5 parts. girls : boys = 3:5.

A cyclist rides 24 km in 90 minutes at a steady speed. How far does she ride in 2 hours?

  1. Change 2 hours to minutes.
  2. Find the distance for 30 minutes, then scale up.
  • That is 24 × 2. The 24 km took 90 minutes, which is 1.5 hours, not 1 hour.
  • That is the distance for 1 hour. She rides for 2 hours.

2 hours = 120 minutes. Every 30 minutes she rides 24 ÷ 3 = 8 km. 120 minutes is 4 lots of 30: 4 × 8 = 32 km.

A site plan has the scale 1 : 500. A building is 8.4 cm long on the plan. How long is the real building, in metres?

  1. Multiply the plan length by 500.
  2. Change centimetres into metres.
  • That is in centimetres. Divide by 100 for metres.
  • You divided by 500. The real building is bigger than the plan.

8.4 × 500 = 4200 cm = 42 m.

Which is the better buy: 4 tomatoes for £1.60 or 6 tomatoes for £2.52?

  • They cost the same per tomato
  • 4 for £1.60
  • 6 for £2.52, because you get more
  1. Work out the price of one tomato for each deal.
  2. Change the prices to pence to make the division easier.

4 tomatoes: £1.60 ÷ 4 = 40p each. 6 tomatoes: £2.52 ÷ 6 = 42p each. The 4 for £1.60 deal is cheaper per tomato.

Two shares are in the ratio 5 : 2. The larger share is 45. What is the total?

  1. 45 is 5 parts. What is one part?
  2. The total is 5 + 2 parts.
  • That is the smaller share (2 parts). The question asks for the total.
  • You doubled 45. Find one part first: 45 ÷ 5 = 9.

5 parts are 45, so one part is 9. Total: 7 parts × 9 = 63.

Write the ratio 750 ml : 1.5 litres in its simplest form, like 1:3.

  1. Change both to millilitres first.
  2. 1.5 litres is 1500 ml. Now simplify 750 : 1500.
  • The units must match before you simplify. 1.5 litres is 1500 ml.
  • Right values, but it can be simplified. Divide both by 750.
  • You did not change the units. 1.5 litres is 1500 ml.

1.5 litres = 1500 ml. 750 : 1500, divide both sides by 750, gives 1:2.

Exam style

Use these like a real paper. The marks tell you how much to write. M1 is a mark for a correct method, A1 a mark for the correct answer.

Not sure what a question word means? Question words explained.

Write your working on paper, then type the answer.

[3 marks] Sofia mixes white paint and blue paint in the ratio 5 : 2. She needs 14 litres of mixed paint. How many litres of blue paint does she use?

  1. Add the parts to find the total number of parts.
  2. One part is the total divided by that number.
  3. Blue is 2 parts.
  • That is the white paint (5 parts). Blue is 2 parts.
  • You divided by 5. Divide by the total number of parts, 5 + 2 = 7.

M1: 5 + 2 = 7 parts. M1: 14 ÷ 7 = 2 litres for one part. A1: blue is 2 × 2 = 4 litres. (White is 10 litres, and 10 + 4 = 14 ✓.)

[4 marks] A recipe for 8 portions uses 600 g of flour. Mia wants to make 20 portions and has 1.2 kg of flour. How many more grams of flour does she need?

  1. Change 1.2 kg into grams first.
  2. Find how much flour one portion needs, then scale to 20 portions.
  3. Compare with what she has.
  • That is the flour needed for 20 portions. The question asks how much more she needs.
  • That is how much she has. Find what she needs, then take it away.
  • That is the flour for one portion. Scale up to 20 portions.

M1: one portion needs 600 ÷ 8 = 75 g (or the scale factor 20 ÷ 8 = 2.5). M1: 20 portions need 75 × 20 = 1500 g. M1: she has 1.2 kg = 1200 g. A1: 1500 − 1200 = 300 g more.

[3 marks] There are 28 pupils in a class and 12 of them are boys. Show that the ratio of boys to girls is 3 : 4. Type the ratio in its simplest form, like 2:3.

  1. How many girls are there?
  2. Write boys : girls in order.
  3. Simplify it by dividing both sides by the same number.
  • That compares boys with the whole class. The question asks for boys to girls.
  • Right values, but it can be simplified. Divide both by 4.

M1: girls = 28 − 12 = 16. M1: boys : girls = 12 : 16. A1: divide both sides by 4 to get 3:4. So the ratio is 3 : 4, as the question says.

Why it works

  • Equivalent ratios are the same mix. Multiplying both sides by 3 is like using 3 buckets of the mix instead of 1. It looks bigger, but the recipe is the same.
  • Sharing by parts. A ratio cuts the total into equal parts. Once you know the size of one part, every share is just a count of parts.
  • The unitary method. In direct proportion, one unit has a fixed price. Finding it is a single division. Every other amount is that price times a number.
  • Best buy. Prices for different sizes cannot be compared directly. Dividing makes them the price of the same amount, so they can be.

Common traps

  • Dividing the total by one number in the ratio instead of by the total number of parts.
  • Adding the same number to both sides to make an equivalent ratio.
  • Writing the ratio in the wrong order.
  • Forgetting to put both sides in the same unit before simplifying.
  • Using the other number in the ratio on the bottom of the fraction instead of the total parts.
  • Giving an answer in the wrong unit (cm instead of m, p instead of £).

Going further

a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 4 : 5. Write a : b : c using whole numbers, like 2:3:4.

  1. b has to be the same number in both ratios. Find a common multiple of 3 and 4.
  2. Rewrite each ratio so that b is 12.
  • The two values of b are different (3 and 4), so you cannot join the ratios yet. Make b the same first.
  • You have not scaled the ratios. Both should show b as 12.

Make b equal 12. a : b = 2 : 3 = 8 : 12. b : c = 4 : 5 = 12 : 15. So a : b : c = 8:12:15. Check: 8 : 12 = 2 : 3 ✓ and 12 : 15 = 4 : 5 ✓.

A bottle holds 10 litres of squash mix, made from concentrate and water in the ratio 1 : 4. How many litres of water must be added to change the ratio to 1 : 9?

  1. How much concentrate is in the 10 litres? That amount stays the same.
  2. In the new mix the concentrate is 1 part. How much water goes with it?
  3. Take away the water you already had.
  • That is the total amount of water in the new mix. You already had some of it.
  • You changed the ratio from 1 : 4 to 1 : 9 by adding 5 to the water part. Find the actual amounts.

1 + 4 = 5 parts, so the 10 litres are 2 litres concentrate and 8 litres water. The concentrate stays 2 litres. For 1 : 9 the water must be 9 × 2 = 18 litres. Add 18 − 8 = 10 litres of water.

Ali and Bea's ages are in the ratio 2 : 3. In 10 years the ratio will be 3 : 4. How old is Ali now?

  1. Call Ali's age 2x and Bea's age 3x. What are the ages in 10 years?
  2. In 10 years: (2x + 10) : (3x + 10) = 3 : 4. Cross-multiply: 4(2x + 10) = 3(3x + 10).
  3. Solve for x, then work out Ali's age, 2x.
  • That is x. Ali's age is 2x.
  • That is Bea's age now (3x). The question asks for Ali.

4(2x + 10) = 3(3x + 10), so 8x + 40 = 9x + 30, which gives x = 10. Ali is 2 × 10 = 20 now, and Bea is 30. Check: in 10 years they are 30 and 40, and 30 : 40 = 3 : 4 ✓.

A map has the scale 1 : 1000. A field has an area of 6 cm² on the map. What is the real area, in m²? (1 m² = 10 000 cm².)

  1. Lengths are multiplied by 1000. By what are areas multiplied?
  2. The real area is in cm² first. Then change to m².
  • You multiplied the area by 1000. An area has two lengths, so multiply by 1000 × 1000.
  • Check the change from cm² to m²: there are 10 000 cm² in 1 m², not 100,000 or 100.

Areas are multiplied by 1000 × 1000 = 1 000 000. 6 × 1 000 000 = 6 000 000 cm². 6 000 000 ÷ 10 000 = 600 m².

Two people share a total T in the ratio a : b. Which expression gives the first share?

  • T × a over a + b
  • T × a over b, the first share over the second
  • T ÷ a, then add b to the answer
  1. Think about the numbers: what is the total number of parts?
  2. One part is T divided by the total number of parts. The first share is a of those parts.

There are a + b parts. One part is T over a + b, so the first share is a × T over a + b = T × a over a + b. Try it with T = 60, a = 2, b = 3: 60 × 2 over 5 = 24 ✓.

Check your knowledge: on the job

Real jobs from the building and plumbing trades. Prices are typical, so check them with your own supplier.

A soldering cleaner is made by mixing flux and spirit in the ratio 3 : 7. A fitter makes 2 litres of it. How many millilitres of flux does she need?

  1. How many parts is the mix, and are the units the same all the way through?
  2. 2 litres is 2000 ml. Divide it by the total number of parts.
  3. One part is 2000 ÷ 10. Flux is 3 of those parts.
  • That is the spirit (7 parts). Flux is the 3 in the ratio.
  • That is in litres. The question asks for millilitres.

3 + 7 = 10 parts. 2 litres = 2000 ml, so one part is 2000 ÷ 10 = 200 ml. Flux is 3 × 200 = 600 ml. (Spirit is 1400 ml, and 600 + 1400 = 2000 ✓.)

A pipework plan is drawn to a scale of 1 : 50. A pipe run measures 18.4 cm on the plan. How long is the real pipe run, in metres?

  1. Is the real pipe bigger or smaller than the drawing?
  2. Multiply the drawing length by 50. The answer is in centimetres.
  3. Change centimetres into metres by dividing by 100.
  • That is in centimetres. The question asks for metres: divide by 100.
  • You divided by 50. The real pipe is bigger than the drawing, so multiply.

18.4 × 50 = 920 cm. 920 ÷ 100 = 9.2 m. (Sense check: 1 cm on the plan is 50 cm = 0.5 m, so 18.4 cm is about 9 m ✓.)

A 4.5 m length of pipe is cut into two pieces in the ratio 2 : 3. How long is the longer piece, in centimetres?

  1. Look at the bar. How many equal parts is the pipe cut into?
  2. Change 4.5 m into centimetres, then divide by the number of parts.
  3. The longer piece is 3 of those parts.
  • That is the shorter piece (2 parts). The longer piece is 3 parts.
  • That is in metres. The question asks for centimetres.

2 + 3 = 5 parts. 4.5 m = 450 cm, so one part is 450 ÷ 5 = 90 cm. The longer piece is 3 × 90 = 270 cm. (The shorter is 180 cm, and 180 + 270 = 450 ✓.)

[3 marks] Solder wire is sold on a 500 g reel for £21.00 or a 250 g reel for £11.40. How much cheaper per 100 g, in pence, is the bigger reel? Type it like 12.

  1. Compare like with like. What amount should you find the price of for both reels?
  2. Work out the price per 100 g of each reel. Use pence to avoid decimals.
  3. 500 g is 5 lots of 100 g, and 250 g is 2.5 lots of 100 g.
  • That is in pounds. The question asks for pence.
  • That is the price per 100 g of the small reel. The question asks how much cheaper the big reel is.

Big reel: £21.00 = 2100p, and 2100 ÷ 5 = 420p per 100 g (1 mark). Small reel: £11.40 = 1140p, and 1140 ÷ 2.5 = 456p per 100 g (1 mark). Difference: 456 − 420 = 36p per 100 g (1 mark).

A heating system holds 140 litres of water. The label says to add 250 ml of inhibitor for every 50 litres of water. How many litres of inhibitor does the system need? Type it like 0.35.

  1. How many lots of 50 litres are in 140 litres?
  2. Multiply that number by 250 ml. The answer is in millilitres.
  3. Change millilitres to litres by dividing by 1000.
  • That is in millilitres. The question asks for litres: divide by 1000.
  • That is the dose for only 50 litres. The system holds 140 litres.

140 ÷ 50 = 2.8 lots. 2.8 × 250 = 700 ml. 700 ml ÷ 1000 = 0.7 litres.
