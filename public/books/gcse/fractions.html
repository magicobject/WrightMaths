    Fractions

    Parts of a whole, from both sides: build them, simplify them, add, subtract, multiply, divide, and then run every one of them backwards.

    Where you will use this: drill bit and socket sizes such as 5 over 8 and 3 over 4 of an inch, a fraction of a 3 m length of conduit, and a job split into fractions of a day.

    Words you need

    Tap a teal word anywhere on the page to see this again.

    Fraction
    A number that describes equal parts of a whole. It is written with one number over another.
    Numerator
    The top number of a fraction. It counts how many parts you have.
    Denominator
    The bottom number of a fraction. It tells you how many equal parts the whole is cut into, and so how big each part is.
    Equivalent fractions
    Fractions that look different but have the same value. Multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number to make one.
    Simplify a fraction
    Write a fraction in its lowest terms: divide the numerator and denominator by the same number until no number except 1 divides both.
    Common denominator
    A denominator that two or more fractions share. You need one before you can add or subtract fractions.

    1. What a fraction is

    A Fraction describes equal parts of a whole. The bottom number is the Denominator. It says how many equal parts the whole is cut into. The top number is the Numerator. It says how many parts you have.

    3 over 8 = 3 parts out of 8 equal parts

    Key idea: the denominator names the size of the parts (eighths, quarters, tenths). The numerator counts them. For the same whole, and the same numerator, a bigger denominator means smaller pieces: 1 over 8 is smaller than 1 over 4. (With different numerators you have to compare more carefully: 3 over 8 is bigger than 1 over 4.) The denominator can never be 0, because you cannot cut a whole into 0 parts.

    Try it: write 1 over 2 as quarters.

    A fraction line also means “divided by”. 3 over 4 is the same as 3 ÷ 4. So 1 ÷ 8 = 1 over 8.

    Equivalent fractions

    Equivalent fractions look different but are worth the same: 1 over 2 = 2 over 4 = 3 over 6. To make one, multiply (or divide) the numerator and the denominator by the same number.

    Worked example

    Fill the gap: 2 over 3 = ? over 12

    1. Look at the denominators: 3 became 12, so it was multiplied by 4.
    2. Do the same to the numerator: 2 × 4 = 8.
    3. So 2 over 3 = 8 over 12.

    To simplify a fraction, go the other way. Divide the numerator and the denominator by the same number. Stop when nothing else divides both. Dividing by the HCF does it in one step.

    Worked example

    Simplify 12 over 18.

    1. HCF of 12 and 18 is 6.
    2. 12 ÷ 6 = 2 and 18 ÷ 6 = 3.
    3. 12 over 18 = 2 over 3. Nothing else divides both 2 and 3, so it is fully simplified.

    2. Adding and subtracting

    You can only add or subtract parts of the same size. That means the same denominator. If they are different, make a Common denominator first. The neatest one is the lowest common multiple of the denominators.

    Key idea: change the pieces to the same size, then add the counts. The size (denominator) never gets added.

    Try it: work out 1 over 5 + 2 over 5.

    Worked example

    Work out 2 over 3 + 1 over 4.

    1. LCM of 3 and 4 is 12, so use twelfths.
    2. 2 over 3 = 8 over 12 and 1 over 4 = 3 over 12.
    3. Add the numerators: 8 + 3 = 11. The denominator stays 12.
    4. 2 over 3 + 1 over 4 = 11 over 12.

    Now you finish it

    Work out 3 over 5 + 1 over 2.

    1. LCM of 5 and 2 is 10, so use tenths.
    2. 3 over 5 = 6 over 10 and 1 over 2 = 5 over 10.
    3. Add the numerators: 6 + 5 = ___.
    4. So the answer is ___ over 10.
    Check my answer

    6 + 5 = 11, so 11 over 10 = 1 1 over 10.

    An answer bigger than 1 is fine. 11 over 10 is an Improper fraction. 1 1 over 10 is a Mixed number. They are the same number.

    Why not add the numerators and the denominators?

    Two halves make a whole, so 1 over 2 + 1 over 2 = 1.

    Adding the numerators and adding the denominators gives 2 over 4, which is only a half.

    The denominator is the size of the pieces. Putting two halves together does not make the pieces smaller.

    3. Multiplying and dividing

    To multiply, multiply the numerators together and multiply the denominators together.

    Cancelling means dividing a numerator and a denominator by the same number before you multiply. It keeps the numbers small. For example, with 5 over 6 × 3 over 10: divide 3 and 6 by 3, and divide 5 and 10 by 5. That leaves 1 over 2 × 1 over 2 = 1 over 4.

    Dividing by a fraction asks a different question: how many of these fit in?

    A half is 2 quarters. Three quarters contain one half, and then half of another half. So 3 over 4 ÷ 1 over 2 = 1 1 over 2. In the same way, a half holds 2 quarters, so 1 over 2 ÷ 1 over 4 = 2.

    Key idea: every whole holds 2 halves, so dividing by 1 over 2 gives twice as many: it is the same as × 2. The number 2 over 1 is the Reciprocal of 1 over 2. Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal, and now you can see why.

    Try it: work out 1 over 2 × 1 over 2.

    Worked example: multiply

    Work out 2 over 3 × 3 over 5.

    1. Numerators: 2 × 3 = 6. Denominators: 3 × 5 = 15.
    2. 6 over 15 simplifies to 2 over 5 (divide the numerator and the denominator by 3).

    Worked example: divide

    Work out 3 over 4 ÷ 1 over 2.

    1. Dividing by 1 over 2 asks how many halves fit. Each whole holds 2 halves, so multiply by the reciprocal, 2 over 1.
    2. Multiply: 3 over 4 × 2 over 1 = 6 over 4.
    3. Simplify: 6 over 4 = 3 over 2 = 1 1 over 2.

    Sense check: how many halves fit into three quarters? One and a half. ✓

    With a Mixed number, convert to an Improper fraction first: 1 1 over 2 = 3 over 2.

    Flip it: run it backwards

    Everything so far goes forwards. Now reverse it. Same idea, other direction.

    Forwards

    3 over 5 of 40 = ?

    Divide by the bottom, multiply by the top.

    Backwards

    3 over 5 of ? = 24

    Divide by the top, multiply by the bottom.

    Why it works

    • Common denominators are about piece size. You can only count pieces of the same size. 3 apples and 2 oranges do not make 5 apples.
    • Dividing by a fraction. Dividing by 1 over 2 asks how many halves fit. Each whole holds two halves, so you multiply by 2. That is multiplying by the reciprocal.
    • Equivalent fractions are the same number. Multiplying the numerator and the denominator by 4 is multiplying by 4 over 4 = 1, which changes nothing.

    Common traps

    • Adding the denominators.
    • Adding the same number to the numerator and the denominator to make an equivalent fraction.
    • Multiplying by the second fraction when you should multiply by its reciprocal for a division.
    • Forgetting to convert a mixed number before multiplying or dividing.
    • Leaving an answer unsimplified.

    2. If materials are 3 over 8 of the bill, what fraction is labour?
    3. Labour is 5 over 8 of 240. Find one eighth first.
    • That is the materials (3 over 8). The question asks for the labour, the rest.
    • That is one eighth. Labour is 5 over 8, so you need five of them.

    Labour is the rest: 8 over 8 − 3 over 8 = 5 over 8. One eighth of 240 is 30, so 5 over 8 is 5 × 30 = £150. (Check: materials are 3 × 30 = 90, and 90 + 150 = 240 ✓.)

    A box holds 240 cable clips. Sam takes 1 over 3 of the box and Ali takes 1 over 4 of the box. How many clips are left in the box?

    1. Is the question asking for the clips taken or the clips left?
    2. Find 1 over 3 of 240 and 1 over 4 of 240 separately. Then take both away from 240.
    3. 240 ÷ 3 = 80 and 240 ÷ 4 = 60.
    • That is the number taken (80 + 60). The question asks how many are left.
    • You took one share from the other. Both are taken from the box, so add them and subtract the total from 240.

    Sam takes 240 ÷ 3 = 80. Ali takes 240 ÷ 4 = 60. Together they take 80 + 60 = 140, so 240 − 140 = 100 clips are left. (Check: they took 7 over 12 of the box, and 5 over 12 of 240 = 100 ✓.)

    Check your knowledge: on the pitch

    Football fractions: time, squads, goals and crowds.

    A football match lasts 90 minutes. After 36 minutes, what fraction of the match has been played? Give it in its simplest form, like 3/4.

    1. What is the whole, and what is the part?
    2. Write the part over the whole: 36 over 90.
    3. Divide the numerator and the denominator by the same number. What is the biggest one that works?
    • Right fraction, but it can be simplified. Divide the numerator and the denominator by 18.
    • Right value. 18 and 45 still divide by 9.

    36 out of 90 is 36 over 90. The HCF of 36 and 90 is 18. 36 ÷ 18 = 2 and 90 ÷ 18 = 5, so the fraction is 2/5.

    A squad has 24 players. 3 over 8 of them are defenders. How many defenders are there?

    1. Look at the bar. How many equal parts is the squad cut into?
    2. Find one part: 24 divided by the number of parts.
    3. Defenders are 3 of those parts.
    • That is one part (1 over 8). Defenders are 3 parts.
    • That is the players who are not defenders. The question asks for the defenders.

    1 over 8 of 24 is 24 ÷ 8 = 3. So 3 over 8 of 24 is 3 × 3 = 9 defenders. (The other 15 players and 9 defenders make 24 ✓.)

    A team scored 30 goals in a season. The striker scored 3 over 5 of them and the winger scored 1 over 6. What fraction of the goals did the rest of the team score? Give it in its simplest form, like 3/4.

    1. What is the question asking? Everything adds to the whole, 1.
    2. Find a common denominator for 5 and 6, then add the two fractions.
    3. Take the total of the two fractions away from 1 (that is 30 over 30).
    • That is the striker and winger together. The question asks for the rest of the team.
    • You added the numerators and the denominators. Use a common denominator first.

    3/5 = 18/30 and 1/6 = 5/30. Together 18/30 + 5/30 = 23/30. The rest scored 1 − 23/30 = 7/30.

    [3 marks] A team played 40 matches in two seasons. They won 5 over 8 of them and drew 1 over 5 of them. How many matches did they lose?

    1. What is the question asking for? The matches that were neither won nor drawn.
    2. Find the number of wins and the number of draws separately.
    3. Take both away from 40.
    • That is the number of wins. Work out the draws too, then find the losses.
    • That is the number of draws. Work out the wins too, then find the losses.

    Wins: 5 over 8 of 40 = 40 ÷ 8 × 5 = 25. Draws: 1 over 5 of 40 = 8. Losses: 40 − 25 − 8 = 7.

    • M1 for 5 over 8 of 40 = 25
    • M1 for 1 over 5 of 40 = 8
    • A1 for 40 − 25 − 8 = 7

    [3 marks] A stadium has 1200 seats. 3 over 4 of the seats were sold, and 2 over 3 of the seats that were sold went to home fans. How many home fans were there?

    1. There are two steps. What is the first fraction of, and what is the second fraction of?
    2. Find the seats sold first.
    3. Then find 2 over 3 of the seats sold, not of 1200.
    • That is 2 over 3 of all 1200 seats. The 2 over 3 is of the seats that were sold.
    • That is the number of seats sold. Home fans are only 2 over 3 of those.

    Seats sold: 3 over 4 of 1200 = 900. Home fans: 2 over 3 of 900 = 900 ÷ 3 × 2 = 600.

    • M1 for 3 over 4 of 1200 = 900
    • M1 for 2 over 3 of 900
    • A1 for 600
