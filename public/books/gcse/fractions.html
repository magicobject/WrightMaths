Fractions

Parts of a whole, from both sides: build them, simplify them, add, subtract, multiply, divide, and then run every one of them backwards.

The "Last time" questions below are a quick check of that base. If they feel shaky, build the base first.

Words you need

Tap a teal word anywhere on the page to see this again.

Fraction
A number that describes equal parts of a whole. It is written with one number over another.
Numerator
The top number of a fraction. It counts how many parts you have.
Denominator
The bottom number of a fraction. It tells you how many equal parts the whole is cut into, and so how big each part is.
Equivalent fractions
Fractions that look different but have the same value. Multiply or divide top and bottom by the same number to make one.
Simplify (a fraction)
Write a fraction in its lowest terms: divide the top and bottom by the same number until nothing else divides both.
Common denominator
A denominator that two or more fractions share. You need one before you can add or subtract fractions.

Warm-up: what do you already know?

Quick warm-up. No marks, and nobody is watching. A wrong answer just shows where to look.

  • A pizza is cut into 8 equal slices.
  • You eat 3 slices.
  • A friend eats 2 slices.

What fraction of the pizza has gone?

  1. How many slices were eaten altogether?
  2. Out of how many slices?
  • The slices are still eighths. Do not add the 8s.
  • That is only one person's slices. Add the 2 slices too.
  • That is only one person's slices. Add the 3 slices too.

5 slices out of 8 is 5 over 8. The slice size did not change, so the bottom stays 8.

Quick recap (no marks)

Work out 7 × 8.

  1. 5, 6, 7, 8: "56 = 7 × 8". Say it out loud.

7 × 8 = 56.

What is the Highest common factor (HCF), the highest common factor, of 12 and 18?

  1. List the factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. Now list those of 18.
  2. Which is the biggest number in both lists?
  • 36 is a multiple of both, not a factor. Find the biggest shared factor.
  • That is a common factor, but not the highest.
  • That is a common factor, but not the highest.

Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. The biggest in both lists is 6.

What is the Lowest common multiple (LCM), the lowest common multiple, of 4 and 6?

  1. List multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12 … and of 6: 6, 12 …
  2. What is the first number in both lists?
  • 24 is a common multiple, but not the lowest. Find the first in both lists.
  • 2 is the HCF. The LCM is a multiple, so it is at least as big as both numbers.

Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12. Multiples of 6: 6, 12. First common: 12.

1. What a fraction is

A Fraction describes equal parts of a whole. The bottom number is the Denominator. It says how many equal parts the whole is cut into. The top number is the Numerator. It says how many parts you have.

3 over 8 = 3 parts out of 8 equal parts

Key idea: the denominator names the size of the parts (eighths, quarters, tenths). The numerator counts them. Bigger denominator means smaller pieces.

Try it: write 1 over 2 as quarters.

A fraction line also means divide. 3 over 4 is the same as 3 ÷ 4. So 1 ÷ 8 = 1 over 8.

Equivalent fractions

Equivalent fractions look different but are worth the same: 1 over 2 = 2 over 4 = 3 over 6. To make one, multiply (or divide) the top and bottom by the same number.

Worked example

Fill the gap: 2 over 3 = ? over 12

  1. Look at the bottoms: 3 became 12, so it was multiplied by 4.
  2. Do the same to the top: 2 × 4 = 8.
  3. So 2 over 3 = 8 over 12.

To simplify a fraction, go the other way. Divide top and bottom by the same number. Stop when nothing else divides both. Dividing by the HCF does it in one step.

Worked example

Simplify 12 over 18.

  1. HCF of 12 and 18 is 6.
  2. 12 ÷ 6 = 2 and 18 ÷ 6 = 3.
  3. 12 over 18 = 2 over 3. Nothing else divides both 2 and 3, so it is fully simplified.

Check your knowledge: parts and equivalents

What is the Denominator of 5 over 8?

  1. It is the number on the bottom.
  • 5 is the numerator (the top number). The denominator is on the bottom.

The denominator is 8: the whole is cut into eighths.

Fill the gap: 2 over 3 = ? over 12. What number goes where the ? is?

  1. What did you multiply 3 by to get 12?
  2. Do the same to the 2.
  • You added 9 to the top and bottom. Multiply both by the same number instead.

3 × 4 = 12 so 2 × 4 = 8. 2 over 3 = 8 over 12.

Simplify 12 over 18 fully. Give your answer as a fraction.

  1. Which number divides into both 12 and 18?
  2. Try the highest one: you found it in the warm-up.
  • Right value. 6 and 9 still divide by 3.
  • Right value. 4 and 6 still divide by 2.

Divide top and bottom by 6: 2 over 3.

Which is bigger: 3 over 5 or 4 over 7?

  • 3 over 5
  • 4 over 7
  • They are equal
  1. Write both over the same denominator. 5 × 7 = 35 works.
  2. 3 over 5 = 21 over 35. What is 4 over 7 over 35?

3 over 5 = 21 over 35 and 4 over 7 = 20 over 35. So 3 over 5 is bigger.

2. Adding and subtracting

You can only add or subtract parts of the same size. That means the same denominator. If they are different, make a Common denominator first. The neatest one is the lowest common multiple of the denominators.

Key idea: change the pieces to the same size, then add the counts. The size (denominator) never gets added.

Try it: work out 1 over 5 + 2 over 5.

Worked example

Work out 2 over 3 + 1 over 4.

  1. LCM of 3 and 4 is 12, so use twelfths.
  2. 2 over 3 = 8 over 12 and 1 over 4 = 3 over 12.
  3. Add the numerators: 8 + 3 = 11. The denominator stays 12.
  4. 2 over 3 + 1 over 4 = 11 over 12.

Now you finish it

Work out 3 over 5 + 1 over 2.

  1. LCM of 5 and 2 is 10, so use tenths.
  2. 3 over 5 = 6 over 10 and 1 over 2 = 5 over 10.
  3. Add the tops: 6 + 5 = ___.
  4. So the answer is ___ over 10.

Answer: 6 + 5 = 11, so 11 over 10 = 1 1 over 10.

An answer bigger than 1 is fine. 11 over 10 is an Improper fraction. 1 1 over 10 is a Mixed number. They are the same number.

Why not just add tops and bottoms?

Two halves make a whole, so 1 over 2 + 1 over 2 = 1.

Adding tops and bottoms gives 2 over 4, which is only a half.

The denominator is the size of the pieces. Putting two halves together does not make the pieces smaller.

Check your knowledge: add and subtract

Work out 1 over 5 + 3 over 5.

  1. Same denominator already, so add the tops only.
  • You added the bottoms too. The pieces are still fifths.

1 + 3 = 4 fifths: 4 over 5.

Work out 1 over 2 + 1 over 3.

  1. What is the LCM of 2 and 3?
  2. 1 over 2 = 3 over 6. What is 1 over 3 as sixths?
  • You added tops and bottoms. Use sixths first.

3 over 6 + 2 over 6 = 5 over 6.

Work out 3 over 4 − 1 over 6.

  1. Twelfths. 3 over 4 = 9 over 12.
  2. Subtract the tops only.
  • You subtracted tops and bottoms. Use twelfths for both fractions.
  • You added the bottoms (4 + 6). The pieces must match first.
  • 3/4 is 9/12, not 3/12. Change the top too.

9 over 12 − 2 over 12 = 7 over 12.

Work out 2 over 3 + 3 over 4. Either an improper fraction or a mixed number is fine.

  1. Twelfths again.
  2. 8 over 12 + 9 over 12

8 over 12 + 9 over 12 = 17 over 12 = 1 5 over 12.

3. Multiplying and dividing

To multiply, multiply the tops and multiply the bottoms.

To divide, flip the second fraction and multiply. The flipped fraction is the Reciprocal.

Key idea: dividing by a fraction asks: how many of these fit in? That is the same as multiplying by the reciprocal.

Try it: work out 1 over 2 × 1 over 2.

Worked example: multiply

Work out 2 over 3 × 3 over 5.

  1. Tops: 2 × 3 = 6. Bottoms: 3 × 5 = 15.
  2. 6 over 15, which simplifies (divide by 3) to 2 over 5.

Worked example: divide

Work out 3 over 4 ÷ 1 over 2.

  1. Flip the second fraction: 1 over 2 becomes 2 over 1.
  2. Multiply: 3 over 4 × 2 over 1 = 6 over 4.
  3. Simplify: 6 over 4 = 3 over 2 = 1 1 over 2.

Sense check: how many halves fit into three quarters? One and a half. ✓

With a Mixed number, convert to an Improper fraction first: 1 1 over 2 = 3 over 2.

Check your knowledge: multiply and divide

Work out 2 over 3 × 3 over 5. Give your answer in its simplest form.

  1. Multiply the tops, multiply the bottoms.
  2. Then simplify: you need the HCF.
  • Right value. Divide top and bottom by 3.

6 over 15 = 2 over 5.

Work out 3 over 4 ÷ 1 over 2. Give your answer in its simplest form, as a fraction or a mixed number (not a decimal).

  1. Flip the second fraction and multiply.
  • You multiplied by the half. Dividing by a half means × 2.
  • Right value, but it can be simplified.

3 over 4 × 2 over 1 = 6 over 4 = 3 over 2 = 1 1 over 2.

Work out 1 1 over 2 × 2 over 3.

  1. 1 1 over 2 as an improper fraction is 3 over 2.
  • You used the 1 and left out the 1/2. Use 3/2.
  • You multiplied only the 1/2 and kept the 1. Use 3/2.

3 over 2 × 2 over 3 = 6 over 6 = 1.

Work out 3 over 5 of 40.

  1. Find one fifth first: 40 ÷ 5.
  2. Then take three of them.
  • That is one fifth. You need three of them.

40 ÷ 5 = 8, so 3 over 5 of 40 = 3 × 8 = 24.

Flip it: run it backwards

Everything so far goes forwards. Now reverse it. Same idea, other direction.

Forwards

3 over 5 of 40 = ?

Divide by the bottom, multiply by the top.

Backwards

3 over 5 of ? = 24

Divide by the top, multiply by the bottom.

Key idea: “3 over 5 of a number is 24” means three fifths equal 24. So one fifth is 24 ÷ 3 = 8. The whole is five fifths: 8 × 5 = 40. Use the two operations in the opposite order.

Another reverse: “What must I add to 2 over 5 to make 1?” is the same as 1 − 2 over 5.

Worked example: decimal to fraction

Write 0.35 as a fraction in its simplest form.

  1. 0.35 is 35 hundredths: 35 over 100.
  2. Divide top and bottom by 5: 7 over 20.
  3. Nothing divides both 7 and 20, so it is simplest.

Try it: write 0.5 as a fraction in its simplest form.

Check your knowledge: flip it

3 over 4 of a number is 18. What is the number?

  1. Three quarters is 18. What is one quarter?
  2. The whole number is four quarters.
  • You found 3/4 of 18. Here 18 is 3/4 of the number.
  • That is one quarter. You need all four.

18 ÷ 3 = 6 is one quarter; 6 × 4 = 24.

What fraction must you add to 2 over 5 to make 1?

  1. This is the reverse of adding: subtract.
  2. 1 is the same as 5 over 5.

5 over 5 − 2 over 5 = 3 over 5.

Write 0.6 as a fraction in its simplest form.

  1. 0.6 is 6 tenths: 6 over 10.
  2. Divide top and bottom by 2.
  • Right value, but it can be simplified.

6 over 10 = 3 over 5. Check the other way: 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6.

Write 0.375 as a fraction in its simplest form.

  1. 0.375 is 375 thousandths: 375 over 1000.
  2. Divide top and bottom by 5 until you cannot: 375 over 1000 = 75 over 200 = 15 over 40 = 3 over 8.
  • Right value, but it can be simplified.
  • Right value. 75 and 200 still divide by 5.
  • Right value. 15 and 40 still divide by 5.

375 over 1000 = 3 over 8. Check the other way: 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.

Write 1 ÷ 8 as a fraction.

  1. A fraction line means divide. 1 ÷ 8 is one whole shared into 8 parts.
  • 0.125 is the right value. The question wants a fraction.

1 ÷ 8 = 1 over 8. Division and fractions are the same thing written two ways: 3 over 4 = 3 ÷ 4.

Spot the mistake

Finding an error in someone else's working helps you learn. For each one, decide what went wrong.

Mia says: 1 over 3 + 1 over 4 = 2 over 7. What went wrong?

  • She added tops and added bottoms. Pieces must match first.
  • She made a slip when adding 3 and 4.
  • The sum cannot be worked out at all.
  1. Is 2 over 7 sensible? 1 over 3 is about 0.33. 2 over 7 is about 0.29. A sum cannot be smaller than one part.

Use twelfths: 4 over 12 + 3 over 12 = 7 over 12.

Sam says: 1 over 2 ÷ 1 over 4 = 1 over 8. What went wrong?

  • He should have added them instead.
  • He multiplied. He should flip the second fraction first.
  • He wrote the two fractions the wrong way round.
  1. How many quarters fit in a half? Think of pizza.

1 over 2 ÷ 1 over 4 = 1 over 2 × 4 over 1 = 2. There are 2 quarters in a half.

Check your knowledge: mixed review

Mixed up on purpose, like a real paper. Work out which method fits.

Which of these is the biggest: 2 over 3, 3 over 5 or 5 over 8?

  • 2 over 3
  • 3 over 5
  • 5 over 8
  1. Use a common denominator of 120: 2 over 3 = 80 over 120.
  2. 3 over 5 = 72 over 120 and 5 over 8 = 75 over 120.

Over 120 they are 80, 72 and 75, so 2 over 3 is the biggest.

Work out 3 over 4 of 24.

  1. One quarter first: 24 ÷ 4.
  • That is one quarter. You need three of them.

24 ÷ 4 = 6, and 3 × 6 = 18.

Work out 5 over 6 − 1 over 3. Give the answer in its simplest form.

  1. Sixths: 1 over 3 = 2 over 6.
  2. Then simplify what you get.
  • Right value, but it can be simplified.
  • You subtracted tops and bottoms. Use sixths first.

5 over 6 − 2 over 6 = 3 over 6 = 1 over 2.

Write 2 1 over 4 as an improper fraction.

  1. Each whole is 4 over 4, so 2 wholes make 8 over 4.
  • That joins the digits. 2 wholes are 8 quarters.

8 over 4 + 1 over 4 = 9 over 4.

In a box of pencils, 2 over 5 are red and 1 over 4 are green. What fraction are neither red nor green?

  1. Add the two fractions first: 2 over 5 + 1 over 4 = ?
  2. Then take that away from 1 (the whole box).
  • That is red or green. Take it from 1 to get the rest.

8 over 20 + 5 over 20 = 13 over 20. So 20 over 20 − 13 over 20 = 7 over 20.

3 over 8 of a number is 15. What is the number?

  1. Flip it: 3 over 8 is 15, so 1 over 8 is 15 ÷ 3.
  • You found 3/8 of 15. Here 15 is 3/8 of the number.

1 over 8 = 5, so the whole is 8 × 5 = 40.

Work out 1 over 2 ÷ 3. Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form.

  1. 3 is 3 over 1. Flip it and multiply.
  • You multiplied by 3. Dividing by 3 means × 1/3.

1 over 2 × 1 over 3 = 1 over 6.

Exam style

Use these like a real paper: no calculator. The marks tell you how much to write.

Not sure what a question word means? Question words explained.

Write your working on paper, then type the answer.

  • Jo has £60. (£ means pounds, the UK money.)
  • She spends 1 over 3 of it on a game.
  • She spends 1 over 4 of it on lunch.

(a) [2 marks] How much does she spend altogether? Answer in pounds.

  1. Find 1 over 3 of 60 and 1 over 4 of 60 separately.
  2. Add them.
  • That is only the game. Add the lunch too.
  • That is only lunch. Add the game too.

60 ÷ 3 = 20 and 60 ÷ 4 = 15. 20 + 15 = £35.

(b) [2 marks] What fraction of Jo's money is left?

  1. She spent 1 over 3 + 1 over 4 = 7 over 12 of it.
  2. What is left is 1 − that.
  • £25 is the money left. The question asks for a fraction.

12 over 12 − 7 over 12 = 5 over 12. Check: £25 left, and 25 over 60 = 5 over 12 ✓

Why it works

  • Common denominators are about piece size. You can only count pieces of the same size. 3 apples and 2 oranges do not make 5 apples.
  • Flipping to divide. Dividing by 1 over 2 asks how many halves fit. Each whole holds two halves, so you multiply by 2.
  • Equivalent fractions are the same number. Multiplying top and bottom by 4 is multiplying by 4 over 4 = 1, which changes nothing.

Common traps

  • Adding the denominators.
  • Adding the same number to the top and bottom to make an equivalent fraction.
  • Multiplying by the second fraction when you should flip it for a division.
  • Forgetting to convert a mixed number before multiplying or dividing.
  • Leaving an answer unsimplified.

Going further

You know 1 over 2 + 1 over 3 = 5 over 6. Without starting a fresh calculation, what is 5 over 6 − 1 over 2?

  1. Taking away what you added gives back what you started with.

5 over 6 − 1 over 2 = 1 over 3. Subtracting undoes adding. You can check it the other way: 1 over 3 + 1 over 2 = 5 over 6.
