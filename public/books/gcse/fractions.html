Fractions

Parts of a whole, from both sides: build them, simplify them, add, subtract, multiply, divide, and then run every one of them backwards.

GCSE Maths Book / Fractions

Before you start: what this stands on

Maths is built like a pyramid, each topic standing on the ones below. This topic needs:

  • Factors and multiples Number
  • Add, subtract, multiply, divide Number

The "Last time" questions below are a quick check of that base. If they feel shaky, build the base first.

Warm-up: what do you already know?

Have a go before you read anything. Wrong answers are welcome: they show where to look.

A pizza is cut into 8 equal slices. You eat 3 and a friend eats 2. What fraction of the pizza has gone?

  1. How many slices were eaten altogether?
  2. Out of how many slices?
  • The pizza is still cut into 8 slices. You are adding slices, not changing the size of the pieces.

5 slices out of 8 is 5 over 8. The slice size did not change, so the bottom stays 8.

Last time: the base this stands on

Work out 7 × 8.

  1. 5, 6, 7, 8: "56 = 7 × 8". Say it out loud.

7 × 8 = 56.

What is the Highest common factor (HCF), the highest common factor, of 12 and 18?

  1. List the factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. Now list those of 18.
  2. Which is the biggest number in both lists?
  • 36 is a multiple of both 12 and 18, not a factor of both. You need the biggest factor they share.

Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. The biggest in both lists is 6.

What is the Lowest common multiple (LCM), the lowest common multiple, of 4 and 6?

  1. List multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12 … and of 6: 6, 12 …
  2. What is the first number in both lists?
  • 24 is a common multiple, but not the lowest one. Look for the first in both lists.
  • 2 is the HCF (a factor). The LCM is a multiple, so it is at least as big as both numbers.

Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12. Multiples of 6: 6, 12. First common: 12.

1. What a fraction is

A Fraction describes equal parts of a whole. The bottom number, the Denominator, says how many equal parts the whole is cut into. The top number, the Numerator, says how many of those parts you have.

3 over 8 = 3 parts out of 8 equal parts

Key idea: the denominator names the size of the parts (eighths, quarters, tenths). The numerator counts them. Bigger denominator means smaller pieces.

Equivalent fractions

Equivalent fractions look different but are worth the same: 1 over 2 = 2 over 4 = 3 over 6. You make one by multiplying (or dividing) the top and bottom by the same number.

Worked example

Fill the gap: 2 over 3 = ? over 12

  1. Look at the bottoms: 3 became 12, so it was multiplied by 4.
  2. Do the same to the top: 2 × 4 = 8.
  3. So 2 over 3 = 8 over 12.

To simplify a fraction, go the other way: divide top and bottom by the same number until nothing else divides both. Dividing by the HCF does it in one step.

Worked example

Simplify 12 over 18.

  1. HCF of 12 and 18 is 6.
  2. 12 ÷ 6 = 2 and 18 ÷ 6 = 3.
  3. 12 over 18 = 2 over 3. Nothing else divides both 2 and 3, so it is fully simplified.

Check your knowledge: parts and equivalents

What is the Denominator of 5 over 8?

  1. It is the number on the bottom.
  • 5 is the numerator (the top number). The denominator is on the bottom.

The denominator is 8: the whole is cut into eighths.

Fill the gap: 2 over 3 = ? over 12. What number goes where the ? is?

  1. What did you multiply 3 by to get 12?
  2. Do the same to the 2.
  • That comes from adding 9 to the top and 9 to the bottom. Adding the same number changes the value. Multiply top and bottom by the same number instead.

3 × 4 = 12 so 2 × 4 = 8. 2 over 3 = 8 over 12.

Simplify 12 over 18 fully. Give your answer as a fraction.

  1. Which number divides into both 12 and 18?
  2. Try the highest one: you found it in the warm-up.
  • Correct value but not fully simplified: 6 and 9 both divide by 3. Keep going, or divide by 6 in one step.
  • Correct value but not fully simplified: 4 and 6 both divide by 2. Keep going.

Divide top and bottom by 6: 2 over 3.

Which is bigger: 3 over 5 or 4 over 7?

  • 3 over 5
  • 4 over 7
  • They are equal
  1. Write both over the same denominator. 5 × 7 = 35 works.
  2. 3 over 5 = 21 over 35. What is 4 over 7 over 35?

3 over 5 = 21 over 35 and 4 over 7 = 20 over 35. So 3 over 5 is bigger.

2. Adding and subtracting

You can only add or subtract when the parts are the same size, which means the same denominator. If they are not, make a Common denominator first. The neatest one is the lowest common multiple of the denominators.

Key idea: change the pieces to the same size, then add the counts. The size (denominator) never gets added.

Worked example

Work out 2 over 3 + 1 over 4.

  1. LCM of 3 and 4 is 12, so use twelfths.
  2. 2 over 3 = 8 over 12 and 1 over 4 = 3 over 12.
  3. Add the numerators: 8 + 3 = 11. The denominator stays 12.
  4. 2 over 3 + 1 over 4 = 11 over 12.

Now you finish it

Work out 3 over 5 + 1 over 2.

  1. LCM of 5 and 2 is 10, so use tenths.
  2. 3 over 5 = 6 over 10 and 1 over 2 = 5 over 10.
  3. Add: 6 + 5 = ___. So the answer is 11 over 10 = 1 1 over 10.

An answer bigger than 1 is fine. 11 over 10 is an Improper fraction and 1 1 over 10 is a Mixed number. They are the same number.

Why not just add tops and bottoms?

Because 1 over 2 + 1 over 2 must be 1 (two halves make a whole), and adding tops and bottoms gives 2 over 4 = 1 over 2. Adding half to half cannot make half. The denominator tells you the size of the pieces, and putting two halves together does not make the pieces smaller.

Check your knowledge: add and subtract

Work out 1 over 5 + 3 over 5.

  1. Same denominator already, so add the tops only.
  • You added the denominators as well. The pieces are still fifths.

1 + 3 = 4 fifths: 4 over 5.

Work out 1 over 2 + 1 over 3.

  1. What is the LCM of 2 and 3?
  2. 1 over 2 = 3 over 6. What is 1 over 3 as sixths?
  • That adds the tops and the bottoms. Convert to sixths first.

3 over 6 + 2 over 6 = 5 over 6.

Work out 3 over 4 − 1 over 6.

  1. Twelfths. 3 over 4 = 9 over 12.
  2. Subtract the tops only.
  • You subtracted the tops and the bottoms. As with adding, change both to twelfths first and keep 12 on the bottom.

9 over 12 − 2 over 12 = 7 over 12.

Work out 2 over 3 + 3 over 4. Either an improper fraction or a mixed number is fine.

  1. Twelfths again.
  2. 8 over 12 + 9 over 12

8 over 12 + 9 over 12 = 17 over 12 = 1 5 over 12.

3. Multiplying and dividing

These are easier than adding. To multiply, multiply the tops and multiply the bottoms. To divide, flip the second fraction (find its Reciprocal) and multiply.

Key idea: "dividing by a fraction" asks "how many of these fit in?", which is the same as multiplying by the reciprocal. This is multiplication and division being each other's reverse, again.

Worked example: multiply

Work out 2 over 3 × 3 over 5.

  1. Tops: 2 × 3 = 6. Bottoms: 3 × 5 = 15.
  2. 6 over 15, which simplifies (divide by 3) to 2 over 5.

Worked example: divide

Work out 3 over 4 ÷ 1 over 2.

  1. Flip the second fraction: 1 over 2 becomes 2 over 1.
  2. Multiply: 3 over 4 × 2 over 1 = 6 over 4.
  3. Simplify: 6 over 4 = 3 over 2 = 1 1 over 2.

Sense check: how many halves fit into three quarters? One and a half. ✓

With a Mixed number, convert to an Improper fraction first: 1 1 over 2 = 3 over 2.

Check your knowledge: multiply and divide

Work out 2 over 3 × 3 over 5. Give your answer in its simplest form.

  1. Multiply the tops, multiply the bottoms.
  2. Then simplify: you need the HCF.
  • Right value but not simplified. Divide top and bottom by 3.

6 over 15 = 2 over 5.

Work out 3 over 4 ÷ 1 over 2. Give your answer in its simplest form, as a fraction or a mixed number (not a decimal).

  1. Flip the second fraction and multiply.
  • You multiplied. Dividing by a half means multiplying by 2, not by a half.
  • Right value but it can be simplified.

3 over 4 × 2 over 1 = 6 over 4 = 3 over 2 = 1 1 over 2.

Work out 1 1 over 2 × 2 over 3.

  1. 1 1 over 2 as an improper fraction is 3 over 2.

3 over 2 × 2 over 3 = 6 over 6 = 1.

Work out 3 over 5 of 40.

  1. Find one fifth first: 40 ÷ 5.
  2. Then take three of them.
  • That is one fifth. You need three fifths.

40 ÷ 5 = 8, so 3 over 5 of 40 = 3 × 8 = 24.

Flip it: run it backwards

Everything so far goes forwards. Now reverse it. This is where most of the real understanding comes from.

Forwards

3 over 5 of 40 = ?

Divide by the bottom, multiply by the top.

Backwards

3 over 5 of ? = 24

Divide by the top, multiply by the bottom.

Key idea: "3 over 5 of a number is 24" means three fifths equal 24. So one fifth is 24 ÷ 3 = 8, and the whole (five fifths) is 8 × 5 = 40. You use the two operations in the opposite order.

Another reversal: adding and subtracting. "What must I add to 2 over 5 to make 1?" is the same as 1 − 2 over 5.

Check your knowledge: flip it

3 over 4 of a number is 18. What is the number?

  1. Three quarters is 18. What is one quarter?
  2. The whole number is four quarters.
  • You found three quarters of 18. The question runs it the other way: 18 is three quarters of the number.
  • That is one quarter. You need all four quarters.

18 ÷ 3 = 6 is one quarter; 6 × 4 = 24.

What fraction must you add to 2 over 5 to make 1?

  1. This is the reverse of adding: subtract.
  2. 1 is the same as 5 over 5.

5 over 5 − 2 over 5 = 3 over 5.

Write 0.375 as a fraction in its simplest form.

  1. 0.375 is 375 thousandths: 375 over 1000.
  2. Divide top and bottom by 125.
  • Right value, but it needs simplifying.

375 over 1000 = 3 over 8. Check the other way: 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.

Write 1 ÷ 8 as a fraction.

  1. Dividing a whole into 8 equal parts gives one eighth.

1 ÷ 8 = 1 over 8. Division and fractions are the same thing written two ways: 3 over 4 = 3 ÷ 4.

Spot the mistake

Finding the error in someone else's working is one of the fastest ways to learn. For each one, decide what went wrong.

Mia says: 1 over 3 + 1 over 4 = 2 over 7. What went wrong?

  • She added the tops and the bottoms. The pieces need to be the same size first.
  • She made an arithmetic slip adding 3 and 4.
  • The question cannot be answered.
  1. Is 2 over 7 sensible? 1 over 3 alone is already about 0.33 and 2 over 7 is about 0.29. A sum cannot be smaller than a part.

The correct answer is 4 over 12 + 3 over 12 = 7 over 12.

Sam says: 1 over 2 ÷ 1 over 4 = 1 over 8. What went wrong?

  • He should have added them
  • He multiplied instead of flipping the second fraction. 1 over 2 ÷ 1 over 4 = 1 over 2 × 4 over 1 = 2.
  • The fractions were the wrong way round
  1. How many quarters fit in a half? Think of pizza.

There are 2 quarters in a half, so the answer is 2.

Check your knowledge: mixed review

Everything shuffled together, like a real paper. Nothing tells you which method to use: choosing it is the skill.

Which of these is the biggest: 2 over 3, 3 over 5 or 5 over 8?

  • 2 over 3
  • 3 over 5
  • 5 over 8
  1. Use a common denominator of 120: 2 over 3 = 80 over 120.
  2. 3 over 5 = 72 over 120 and 5 over 8 = 75 over 120.

Over 120 they are 80, 72 and 75, so 2 over 3 is the biggest.

Work out 3 over 4 of 24.

  1. One quarter first: 24 ÷ 4.
  • That is one quarter. You need three of them.

24 ÷ 4 = 6, and 3 × 6 = 18.

Work out 5 over 6 − 1 over 3. Give the answer in its simplest form.

  1. Sixths: 1 over 3 = 2 over 6.
  2. Then simplify what you get.
  • Right value, but it can be simplified.
  • That is the tops and bottoms subtracted wrongly. Convert to sixths first.

5 over 6 − 2 over 6 = 3 over 6 = 1 over 2.

Write 2 1 over 4 as an improper fraction.

  1. Each whole is 4 over 4, so 2 wholes make 8 over 4.

8 over 4 + 1 over 4 = 9 over 4.

In a bag of sweets, 2 over 5 are red and 1 over 4 are green. What fraction are neither red nor green?

  1. Add the two fractions first: 2 over 5 + 1 over 4 = ?
  2. Then take that away from 1 (the whole bag).
  • That is the fraction that are red or green. The question asks for the rest: take it from 1.

8 over 20 + 5 over 20 = 13 over 20. So 20 over 20 − 13 over 20 = 7 over 20.

3 over 8 of a number is 15. What is the number?

  1. Flip it: 3 over 8 is 15, so 1 over 8 is 15 ÷ 3.
  • You found 3 over 8 of 15. This question runs the other way round.

1 over 8 = 5, so the whole is 8 × 5 = 40.

Work out 1 over 2 ÷ 3.

  1. 3 is 3 over 1. Flip it and multiply.
  • You multiplied by 3. Dividing by 3 means multiplying by 1 over 3.

1 over 2 × 1 over 3 = 1 over 6.

Exam style

Use these like a real paper: no calculator, show your working. The marks tell you how much to write.

(a) [2 marks] Jo has £60. She spends 1 over 3 of it on a game and 1 over 4 of it on lunch. How much does she spend altogether? (Answer in pounds, without the £ sign if you like.)

  1. Find 1 over 3 of 60 and 1 over 4 of 60 separately.
  2. Add them.
  • That is only the game. Add the lunch too.
  • That is only lunch. Add the game too.

60 ÷ 3 = 20 and 60 ÷ 4 = 15. 20 + 15 = £35.

(b) [2 marks] What fraction of Jo's money is left?

  1. She spent 1 over 3 + 1 over 4 = 7 over 12 of it.
  2. What is left is 1 − that.
  • £25 is the amount left, which is right as money, but the question asks for a fraction of her money.

12 over 12 − 7 over 12 = 5 over 12. Check: £25 left, and 25 over 60 = 5 over 12 ✓

Why it works

  • Common denominators are about piece size. You can only count pieces that are the same size, the way you cannot say "3 apples + 2 oranges = 5 apples".
  • Flipping to divide. Dividing by 1 over 2 asks how many halves fit. Each whole holds two halves, so you multiply by 2.
  • Equivalent fractions are the same number. Multiplying top and bottom by 4 is multiplying by 4 over 4 = 1, which changes nothing.

Common traps

  • Adding the denominators.
  • Adding the same number to the top and bottom to "make equivalent".
  • Multiplying by the second fraction when you should flip it for a division.
  • Forgetting to convert a mixed number before multiplying or dividing.
  • Leaving an answer unsimplified.

Going further

You know 1 over 2 + 1 over 3 = 5 over 6. Without starting a fresh calculation, what is 5 over 6 − 1 over 2?

  1. Taking away what you added should give you back what you started with.

5 over 6 − 1 over 2 = 1 over 3. Subtracting undoes adding: that is the "flip" in action. You can check it the other way: 1 over 3 + 1 over 2 = 5 over 6.
