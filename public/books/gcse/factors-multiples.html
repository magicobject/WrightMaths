1. Multiples

A Multiple of a number is a number in its times table: what you get by multiplying it by 1, 2, 3, 4 … The multiples of 7 are 7, 14, 21, 28, 35 …

Key idea: multiples go on for ever, and the 5th multiple of 8 is 5 × 8 = 40. To test whether a number is a multiple, divide: it is a multiple of 7 if it divides by 7 with no remainder.

Worked example

Is 91 a multiple of 7?

  1. Divide: 91 ÷ 7 = 13 exactly.
  2. There is no remainder, so yes: 7 × 13 = 91.

Check your knowledge: multiples

What is the 5th multiple of 8?

  1. 5 × 8.
  • That is 5 + 8. A multiple is multiplied.

5 × 8 = 40.

Which of these is a multiple of 9?

  • 54
  • 56
  • 65
  1. Divide each by 9 and look for no remainder.
  2. Quick test: the digits of a multiple of 9 add up to a multiple of 9.

54 ÷ 9 = 6, and 5 + 4 = 9.

What is the first multiple of 12 that is bigger than 100?

  1. 12 × 8 = 96. Go one more.
  • That is smaller than 100.
  • That is a multiple, but 108 is the first one over 100.

12 × 8 = 96 is too small; 12 × 9 = 108.

Is 119 a multiple of 7?

  • Yes: 7 × 17 = 119
  • No: 7 × 16 = 112 and 7 × 18 = 126
  1. Try 119 ÷ 7.

119 ÷ 7 = 17 exactly, so yes.

The 3rd multiple of a number is 21. What is the number?

  1. 3 × ? = 21.
  • That is 21 × 3. To go backwards, divide.

21 ÷ 3 = 7.

2. Factors

A Factor of a number is a whole number that divides into it exactly. Factors come in pairs that multiply to give the number, so find them in order: 1 × 12, 2 × 6, 3 × 4. The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12.

Key idea: start at 1 and test each whole number in order. Stop when the numbers you are testing meet the partners you have already found. Each factor goes with its partner, so factors are multiples seen from the other side: 3 is a factor of 12, and 12 is a multiple of 3.

Worked example

Find all the factors of 20.

  1. 1 × 20 = 20: factors 1 and 20.
  2. 2 × 10 = 20: factors 2 and 10.
  3. 3 does not go into 20. 4 × 5 = 20: factors 4 and 5.
  4. Next is 5, which we already have, so stop.
  5. Factors of 20: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20.

Factors always come in pairs, except for square numbers, which have one factor paired with itself (6 × 6 = 36). That is why a square number has an odd number of factors, and every other number has an even number.

Quick divisibility tests save time: even numbers divide by 2; a number divides by 3 if its digit sum divides by 3, and by 9 if its digit sum divides by 9; by 5 if it ends in 0 or 5; by 10 if it ends in 0.

Why the tests for 3 and 9 work: 10 leaves remainder 1 when you divide by 9, so 100 and 1000 do too (99 and 999 are multiples of 9). So a number is its digit sum plus a multiple of 9. For example 4,563 = 4 × 999 + 5 × 99 + 6 × 9 + (4 + 5 + 6 + 3). The first three parts divide by 9, so only the digit sum, 18, decides. Since 3 divides 9, the same test works for 3.

Check your knowledge: factors

How many factors does 50 have?

  1. Find the factor pairs in order, starting with 1 × 50.
  • That counts the pairs. Each pair has two factors.
  • You missed one. Check every pair, then count both numbers in each.

1 × 50, 2 × 25, 5 × 10 give 1, 2, 5, 10, 25, 50: six factors.

Which of these is NOT a factor of 30?

  • 5
  • 6
  • 9
  • 10
  1. Divide 30 by each.

30 ÷ 9 = 3 remainder 3, so 9 is not a factor.

How many factor pairs does 18 have?

  1. 1 × 18, 2 × 9, 3 × 6.
  • That counts the factors. Each pair is two of them.

Three pairs: 1 × 18, 2 × 9, 3 × 6.

What is the biggest factor of 45 that is smaller than 45?

  1. Factor pairs: 1 × 45, 3 × 15, 5 × 9.
  • 15 is bigger: 3 × 15 = 45.

The factors are 1, 3, 5, 9, 15, 45. The biggest below 45 is 15.

Is 4,563 divisible by 3?

  • Yes: the digits add up to 18, a multiple of 3
  • No: 4,563 is odd
  • No: it does not end in 0 or 3
  1. Add the digits: 4 + 5 + 6 + 3.

4 + 5 + 6 + 3 = 18, which divides by 3, so 4,563 divides by 3 (and by 9).

3. Prime numbers

A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. The first few are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23. Every other whole number bigger than 1 is built from primes by multiplying, which is called its prime factorisation.

Key idea: 1 is not prime (it has only one factor). 2 is the only even prime. Break any number into primes with a factor tree: split it into two factors, keep splitting until every branch ends in a prime.

To test whether n is prime you only need to try the primes up to √n. Factors come in pairs, and in every pair one factor is at most √n. So if there is no factor up to √n, there is none above it either.

Worked example

Write 84 as a product of prime factors.

  1. 84 = 2 × 42.
  2. 42 = 2 × 21.
  3. 21 = 3 × 7.
  4. All of 2, 2, 3, 7 are prime, so 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 = 2² × 3 × 7.

Check: 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 = 84. ✓

Check your knowledge: primes

Which of these is a prime number: 21, 23 or 27?

  • 21
  • 23
  • 27
  1. A prime has no factors other than 1 and itself. Try dividing each by 2, 3, 5.

23 has no factors except 1 and 23, so it is prime.

Is 1 a prime number?

  • Yes
  • No
  1. How many factors does 1 have?

1 has only one factor (itself), so it is not prime.

How many prime numbers are there between 10 and 20?

  1. 11, 13, 17, 19. Check 15 (5 × 3) and 12, 14, 16, 18 (even).
  • Check each: 15 = 3 × 5, so it is not prime.
  • You missed one. The primes are 11, 13, 17 and 19.

The primes are 11, 13, 17 and 19: four.

What is the smallest prime number?

  1. It is even.
  • 1 is not prime: it has only one factor.

2 is the smallest prime, and the only even one.

150 = 2 × 3 × 5 × 5. What is the largest prime factor of 150?

  1. Look at the primes in the product.
  • 150 is not prime. The prime factors are 2, 3 and 5.
  • 25 is 5 × 5, so it is not prime. Use 5.

The primes are 2, 3 and 5. The largest is 5.

To decide whether 89 is prime, you only need to test primes up to √89. What is the largest prime you need to test?

  1. √89 is between 9 and 10, because 9² = 81 and 10² = 100.
  2. Which primes are less than 9.4?
  • 9 = 3 × 3 is not prime. The primes up to 9 are 2, 3, 5, 7.
  • 11² = 121 is already bigger than 89, so 11 is not needed.

√89 = 9.43…, so test 2, 3, 5 and 7. The largest is 7. None divides 89, so 89 is prime.

How many prime factors does 36 have, counting repeats? (36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3)

  1. Count each prime in the product.
  • That counts the different primes (2 and 3). Counting repeats gives 4.

2, 2, 3, 3: four, counting repeats.

4. Highest common factor and lowest common multiple

The HCF of two numbers is the biggest number that is a factor of both. The LCM is the smallest number that is a multiple of both. List them and find where they first meet.

Key idea: HCF answers “what is the biggest piece size that fits both exactly?” LCM answers “when do the two cycles line up next?” The HCF is never bigger than the smaller number; the LCM is never smaller than the bigger.

Worked example: HCF

Find the HCF of 24 and 36.

  1. Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24.
  2. Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36.
  3. Common: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. The biggest is 12.

Worked example: LCM

Find the LCM of 6 and 8.

  1. Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30 …
  2. Multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24 …
  3. The first in both lists is 24.

Check your knowledge: HCF and LCM

Find the HCF of 12 and 18.

  1. Factors of 12 and of 18. Which is the biggest in both?
  • 36 is the LCM (a common multiple). The HCF is a factor.

Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 6. The HCF is 6.

Find the LCM of 4 and 6.

  1. List multiples of each.
  • 24 is a common multiple, but not the lowest.
  • 2 is the HCF. The LCM is a multiple.

Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12. Multiples of 6: 6, 12. First common: 12.

Find the HCF of 40 and 56.

  1. List the factors of each and look for the biggest one in both.
  • 4 is a common factor, but 8 is bigger.
  • 280 is the LCM, a multiple. The HCF is a factor.

Factors of 40: 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20, 40. Factors of 56: 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 14, 28, 56. The biggest in both is 8.

Find the LCM of 10 and 15.

  1. List multiples of the bigger number and check each against 10.
  • 60 is a common multiple, but 30 comes first.
  • 5 is the HCF. The LCM is a multiple.

Multiples of 15: 15, 30. 30 is also a multiple of 10, so the LCM is 30.

Find the LCM of 5 and 7.

  1. They share no factor except 1.
  • That is 5 + 7. The LCM is a multiple.

When two numbers share no factors, their LCM is their product: 5 × 7 = 35.

Find the HCF of 15 and 28.

  1. Factors of 15: 1, 3, 5, 15. Factors of 28: 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 28.
  • That is their LCM, a multiple. The HCF is a factor.

The only common factor is 1.

Flip it: factors and multiples are the same fact, two ways round

So far you have gone one way. Now run it backwards: this is where much of the real understanding comes from.

Forwards

3 is a factor of 12.

Look at the small number.

Backwards

12 is a multiple of 3.

Look at the big number.

Key idea: one multiplication fact gives you four statements. 3 × 4 = 12 means 3 and 4 are factors of 12, and 12 is a multiple of both 3 and 4. Prime factorisation also goes both ways: break a number into primes, or multiply primes together to rebuild it.

Forwards

Break 84 into primes: 2 × 2 × 3 × 7.

A factor tree.

Backwards

Multiply 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 to get 84.

Rebuild the number.

Check your knowledge: flip it

7 is a factor of 56. So 56 is a ___ of 7.

  • factor
  • multiple
  • prime
  1. 7 × 8 = 56.

56 is a multiple of 7.

What is the product of the prime factors 2 × 3 × 3 × 5?

  1. Multiply them in order: 2 × 3, then × 3, then × 5.
  • That is 2 + 3 + 3 + 5. Multiply the primes to rebuild the number.

2 × 3 = 6, 6 × 3 = 18 and 18 × 5 = 90.

I am a multiple of 8 and a factor of 48. I am bigger than 8. What is the smallest number I could be?

  1. Multiples of 8: 16, 24, … Which of them divide into 48?
  • 24 works too, but 16 is smaller.
  • The question says I am bigger than 8.

16 × 3 = 48, so 16 is a factor of 48 and a multiple of 8.

A number has exactly the factors 1, 2, 4 and 8. What is it?

  1. The biggest factor of a number is the number itself.
  • 16 has more factors: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16.

8 = 1 × 8 = 2 × 4. The factors are 1, 2, 4 and 8.

I am a prime number between 30 and 40. What could I be? (Either answer is fine.)

  1. Test 31, 33, 35, 37, 39. Even numbers are out.
  • 33 = 3 × 11, so it is not prime.
  • 35 = 5 × 7, so it is not prime.

31 and 37 are prime. (33 = 3 × 11, 35 = 5 × 7, 39 = 3 × 13.)

5. Using it in real situations

Two kinds of question come up again and again. “When will they next happen together?” is an LCM question. “What is the biggest equal piece or group?” is an HCF question.

Check your knowledge: in context

One bus leaves a station every 12 minutes and another leaves every 18 minutes. They both leave at 9:00. After how many minutes do they next leave together?

  1. A “next time together” question: LCM of 12 and 18.
  • 6 is the HCF. They next leave together at a multiple of both.

Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36. Multiples of 18: 18, 36. LCM = 36 minutes.

Ribbons of 36 cm and 54 cm are cut into equal pieces with no ribbon left over. What is the longest piece possible, in cm?

  1. “Biggest piece that fits both”: HCF of 36 and 54.
  • 108 is the LCM. The pieces must fit into both ribbons, so use the HCF.
  • 9 fits both, but a longer piece also does.
  • 12 goes into 36 but not into 54.

HCF of 36 and 54 is 18 cm: 36 = 2 × 18 and 54 = 3 × 18.

Pencils are sold in packs of 10 and notebooks in packs of 8. What is the smallest number of notebooks you can buy so that you have exactly the same number of pencils and notebooks?

  1. The two numbers must match, so it is a common multiple of 10 and 8.
  • 80 works too, but 40 is smaller.
  • 2 is the HCF. The number bought is a multiple.
  • 24 is a multiple of 8 but not of 10.

LCM of 8 and 10 is 40. That is 5 packs of notebooks and 4 packs of pencils.

Spot the mistake

Sam says: “1 is a prime number because you can only divide it by itself.” What went wrong?

  • A prime must have exactly two different factors. 1 has only one, so it is not prime.
  • Nothing: 1 is prime
  • 1 is prime but 2 is not
  1. How many factors does 1 have? How many does a prime have?

1 is neither prime nor composite.

Lia says: “The LCM of 6 and 9 is 3.” What went wrong?

  • She found the HCF. The LCM is a multiple: 18.
  • Nothing: 3 goes into both
  • The LCM is 54
  1. A multiple is at least as big as the number.

Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18. Multiples of 9: 9, 18. LCM = 18.

Max says: “The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6.” What went wrong?

  • He left out 12. A number is always a factor of itself.
  • Nothing: 12 is too big to be a factor
  • He left out 5
  1. Use the pairs: 1 × 12, 2 × 6, 3 × 4.

The factors are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12.

Check your knowledge: mixed review

Write all the factors of 40 and count them. How many factors does 40 have?

  1. 1 × 40, 2 × 20, then keep going in order.
  • That counts the pairs. Each has two factors.
  • You missed one. Check every pair, then count both numbers in each.

1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20, 40: eight.

What is the 9th multiple of 6?

  1. 9 × 6.
  • That is 9 + 6.

9 × 6 = 54.

Find the HCF of 30 and 45.

  1. Factors of 30: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30.
  • 90 is the LCM. The HCF is a factor.

15 × 2 = 30 and 15 × 3 = 45.

Find the LCM of 9 and 12.

  1. Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36.
  • 3 is the HCF. The LCM is a multiple.

36 = 9 × 4 = 12 × 3.

Which of these numbers is a prime number: 51, 57 or 59?

  • 51
  • 57
  • 59
  1. Use the digit sum test for 3.

5 + 1 = 6 and 5 + 7 = 12 both divide by 3, so 51 and 57 are not prime. 59 is prime.

Exam style

Show your working.

(a) [2 marks] Write 28 as a product of prime factors. Then add up the prime factors in your product, counting repeats. Give the total.

  1. Start a factor tree: 28 = 2 × ?
  • That adds 2 and 7 once each. The 2 appears twice, so count it twice.
  • That counts how many primes there are, not their sum.

28 = 2 × 14 = 2 × 2 × 7. Total: 2 + 2 + 7 = 11.

(b) [2 marks] Find the highest common factor (HCF) of 28 and 42.

  1. Factors of 28: 1, 2, 4, 7, 14, 28.
  2. Factors of 42: 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 14, 21, 42.
  • 7 is a common factor but 14 is bigger.
  • 84 is the LCM. The HCF is a factor.

The biggest factor in both lists is 14.

(c) [2 marks] One bell rings every 28 seconds and another every 42 seconds. They ring together at noon. After how many seconds do they next ring together?

  1. LCM of 28 and 42.
  • 14 is the HCF. They next ring together at a common multiple.

Multiples of 28: 28, 56, 84. Multiples of 42: 42, 84. LCM = 84 seconds.

Going further

Spot a pattern: HCF and LCM

Find the HCF of 8 and 12 and the LCM of 8 and 12. Multiply them together. What do you get?

  1. List the factors of 8 and 12, then the multiples of 8 and 12.
  • That is 4 + 24. Multiply the HCF and the LCM.

HCF = 4 and LCM = 24, so 4 × 24 = 96. Notice that 8 × 12 is also 96.

Do the same for 6 and 15: work out HCF × LCM.

  1. Common factors of 6 and 15, then common multiples.
  • That is 3 + 30. Multiply the HCF and the LCM.

HCF = 3 and LCM = 30, so 3 × 30 = 90. And 6 × 15 = 90 as well.

What pattern do the last two questions suggest for any two whole numbers?

  • HCF × LCM equals the two numbers multiplied together
  • HCF + LCM equals the two numbers added together
  • HCF × LCM equals the bigger number squared
  1. Compare your two answers with 8 × 12 and 6 × 15.

In both cases HCF × LCM matched the product of the two numbers. This is true for every pair of whole numbers.

The HCF of two numbers is 4 and their LCM is 24. One of the numbers is 8. What is the other?

  1. Use the pattern you just found.
  • HCF(8, 24) = 8, not 4.
  • LCM(8, 4) = 8, not 24.

8 × ? = 4 × 24 = 96, so ? = 12. Check: HCF(8, 12) = 4 and LCM(8, 12) = 24.

Counting factors

How many numbers from 1 to 50 have an odd number of factors?

  1. Try a few: 9 has factors 1, 3, 9. 10 has 1, 2, 5, 10. Look for what the odd ones share.
  • That counts the odd numbers. Being odd does not decide the number of factors.
  • You missed one square number. Check the squares up to 50.

The numbers are the squares 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36 and 49: seven numbers.

Why do the square numbers have an odd number of factors?

  • Factors come in pairs, except when a number pairs with itself, as in 6 × 6
  • Square numbers are always odd, so they have odd numbers of factors
  • Square numbers always have more factors than other numbers
  1. Write the factors of 36 as pairs d and 36 ÷ d.

Each factor d of n pairs with n ÷ d. A pair collapses to one factor only when d = n ÷ d, which means n = d²: a square number.

Two whole numbers have HCF 6 and LCM 180. How many different pairs (smaller number, larger number) are possible?

  1. Both numbers are multiples of 6, so write them as 6p and 6q, where p and q have no common factor.
  2. Use the pattern for HCF × LCM to find p × q.
  • That counts each pair twice, once each way round. Count (smaller, larger) once.
  • You missed a pair. Check every way to split the number into p × q.

6p × 6q = 6 × 180, so p × q = 30 with no common factor: (1, 30), (2, 15), (3, 10), (5, 6). The pairs are (6, 180), (12, 90), (18, 60) and (30, 36): four pairs.

What is the smallest number with exactly 6 factors?

  1. Test numbers in order and count their factors.
  • 18 also has 6 factors but is not the smallest.
  • 32 has 6 factors, but a smaller number has too.
  • 6 has only four factors: 1, 2, 3 and 6.

12 = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. Every smaller number has fewer: 8 has 4 factors, 10 has 4, 6 has 4.

Why it works

  • One fact, two names. 3 × 4 = 12 makes 3 and 4 factors of 12 and 12 a multiple of both.
  • Primes are the building blocks. Every whole number above 1 is a unique product of primes.
  • HCF and LCM answer different questions. HCF is the biggest equal piece; LCM is when cycles next line up.

Common traps

  • Calling 1 a prime number.
  • Mixing up factor and multiple.
  • Leaving the number itself (and 1) out of its factor list.
  • Giving the HCF when asked for the LCM, or the other way round.
  • Stopping a factor tree before every branch is prime.
