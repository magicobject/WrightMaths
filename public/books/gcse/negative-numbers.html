Negative numbers

Numbers below zero: how to order them, add and subtract them, multiply and divide them, and why two minuses make a plus.

GCSE Maths Book / Negative numbers

Before you start: what this stands on

Maths is built like a pyramid, each topic standing on the ones below. This topic needs:

  • Add, subtract, multiply, divide Number

The "Last time" questions below are a quick check of that base. If they feel shaky, build the base first.

Warm-up: what do you already know?

Have a go before you read anything. The last three are from Add, subtract, multiply, divide, the layer this stands on.

The temperature is −4°C and rises by 9 degrees. What is the new temperature in °C?

  1. Picture a thermometer. Go up 4 to reach 0, then up 5 more.
  • That adds 4 and 9, but the start is below zero. From −4, go up 4 to reach 0, then 5 more.
  • The temperature rises, so it ends up higher than −4.

−4 + 9 = 5.

Last time: work out 63 − 28.

  1. 63 − 30 = 33, then add 2.
  • Check the borrow: 13 − 8 = 5 and 5 − 2 = 3.

63 − 28 = 35.

Work out 7 × 9.

  1. Ten nines is 90, less three nines.

7 × 9 = 63.

Work out 144 ÷ 12.

  1. 12 × 12 = 144.

144 ÷ 12 = 12.

1. The number line

A negative number is below zero. On a number line the numbers keep going to the left of zero, and the further left you go, the smaller the number.

… −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 …

Key idea: bigger numbers are further to the right. So −2 is bigger than −10, even though 10 looks bigger than 2. A debt of £2 is better than a debt of £10.

Worked example

Put −7, 3, −2 and 0 in order from smallest to largest.

  1. Negatives are smaller than zero, and the further from zero, the smaller: −7 is smallest, then −2.
  2. Then 0, then 3.
  3. −7, −2, 0, 3.

Check your knowledge: the number line

Which is the biggest: −7, −2 or −10?

  • −7
  • −2
  • −10
  1. Picture the number line. Which is furthest to the right?

−2 is closest to zero, so it is the biggest.

Which is the smaller number: −8 or −3?

  • −8
  • −3
  1. Which is further to the left?

−8 is further left, so it is smaller.

What number is halfway between −6 and 2?

  1. Find the distance between them, then halve it.
  2. From −6 to 2 is 8. Half of 8 is 4. Start at −6 and go up 4.
  • That is halfway between −6 and −2. The other end is 2.
  • 4 is half the distance, not the position. Halfway must lie between −6 and 2, so start at −6 and go up 4.

−6 + 4 = −2.

How far is −3 from 4 on the number line?

  1. Count from −3 up to 0, then on to 4.
  • That is 4 − 3. The −3 is on the other side of zero, so add the distances.

3 to reach zero, then 4 more: 7.

What is 3 more than −5?

  1. Start at −5 and go up 3.
  • That goes down. “More” means up the number line.
  • That is 5 − 3. Start at −5 and go up 3: you have not reached zero yet.

−5 + 3 = −2.

2. Adding and subtracting positive numbers

Think of the number line (or a thermometer). Adding a positive number moves you right (up). Subtracting a positive number moves you left (down).

Key idea: start at the first number, then move. −3 + 5: start at −3, go up 5, land on 2. −4 − 3: start at −4, go down 3, land on −7.

Worked example

Work out −6 + 10.

  1. Start at −6.
  2. Go up 10: up 6 reaches 0, then 4 more.
  3. −6 + 10 = 4.

Check your knowledge: adding and subtracting

Work out −3 + 5.

  1. Start at −3 and move 5 up.
  • Adding moves you up the line, not down.
  • You ignored the sign of the 3: it starts below zero.

−3 + 5 = 2.

Work out −4 − 3.

  1. Start at −4 and move 3 down.
  • Subtracting moves you further down when you are already below zero.
  • The answer is below zero: you began at −4 and went down.

−4 − 3 = −7.

Work out 2 − 7.

  1. Start at 2, go down 7: 2 reaches zero, then 5 more below.
  • You took the smaller from the bigger, but the answer goes below zero.

2 − 7 = −5.

Work out −6 + 10.

  1. Up 6 gets to 0, then 4 more.
  • Adding 10 from −6 ends up above zero.
  • The start is −6, not 6.

−6 + 10 = 4.

The temperature is 3°C and falls by 8 degrees. What is the new temperature in °C?

  1. 3 − 8.
  • It falls below zero: the answer is negative.

3 − 8 = −5°C.

3. Subtracting a negative

This is the bit that surprises people. Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding the positive. A minus followed by a negative number combines to a plus.

5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8

Key idea: subtracting a debt makes you richer. If someone takes away a £3 debt of yours, you are £3 better off. So 5 − (−3) is 5 + 3.

Worked example

Work out −2 − (−6).

  1. Change “− (−6)” to “+ 6”.
  2. −2 + 6 = 4.

And adding a negative is the same as subtracting the positive: 3 + (−5) = 3 − 5 = −2.

Check your knowledge: subtracting negatives

Work out 5 − (−3).

  1. Two minus signs make a plus.
  • That treats it as 5 − 3. The second minus turns it into a plus.

5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8.

Work out −2 − (−6).

  1. Change to −2 + 6.
  • That treats it as −2 − 6. Subtracting a negative adds.

−2 + 6 = 4.

Work out 3 + (−5).

  1. Adding a negative is the same as subtracting.
  • Adding a negative number makes the answer smaller, not bigger.

3 + (−5) = 3 − 5 = −2.

Work out −4 − (−4).

  1. Subtracting a negative adds: −4 + 4.
  • That subtracts a positive 4. Here the 4 being taken away is negative.

−4 + 4 = 0.

Work out 7 − (−2) + (−3).

  1. Do it left to right: first 7 − (−2).
  2. Then add (−3), which is the same as subtracting 3.
  • That adds the 3. But + (−3) is the same as subtracting 3.
  • Check the first step: 7 − (−2) is 7 + 2 = 9, not 5.

7 + 2 = 9, then 9 − 3 = 6.

4. Multiplying and dividing

For multiplication and division, work out the size as usual, then decide the sign with one rule:

same signs → positive
different signs → negative

Key idea: (−5) × (−6): same signs, so positive: 30. 3 × (−4): different signs, so negative: −12. Division follows exactly the same rule.

Worked example

Work out −20 ÷ (−4).

  1. Size: 20 ÷ 4 = 5.
  2. Signs: both negative, so the same signs: positive.
  3. −20 ÷ (−4) = 5.

Check your knowledge: multiply and divide

Work out 3 × (−4).

  1. Different signs.
  • The signs are different, so the answer is negative.

3 × 4 = 12, with different signs the answer is negative: −12.

Work out (−5) × (−6).

  1. Same signs.
  • Two negatives multiplied give a positive.

5 × 6 = 30, with the same signs it is positive: 30.

Work out −24 ÷ 6.

  1. Different signs.
  • The signs are different, so the answer is negative.

24 ÷ 6 = 4, with different signs: −4.

Work out −20 ÷ (−4).

  1. Same signs.
  • Dividing a negative by a negative gives a positive.

20 ÷ 4 = 5, same signs: 5.

Work out (−2) × 5 × (−3).

  1. Do it in steps: (−2) × 5 first.
  2. Then multiply by (−3).
  • There are two negative signs, which cancel to a positive.

(−2) × 5 = −10, then −10 × (−3) = 30. Two negatives make a positive.

Work out (−3) × (−3).

  1. Same signs.
  • Squaring a negative number gives a positive, because two negatives multiply to a positive.

3 × 3 = 9, and the answer is positive: 9. (This is also (−3)².)

Flip it: run it backwards

So far you have gone one way. Now run it backwards: this is where much of the real understanding comes from.

Forwards

−7 + 10 = 3

Add and find where you land.

Backwards

−7 + ? = 3

Find what must be added.

Key idea: to find a missing number, undo what was done to it. Adding undoes subtracting, and multiplying undoes dividing, exactly as before. The signs make it feel strange, but the method is the same.

Forwards

5 − (−3) = 8

Subtracting a negative adds.

Backwards

5 + 3 = 8 … so 8 − 3 = 5, and 8 + (−3) = 5

Adding a negative takes away.

Check your knowledge: flip it

−7 + ? = 3. Find the missing number.

  1. From −7, how far up to get to 3?
  • That is −7 + 3. The missing number is how far you must go to reach 3.
  • From −7 you need to go all the way up to 3.

−7 to 0 is 7, then 3 more: 10. Check: −7 + 10 = 3.

? − (−4) = 9. Find the missing number.

  1. Subtracting −4 is the same as adding 4. So ? + 4 = 9.
  • That is 9 + 4. The sum ? + 4 = 9 means you take 4 from 9.

? + 4 = 9, so ? = 5. Check: 5 − (−4) = 9.

(−3) × ? = 18. Find the missing number.

  1. Undo with division: 18 ÷ (−3).
  • Check the sign: −3 × 6 = −18, not 18. The missing number must be negative.

18 ÷ (−3) = −6. Check: (−3) × (−6) = 18.

The temperature rises from −5°C to 8°C. By how many degrees did it rise?

  1. Count up to zero (5), then on to 8.
  • That is 8 − 5. The start is below zero, so add the two distances.

5 + 8 = 13 degrees. Check: −5 + 13 = 8.

? ÷ (−2) = 6. Find the missing number.

  1. Undo ÷ (−2) with × (−2).
  • Check the sign: 12 ÷ (−2) = −6, not 6.
  • That is 6 ÷ (−2). To undo a division, multiply.

6 × (−2) = −12. Check: −12 ÷ (−2) = 6.

5. Negative numbers in real life

Negatives show up as temperatures, bank balances, floors below ground and heights below sea level. In each, a question like “how far apart?” is top minus bottom.

Key idea: the difference between two values is the higher one minus the lower one. From −30 m to 150 m is 150 − (−30) = 180 m.

Check your knowledge: in context

A bank account is £45 overdrawn (−45). A payment of £120 goes in. What is the balance in pounds?

  1. −45 + 120.
  • That adds 45 and 120. The account starts £45 below zero.
  • The payment is bigger than the overdraft, so the balance is positive.

−45 + 120 = 75. The balance is £75.

A shipwreck is 30 m below sea level (−30) and a cliff is 150 m above. How many metres higher is the cliff than the wreck?

  1. Top minus bottom: 150 − (−30).
  • That is 150 − 30. The wreck is below sea level, so add.

150 + 30 = 180 m.

Find the next number in the sequence −8, −5, −2, …

  1. What is added each time?
  • That is the second number. The sequence goes up by 3.
  • Add 3 to the last number: −2 + 3.

It goes up by 3 each time: −2 + 3 = 1.

A lift goes from floor −2 (a basement) to floor 5. How many floors does it travel?

  1. 2 floors to reach floor 0, then 5 more.
  • That is 5 − 2. The basement is below ground, so add.

2 + 5 = 7 floors.

The temperatures one day were −3, 4, −8 and 0 degrees. What is the range (highest minus lowest)?

  1. Highest is 4, lowest is −8.
  2. 4 − (−8).
  • That is 4 − 0. The lowest is −8, not 0.
  • That is lowest minus highest. The range is highest minus lowest, which is positive.

4 − (−8) = 12 degrees.

Spot the mistake

Ben says: “−3 − 4 = 1.” What went wrong?

  • He treated it as 4 − 3. Starting at −3 and going down 4 gives −7.
  • Nothing: it is right
  • Two minus signs make a plus, so it should be 7
  1. Picture the number line: you start at −3 and move 4 to the left.

The correct answer is −7.

Cara says: “(−2) × (−5) = −10.” What went wrong?

  • She forgot that two negative signs make a positive. The answer is 10.
  • Nothing: a negative is always involved so the answer is negative
  • It should be −7
  1. Same signs give a positive.

(−2) × (−5) = 10.

Dan says: “5 − (−2) = 3.” What went wrong?

  • He subtracted 2. Subtracting a negative adds: 5 + 2 = 7.
  • Nothing: 5 − 2 = 3
  • It should be −3
  1. Subtracting a debt makes you better off.

5 − (−2) = 7.

Check your knowledge: mixed review

Work out −8 + 3.

  1. Start at −8, up 3.
  • The start is below zero, and 3 is not enough to reach zero.
  • Adding moves up the line.

−8 + 3 = −5.

Work out 6 − (−6).

  1. Change to 6 + 6.
  • Subtracting a negative adds: 6 + 6, not 6 − 6.

12.

Work out (−2) × (−2) × (−2).

  1. (−2) × (−2) = 4. Then 4 × (−2).
  • There are three negative signs, an odd number, so the answer is negative.

4 × (−2) = −8. Three negatives make a negative.

Which is the biggest: −0.5, −0.05 or −5?

  • −0.5
  • −0.05
  • −5
  1. Which is closest to zero?

−0.05 is closest to zero, so it is the biggest.

Work out −12 ÷ (−3) × (−2).

  1. Left to right: −12 ÷ (−3) first.
  • The last step multiplies by −2, so the answer is negative.
  • Do the division first, then multiply.

−12 ÷ (−3) = 4, then 4 × (−2) = −8.

Exam style

The temperature one Monday was −3°C and by Tuesday it was 4°C. Over five nights the minimum temperatures were −6, −2, 3, 1 and −4 degrees.

(a) [1 mark] By how many degrees did the temperature rise from Monday to Tuesday?

  1. 4 − (−3).
  • That is 4 − 3. Monday was below zero, so add the two distances.

4 − (−3) = 4 + 3 = 7 degrees.

(b) [2 marks] Work out the range of the five minimum temperatures.

  1. Highest is 3, lowest is −6.
  2. Range = highest − lowest.
  • The range is highest minus lowest, so 3 − (−6).
  • That ignores the lowest, which is −6, not 0.

3 − (−6) = 9 degrees.

(c) [2 marks] A submarine at −120 m rises 45 m. What is its new depth, as a number in metres (use a minus sign)?

  1. −120 + 45.
  • It is still below sea level, so the number is negative.
  • It rises, which means moving up: add 45.

−120 + 45 = −75 m.

Going further

Work out (−2)3.

  1. (−2) × (−2) × (−2).
  • An odd number of negatives gives a negative.
  • The power means multiply three of them, not add.

4 × (−2) = −8.

Two numbers multiply to give −12 and add to give 1. What is the larger of the two?

  1. Pairs that multiply to −12: 3 and −4, −3 and 4, 2 and −6 …
  2. Which pair adds to 1?
  • That is the smaller one. The larger number is the positive one.

−3 and 4: −3 × 4 = −12 and −3 + 4 = 1. The larger is 4. (This is the idea behind factorising quadratics.)

Why it works

  • The number line goes both ways. Adding moves right, subtracting moves left, and the signs just tell you the direction.
  • Subtracting a negative adds because taking away a debt leaves you better off.
  • Same signs, positive; different signs, negative is the same pattern for multiplying and dividing, because dividing is multiplying by a reciprocal.

Common traps

  • Thinking −10 is bigger than −2.
  • Treating −3 − 4 as 4 − 3.
  • Forgetting that subtracting a negative adds.
  • Ignoring the signs when multiplying: an odd number of negatives gives a negative, an even number gives a positive.
  • Losing a sign in the last step of a long calculation.
