Similar shapes

Similar shapes have the same shape but different sizes: one is an exact enlargement of the other. Find the scale factor from two matching sides, use it to find missing lengths, then run it backwards. Higher parts add area and volume scale factors and proving triangles congruent.

Where you will use this: a scale drawing of a room, a model of a roof truss, finding the height of a pole from its shadow, and reading a map.

Words you need

Tap a teal word anywhere on the page to see this again.

Similar shapes
Shapes that are enlargements of each other: the same shape, different size. All the angles match and all the lengths are in the same ratio.
Scale factor
The number all lengths are multiplied by in an enlargement. If lengths are multiplied by k, areas are multiplied by k² and volumes by k3.
Congruent
Exactly the same shape and exactly the same size. One can be slid, turned or flipped to fit onto the other.
Enlargement
A change of size where every length is multiplied by the same scale factor. The shape stays the same.

Units and typing

A scale factor is just a number: it has no units. Lengths are in cm or m, areas in cm² or m², volumes in cm³ or m³.

A scale of 1 : 50 means 1 cm on the drawing is 50 cm in real life. Change units at the end: 100 cm = 1 m, and 100 000 cm = 1 km.

Type scale factors as numbers, like 2.5 or 0.4. For a yes or no question, type yes or no. Money is 2 decimal places.

1. Same shape, different size

Two shapes are similar when one is an exact copy of the other, made bigger or smaller. The shape stays the same. Only the size changes. A photo and its enlargement are similar.

For two shapes to be similar, two things must be true:

  • All the angles match.
  • Every length is multiplied by the same number. That number is the Scale factor.

Shapes that are the same size and the same shape are Congruent. You can slide, turn or flip one onto the other. Two identical floor tiles are congruent. Congruent shapes are similar shapes with a scale factor of 1.

Be careful with rectangles. They always have four right angles, so the angles always match. You must also check the lengths: a 2 by 1 rectangle and a 2 by 2 square are not similar.

Key idea: similar means the same angles and every length multiplied by the same scale factor. Check every pair of matching sides, not just one.

Worked example

Are a 3 cm by 2 cm rectangle and a 9 cm by 6 cm rectangle similar?

  1. Both are rectangles, so all the angles are 90° and they match.
  2. Long sides: 9 / 3 = 3.
  3. Short sides: 6 / 2 = 3.
  4. Both sides are multiplied by 3, so the rectangles are similar. The scale factor is 3.

2. Scale factor and missing sides

Sides in the same place on both shapes are Corresponding sides. To find the scale factor, pick a pair of corresponding sides where you know both lengths, and divide the new length by the old length.

scale factor = new length ÷ old length

Then multiply any other length of the old shape by the scale factor to get the matching length of the new shape. Making a shape bigger by a scale factor is called an Enlargement.

Key idea: find the scale factor from a pair of sides where you know both. Then use it on the side you want.

Worked example: a missing side

The two rectangles are similar. Find the length x.

  1. The 4 cm side matches the 10 cm side. Scale factor = 10 / 4 = 2.5.
  2. The 3 cm side matches x. x = 3 × 2.5 = 7.5.
  3. So x = 7.5 cm.
  4. Check: 7.5 / 3 = 2.5, the same scale factor.

Now you finish it

A triangle has sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm. It is enlarged by a scale factor of 3. Find the sides of the new triangle.

  1. 5 × 3 = ___.
  2. 6 × 3 = ___.
  3. 7 × 3 = ___.
Check my answer

5 × 3 = 15, 6 × 3 = 18 and 7 × 3 = 21. The new triangle has sides 15 cm, 18 cm and 21 cm.

Check your knowledge: scale factor and missing sides

  • That is the short side, 3 × 4. The question asks for the long side, 5 × 4.
  • That looks like 5 + 4. A scale factor multiplies. It does not add.

The long side is 5 cm. 5 × 4 = 20 cm.

  2. Which triangle is the new one: small or big?

A photo is 12 cm wide and 8 cm tall. It is enlarged so that it is 30 cm wide. How tall is the enlargement, in cm?

  2. Then multiply the height by the scale factor.

  • That is the scale factor, 30 / 12. Multiply the height 8 cm by it.

Scale factor = 30 / 12 = 2.5. Height = 8 × 2.5 = 20 cm.

  2. Multiply the model height by that number.
  • That is the scale factor, 120 / 40. Multiply the height 25 cm by it.
  • That looks like 25 + 80, adding the extra width to the height. Use the scale factor to multiply.

3. Similar triangles

Triangles are special: you do not have to check everything. Two triangles are similar if any one of these is true:

  • Two angles of one triangle match two angles of the other. (The third angles match too, because the angles in a triangle add up to 180°.)
  • All three pairs of sides have the same scale factor.

A common way to meet similar triangles is a line drawn across a triangle parallel to one side. The small triangle at the top has the same angles as the big one, because corresponding angles on parallel lines are equal. So the two triangles are similar.

Shadows give another example. At the same time of day the sun's rays are parallel, so the angles match for every object. A short stake and a tall pole make similar triangles with their shadows.

Key idea: for triangles, two matching angles are enough to show they are similar. Then find the scale factor from a pair of sides.

Worked example: a line parallel to the base

In triangle ABC, the line DE is parallel to the base BC. AD = 4 cm, DB = 2 cm and DE = 5 cm. Find BC.

  1. Triangles ADE and ABC are similar (the angle at A is shared and DE is parallel to BC).
  2. The whole side AB = 4 + 2 = 6 cm. It matches AD = 4 cm.
  3. Scale factor from the small triangle to the big one = 6 / 4 = 1.5.
  4. DE matches BC. BC = 5 × 1.5 = 7.5 cm.

Now you finish it

A 1.5 m stake casts a shadow 2 m long. At the same moment a flagpole casts a shadow 10 m long. How tall is the flagpole?

  1. The shadows match: scale factor = 10 / 2 = ___.
  2. Height of flagpole = 1.5 × ___ = ___ m.
Check my answer

Scale factor = 10 / 2 = 5. Height = 1.5 × 5 = 7.5 m.

Check your knowledge: similar triangles

Triangle A has angles of 40° and 75°. Triangle B has angles of 40° and 65°. Are the two triangles similar? Type yes or no.

  1. Work out the third angle in each triangle.
  2. Do both triangles have the same three angles?
  • That looks like comparing 75° with 65°. Those are not matching angles. Find the third angle of each triangle first.

Third angle in A: 180 − 40 − 75 = 65°. Third angle in B: 180 − 40 − 65 = 75°. Both triangles have angles 40°, 65° and 75°. So they are similar. The answer is yes.

In triangle ABC the line DE is parallel to BC. AD = 3 cm, AB = 9 cm and DE = 4 cm. Find BC, in cm.

  1. Triangles ADE and ABC are similar. AD matches AB.
  2. Scale factor from the small triangle to the big one = AB / AD. Then use it on DE.
  • That looks like 4 / 3. Use AB / AD = 9 / 3 for the scale factor, then multiply DE by it.
  • That looks like 9 + 4 − 3. Use the scale factor to multiply, not to add and subtract.

Scale factor = AB / AD = 9 / 3 = 3. BC = DE × 3 = 4 × 3 = 12 cm.

A model roof truss is a triangle with a base of 30 cm and a height of 12 cm. The real truss is similar, with a base of 4.5 m. How tall is the real truss, in m?

  1. Change the units first, so the two bases are in the same unit.
  2. Find the scale factor from the bases, then use it on the height of 12 cm.
  • That is the height in cm. The question asks for metres, so divide by 100.
  • That looks like 12 cm in metres. That is the model height. Multiply by the scale factor first.

4.5 m = 450 cm. Scale factor = 450 / 30 = 15. Height = 12 × 15 = 180 cm = 1.8 m.

A stake and a flagpole both stand upright. Why are the triangles made by them and their shadows similar?

  • The sun's rays are parallel, so the angles match
  • The pole and the stake are the same height, so the triangles are congruent
  • Shadows always point north, so the triangles must be similar
  • The ground is flat and both shadows are measured in metres
  1. Which angles in the two triangles do you know match?
  2. What can you say about the sun's rays at the same moment?

At the same moment the sun's rays are parallel. So the angle between ray and ground is the same for both, and both objects make a right angle with the ground. Two matching angles are enough: the triangles are similar.

4. Scale drawings and maps

A Scale drawing is a shape drawn similar to the real thing. The scale tells you the scale factor. A scale of 1 : 50 means 1 cm on the drawing is 50 cm in real life. So every real length is 50 times the drawing length. The Map scale on a map works the same way.

  • Drawing to real: multiply by the second number of the scale.
  • Real to drawing: divide by it.
  • Change the units at the end: 100 cm = 1 m and 100 000 cm = 1 km.

Key idea: write the units after the multiplication. 8 cm on a 1 : 50 plan is 8 × 50 = 400 cm, and 400 cm is 4 m.

Worked example: a plan

A plan of a room uses a scale of 1 : 50. The room is 8 cm long on the plan. How long is the real room, in m?

  1. Real length = 8 × 50 = 400 cm.
  2. Change to metres: 400 / 100 = 4 m.

Worked example: a map

A map has a scale of 1 : 25 000. Two villages are 6 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they in km?

  1. Real distance = 6 × 25 000 = 150 000 cm.
  2. Change to metres: 150 000 / 100 = 1500 m.
  3. Change to kilometres: 1500 / 1000 = 1.5 km.

Now you finish it

  2. Change to metres: ___ / 100 = ___ m.

Real length = 18 × 20 = 360 cm = 3.6 m. Cost = 3.6 × 4.80 = £17.28.

A scale of 1 : 200 means…

  • 1 cm on the drawing is 200 cm in real life
  • 200 cm on the drawing is 1 cm in real life
  • 1 cm on the drawing is 200 mm in real life, which is 20 cm
  1. The drawing is smaller than the real thing.
  2. The first number is the drawing and the second number is real life.

In 1 : 200, 1 unit on the drawing stands for 200 of the same unit in real life. So 1 cm on the drawing is 200 cm (2 m) in real life.

Flip it: run it backwards

So far you were told the scale factor and found a new length. Now go the other way: you know two matching lengths, and you find the scale factor. You will also go from a big shape down to a small one.

Forwards

old side = 4, scale factor = 3, new side = ?

Multiply: 4 × 3 = 12.

Backwards

old side = 4, new side = 12, scale factor = ?

Divide: 12 ÷ 4 = 3.

  • Scale factor = new ÷ old, using a pair of sides you know.
  • Going from a big shape to a small one, the scale factor is less than 1, like 0.4 or 2 over 3. The small shape is the "new" one.
  • To find the old length when you know the new length, divide by the scale factor.
  • The scale factor back from B to A is 1 ÷ k. If A to B is 3, then B to A is 1 over 3.

Key idea: "new ÷ old" always works. Before you divide, decide which shape is the new one. A scale factor below 1 means the new shape is smaller.

Worked example: big to small

The big triangle and the small triangle are similar. Find x.

  1. Match the sides: 15 cm (big) matches 10 cm (small).
  2. The small triangle is the new shape. Scale factor = new / old = 10 / 15 = 2 over 3.
  3. x matches 9 cm. x = 9 × 2 over 3 = 6 cm.
  4. Check: the small triangle should be smaller, and 6 is less than 9. ✓

Worked example: finding the original

A drawing was enlarged by a scale factor of 4 to make a copy with a long side of 28 cm. How long was the long side of the drawing?

  1. Going from the drawing to the copy: copy = drawing × 4.
  2. To go back, divide: drawing = 28 / 4 = 7 cm.

Now you finish it

5. Area and volume scale factors Higher

When you enlarge a shape by a scale factor k, lengths are multiplied by k, but areas and volumes grow faster.

  • Lengths: multiplied by k.
  • Areas: multiplied by k² (k times k).
  • Volumes: multiplied by k3 (k times k times k).

Try it. A 3 by 2 rectangle has area 6. Double the lengths (k = 2) to get a 6 by 4 rectangle with area 24. The area is 4 times bigger, and 4 = 2². A cube with sides of 1 cm has volume 1 cm³. Double the sides and the volume is 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 cm³.

Key idea: area scale factor = k² and volume scale factor = k3. Working backwards from an area ratio, take a square root. From a volume ratio, take a cube root.

Worked example: area

Two similar shapes have matching lengths 4 cm and 12 cm. The small shape has an area of 8 cm². Find the area of the big shape.

  1. Length scale factor k = 12 / 4 = 3.
  2. Area scale factor = 3² = 9.
  3. Area of big shape = 8 × 9 = 72 cm².

Worked example: volume

Two similar tins have heights 10 cm and 15 cm. The small tin holds 400 cm³. How much does the big tin hold?

  1. Length scale factor k = 15 / 10 = 1.5.
  2. Volume scale factor = 1.53 = 3.375.
  3. Volume of big tin = 400 × 3.375 = 1350 cm³.

Now you finish it

Two similar shapes have areas of 20 cm² and 45 cm². The small shape has a side of 6 cm. Find the matching side of the big shape.

  1. Area scale factor = 45 / 20 = ___.
  2. Length scale factor = the square root of ___ = ___.
  3. Matching side = 6 × ___ = ___ cm.
Check my answer

Area scale factor = 45 / 20 = 2.25. Length scale factor = square root of 2.25 = 1.5. Matching side = 6 × 1.5 = 9 cm.

Check your knowledge: area and volume scale factors Higher

6. Proving triangles congruent Higher

Two triangles are congruent when they are exactly the same size and shape. You do not need to check every side and angle. Any one of these four sets of facts is enough:

SSS: three sides

All three pairs of sides are equal.

SAS: two sides and the angle between them

The angle must be the one between the two sides.

ASA: two angles and a side

Two angles and a side in the same place in both triangles. The third angles are equal too, because angles in a triangle add up to 180°.

RHS: right angle, hypotenuse, side

Both triangles have a right angle. The longest sides (the hypotenuses) are equal, and one other side is equal.

Two things are not enough. Three equal angles (AAA) only make the triangles similar: a big and a small triangle can have the same angles. Two sides and an angle that is not between them (SSA) can make two different triangles.

Key idea: to prove congruence, name the condition (SSS, SAS, ASA or RHS) and say which three facts match. Equal angles alone never prove it.

Worked example

Triangles ABC and PQR have AB = PQ = 7 cm, AC = PR = 5 cm and angle A = angle P = 40°. Show they are congruent.

  1. AB = PQ (7 cm). That is one pair of equal sides.
  2. AC = PR (5 cm). That is a second pair of equal sides.
  3. The angle at A is between AB and AC. The angle at P is between PQ and PR. They are equal (40°).
  4. Two sides and the angle between them match, so the triangles are congruent (SAS).

Now you finish it

Two triangles each have angles of 50° and 60°, and the 8 cm side between those two angles. Which condition shows they are congruent?

  1. Two angles are equal: ___ and ___.
  2. The side between them is equal: ___ cm.
  3. The condition is: ___.
Check my answer

50° and 60° are equal in both. The 8 cm side between them is equal. That is ASA, so the triangles are congruent.

Check your knowledge: congruent triangles Higher

Which of these is not enough to prove two triangles congruent?

  • All three angles are equal
  • All three sides are equal
  • Two sides and the angle between them are equal
  • Two angles and the side between them are equal
  1. Think of a small and a big triangle with the same shape.
  2. Which fact would those two triangles share, even though they are different sizes?

Three equal angles only make the triangles similar. A big triangle and a small one can have the same angles, so they are not congruent.

Two right-angled triangles each have a hypotenuse of 13 cm and another side of 5 cm. Which condition shows they are congruent?

  • RHS: right angle, hypotenuse, side
  • SAS: two sides and the angle between them
  • SSS: all three sides are known to be equal
  • ASA: two angles and a side between them
  1. What is special about the triangle, apart from the lengths?
  2. Which condition has the word hypotenuse in it?

Both triangles have a right angle, equal hypotenuses (13 cm) and one equal side (5 cm). That is RHS.

Higher Triangle ABC is congruent to triangle PQR, with A matching P, B matching Q and C matching R. Angle B = 55°. What is angle Q, in degrees?

  1. Congruent triangles have all matching angles equal.
  2. Which letter matches B?
  • That is 180 − 55. Angle Q is the matching angle, so it is equal to B, not the angles on a line.

The triangles are congruent, so matching angles are equal. B matches Q, so angle Q = angle B = 55°.

Spot the mistake

Finding an error in someone else's working helps you learn. For each one, decide what went wrong.

Ali says: a 2 cm by 5 cm rectangle and a 6 cm by 10 cm rectangle are similar, because 6 is 3 times 2. What went wrong?

  • He checked only one pair of sides
  • Rectangles can never be similar to each other
  • He should have added the same number to each side instead of multiplying
  1. Check the other pair of sides. What do you multiply 5 by to get 10?
  2. Is that the same number as for 2 and 6?

6 / 2 = 3, but 10 / 5 = 2. The scale factors are different, so the rectangles are not similar. Ali must check every pair of matching sides.

Bea says: the scale factor from a side of 12 cm to a side of 8 cm is 12 / 8 = 1.5. What went wrong?

  • She divided old by new, not new by old
  • Nothing is wrong. A scale factor is never less than 1
  • She should have subtracted: 12 − 8 = 4
  1. Which length is the new one, 12 cm or 8 cm?
  2. Scale factor = new divided by old.

The new length is 8 cm and the old length is 12 cm. Scale factor = 8 / 12 = 2 over 3, which is less than 1 because the new shape is smaller.

Higher Cal says: two similar tins have heights in the ratio 1 : 3, so the big tin holds 3 times as much. What went wrong?

  • Volume is multiplied by 3 × 3 × 3 = 27
  • Volume is multiplied by 3 × 3 = 9, which is the area factor
  • Nothing is wrong: volume is measured in the same way as height
  1. Height is one length. Volume has length, width and height.
  2. All three lengths are multiplied by 3.

Each of the three lengths is multiplied by 3, so the volume is multiplied by 3 × 3 × 3 = 27. The big tin holds 27 times as much.

Check your knowledge: mixed review

A map has a scale of 1 : 50 000. Two lighthouses are 12 km apart in real life. How far apart are they on the map, in cm?

  1. Change 12 km into cm first. There are 100 000 cm in 1 km.
  2. Going from real life to the map, divide by 50 000.
  • That looks like 12 × 50 000. Going from real life to the map, divide by 50 000.
  • That looks like a slip with the zeros. 12 km is 1 200 000 cm, so check how many zeros you used.

12 km = 12 × 100 000 = 1 200 000 cm. On the map: 1 200 000 / 50 000 = 24 cm.

Triangle A has sides of 4 cm, 6 cm and 9 cm. Triangle B is similar to A, and its shortest side is 10 cm. How long is the longest side of B, in cm?

  1. The shortest sides match. Find the scale factor from them.
  2. The longest side of A is 9 cm. Multiply it by the scale factor.
  • That is the middle side of B, 6 × 2.5. The longest side of A is 9 cm.

Scale factor = 10 / 4 = 2.5. Longest side of B = 9 × 2.5 = 22.5 cm.

A small right-angled triangle has short sides of 3 cm and 4 cm. A similar triangle has a shortest side of 9 cm. Find the length of the hypotenuse of the larger triangle, in cm.

  1. Find the scale factor from the two shortest sides.
  2. Either find the other sides and use Pythagoras, or find the small hypotenuse first and multiply it.
  • That is the hypotenuse of the small triangle. Multiply it by the scale factor 3.
  • That is the other short side of the large triangle, 4 × 3. The hypotenuse is the longest side.

Small hypotenuse: the square root of (3² + 4²) = the square root of 25 = 5 cm. Scale factor = 9 / 3 = 3. Large hypotenuse = 5 × 3 = 15 cm.

Skylights take up 15% of a roof with an area of 240 m². How many square metres of the roof are skylights?

  1. Change 15% to a decimal or a fraction of 240.
  2. 15% is 15 hundredths. Multiply it by 240.
  • That looks like 240 / 15. Percent means out of 100: find 15 hundredths of 240.
  • That looks like 240 + 15. Find 15% of 240 by multiplying.

15% of 240 = 0.15 × 240 = 36 m².

Check your knowledge: on the job

Electrical. A wiring plan uses a scale of 1 : 50. A cable run measures 14.2 cm on the plan. Twin and earth cable costs £1.80 per metre. How much does the cable for the run cost? Give your answer in pounds, like 12.50.

  1. Find the real length in cm, then in metres.
  2. Multiply the length in metres by the cost per metre.
  • That is the real length in metres. Multiply it by £1.80 to get the cost.
  • That looks like using 14.2 m. The plan length is 14.2 cm, so the real length is 7.1 m.

Real length = 14.2 × 50 = 710 cm = 7.1 m. Cost = 7.1 × 1.80 = £12.78.

Plumbing. A heating plan uses a scale of 1 : 20. A pipe run measures 36 cm on the plan. 15 mm copper pipe costs £6.40 per metre. How much does the pipe cost? Give your answer in pounds, like 12.50.

  1. Real length = plan length × 20. Change it from cm to m.
  2. Multiply the length in metres by £6.40.
  • That is the real length in metres. Multiply it by £6.40 to find the cost.
  • That looks like 36 × 6.40. The 36 cm is the plan length, so find the real length first.

Real length = 36 × 20 = 720 cm = 7.2 m. Cost = 7.2 × 6.40 = £46.08.

Carpentry. A model of a roof truss is 20 cm wide at the base and 8 cm high. The real truss is similar and 6 m wide at the base. How high is the real truss, in m?

  1. Change 6 m to cm, or the model to metres, so the units match.
  2. Find the scale factor from the bases, then use it on the height.
  • That is the height in cm. The question asks for metres, so divide by 100.
  • That is the model height in metres. Multiply it by the scale factor to get the real height.

6 m = 600 cm. Scale factor = 600 / 20 = 30. Height = 8 × 30 = 240 cm = 2.4 m.

Shadows. A 2 m spirit level standing upright casts a shadow 1.25 m long. At the same time a chimney casts a shadow 7.5 m long. How tall is the chimney, in m?

  1. The sun's rays are parallel, so the two triangles are similar.
  2. The shadows match. Find the scale factor from them, then use it on the 2 m height.
  • That looks like 7.5 / 1.25. That is the scale factor. Multiply the 2 m by it.
  • That looks like 2 + 7.5 − 1.25. Use the scale factor to multiply, not to add and subtract.

Scale factor = 7.5 / 1.25 = 6. Height of chimney = 2 × 6 = 12 m.

Kitchen fitting. A kitchen plan has a scale of 1 : 10. A worktop is 36 cm long on the plan. Worktop costs £95 per metre. What is the cost of the worktop, in pounds?

  1. Real length = plan length × 10. Change it to metres.
  2. Multiply the length in metres by £95.
  • That is the real length in metres. Multiply it by £95 to find the cost.
  • That looks like 36 × 95. The 36 cm is the plan length, so find the real length in metres first.

Real length = 36 × 10 = 360 cm = 3.6 m. Cost = 3.6 × 95 = £342.

Use these like a real paper. The marks tell you how much to write. A calculator is fine here.

[3 marks] Triangle PQR is similar to triangle ABC. AB = 6 cm, BC = 9 cm and AC = 12 cm. PQ = 8 cm, and PQ matches AB. Work out the perimeter of triangle PQR, in cm.

  1. Find the scale factor from the two sides that match: PQ and AB.
  2. Multiply the other two sides of ABC by the scale factor.
  3. Add the three sides of PQR.
  • That is the perimeter of triangle ABC, 6 + 9 + 12. The question asks for the perimeter of PQR.

Scale factor = 8 / 6 = 4 over 3.

QR = 9 × 4 over 3 = 12 cm and PR = 12 × 4 over 3 = 16 cm.

Perimeter = 8 + 12 + 16 = 36 cm.

  • M1 for the scale factor 8 / 6 (or 4 over 3)
  • M1 for finding QR = 12 or PR = 16
  • A1 for the perimeter, 36

[3 marks] A model of a bridge is made to a scale of 1 : 40. The model is 55 cm long. A rope along the real bridge costs £3.20 per metre. Work out the cost of a rope as long as the real bridge. Give your answer in pounds, like 12.50.

  1. Find the real length in cm: model length times 40.
  2. Change cm to m (divide by 100).
  3. Multiply the length in metres by £3.20.
  • That is the length of the real bridge in metres. Now multiply by £3.20 to find the cost.
  • That looks like using 2200 cm instead of 22 m. The rope is priced per metre.

Real length = 55 × 40 = 2200 cm.

2200 cm = 22 m.

Cost = 22 × 3.20 = £70.40.

  • M1 for 55 × 40 = 2200 (the real length in cm)
  • M1 for changing 2200 cm to 22 m
  • A1 for 22 × 3.20 = 70.40

[4 marks] In triangle ABC, the line DE is parallel to BC. AD = 6 cm, DB = 3 cm and DE = 8 cm. Show that BC = 12 cm.

  1. Say why triangles ADE and ABC are similar before you use any numbers.
  2. Find the length of AB, then the scale factor from ADE to ABC.
  3. Use the scale factor on DE.

DE is parallel to BC, so the angles ADE and ABC are equal (corresponding angles). The angle at A is shared. So triangles ADE and ABC are similar.

AB = 6 + 3 = 9 cm. The scale factor from ADE to ABC = 9 / 6 = 1.5.

BC = DE × 1.5 = 8 × 1.5 = 12 cm.

  • C1 for saying the triangles ADE and ABC are similar, because the angle at A is shared and the corresponding angles are equal (DE is parallel to BC)
  • M1 for AB = 6 + 3 = 9 cm
  • M1 for the scale factor 9 / 6 = 1.5 (or 9 / 6)
  • A1 for BC = 8 × 1.5 = 12 cm shown

Higher [3 marks] Two paint tins are similar. The small tin is 8 cm tall and the large tin is 12 cm tall. The label on the small tin has an area of 60 cm². Work out the area of the label on the large tin, in cm².

  1. Find the length scale factor from the two heights.
  2. Square it to get the area scale factor.
  3. Multiply the small label area by it.
  • That is 60 × 1.5. Areas need the scale factor squared.
  • That is 60 × 1.53. Volume uses the cube. Area uses the square.

Length scale factor = 12 / 8 = 1.5.

Area scale factor = 1.5² = 2.25.

Area = 60 × 2.25 = 135 cm².

  • M1 for the length scale factor 12 / 8 = 1.5
  • M1 for the area scale factor 1.5² = 2.25
  • A1 for 60 × 2.25 = 135

Higher [4 marks] Two water tanks are similar. The small tank is 50 cm tall and holds 80 litres. The large tank holds 270 litres. Work out the height of the large tank, in cm.

  1. The volumes give the volume scale factor. Divide the big volume by the small one.
  2. The length scale factor is the cube root of the volume scale factor.
  3. Multiply the small height by the length scale factor.
  • That is 50 × 3.375. That uses the volume scale factor. For a height, take its cube root first.
  • That is the length scale factor. Multiply the 50 cm by it to find the height.

Volume scale factor = 270 / 80 = 3.375.

Length scale factor = the cube root of 3.375 = 1.5.

Height = 50 × 1.5 = 75 cm.

  • M1 for the volume scale factor 270 / 80 = 3.375
  • M1 for the cube root of 3.375 = 1.5
  • M1 for 50 × 1.5
  • A1 for 75

Why it works

  • Similar means scaled. Zoom a photo in or out and nothing is squashed. Every length is multiplied by the same number, so the angles stay the same.
  • Two angles are enough for triangles. The third angle is 180° minus the other two, so it is already decided. Matching two angles means matching all three.
  • Scale factors flip. If A to B is multiplied by 3, then B to A is divided by 3, which is multiplying by 1 over 3.
  • Area uses k². A rectangle has a length and a width, and both are multiplied by k. So the area is multiplied by k × k. For volume there are three lengths, so k × k × k.
  • Congruent is a special similar. A scale factor of 1 means no change in size: the shapes are identical.

Common traps

  • Checking only one pair of sides on a rectangle or other shape. Check all of them.
  • Dividing old by new. The scale factor is new ÷ old.
  • Adding a number to each side instead of multiplying by the scale factor.
  • Matching the wrong sides. Match the shortest to the shortest, and so on.
  • Forgetting to change units on a map or plan (cm to m to km).
  • Using k for an area or a volume. Areas use k² and volumes use k3.

Going further

A sheet of A4 paper is 29.7 cm by 21 cm. Fold it in half across its long side to make A5, which is 21 cm by 14.85 cm. For each sheet, work out the long side divided by the short side, to 2 decimal places. What do you notice? (This is why you can enlarge A5 to A4 on a photocopier without any gaps.)

  1. Work out 29.7 / 21 for A4, and 21 / 14.85 for A5.
  2. If the two answers are the same, what does that say about the two sheets?
  • That looks like short divided by long, 21 / 29.7. The question asks for the long side divided by the short side.

A4: 29.7 / 21 = 1.414… ≈ 1.41. A5: 21 / 14.85 = 1.414… ≈ 1.41. The ratio is the same, so the two sheets are similar. Each fold makes a smaller sheet of the same shape, and that works because the ratio of long to short is the square root of 2.

You stand 2 m from a mirror lying flat on the ground. The mirror is 12 m from the foot of a tree. You can just see the top of the tree in the mirror. Your eyes are 1.6 m above the ground. How tall is the tree, in m? (Light bounces off a flat mirror at the same angle it arrives, so the two triangles are similar.)

  1. Draw two right-angled triangles: one from your eyes to the mirror, one from the mirror to the top of the tree.
  2. The angles at the mirror are equal, so the triangles are similar. Find the scale factor from the distances along the ground.
  • That is your eye height, 1.6 m, which is the smaller triangle. The tree is the bigger triangle.
  • That looks like 12 / 2. That is the scale factor. Multiply it by your eye height.

The triangles are similar (equal angles at the mirror, and a right angle at the ground at each end). Scale factor = 12 / 2 = 6. Height of tree = 1.6 × 6 = 9.6 m.

Higher A cube has sides of 4 cm. A second cube has exactly twice the volume. By what scale factor are the side lengths multiplied? Give your answer to 2 decimal places. (Is it 2? Check what doubling the sides does to the volume.)

  1. Doubling the sides multiplies the volume by 2 × 2 × 2 = 8, not 2.
  2. You need a scale factor k with k × k × k = 2. Use the cube root.
  • That is the scale factor for doubling the sides, which makes the volume 8 times bigger. You need a smaller scale factor.
  • That is the square root of 2. For volumes, use the cube root.

The volume scale factor is 2, so k3 = 2. k = the cube root of 2 = 1.2599… ≈ 1.26. The new side is 4 × 1.26 = 5.04 cm.
