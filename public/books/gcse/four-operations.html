Add, subtract, multiply, divide

The four operations with whole numbers and decimals, how each one undoes another, and how to choose the right one in a real problem.

GCSE Maths Book / Add, subtract, multiply, divide

Before you start: what this stands on

Maths is built like a pyramid, each topic standing on the ones below. This topic needs:

  • Place value Number

The "Last time" questions below are a quick check of that base. If they feel shaky, build the base first.

Warm-up: what do you already know?

Have a go before you read anything. The last three are from Place value, the layer this stands on.

A pack has 24 cards. How many cards are in 6 packs?

  1. Six lots of 24.
  2. 6 × 20 and 6 × 4, then add.
  • That is 24 + 6. The question has 6 packs of 24, so multiply.

6 × 24 = 120 + 24 = 144.

Last time: what is the value of the 4 in 3,412?

  1. Units 2, tens 1, hundreds 4.
  • That is the digit. Its value depends on its column.

The 4 is in the hundreds column: 400.

Round 4,862 to the nearest 100.

  1. Look at the tens digit (6) after the hundreds.
  • The 6 rounds up.

6 rounds up, so 4,900.

Work out 3.4 × 10.

  1. Move each digit one place left.
  • That is the same as 3.4. Multiplying by 10 makes it bigger.

3.4 × 10 = 34.

1. Adding and subtracting

The sum of two numbers is what you get by adding them. The difference is what you get by subtracting the smaller from the larger. Both written methods use columns, so line up the place values: units under units, tens under tens, and the decimal points under each other.

Key idea: start at the right-hand column. When a column adds to 10 or more, carry into the next one. When you cannot take away, borrow from the next column. Keep the digits tidy and in line, because most mistakes are untidiness.

Worked example: add

Work out 4,578 + 3,964.

  1. Units: 8 + 4 = 12. Write 2, carry 1.
  2. Tens: 7 + 6 + 1 = 14. Write 4, carry 1.
  3. Hundreds: 5 + 9 + 1 = 15. Write 5, carry 1.
  4. Thousands: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8.
  5. 4,578 + 3,964 = 8,542.

Now you finish it: subtract

Work out 6,003 − 2,458.

  1. Units: 3 − 8 cannot be done. Borrow from the tens, but the tens and hundreds are zero, so borrow from the thousands: 6,003 becomes 5 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens and 13 units.
  2. 13 − 8 = 5, 9 − 5 = 4, 9 − 4 = 5, 5 − 2 = 3.
  3. So the answer is ___.

3,545. Check by adding: 3,545 + 2,458 = 6,003.

Check your knowledge: add and subtract

Work out 4,578 + 3,964.

  1. Units first: 8 + 4 = 12.
  • You missed a carry. 8 + 4 = 12: write 2 and carry the 1 into the tens, which then add to 14.

8,542 (carry 1 three times).

Work out 6,003 − 2,458.

  1. Borrow across the zeros, or count up from 2,458 to 6,003.
  • That subtracts the smaller digit from the bigger in each column. When the top digit is smaller you must borrow.

6,003 − 2,458 = 3,545. Check: 3,545 + 2,458 = 6,003.

Work out 12.7 + 8.45.

  1. Line up the points: 12.70 + 8.45.
  • You missed a carry: tenths 7 + 4 = 11, write 1 and carry 1 into the units.
  • Line up the decimal points, not the right-hand ends: 12.70 and 8.45.

12.70 + 8.45 = 21.15.

Work out 50 − 17.38.

  1. Write 50 as 50.00 and subtract.
  • Check by adding back: 33.62 + 17.38 = 51.

50.00 − 17.38 = 32.62. Check: 32.62 + 17.38 = 50.

What is the Difference between 83 and 127?

  1. Difference means subtract the smaller from the larger.
  • That is the sum. The difference is how far apart they are.

127 − 83 = 44.

2. Multiplying

The product of two numbers is the answer when you multiply them. For bigger numbers, split one number into easy pieces (partitioning), multiply each, then add.

Key idea: multiplying a decimal is the same as multiplying the whole numbers, then putting the point back. Count the decimal places in the question and give the answer the same number in total.

Worked example

Work out 36 × 24.

  1. Split 24 into 20 + 4.
  2. 36 × 20 = 720 and 36 × 4 = 144.
  3. 720 + 144 = 864.

Worked example: decimals

Work out 0.3 × 0.4.

  1. Ignore the points: 3 × 4 = 12.
  2. There is 1 decimal place in 0.3 and 1 in 0.4, so 2 in the answer.
  3. 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12.

Sense check: multiplying by a number less than 1 makes things smaller, so the answer must be less than 0.3 (and less than 0.4).

Check your knowledge: multiply

Work out 47 × 6.

  1. 40 × 6 and 7 × 6.
  • 7 × 6 = 42, not 12. Try 240 + 42.

240 + 42 = 282.

Work out 124 × 5.

  1. Half of 124 × 10.
  • That leaves out 4 × 5 = 20: 500 + 100 + 20 = 620.

124 × 10 = 1,240, and half of that is 620.

Work out 36 × 24.

  1. Split 24 into 20 + 4.
  • That is only 36 × 4. You also need 36 × 20.
  • That is only 36 × 20. You also need 36 × 4.

720 + 144 = 864.

Work out 3.2 × 4.

  1. 32 × 4, then put the point back.
  • Put the decimal point back: there is one decimal place in 3.2.

32 × 4 = 128. One decimal place in the question, so 12.8.

Work out 0.3 × 0.4.

  1. 3 × 4 = 12. Two decimal places in total.
  • There are two decimal places in the question (one in each number), so two in the answer.
  • Put the decimal point back: two decimal places in total.

0.12.

3. Dividing

The quotient is the answer to a division. When a number does not divide exactly, what is left over is the remainder. Use short division when you can, laying the digits out so you can see each step.

Key idea: division asks “how many lots of this fit in?” or “share equally between this many”. To divide by a decimal, make the divisor whole by multiplying both numbers by the same power of ten: 3.6 ÷ 0.4 = 36 ÷ 4.

Worked example

Work out 1,456 ÷ 7.

  1. How many 7s in 14? 2. Write 2 above the 4 (the thousands and hundreds 14).
  2. 5 ÷ 7 does not go (0), so carry 5 into the next digit: 56 ÷ 7 = 8.
  3. 1,456 ÷ 7 = 208.

Check by multiplying: 7 × 208 = 1,456. ✓

Check your knowledge: divide

Work out 252 ÷ 6.

  1. 25 ÷ 6 = 4 remainder 1.
  • Carry the remainder: 25 ÷ 6 = 4 remainder 1, then 12 ÷ 6 = 2.

252 ÷ 6 = 42. Check: 6 × 42 = 252.

Work out 1,456 ÷ 7.

  1. 14 ÷ 7 = 2, then 5, then 56.
  • Watch the zero: 5 ÷ 7 does not go, so there is a 0 in the answer.

208. Check: 7 × 208 = 1,456.

What is the remainder when 95 is divided by 4?

  1. 4 × 23 = 92.
  • That is the quotient (how many whole 4s). The remainder is what is left over.

4 × 23 = 92 and 95 − 92 = 3.

Write 95 ÷ 4 as a decimal.

  1. 23 remainder 3: the 3 is three quarters.
  • The remainder 3 out of 4 is 0.75, not 0.3.

95 ÷ 4 = 23.75.

Work out 3.6 ÷ 0.4.

  1. Multiply both numbers by 10: 36 ÷ 4.
  • Multiply both numbers by 10 to make the divisor whole: 36 ÷ 4.

36 ÷ 4 = 9.

Flip it: every operation has a partner that undoes it

So far you have gone one way. Now run it backwards: this is where much of the real understanding comes from.

Forwards

342 + 289 = 631

Add to build up.

Backwards

631 − 289 = 342

Subtract to undo it.

Key idea: add and subtract are inverse operations, and so are multiply and divide. This lets you check any answer by reversing it, and find a missing number by undoing the steps.

Forwards

24 × 6 = 144

Lots of 24.

Backwards

144 ÷ 6 = 24, and 144 ÷ 24 = 6

One multiplication gives you two divisions.

Check your knowledge: flip it

You know 342 + 289 = 631. What is 631 − 289?

  1. Subtracting undoes adding.
  • That is 631 + 289. Subtract to undo the addition.

631 − 289 = 342: you get back to where you started.

I think of a number, multiply it by 7 and get 91. What is my number?

  1. Undo × 7 with ÷ 7.
  • That is 91 × 7. To undo a multiplication, divide.

91 ÷ 7 = 13. Check: 13 × 7 = 91.

46 × ? = 1,196. Find the missing number.

  1. Undo with division: 1,196 ÷ 46.
  • That is 46 × 1,196. Divide to find the missing factor.

1,196 ÷ 46 = 26. Check: 46 × 26 = 1,196.

? − 138 = 267. Find the missing number.

  1. The missing number is bigger. Undo − 138 with + 138.
  • That is 267 − 138. To undo a subtraction, add.

267 + 138 = 405. Check: 405 − 138 = 267.

? ÷ 8 = 15. Find the missing number.

  1. Undo ÷ 8 with × 8.
  • That is 15 ÷ 8 (about 2). To undo a division, multiply.

15 × 8 = 120.

You know 24 × 6 = 144. Write the answer to 144 ÷ 24.

  1. One multiplication gives two divisions.
  • Look at which factor is left over after you divide by 24.

144 ÷ 24 = 6, because 24 × 6 = 144.

4. Using it in real situations

In a problem, first decide which operation. Words can give clues (“altogether” often means adding, “change” subtracting, “shared equally” dividing), but keywords can mislead: “each” can mean multiply or divide. Picture what is happening first. Then do a quick estimate to check the size of your answer.

Key idea: if the answer is a number of whole things (people, buses, boxes), a remainder usually means you need one more. 70 students in minibuses that each hold 16 need 5 minibuses, not 4.375.

Check your knowledge: problems

Four tickets cost £8.50 each, and there is a £3 booking fee on the whole order. What is the total cost in pounds?

  1. Four tickets first: 4 × 8.50.
  2. Then add the booking fee once.
  • That adds instead of multiplying. There are four tickets at £8.50 each.
  • That charges the £3 fee on each ticket: 4 × 11. It is charged once on the whole order.
  • That leaves out the booking fee. Add the £3 once.

4 × 8.50 = 34, and 34 + 3 = £37.

156 sweets are shared equally between 12 children. How many sweets does each child get?

  1. Shared equally means divide.
  • That is 156 + 12. Sharing means dividing.

156 ÷ 12 = 13. Check: 12 × 13 = 156.

70 students go on a trip. Each minibus holds 16 students. How many minibuses are needed?

  1. 70 ÷ 16 is a bit more than 4.
  2. You cannot have part of a minibus.
  • 4 minibuses hold 64 students, so 6 would be left behind.
  • That is the exact division, but you cannot hire part of a minibus.

70 ÷ 16 = 4.375, so 4 minibuses would leave some students behind. You need 5.

A bag holds 1.5 kg of flour. How many 250 g portions can you make? (1 kg = 1,000 g.)

  1. Change to grams first: 1.5 kg = 1,500 g.
  • That divides 1.5 by 250. Change both to the same unit first: 1,500 g ÷ 250 g.
  • That multiplies. You want to know how many 250 g portions fit, so divide.

1,500 ÷ 250 = 6.

Jo saves £12.50 each week. How many weeks does it take her to save at least £100?

  1. 100 ÷ 12.50 = 8 exactly.
  • 7 weeks gives only £87.50.

12.50 × 8 = 100, so 8 weeks.

Spot the mistake

Mia says: “6,003 − 2,458 = 4,455.” (She did each column by taking the smaller digit from the bigger.) What went wrong?

  • She took the smaller digit from the bigger each time instead of borrowing when the top digit was smaller.
  • Nothing: it is right
  • She should have added
  1. Check by adding back: 4,455 + 2,458.

The correct answer is 3,545. Check: 3,545 + 2,458 = 6,003.

Tom says: “3.5 × 0.2 = 7.” What went wrong?

  • He forgot the decimal point. 35 × 2 = 70, with two decimal places: 0.70 = 0.7.
  • Nothing: 35 × 2 = 70, so 7 is right
  • He should have divided
  1. Estimate: 3.5 is about 4, 0.2 is a fifth. A fifth of 4 is a bit less than 1.

0.7. A fifth of 3.5 is 0.7.

Check your knowledge: mixed review

Work out 52 − 17 + 8.

  1. Work from left to right.
  • That does 17 + 8 first. With only + and − you go left to right.

52 − 17 = 35, and 35 + 8 = 43.

Work out 3.2 + 0.85.

  1. 3.20 + 0.85.
  • Line up the decimal points: 3.20 + 0.85.

4.05.

Work out 125 × 8.

  1. Half of 125 × 16, or 100 × 8 + 25 × 8.
  • Check: 25 × 8 = 200, so 800 + 200.

800 + 200 = 1,000.

Work out 432 ÷ 12.

  1. 12 × 30 = 360, then how many more 12s?
  • That is only the first part. 432 − 360 = 72, and 72 ÷ 12 = 6 more.

12 × 36 = 432.

A box holds 12 eggs. How many boxes are needed for 100 eggs?

  1. 100 ÷ 12 = 8 remainder 4.
  • 8 boxes hold only 96 eggs.
  • You cannot buy part of a box.

8 boxes hold 96 eggs. 4 eggs are left over, so you need 9 boxes.

Exam style

Concert tickets cost £12.50 for an adult and £7.25 for a child. A group buys tickets for 2 adults and 3 children.

(a) [3 marks] Work out the total cost, in pounds.

  1. Adults: 2 × 12.50. Children: 3 × 7.25.
  2. Add the two totals.
  • That adds one adult and one child price. There are 2 adults and 3 children.
  • That is only the children. Add the adults too.

2 × 12.50 = 25 and 3 × 7.25 = 21.75. 25 + 21.75 = £46.75.

(b) [2 marks] They pay with a £50 note. How much change do they get, in pounds?

  1. Change = 50 − the total.
  • That adds. Change is what is left from the £50.

50 − 46.75 = £3.25. Check: 46.75 + 3.25 = 50.

(c) [3 marks] A coach has 52 seats. 4 coaches are completely full and a fifth has 37 people. How many people are there altogether?

  1. 4 full coaches: 4 × 52.
  2. Then add the 37.
  • That is 52 + 37. There are 4 full coaches.

4 × 52 = 208 and 208 + 37 = 245.

Going further

Use the digits 2, 3 and 4 once each to make a two-digit number multiplied by a one-digit number. What is the largest product you can make?

  1. There are only six ways to arrange 2, 3 and 4. Try them all and compare.
  • Putting the biggest digits together does not give the biggest product. Try all six.

43 × 2 = 86, 42 × 3 = 126, 32 × 4 = 128, 34 × 2 = 68, 24 × 3 = 72, 23 × 4 = 92. The largest is 32 × 4 = 128. Putting the biggest digit (4) on its own as the multiplier wins.

You know 4,872 ÷ 24 = 203. What is 203 × 24?

  1. Multiplying undoes dividing.
  • That is 203 + 24. Multiplying by 24 undoes dividing by 24.

203 × 24 = 4,872, the number you started with.

Why it works

  • Carrying and borrowing are just place value: ten units become one ten, and one ten becomes ten units.
  • Partitioning works because multiplication spreads over addition: 36 × 24 = 36 × 20 + 36 × 4.
  • Inverse operations undo each other, so every answer can be checked by running it backwards.

Common traps

  • Not lining up place values or decimal points.
  • Taking the smaller digit from the bigger in every column.
  • Losing the decimal point after multiplying.
  • Forgetting the zero in an answer such as 208.
  • Giving 4.375 minibuses when a whole number was needed.
