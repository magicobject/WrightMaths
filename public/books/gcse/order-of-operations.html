Order of operations

Why a calculation has to be read in a particular order, how brackets and powers change it, and how to run it backwards.

GCSE Maths Book / Order of operations

Before you start: what this stands on

Maths is built like a pyramid, each topic standing on the ones below. This topic needs:

  • Add, subtract, multiply, divide Number

The "Last time" questions below are a quick check of that base. If they feel shaky, build the base first.

Words you need

Tap a teal word anywhere on the page to see this again.

BIDMAS
The agreed order for working out a calculation: Brackets, Indices (powers), Division and Multiplication together, then Addition and Subtraction together. Some people call it BODMAS or PEMDAS.
Operation
A mathematical process such as add, subtract, multiply or divide. Squaring and square-rooting are operations too.
Power (index)
A small raised number that says how many times to multiply a number by itself. The plural of index is indices.
Square root
The positive number that, multiplied by itself, gives the number you started with. Written with the sign √.

Quick warm-up. No marks.

A gamer starts with 5 coins and wins 2 coins on each of 6 levels. The total is 5 + 2 × 6. How many coins is that?

  1. Is it (5 + 2) × 6 or 5 + (2 × 6)? Everyone must get the same answer.
  • You added first: 7 × 6. Multiply before you add.

Multiplication comes before addition: 2 × 6 = 12 coins won, then 5 + 12 = 17.

Work out 6 × 7.

  1. Six groups of seven.
  • That is 6 × 6. Count six groups of seven.
  • You added 6 + 7. This is a multiplication.

6 × 7 = 42.

Work out 56 − 19.

  1. 56 − 20 = 36, then add 1 back.
  • That takes away only 9. Take away 20, then add 1 back.

56 − 19 = 37.

Work out 90 ÷ 6.

  1. 6 × 15 = 90.
  • 6 × 14 = 84, not 90. Try 6 × 15.
  • 6 × 16 = 96, not 90. Try 6 × 15.

90 ÷ 6 = 15.

1. Why there has to be an order

What is 3 + 4 × 2? One person adds first and gets 14. Another multiplies first and gets 11. Maths must mean the same to everybody. So there is an agreed order for the operations. It is a convention: people agreed it, so that everyone gets the same answer from the same calculation (it is not a law of nature). The order is BIDMAS:

Brackets first
Indices (powers) next
Division and Multiplication together
Addition and Subtraction together

Key idea: × and ÷ are equal. + and − are equal. Equal? Go left to right.

10 − 2 + 3 = 8 + 3 = 11

8 ÷ 2 × 4 = 4 × 4 = 16

Try it: work out 2 + 3 × 4.

Worked example

Work out 20 − 6 ÷ 2.

  1. Divide before you subtract: 6 ÷ 2 = 3.
  2. 20 − 3 = 17.

A scientific calculator gives 17 too. If yours gives 7, it works left to right only.

Check your knowledge: the order

Work out 7 + 2 × 4.

  1. Multiply first.
  • You added first: 9 × 4. Multiply before you add.

2 × 4 = 8, then 7 + 8 = 15.

Work out 20 − 6 ÷ 2.

  1. Divide first.
  • You subtracted first. Divide before you subtract.

6 ÷ 2 = 3, then 20 − 3 = 17.

A player has 10 lives, loses 2 and then wins 3 back. Work out 10 − 2 + 3.

  1. Same priority: work left to right.
  • You did 2 + 3 first. Go left to right: 10 − 2 first.

10 − 2 = 8, then 8 + 3 = 11.

Work out 8 ÷ 2 × 4.

  1. Same priority: left to right.
  • You did 2 × 4 first. Go left to right: 8 ÷ 2 first.

8 ÷ 2 = 4, then 4 × 4 = 16.

A playlist has 5 songs. You add 6 albums of 3 songs each, then delete 4 songs. Work out 5 + 6 × 3 − 4.

  1. Multiplication first: 6 × 3.
  2. Then left to right.
  • You added 5 + 6 first. Multiply first.

6 × 3 = 18, then 5 + 18 = 23, then 23 − 4 = 19.

2. Brackets and powers

Brackets mean “do this first”. They change the normal order.

(3 + 4) × 2 = 7 × 2 = 14

A fraction line works like brackets. Do the top first. A square root sign works the same way: work out what is under it first.

Indices (powers) come next, before multiplying.

3 + 2² = 3 + 4 = 7

(3 + 2)² = 5² = 25

Key idea: a power belongs only to the number just before it. In 2 × 3² the square belongs to the 3. So 3² = 9 first, then 2 × 9 = 18. To square both numbers, use brackets: (2 × 3)² = 36.

Try it: work out 4 + 3².

Worked example

Work out 5 + (6 − 2)² × 3.

  1. Brackets: 6 − 2 = 4.
  2. Indices: 4² = 16.
  3. Multiply: 16 × 3 = 48.
  4. Add: 5 + 48 = 53.

Now you finish it: a fraction bar

Work out 12 + 8 over 4.

  1. The fraction line works like brackets. Do the top first: 12 + 8 = 20.
  2. Then 20 ÷ 4 = ___.
Check my answer

20 ÷ 4 = 5. Without the line, 12 + 8 ÷ 4 would be 12 + 2 = 14. The line changes the order.

Check your knowledge: brackets and powers

Zoe mixes 2 batches of paint. Each batch has 5 ml of red and 3 ml of blue. Work out 2 × (5 + 3), the total ml of paint.

  1. Brackets first: 5 + 3.
  • You did 2 × 5 + 3. Do the brackets first.

5 + 3 = 8, then 2 × 8 = 16.

Work out (12 − 4) ÷ 2.

  1. Brackets first.
  • You did 4 ÷ 2 first. Brackets come first.

12 − 4 = 8, then 8 ÷ 2 = 4.

Work out 3 + 2².

  1. Powers before adding: 2² = 4.
  • You squared 3 + 2. The square belongs only to the 2.
  • You added first and then doubled. 2² means 2 × 2, added to 3.

2² = 4, then 3 + 4 = 7.

Work out (3 + 2)².

  1. Brackets first: 3 + 2 = 5.
  • You squared only the 2. The brackets mean add first.
  • You doubled 5. Squaring is 5 × 5.

5² = 25.

Work out 2 × 3².

  1. Square the 3 first.
  • You squared 2 × 3. The square belongs only to the 3.
  • You worked out 3 × 2 = 6 for the square. 3² = 3 × 3 = 9.

3² = 9, then 2 × 9 = 18.

Work out 12 + 8 over 4.

  1. The top of a fraction is worked out first.
  • You did 8 ÷ 4 first. The fraction line groups 12 + 8.

12 + 8 = 20, then 20 ÷ 4 = 5.

Flip it: build it and unpick it

So far you have gone one way. Now run it backwards. Same idea, other direction.

Forwards

Given 2 + 3 × 4, work out the answer: 14.

Follow the rules to get an answer.

Backwards

Given the answer 20 and the numbers 2, 3 and 4, put in brackets to make it work: (2 + 3) × 4 = 20.

Choose the brackets (or operations) to hit a target.

Key idea: you can go forwards (work out the answer). You can go backwards (decide where the brackets go, or find a missing number). Going backwards also checks a calculation.

For a missing number, undo the operations in the opposite order. For 3 × (? + 2) = 21: divide 21 by 3, then take 2 away.

Check your knowledge: flip it

Where must the brackets go to make 2 + 3 × 4 = 20?

  • 2 + (3 × 4)
  • (2 + 3) × 4
  • 2 + 3 × (4)
  1. You need the addition done first.

(2 + 3) × 4 = 5 × 4 = 20.

Which operation makes 6 ? 2 + 3 = 6 true?

  • +
  • −
  • ÷
  • ×
  1. Try each one in turn, using the order of operations.

6 ÷ 2 + 3 = 3 + 3 = 6.

3 × (? + 2) = 21. Find the missing number.

  1. Undo the × 3 first: 21 ÷ 3.
  2. Then undo the + 2.
  • That is 21 ÷ 3, the whole bracket. Now take away 2.
  • That is 21 − 2. Undo the × 3 first.

21 ÷ 3 = 7, and 7 − 2 = 5. Check: 3 × (5 + 2) = 21.

? ÷ 4 + 1 = 6. Find the missing number.

  1. Undo the + 1 first: 6 − 1.
  2. Then undo the ÷ 4.
  • You undid ÷ 4 but forgot to undo the + 1.
  • That is 6 − 1. Now undo ÷ 4.

6 − 1 = 5, and 5 × 4 = 20. Check: 20 ÷ 4 + 1 = 6.

Leo works out the total for a relay kit as 9 + 18 ÷ 3 and gets 9. What should the answer be?

  1. Divide first.
  • That is Leo's answer: 9 + 18 first.

18 ÷ 3 = 6, then 9 + 6 = 15. Leo added first (27 ÷ 3 = 9).

Spot the mistake

Ali says: “6 + 4 × 2 = 20.” What went wrong?

  • He added first, not multiplied first.
  • Nothing is wrong: 6 + 4 = 10, then 10 × 2 = 20.
  • He should have subtracted, not added, in the first step.
  1. Which operation has priority?

He added first, not multiplied first. 4 × 2 = 8, then 6 + 8 = 14.

Bea says: “12 ÷ 3 × 2 = 2.” What went wrong?

  • She did 3 × 2 first.
  • Nothing is wrong: × always comes before ÷, so 3 × 2 first.
  • She should have added the numbers before dividing.
  1. Equal priority means left to right.

She did 3 × 2 first. ÷ and × are equal, so go left to right: 12 ÷ 3 = 4, then 4 × 2 = 8.

Cal says: “3² = 6.” What went wrong?

  • He doubled the 3.
  • Nothing is wrong: 3 × 2 = 6, so the answer is right.
  • It should be 3 + 2, which is 5.
  1. What does the small 2 mean?

He doubled the 3. 3² = 3 × 3 = 9: the little 2 says how many 3s to multiply.

Check your knowledge: mixed review

Work out 7 + 3 × (8 − 2).

  1. Brackets first, then multiply, then add.
  • You added 7 + 3 first. Multiply before you add.

8 − 2 = 6, 3 × 6 = 18, 7 + 18 = 25.

Work out 50 − 3² × 2.

  1. Power, then multiply, then subtract.
  • You did 50 − 9 first. Multiply before you subtract.

3² = 9, 9 × 2 = 18, 50 − 18 = 32.

Work out 2² + 3².

  1. Square each, then add.
  • That is (2 + 3)². Square each number on its own.

4 + 9 = 13.

Work out (10 − 4)² ÷ 3.

  1. Brackets, then the power, then divide.
  • You did 6 ÷ 3 first. Powers come before division.

10 − 4 = 6, 6² = 36, 36 ÷ 3 = 12.

Work out 36 ÷ (2 + 4) × 3.

  1. Brackets first.
  2. Then left to right.
  • You did 6 × 3 first. Go left to right: 36 ÷ 6.
  • That is 36 ÷ 6. Now multiply by 3.
  • You ignored the brackets. Do 2 + 4 first.

2 + 4 = 6, 36 ÷ 6 = 6, 6 × 3 = 18.

Exam style

No calculator. Marks are given for the right order.

Write your working on paper, then type the answer.

(a) [1 mark] Work out 3 + 4 × 5².

  1. Power first, then multiply, then add.
  • You added 3 + 4 first. Power, then multiply, then add.

5² = 25, 4 × 25 = 100, 3 + 100 = 103.

(b) [2 marks] Work out (12 − 3)² ÷ 3.

  1. Brackets: 12 − 3.
  • That is only the bracket. Now square it and divide by 3.
  • That stops before dividing by 3.

9² = 81 and 81 ÷ 3 = 27.

  • M1 for the bracket first: 12 − 3 = 9, then 9² = 81
  • A1 for 81 ÷ 3 = 27

(c) [2 marks] Sam works out 6 + 18 ÷ 3 and gets 8. What is the correct answer?

  1. Divide before you add.
  • That is Sam's answer: adding first.

18 ÷ 3 = 6, then 6 + 6 = 12.

  • M1 for dividing first: 18 ÷ 3 = 6
  • A1 for 6 + 6 = 12

Going further

Work out 2 + 10 over 3 + 4 × 2.

  1. Top of the fraction first: 2 + 10 = 12.
  2. Then 12 ÷ 3, then add 4 × 2.
  • The top is 12. 12 over 3 = 4, not 12.

12 over 3 = 4 and 4 × 2 = 8. 4 + 8 = 12.

Work out 2 × 3² ÷ 6 + (−1).

  1. Power, then left to right. Adding (−1) is the same as subtracting 1.
  • Adding −1 takes 1 away. It does not add 1.
  • You did not square the 3. 3² = 9, so 2 × 9 = 18.

3² = 9, 2 × 9 = 18, 18 ÷ 6 = 3, 3 + (−1) = 2.

A classic puzzle: use four 4s, once each, with the usual operations and brackets, to make a target number. For example, 4 × 4 + 4 ÷ 4 = 17. Which sign goes in the square brackets to make 4 × 4 [ ] (4 ÷ 4) = 15?

  • +
  • −
  • ×
  • ÷
  1. Is 15 bigger or smaller than 4 × 4?
  2. What is 4 ÷ 4? So 4 × 4 has to change by that much, in which direction?
  3. Try each sign in the gap. Brackets first: (4 ÷ 4) = 1, then 16 and 1 with each sign.

(4 ÷ 4) = 1 and 4 × 4 = 16. To get 15, take 1 away: 4 × 4 − (4 ÷ 4) = 16 − 1 = 15, so the sign is −. Can you make 7, or 0, or 8 with four 4s too?

Negatives, powers and hidden brackets Higher

Two places where the order catches people out.

A minus sign and a power. The power belongs to the number just before it, so in −3² the square belongs to the 3 only: −3² = −(3²) = −9. With brackets, (−3)² = (−3) × (−3) = 9. Calculator note: some calculators treat the minus sign as part of the number and give 9 for −3², and others give −9. Always type the brackets, (−3)², so it does not matter which kind you have.

Hidden brackets. A fraction line groups its top and bottom. On one line, the same thing needs brackets. 1 over 2x means half of x, which is (1/2)x = x/2. But typed as 1/2x, some calculators and people read it as 1/(2x), which is different. When it is not a fraction layout, put the brackets in.

Work out −3², using the standard order of operations.

  1. Which number does the little 2 belong to: the 3, or the −3?
  2. Do the power first, then apply the minus sign.
  3. 3² = 9. Now put the minus in front.
  • That is (−3)², with brackets. Without brackets the square belongs only to the 3, and then the minus is applied.
  • That is −3 × 2. The power means 3 × 3.

The power comes before the minus sign: 3² = 9, so −3² = −9.

Work out −2² + (−2)².

  1. Are the two parts the same, or does one have brackets?
  2. Work out each part on its own. Power first in each.
  3. −2² = −4 and (−2)² = 4. Now add them.
  • You treated both as +4. Without brackets, −2² = −4.
  • You treated both as −4. With brackets, (−2)² = (−2) × (−2) = 4.

−2² = −4 and (−2)² = 4. −4 + 4 = 0.

When x = 6, work out the value of (1/2)x (half of x).

  1. What does “half of x” mean when x is 6?
  2. Put 6 in for x and do the bracket first.
  3. (1/2) × 6 = ?
  • That is 1/(2x), with 2x on the bottom. The brackets in (1/2)x group only the half.
  • That doubles x. A half of 6 is smaller than 6.

(1/2) × 6 = 3. (By contrast 1/(2x) would be 1/12, which is why the brackets matter.)

Why it works

  • An agreed order means everyone gets the same answer from the same calculation, on paper and on a calculator.
  • Brackets, fraction bars and root signs all say “do this part first”.
  • Equal priority means left to right: division and multiplication share a level, and so do addition and subtraction.

Common traps

  • Working only from left to right.
  • Doing all the multiplication before any division. They are equal.
  • Squaring more than the number just before the power.
  • Forgetting that a fraction bar acts like brackets.
  • Undoing steps in the wrong order when finding a missing number.
