    How to use this book

    Short answers to the questions people ask most: how to hear a question read out, how to type an answer, and how to get help when you are stuck.

    How to type answers Back to the book
    GCSE Maths Book / How to use this book

    The short version: read the question, type your answer, press Check. If you are stuck, press “Give me a clue”. Nothing you do is sent anywhere.

    Hearing a question: the Listen button

    • Press Listen next to a question to hear it read out. Press it again to stop.
    • The first time, the voice can take a moment to load. Wait until it has loaded.
    • To change the speed, open Reading options and pick Slow, Normal or Fast.
    • Do not want the Listen buttons? In Reading options, tick Hide the Listen buttons. The book remembers your choice on this device.

    Reading options

    Open Reading options near the top of a page. You can:

    • make the text bigger or smaller (A+ and A−);
    • turn on Easy-read font, a plainer and more spaced-out letter style;
    • turn on Tinted page, a soft background colour that is gentler on the eyes;
    • turn on Reading ruler, a band that follows your mouse or finger so you keep your place;
    • choose the Listen speed, and hide the Listen buttons.

    The book remembers your choices on this device. Reset puts everything back.

    When you are stuck: the clue ladder

    1. Press Give me a clue. You get Clue A. Press again for Clue B, and so on. Each clue gives away a little more.
    2. Type what you know so far, even half an answer. Writing it down often shows you the next step.
    3. Or write what you know on paper, or say it quietly in your head.
    4. Last of all, you can see the answer and the worked solution. You always do the last step yourself.

    Getting a clue is not cheating. It is how you learn.

    How to type your answer

    The answer box accepts a number or a short expression. You do not need a special keyboard.

    What to type
    You wantType
    A decimal5.60 (use a point, not a comma)
    A negative number-2 (a minus sign, then the number)
    A fraction3/4 (the numerator, a slash, then the denominator)
    A powerx^2 for x squared, 10^3 for 10 cubed
    A square rootsqrt(3), or sqrt3. So 2 root 3 is 2sqrt3
    Pipi, so 10 times pi is 10pi
    Plus or minus+- (a plus sign and a minus sign together)
    A percentage35% (only when the question asks for a percentage)
    A unitYou can leave it off. If you add one, it must match the question, for example 12 cm
    • Many questions end with “Type it like …”. Follow that example.
    • On a phone, the small keys under the box type −, / , . and % for you.
    • Money: type 84 or 84.00 for £84. You do not need the pound sign.

    If you use a comma as a decimal point (as in 5,60), the book still reads it as 5.60 and reminds you to use a point.

    Marks and ticks: saved on this device only

    The book remembers which questions you finished, your self-marked scores and your reading options. They are kept in this browser, on this device. They are not sent to anyone. If you share the device, the person who uses it next can see them. The “Clear my ticks on this device” button on the front page wipes them.

    For questions with a mark scheme, write your answer on paper first, press I have written my answer: show me the mark scheme, then tick each mark you earned.

    Timed sets

    On the Foundation and Stretch pages you can start a timed set of five questions.

    • Before you start, choose No clock to practise without time. You still get marks and ticks.
    • Choose Extra time (+25%) if you get extra time in your exams.
    • You can hide the clock while it runs, and add five minutes if you need more time.

    Using a screen reader

    • Every page has headings in order. Jump from heading to heading to find a topic, a section or a question.
    • The search box at the top of each page is a combobox. Type, press the down arrow to move through the results, Enter to open one, and Escape to close the list.
    • Pictures and graphs have a Text version of the picture, read out where the picture is. It gives the numbers a sighted student would read from the picture.
    • When you press Check, the result (right, or a hint about what went wrong) is read out for you.
    • Press Escape to close the pop-up for a highlighted word. On the Listen button, the name changes to Stop while it reads.
    • There is a timed set with a No clock choice and extra time, so pressure does not have to be part of it.

    Keyboard

    • Everything can be done with Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter and Space. Press Enter in the answer box to Check.
    • Escape closes the search results and the word pop-ups.
    • In the search results, use the up and down arrows and Enter.

    Printing a worksheet

    On a topic page, press Print worksheet (questions only) for a sheet with a name line and room to write. Print with answers adds the worked solutions. There is also an answer key for every topic on the teachers’ page.

    Setting homework (for teachers and parents)

    Add ?q= and the question numbers to the end of a topic address, and the page shows only those questions. For example, percentages.html?q=5-12 shows questions 5 to 12, and percentages.html?q=1-3,40-42 shows 1 to 3 and 40 to 42. The teachers’ page has more.

    Words in the questions

    Some questions use everyday words that you may not know. Here are the common ones. The glossary has the maths words.

    UK money

    • Pence (p): 100p is £1.
    • Wage: pay for each hour or week worked. Salary: a fixed amount of pay for a year.
    • Profit: the money you take in, minus your costs. Loss: when your costs are more than the money you take in.
    • Discount: money taken off a price. Deposit: part of the price, paid at the start. Commission: extra pay worked out as a percentage of the sales you make. Tip: a thank-you amount added to a bill.
    • VAT: a tax added to many prices. Time and a half: pay at 1.5 times the normal rate.
    • Call-out fee: a fixed charge for a worker to come to your home.

    Building and trade words

    • Skirting board: the wooden strip along the bottom of a wall.
    • Joist: a beam under a floor. Stud wall: a wall with a wooden frame inside.
    • Turf: rolls of ready-grown grass for a lawn.
    • Ring and spur: kinds of electrical cable circuit in a house.

    Games, sport and shops

    • Raffle: a prize draw with numbered tickets.
    • 5-a-side: football with five players on each team.
    • Squash (the drink): a fruit drink you mix with water.
    • Sweets: small sugary things to eat (candy, in American English).

    Stuck on a word that is not here? Use the search box at the top of the page, or ask a teacher.
