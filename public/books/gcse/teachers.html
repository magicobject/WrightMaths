    For teachers

    Using the book in a classroom

    Every topic has a printable worksheet and a separate answer key. Students need no account, and nothing they do is sent to anyone.

    Jump to the topics table Back to the book
    GCSE Maths Book / For teachers

    What you can do with it

    • Print a worksheet. On any topic page, press “Print worksheet (questions only)”. The printout has a name line, the questions and room to write, and no teaching text, clues or solutions. “Print with answers” adds the worked solutions.
    • Print an answer key. The table below links to a key for every topic: question numbers match the page, with the answer, other accepted forms, and the mark scheme for each marked question.
    • Set questions by number. Add ?q=5-12 to the end of a topic page address (for example percentages.html?q=5-12) and the page shows only those questions. A question can also be linked directly, as percentages.html#q12. Numbers match the answer key.
    • Plan a term. The study routes give an 8-week resit plan, a 12-week plan for the whole book and a 6-week Higher top-up. The table below shows roughly how many 20-minute sessions each topic needs.
    • Set a section. Each section of a topic page ends with a short “Check your knowledge” set. Tell students which numbered section to do. The topic pages have an “On this page” list, so you can send students to a section by name.
    • Practise exam technique. Questions worth two marks or more have a mark scheme students tick for themselves (M for method, A for accuracy, B for independent marks, C for explaining). The Grade 7–9 stretch papers have a timed set. Marks students give themselves are stored only in their own browser.
    • Find the gaps. “Find my gaps” is an eleven-question check that suggests three starting topics. Results stay on the student’s own device. A student can copy a one-line summary to show you.
    • Check the maths. Add ?review=1 to the end of any topic page address to open every clue, wrong-answer message and solution. It saves nothing.

    What is not here

    There are no teacher accounts, class lists or dashboards, because nothing a student does leaves their device. That keeps students’ work private, but it also means you cannot see a student’s progress unless they show you. Ask students to copy the summary line from “Find my gaps” or to show the “Where you lost marks” list on the front page.

    The book does not yet cover the whole specification (see the front page for what is still to come), and it is written to follow the national curriculum content, not one exam board’s wording. The curriculum statement column below shows which statements each topic is built around.
