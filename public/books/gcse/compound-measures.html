    Speed, density and other compound measures

    Some measures are built from two others: speed is distance and time, density is mass and volume, pressure is force and area. Learn the three big formulae, use the triangle or rearrange them, change units, read distance-time graphs, and run each formula backwards.

    Where you will use this: journey times for deliveries, the mass of a steel plate or a slab of concrete, the pressure in a heating system, and the power, energy and cost of electrical kit.

    Words you need

    Tap a teal word anywhere on the page to see this again.

    Speed
    How far something travels in each unit of time. Speed = distance ÷ time.
    Density
    How much mass there is in each unit of volume. Density = mass ÷ volume.
    Pressure
    How hard a force pushes on each unit of area. Pressure = force ÷ area.
    Compound measure
    A measure made by combining two other measures, written with “per” or a division. Speed, density and pressure are compound measures.

    Units and typing

    Speed: km/h, m/s or mph. Density: g/cm3 or kg/m3. Pressure: N/m2 (newtons per square metre). Power: W or kW. Energy: J or kWh.

    Where a question gives the units, type the number only. Use a decimal for part of an hour: 30 minutes is 0.5 hours.

    Money answers have two decimal places, like 12.50. Type a rule such as d = s × t as s*t, and a fraction rule such as d/s.

    1. Speed

    A Compound measure is a measure made from two other measures. Speed is made from distance and time. It tells you how far you go in each unit of time. The word “per” means “divided by”: 60 miles per hour means 60 miles divided by 1 hour.

    speed = distance ÷ time    or    S = D ÷ T

    The units of speed come from the units you put in. Kilometres and hours give km/h. Metres and seconds give m/s. Miles and hours give mph.

    Using the triangle

    • Cover the letter you want to find. What is left tells you what to do.
    • Cover D: S and T are side by side, so multiply. D = S × T.
    • Cover S: D is over T, so divide. S = D ÷ T.
    • Cover T: D is over S, so divide. T = D ÷ S.

    Or rearrange the formula

    Start with S = D ÷ T. Multiply both sides by T to get D = S × T. Then divide both sides by S to get T = D ÷ S. The triangle is just a short way to remember these three.

    Key idea: the units must match. If the speed is in km/h, the time must be in hours. Change minutes to hours by dividing by 60: 30 minutes = 0.5 h, 15 minutes = 0.25 h, 45 minutes = 0.75 h.

    Worked example: finding speed

    A van travels 150 km in 2.5 hours. Find its average speed.

    1. S = D ÷ T = 150 ÷ 2.5 = 60.
    2. The average speed is 60 km/h.

    Worked example: finding distance

    A cyclist rides at 18 km/h for 2 hours 30 minutes. How far does she go?

    1. 2 hours 30 minutes = 2.5 hours.
    2. D = S × T = 18 × 2.5 = 45.
    3. She goes 45 km.

    Worked example: finding time

    A train travels 240 miles at 80 mph. How long does it take? Then find how many minutes it takes to go 40 miles at 60 mph.

    1. T = D ÷ S = 240 ÷ 80 = 3 hours.
    2. For the second part: T = 40 ÷ 60 = 0.666… hours.
    3. Change to minutes: 40 ÷ 60 × 60 = 40 minutes.

    Now you finish it

    A lorry travels 210 km in 3 hours 30 minutes. Find its average speed.

    1. 3 hours 30 minutes = ___ hours.
    2. S = D ÷ T = 210 ÷ ___ = ___ km/h.
    Check my answer

    3 hours 30 minutes = 3.5 hours. S = 210 ÷ 3.5 = 60 km/h.

    Check your knowledge: speed

    A car travels 180 miles in 4 hours. What is its average speed in mph?

    1. Speed = distance ÷ time.
    2. Work out 180 ÷ 4.
    • That looks like 180 × 4. Speed is distance divided by time.
    • That is 4 ÷ 180, the wrong way round. Divide the distance by the time.

    S = D ÷ T = 180 ÷ 4 = 45 mph.

    A runner goes at 12 m/s for 5 seconds. How far does she go? Answer in metres.

    1. You know the speed and the time, and you want the distance.
    2. D = S × T.
    • That looks like 12 ÷ 5. To find the distance, multiply speed by time.
    • That looks like 12 + 5. Multiply speed by time.

    D = S × T = 12 × 5 = 60 m.

    Which formula for time is correct?

    • time = distance ÷ speed
    • time = distance × speed

    1. Check with an easy example: 100 miles at 50 mph takes 2 hours.
    2. Which calculation turns 100 and 50 into 2?

    T = D ÷ S. For example 100 ÷ 50 = 2 hours.

    Use the triangle. A coach travels 90 km at 45 km/h. How many hours does it take?

    1. Cover the T in the triangle. What is left?
    2. D is above S, so divide D by S.
    • That looks like 90 × 45. Cover T and you see D over S, which means divide.
    • That is 45 ÷ 90, the wrong way round. Divide the distance by the speed.

    Cover T: T = D ÷ S = 90 ÷ 45 = 2 hours.

    A train travels at 75 mph for 3 hours. How far does it travel? Answer in miles.

    1. Distance = speed × time.
    2. Work out 75 × 3.
    • That is 75 ÷ 3. To find the distance, multiply speed by time.

    D = 75 × 3 = 225 miles.

    A cyclist rides 36 km in 1 hour 30 minutes. What is her average speed in km/h?

    1. Change 1 hour 30 minutes into hours. 30 minutes is half an hour.
    2. Divide 36 by the time in hours.
    • That looks like 36 × 1.5. Speed is distance divided by time.
    • That looks like 36 ÷ 1.3. 1 hour 30 minutes is 1.5 hours, not 1.3.

    1 hour 30 minutes = 1.5 hours. S = 36 ÷ 1.5 = 24 km/h.

    A delivery van drives 45 miles at 60 mph. How many minutes does the drive take?

    1. Find the time in hours first: distance ÷ speed.
    2. Then change hours into minutes by multiplying by 60.
    • That is the time in hours. The question asks for minutes, so multiply by 60.
    • That is 60 ÷ 45, the wrong way round. Divide the distance by the speed.

    T = 45 ÷ 60 = 0.75 hours. In minutes: 0.75 × 60 = 45 minutes.

    2. Average speed over a whole journey

    Real journeys have fast parts and slow parts. The Average speed is the speed you would need to keep up all the way to cover the same distance in the same time.

    average speed = total distance ÷ total time

    Key idea: do NOT just average the speeds. The time spent at each speed is different, so work out the total distance and the total time first. Include any stops in the total time.

    Worked example: two parts

    A van drives 60 km at 30 km/h, then 60 km at 60 km/h. Find the average speed for the whole journey.

    1. First part: T = 60 ÷ 30 = 2 hours.
    2. Second part: T = 60 ÷ 60 = 1 hour.
    3. Total distance = 60 + 60 = 120 km. Total time = 2 + 1 = 3 hours.
    4. Average speed = 120 ÷ 3 = 40 km/h.
    5. Notice it is not 45 km/h, the average of 30 and 60. The van spent longer going slowly.

    Now you finish it

    A cyclist rides 20 km at 10 km/h, then 40 km at 40 km/h. Find the average speed.

    1. First part: T = 20 ÷ 10 = ___ hours. Second part: T = 40 ÷ 40 = ___ hour.
    2. Total distance = ___ km. Total time = ___ hours.
    3. Average speed = ___ ÷ ___ = ___ km/h.
    Check my answer

    Times: 2 hours and 1 hour. Total distance = 60 km, total time = 3 hours. Average speed = 60 ÷ 3 = 20 km/h.

    Check your knowledge: average speed

    A walker goes 6 km in 3 hours, then 4 km in 1 more hour. What is her average speed for the whole walk? Answer in km/h.

    1. Add the distances. Add the times.
    2. Divide the total distance by the total time.
    • That is the total distance. Divide by the total time, 4 hours.
    • That is the mean of 2 km/h and 4 km/h. She spent longer at the slower speed, so use total distance ÷ total time.

    Total distance = 6 + 4 = 10 km. Total time = 3 + 1 = 4 hours. Average speed = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 km/h.

    A driver covers 100 miles in 2 hours, then 150 miles in 3 hours. What is the average speed for the whole journey? Answer in mph.

    1. Find the total distance and the total time.
    2. Divide the total distance by the total time.
    • That looks like 250 ÷ 10. The total time is 2 + 3 = 5 hours.

    Total distance = 250 miles. Total time = 5 hours. Average speed = 250 ÷ 5 = 50 mph.

    A bus goes 30 miles at 20 mph, then 30 miles at 30 mph. What is its average speed for the whole journey? Answer in mph.

    1. Find the time for each part: distance ÷ speed.
    2. Then total distance ÷ total time.
    • That is the mean of 20 and 30. The bus spends longer at the slower speed, so use total distance ÷ total time.
    • That is the total distance. Divide by the total time.

    First part: 30 ÷ 20 = 1.5 hours. Second part: 30 ÷ 30 = 1 hour. Total time = 2.5 hours. Average speed = 60 ÷ 2.5 = 24 mph.

    How do you find the average speed of a whole journey?

    • Total distance ÷ total time
    • Add the speeds and divide by how many there are
    • Multiply the fastest speed by the time
    1. Average speed is still distance ÷ time, but for the whole journey.
    2. Which choice uses the whole distance and the whole time?

    Average speed = total distance ÷ total time. The speeds alone are not enough, because you spend different amounts of time at each.

    A driver sets off at 09:15 and arrives at 11:45. The journey is 150 miles, including a 30-minute rest. What is the average speed for the whole journey? Answer in mph.

    1. Work out the whole journey time from 09:15 to 11:45, in hours.
    2. Divide 150 miles by that time.
    • That leaves out the rest. The whole journey time includes the 30-minute stop.
    • That is 2.5 ÷ 150, the wrong way round. Divide the distance by the time.

    09:15 to 11:45 is 2 hours 30 minutes = 2.5 hours. Average speed = 150 ÷ 2.5 = 60 mph.

    3. Distance-time graphs

    A Distance-time graph shows how far from the start you are at each time. Time goes along the bottom and distance goes up the side.

    • The gradient of a line is the speed. Steeper means faster.
    • A flat line means the distance is not changing: stopped.
    • A line that goes down means coming back towards the start.

    Key idea: speed = distance ÷ time is the gradient. To find a speed, pick the two ends of a straight section: the change in distance divided by the change in time.

    Worked example: reading the graph

    Look at the graph above. Find the speed in the first hour, and say what happens between 1 hour and 1.5 hours.

    1. In the first hour the line goes from 0 km to 30 km, so the distance is 30 km and the time is 1 hour.
    2. Speed = 30 ÷ 1 = 30 km/h.
    3. Between 1 and 1.5 hours the line is flat at 30 km. The traveller is stopped for half an hour.

    Now you finish it

    Use the same graph. Find the speed between 1.5 hours and 2.5 hours.

    1. The distance goes from ___ km to ___ km, so the change is ___ km.
    2. The time goes from 1.5 h to 2.5 h, so the change is ___ hour.
    3. Speed = ___ ÷ ___ = ___ km/h.
    Check my answer

    The distance goes from 30 km to 90 km, a change of 60 km, in 1 hour. Speed = 60 ÷ 1 = 60 km/h.

    Check your knowledge: distance-time graphs

    The graph shows a journey. What is the speed in the first hour? Answer in km/h.

    1. Speed is the distance divided by the time for a straight section.
    2. Read the distance at 1 hour from the graph.
    • That is the time. Divide the distance by the time.

    In the first hour the line goes up 30 km in 1 hour. Speed = 30 ÷ 1 = 30 km/h.

    On the same graph, for how many hours was the traveller stopped? Answer in hours.

    1. A stop is a flat line.
    2. Read the times where the flat line starts and ends.
    • That is the distance, 30 km. The question asks for the time of the stop in hours.

    The flat section runs from 1 hour to 1.5 hours. 1.5 − 1 = 0.5 hours.

    On the same graph, what was the speed on the last section, when the traveller came back? Give the speed as a positive number, in km/h.

    1. Find the distance the line falls and the time it takes.
    2. The line goes from 90 km at 2.5 hours to 0 km at 4 hours.
    • That is the distance, 90 km. Divide by the time, 1.5 hours.
    • That looks like 90 ÷ 2.5, using the wrong time. The last section takes 4 − 2.5 = 1.5 hours.

    The line falls 90 km in 4 − 2.5 = 1.5 hours. Speed = 90 ÷ 1.5 = 60 km/h.

    On the same graph, how far did the traveller go altogether, there and back? Answer in km.

    1. The graph shows how far from the start she is. She went out and came back.
    2. Add the distance out and the distance back.
    • That is the distance out only. She came back too, so add the distance back.
    • That is how far from the start she ended up. The total distance travelled includes both ways.

    Out: 90 km. Back: 90 km. Total = 90 + 90 = 180 km.

    On the same graph, what was the average speed for the whole journey from 0 to 4 hours? Answer in km/h.

    1. You need the total distance travelled and the total time.
    2. Divide 180 km by the total time.
    • That uses how far she is from the start at the end. Use the total distance travelled, 180 km.
    • That uses 90 km, the distance out only. She also travelled 90 km back.

    Total distance travelled = 90 + 90 = 180 km. Total time = 4 hours. Average speed = 180 ÷ 4 = 45 km/h.

    On a distance-time graph, which part shows the fastest travel?

    • The steepest straight section
    • The flat section
    • The section that is highest up the graph
    1. The gradient of the line is the speed.
    2. Which line has the biggest gradient?

    Speed is the gradient. The steepest section covers the most distance in the least time.

    4. Changing the units of speed

    Sometimes you need a speed in different units. The trick is to change each unit one at a time.

    • 1 km = 1000 m and 1 hour = 3600 seconds.
    • So 1 km/h = 1000 m ÷ 3600 s = 0.277… m/s. This is the same as dividing by 3.6.
    • To go back, from m/s to km/h, multiply by 3.6.

    Key idea: km/h to m/s: divide by 3.6. m/s to km/h: multiply by 3.6. A quick check: m/s numbers are always smaller than km/h numbers.

    Worked example: km/h to m/s

    Change 72 km/h into m/s.

    1. 72 km = 72 × 1000 = 72 000 m, so the speed is 72 000 m per hour.
    2. 1 hour = 3600 seconds, so divide by 3600: 72 000 ÷ 3600 = 20.
    3. The speed is 20 m/s. (The shortcut: 72 ÷ 3.6 = 20.)

    Worked example: m/s to km/h

    Change 15 m/s into km/h.

    1. In 1 second, 15 m. In 1 hour (3600 seconds): 15 × 3600 = 54 000 m = 54 km.
    2. So 15 m/s = 54 km/h. (The shortcut: 15 × 3.6 = 54.)

    Now you finish it

    Change 90 km/h into m/s.

    1. 90 km/h = 90 × 1000 ÷ 3600 = ___ m/s.
    2. Shortcut check: 90 ÷ 3.6 = ___.
    Check my answer

    90 × 1000 = 90 000, and 90 000 ÷ 3600 = 25. So 90 km/h = 25 m/s. Check: 90 ÷ 3.6 = 25.

    Check your knowledge: changing units of speed

    Change 36 km/h into m/s.

    1. km/h to m/s means divide by 3.6.
    2. Work out 36 ÷ 3.6.
    • That multiplies by 3.6. To change km/h into m/s, divide by 3.6.
    • That is 36 ÷ 60. Use 3.6, because 1 km/h is 1000 ÷ 3600 m/s.

    36 ÷ 3.6 = 10 m/s.

    Change 20 m/s into km/h.

    1. m/s to km/h means multiply by 3.6.
    2. Work out 20 × 3.6.
    • That divides by 3.6. To change m/s into km/h, multiply by 3.6.

    20 × 3.6 = 72 km/h.

    A car travels at 108 km/h. What is its speed in m/s?

    1. Divide the km/h speed by 3.6.
    2. Work out 108 ÷ 3.6.
    • That multiplies by 3.6. To go from km/h to m/s, divide.
    • That divides by 60. Use 3.6.

    108 ÷ 3.6 = 30 m/s.

    A sprinter runs 100 m in 10 seconds. What is her average speed in km/h? (Find the speed in m/s first.)

    1. In m/s, speed = distance ÷ time = 100 ÷ 10.
    2. Then change m/s into km/h by multiplying by 3.6.
    • That is the speed in m/s. The question asks for km/h, so multiply by 3.6.
    • That divides by 3.6. To go from m/s to km/h, multiply.

    Speed = 100 ÷ 10 = 10 m/s. In km/h: 10 × 3.6 = 36 km/h.

    A cyclist rides at 18 km/h. How far does she go in 20 seconds? Answer in metres. (Change the speed to m/s first.)

    1. Change 18 km/h into m/s by dividing by 3.6.
    2. Then distance = speed × time, with the time in seconds.
    • That is the speed in m/s. Multiply by the 20 seconds to find the distance.
    • That looks like 18 × 20. Change the speed to m/s first, so the time in seconds matches.

    18 ÷ 3.6 = 5 m/s. In 20 seconds: 5 × 20 = 100 m.

    To change a speed from km/h to m/s, what do you do?

    • Divide by 3.6
    • Multiply by 3.6
    • Multiply by 60, because there are 60 minutes in an hour
    1. 1 km/h is 1000 m per 3600 s.
    2. Is the m/s number bigger or smaller than the km/h number?

    1000 ÷ 3600 = 1 ÷ 3.6, so km/h to m/s means dividing by 3.6.

    5. Density

    Density tells you how much mass is packed into each unit of volume. Lead is dense: a small block feels very heavy. Foam is not dense: a big block feels light.

    density = mass ÷ volume    or    D = M ÷ V

    If the mass is in grams (g) and the volume is in cubic centimetres (cm³), the density is in g/cm³. For a box shape, volume = length × width × height.

    • Cover M: D and V are side by side, so multiply. M = D × V.
    • Cover D: M is over V, so divide. D = M ÷ V.
    • Cover V: M is over D, so divide. V = M ÷ D.

    Key idea: water has a density of 1 g/cm³. Anything with a density less than 1 floats in water. Anything with a density more than 1 sinks.

    Worked example: finding density

    A metal block has a mass of 540 g and a volume of 200 cm³. Find its density.

    1. D = M ÷ V = 540 ÷ 200 = 2.7.
    2. The density is 2.7 g/cm³.

    Worked example: finding mass

    Concrete has a density of 2.4 g/cm³. Find the mass of 500 cm³ of it.

    1. M = D × V = 2.4 × 500 = 1200.
    2. The mass is 1200 g, which is 1.2 kg.

    Worked example: finding volume

    A cooking oil has a density of 0.9 g/cm³. What is the volume of 360 g of it?

    1. V = M ÷ D = 360 ÷ 0.9 = 400.
    2. The volume is 400 cm³.

    Now you finish it

    Sand has a density of 1.6 g/cm³. Find the mass of 2500 cm³ of sand, in grams.

    1. M = D × V = ___ × ___ = ___ g.
    2. In kilograms, that is ___ kg.
    Check my answer

    M = 1.6 × 2500 = 4000 g. In kilograms that is 4 kg.

    Check your knowledge: density

    A block of metal has a mass of 540 g and a volume of 200 cm³. What is its density? Answer in g/cm³.

    1. Density = mass ÷ volume.
    2. Work out 540 ÷ 200.
    • That is 200 ÷ 540, the wrong way round. Divide the mass by the volume.
    • That looks like 540 × 200. Density is mass divided by volume.

    D = M ÷ V = 540 ÷ 200 = 2.7 g/cm³.

    A metal has a density of 8 g/cm³. What is the mass of 25 cm³ of it? Answer in grams.

    1. You know the density and the volume, and you want the mass.
    2. M = D × V.
    • That looks like 8 ÷ 25. To find the mass, multiply density by volume.

    M = D × V = 8 × 25 = 200 g.

    A liquid has a density of 0.9 g/cm³. It has a mass of 720 g. What is its volume? Answer in cm³.

    1. Cover V in the triangle. What is left?
    2. V = M ÷ D.
    • That looks like 720 × 0.9. To find the volume, divide the mass by the density.
    • That is 0.9 ÷ 720, the wrong way round. Divide the mass by the density.

    V = M ÷ D = 720 ÷ 0.9 = 800 cm³.

    A piece of wood has a mass of 150 g and a volume of 200 cm³. Water has a density of 1 g/cm³. What happens in water?

    • It floats, because its density is 0.75 g/cm³
    • It sinks, because its density is 1.33 g/cm³ which is more than the density of water
    • It sinks, because 200 is more than 150
    1. Work out the density of the wood first.
    2. Compare it with the density of water, 1 g/cm³.

    D = 150 ÷ 200 = 0.75 g/cm³. That is less than 1, so it floats.

    A tub 10 cm long, 5 cm wide and 4 cm high is filled with sand of mass 300 g. What is the density of the sand? Answer in g/cm³.

    1. Find the volume of the tub first: length × width × height.
    2. Then divide the mass by the volume.
    • That divides by 2000 or uses a wrong volume. Check: 10 × 5 × 4 = 200 cm³.
    • That is the volume divided by the mass. Density is mass divided by volume.

    Volume = 10 × 5 × 4 = 200 cm³. Density = 300 ÷ 200 = 1.5 g/cm³.

    A steel plate has a volume of 400 cm³ and a density of 7.8 g/cm³. What is its mass in kilograms, like 3.12?

    1. Find the mass in grams first: density × volume.
    2. Then change grams into kilograms by dividing by 1000.
    • That is the mass in grams. Divide by 1000 to change it to kilograms.

    M = D × V = 7.8 × 400 = 3120 g. In kilograms: 3120 ÷ 1000 = 3.12 kg.

    A block of concrete has a mass of 2.4 kg and a density of 2.4 g/cm³. What is its volume? Answer in cm³. (Change the mass to grams first.)

    1. 2.4 kg is 2400 g.
    2. V = M ÷ D.
    • That divides 2.4 by 2.4 without changing kg into g. Use 2400 g.
    • That looks like 2400 × 2.4. To find the volume, divide the mass by the density.

    2.4 kg = 2400 g. V = M ÷ D = 2400 ÷ 2.4 = 1000 cm³.

    Density in kg/m³ Higher

    Builders and engineers often give density in kg/m³. To change between the two units, use these facts: 1 m³ = 100 cm × 100 cm × 100 cm = 1 000 000 cm³, and 1 kg = 1000 g.

    1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³

    So to change g/cm³ into kg/m³, multiply by 1000. To change kg/m³ into g/cm³, divide by 1000. Water is 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.

    Worked example

    Concrete has a density of 2.4 g/cm³. Give this in kg/m³. Then find the mass of 1.5 m³ of concrete.

    1. g/cm³ to kg/m³: multiply by 1000. 2.4 × 1000 = 2400 kg/m³.
    2. M = D × V = 2400 × 1.5 = 3600 kg.

    Now you finish it

    A wood has a density of 0.9 g/cm³. Give this in kg/m³.

    1. 0.9 × 1000 = ___ kg/m³.
    Check my answer

    0.9 × 1000 = 900 kg/m³.

    6. Pressure

    Pressure tells you how hard a force pushes on each unit of area. The same force on a small area gives a large pressure. That is why a knife cuts, and why a stiletto heel sinks into soft ground when a flat shoe does not.

    pressure = force ÷ area    or    P = F ÷ A

    Force is measured in newtons (N). If the area is in m², the pressure is in N/m². If the area is in cm², the pressure is in N/cm².

    • Cover F: P and A are side by side, so multiply. F = P × A.
    • Cover P: F is over A, so divide. P = F ÷ A.
    • Cover A: F is over P, so divide. A = F ÷ P.

    Worked example: finding pressure

    A force of 600 N acts on an area of 2 m². Find the pressure.

    1. P = F ÷ A = 600 ÷ 2 = 300.
    2. The pressure is 300 N/m².

    Worked example: finding force and area

    (a) A pressure of 40 N/m² acts on 3 m². Find the force. (b) A force of 900 N gives a pressure of 300 N/m². Find the area.

    1. (a) F = P × A = 40 × 3 = 120 N.
    2. (b) A = F ÷ P = 900 ÷ 300 = 3 m².

    Now you finish it

    A force of 1200 N acts on an area of 0.4 m². Find the pressure.

    1. P = F ÷ A = 1200 ÷ ___ = ___ N/m².
    Check my answer

    P = 1200 ÷ 0.4 = 3000 N/m².

    7. Rates of pay and unit pricing

    A rate is another compound measure: pounds per hour, pence per unit, pounds per square metre. The idea is exactly the same as for speed.

    • total = rate × amount, for example pay = £ per hour × hours.
    • rate = total ÷ amount, for example price per metre = total price ÷ metres. This is a unit price.
    • amount = total ÷ rate, for example hours = pay ÷ £ per hour.

    Key idea: to compare prices, work out the unit price of each (the price for one metre, one kilogram or one item), then compare. The lower unit price is the better buy.

    8. Power, energy and kWh

    Electrical kit is full of compound measures. Power is how fast energy is used: 1 watt (W) is 1 joule per second.

    power = energy ÷ time    or    P = E ÷ T

    In the home, energy is sold in kilowatt-hours (kWh). One kWh is the energy used by a 1 kW appliance running for 1 hour. 1 kW = 1000 W.

    energy (kWh) = power (kW) × time (hours)

    Key idea: change watts to kilowatts by dividing by 1000, and minutes to hours by dividing by 60, BEFORE you multiply. Then cost = number of kWh × price per kWh.

    Worked example: the cost of running a heater

    A 3 kW heater runs for 4 hours. Electricity costs 28p per kWh. Find the cost in pounds.

    1. Energy = 3 × 4 = 12 kWh.
    2. Cost = 12 × 28 = 336p.
    3. In pounds: 336 ÷ 100 = £3.36.

    Worked example: changing watts to kilowatts

    A 500 W drill runs for 2 hours. How many kWh does it use?

    1. 500 W = 500 ÷ 1000 = 0.5 kW.
    2. Energy = 0.5 × 2 = 1 kWh.

    Worked example: power from energy and time

    A lamp uses 7200 joules of energy in 60 seconds. Find its power.

    1. P = E ÷ T = 7200 ÷ 60 = 120.
    2. The power is 120 W.

    Now you finish it

    Flip it: run it backwards

    A compound measure can be used two ways. You can find the rate (speed, density, pressure, price per unit) from a total and an amount. Or you can use the rate to find a distance, mass, force or time.

    Finding the rate

    D = 120 km, T = 2 h, S = ?

    Divide: S = 120 ÷ 2 = 60 km/h.

    Using the rate

    S = 60 km/h, T = 2 h, D = ?

    Multiply: D = 60 × 2 = 120 km.

    • To find a rate, divide the total by the amount.
    • To find a total, multiply the rate by the amount.
    • To find an amount (a time, a volume, an area), divide the total by the rate.

    Key idea: the triangle gives you all three at once. Cover the one you want. Always check the units match before you work anything out.

    3. It takes 25 minutes.

    [3 marks] Higher A bar has a volume of 0.002 m³. It is made of a metal with a density of 7.8 g/cm³. Work out the mass of the bar. Answer in kg.

    1. The volume is in m³, so change the density into kg/m³.
    2. Multiply g/cm³ by 1000 to get kg/m³.
    3. Mass = density × volume.
    • That multiplies 7.8 by 0.002 without changing the units. Change 7.8 g/cm³ into 7800 kg/m³ first.
    • That is the mass in grams. The question asks for kg.

    7.8 g/cm³ = 7800 kg/m³. Mass = 7800 × 0.002 = 15.6 kg.

    • M1 for changing 7.8 g/cm³ to 7800 kg/m³
    • M1 for 7800 × 0.002
    • A1 for 15.6

    Why it works

    • “Per” means “divided by”. Miles per hour is miles ÷ hours. Grams per cubic centimetre is grams ÷ cm³. Newtons per square metre is newtons ÷ m². The unit tells you the formula.
    • The triangle is rearranging. If S = D ÷ T, then D = S × T and T = D ÷ S. Covering a letter in the triangle does the same steps without writing them out.
    • Average speed uses totals. Speed is distance divided by time, so for a whole journey you need the whole distance and the whole time. Averaging two speeds ignores how long you spend at each.
    • A graph’s gradient is a rate. On a distance-time graph, rise ÷ run is distance ÷ time, which is speed.
    • The factor 3.6 comes from the units. 1 km/h is 1000 m in 3600 s, and 1000 ÷ 3600 = 1 ÷ 3.6.
    • Same force, smaller area, bigger pressure. That is why a thin nail goes into wood when a thumb pushes on its blunt end.

    Common traps

    • Mixing units: minutes with km/h, or kg with g/cm³. Change them to match first.
    • Writing 1 hour 30 minutes as 1.3 hours. It is 1.5 hours.
    • Averaging two speeds instead of using total distance ÷ total time.
    • Leaving a stop out of the total time when finding an average speed.
    • Multiplying instead of dividing for speed, density or pressure.
    • Mixing up km/h and m/s: divide by 3.6 to go from km/h to m/s.

    Going further

    A driver goes up a hill 1 mile long at 30 mph. How fast must she drive down the same mile to average 60 mph over the whole 2 miles?

    • It is impossible, however fast she goes
    • 90 mph, so that the two speeds average 60 mph
    • 120 mph, twice as fast as 60
    1. To average 60 mph over 2 miles, how long can the whole trip take? Use time = distance ÷ speed.
    2. How long did the trip up the hill take at 30 mph?

    For 2 miles at an average of 60 mph, the whole trip must take 2 ÷ 60 = 1 over 30 hour = 2 minutes. Going up 1 mile at 30 mph already takes 1 ÷ 30 = 1 over 30 hour = 2 minutes. There is no time left for the way down, so it is impossible.

    Show that a speed of 1 m/s is the same as 3.6 km/h.

    1. How many metres does something travelling at 1 m/s cover in 1 second? In 3600 seconds?
    2. There are 3600 seconds in 1 hour, and 1000 m in 1 km.

    At 1 m/s, an object goes 1 m every second. In 1 hour there are 60 × 60 = 3600 seconds, so it goes 3600 m in 1 hour. 3600 m = 3.6 km. So 1 m/s = 3.6 km in 1 hour = 3.6 km/h.

    Block A has a volume of 600 cm³ and a density of 2 g/cm³. Block B has a volume of 400 cm³ and a density of 7 g/cm³. They are stuck together. What is the density of the combined block? Answer in g/cm³.

    1. Find the mass of each block.
    2. Density of the whole = total mass ÷ total volume. Is it just the mean of 2 and 7?
    • That is the mean of 2 and 7. Block A is bigger, so use total mass ÷ total volume.
    • That adds the two densities. Find the total mass and total volume first.

    Mass A = 2 × 600 = 1200 g. Mass B = 7 × 400 = 2800 g. Total mass = 4000 g. Total volume = 600 + 400 = 1000 cm³. Density = 4000 ÷ 1000 = 4 g/cm³. (Just as with average speed, you use totals.)
