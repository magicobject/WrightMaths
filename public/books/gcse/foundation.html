    Grade 4, 5 and 6

    Practice papers for grades 4 to 6

    Questions in the style of a real paper, worth 2 to 5 marks each. Choose your method, write your working on paper, then mark yourself against the mark scheme.

    Where you will use this: the weeks before a mock exam, when you want practice that feels like the real thing, or to check a topic is secure.

    Back to the pyramid
    GCSE Maths Book / Practice papers, grades 4 to 6

    How it works: read the question, write your answer on paper, then press the button to see the mark scheme. Tick every mark you earned. M marks are for a correct method, A marks for an accurate answer, B marks stand on their own, and C marks are for explaining clearly. Each question says whether to use a calculator, and has a clue if you are stuck. Real exams do not give clues, so try first. The ticks are saved only on this device.

    Some questions have a box for a final answer, and those mark the answer for you. Aiming higher? Try the Grade 7 to 9 stretch papers.

    Number

    [2 marks] Non-calculator Grade 4 Write 0.35 as a fraction in its simplest form. Type your answer like 3/8. Uses: Place value Uses: Fractions

    1. How many decimal places does 0.35 have? That tells you the bottom number of the fraction.
    2. Write the fraction with that bottom number, then divide the top and bottom by the same number until you cannot go further.
    • That looks like the right fraction, but it is not in its simplest form yet. Divide the top and bottom by the same number.
    • That looks like 0.7. 0.35 has two decimal places, so the bottom number is 100, not 10.

    0.35 = 35/100.

    Divide the top and bottom by 5: 35 ÷ 5 = 7 and 100 ÷ 5 = 20.

    Answer: 7/20.

    • M1 for 35/100
    • A1 for 7/20

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 4 Work out 2 1 over 3 + 1 3 over 4. Give your answer as an improper fraction, like 11/6. Uses: Fractions

    1. Mixed numbers are hard to add. What can you turn them into first?
    2. Change both to improper fractions, then find a common denominator for 3 and 4.
    3. The common denominator is 12. Write each fraction over 12, then add the tops.
    • That looks like adding the tops (1 + 3) and the bottoms (3 + 4). You need a common denominator first, then you add only the tops.
    • That looks like the right value as a mixed number. The question asks for an improper fraction.

    2 1 over 3 = 7 over 3 and 1 3 over 4 = 7 over 4.

    The common denominator is 12: 7 over 3 = 28 over 12 and 7 over 4 = 21 over 12.

    28 over 12 + 21 over 12 = 49 over 12. Answer: 49/12.

    • M1 for both mixed numbers written as improper fractions, 7 over 3 and 7 over 4
    • M1 for a common denominator of 12, such as 28 over 12 and 21 over 12
    • A1 for 49/12

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 5 In a sale, a set of spanners is reduced by 30%. The sale price is £84. Show that the original price was £120. Uses: Percentages

    1. The sale price is what is left after the reduction. What percentage of the original price is that?
    2. Divide to find 1%, then find 100%. Or use a multiplier and divide by it.

    The price is reduced by 30%, so the sale price is 100% − 30% = 70% of the original.

    70% = £84, so 1% = 84 ÷ 70 = £1.20, and 100% = £120.

    Check: 30% of 120 = 36 and 120 − 36 = 84, so the original price was £120, as required.

    • M1 for 70% (or a multiplier of 0.7) linked to £84
    • M1 for 84 ÷ 0.7 (or 84 ÷ 70 × 100)
    • C1 for 120, with the check or a clear conclusion

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 5 By rounding each number to 1 significant figure, work out an estimate for 4.87 × 21.2 over 0.52. You must show your working. Uses: Place value Uses: Four operations

    1. Round each of the three numbers so that only one digit is not zero.
    2. Do the multiplication on the top first, then divide.
    3. Dividing by 0.5 is the same as doubling.
    • That looks like dividing 100 by 5. The bottom number rounds to 0.5, not 5.
    • That looks like multiplying 100 by 0.5. You need to divide by 0.5, which is the same as doubling.

    4.87 ≈ 5, 21.2 ≈ 20 and 0.52 ≈ 0.5.

    5 × 20 over 0.5 = 100 ÷ 0.5 = 200.

    The estimate is 200.

    • M1 for 5, 20 and 0.5
    • M1 for 5 × 20 ÷ 0.5, or 100 ÷ 0.5
    • A1 for 200

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 6 A carpenter puts £2500 into a savings account. The account pays compound interest of 4% a year. How much is in the account after 3 years? Type your answer in pounds, like 1234.56. Uses: Percentage change

    1. Interest is added again each year, so the money is multiplied by the same number each year.
    2. The multiplier for an increase of 4% is 1.04. You use it three times.
    3. Work out 2500 × 1.04 × 1.04 × 1.04, or use a power.
    • That looks like simple interest, the same £100 each year. Compound interest earns interest on the interest too.
    • That looks like one year of simple interest. The money is in for 3 years.

    Multiplier for +4% = 1.04.

    After 3 years: 2500 × 1.04³ = 2500 × 1.124864 = 2812.16.

    The account holds £2812.16.

    • M1 for the multiplier 1.04
    • M1 for 2500 × 1.04³
    • A1 for 2812.16

    Ratio and proportion

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 4 A plumber and her apprentice share £360 in the ratio 5 : 4. Work out how much the apprentice gets. Type the number of pounds. Uses: Ratio

    1. How many equal parts are there altogether?
    2. Divide the total by the number of parts to find one part.
    3. The apprentice has the second number in the ratio. Multiply one part by it.
    • That looks like the plumber's share (5 parts). The apprentice has 4 parts.
    • That is the value of one part. The apprentice has 4 parts, so multiply.

    5 + 4 = 9 parts, so one part = 360 ÷ 9 = £40.

    The apprentice has 4 parts: 4 × 40 = £160.

    Check: the plumber gets 5 × 40 = £200, and 200 + 160 = 360.

    • M1 for 360 ÷ (5 + 4)
    • M1 for 4 × (their 40)
    • A1 for 160

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 4 A shop sells paint in two tins. A 2.5 litre tin costs £14.50. A 4 litre tin costs £22.00. Which tin is the better value for money? You must show your working. Uses: Ratio Uses: Four operations

    1. To compare, work out the price of the same amount of paint in each tin.
    2. The easiest amount to use is 1 litre. Divide each price by the number of litres.

    2.5 litre tin: 14.50 ÷ 2.5 = £5.80 per litre.

    4 litre tin: 22.00 ÷ 4 = £5.50 per litre.

    The 4 litre tin costs less per litre (£5.50 is less than £5.80), so it is the better value.

    • M1 for 14.50 ÷ 2.5 (= 5.80)
    • M1 for 22 ÷ 4 (= 5.50)
    • C1 for choosing the 4 litre tin, with a comparison of the two prices per litre

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 5 A recipe for 15 pancakes uses 240 g of flour. Work out how much flour is needed for 25 pancakes. Type the number of grams. Uses: Ratio

    1. The amount of flour goes up in step with the number of pancakes.
    2. Find the flour for 1 pancake first, then scale up.
    • That looks like adding 10 g because there are 10 more pancakes. Each pancake needs its own share of flour, so multiply instead.
    • That is the flour for 1 pancake. The question asks for 25 pancakes.

    240 ÷ 15 = 16 g of flour for 1 pancake.

    16 × 25 = 400 g.

    Answer: 400 g.

    • M1 for 240 ÷ 15 (= 16)
    • M1 for (their 16) × 25
    • A1 for 400

    [4 marks] Calculator Grade 5 A builder mixes cement, sand and gravel in the ratio 1 : 2 : 5 by mass. He makes 2400 kg of the mix. Cement costs £0.22 per kg, sand costs £0.04 per kg and gravel costs £0.03 per kg. Work out the total cost of the cement, sand and gravel. Type the number of pounds. Uses: Ratio

    1. How many parts are there altogether?
    2. Find the mass of one part, then the mass of cement, sand and gravel.
    3. Multiply each mass by its cost per kg, then add the three costs.
    • That is the mass of one part (and of the cement). Find the mass of each material, then its cost.
    • That looks like the cost of the cement only. Add the sand and the gravel too.

    1 + 2 + 5 = 8 parts, so one part = 2400 ÷ 8 = 300 kg.

    Cement 300 kg, sand 600 kg, gravel 1500 kg.

    Cost = 300 × 0.22 + 600 × 0.04 + 1500 × 0.03 = 66 + 24 + 45 = £135.

    • M1 for 2400 ÷ 8 (= 300)
    • A1 for 300, 600 and 1500 kg
    • M1 for multiplying each mass by its cost per kg
    • A1 for 135

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 6 A paint store has red tins and blue tins in the ratio 3 : 7. There are 12 more blue tins than red tins. Work out the total number of tins in the store. Uses: Ratio

    1. The ratio compares blue with red. How many parts is the difference between them?
    2. Those parts are worth 12 tins. Work out one part.
    3. Then count all the parts.
    • That is the difference between the colours, which is given in the question. You need the total number of tins.
    • That looks like the number of blue tins (7 parts). The question asks for all the tins.

    The difference is 7 − 3 = 4 parts, and 4 parts = 12 tins, so one part = 3 tins.

    There are 3 + 7 = 10 parts altogether: 10 × 3 = 30 tins.

    Check: red is 9, blue is 21, and 21 − 9 = 12.

    • M1 for 7 − 3 = 4 parts, equal to 12 tins
    • M1 for 12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 10 parts in total
    • A1 for 30

    Algebra

    [2 marks] Non-calculator Grade 4 Work out the value of 5x² − 3x when x = −2. Uses: Algebra basics Uses: Negative numbers

    1. Put −2 in place of each x. Use brackets round it to keep the minus sign safe.
    2. Do the square first: (−2)². Then multiply, then subtract.
    • That looks like squaring 2 and keeping the minus sign. (−2)² = 4, because a negative times a negative is positive.
    • That looks like 20 − 6. Subtracting −6 is the same as adding 6.

    5 × (−2)² − 3 × (−2) = 5 × 4 + 6 = 20 + 6 = 26.

    Answer: 26.

    • M1 for 5 × 4 (or 20), or for −3 × −2 = +6
    • A1 for 26

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 4 Solve 5(x − 2) = 3x + 8. Uses: Solving equations Uses: Algebra basics

    1. Start by multiplying out the bracket. Both terms inside get multiplied by 5.
    2. Get all the x terms on one side and the numbers on the other.
    3. After expanding you have 5x − 10 = 3x + 8. Take 3x from both sides.
    • That looks like 5(x − 2) expanded to 5x − 2. The 5 multiplies the 2 as well, so it is 5x − 10.
    • That looks like adding 3x to both sides instead of taking it away. Do the opposite of what is on the right.

    5x − 10 = 3x + 8

    2x − 10 = 8

    2x = 18, so x = 9.

    Check: 5 × (9 − 2) = 35 and 3 × 9 + 8 = 35.

    • M1 for 5x − 10
    • M1 for collecting terms, such as 2x = 18
    • A1 for 9

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 5 Here are the first four terms of a sequence: 5, 9, 13, 17. Show that 101 is a term in this sequence. Uses: Sequences

    1. Find the rule that links one term to the next, then the nth term.
    2. If 101 is a term, then the nth term equals 101 for some whole number n. Solve for n.

    The terms go up by 4 each time, so the nth term is 4n + something. When n = 1, 4n = 4, so we add 1: the nth term is 4n + 1.

    Set 4n + 1 = 101. Then 4n = 100 and n = 25.

    25 is a whole number, so 101 is a term in the sequence. It is the 25th term.

    • M1 for a rule of 4n + 1 (or 4n + c with a correct method for c)
    • M1 for setting 4n + 1 = 101 and solving, to get n = 25
    • C1 for stating that n = 25 is a whole number, so 101 is the 25th term

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 5 A plumber charges a call-out fee of £40 plus £36 for each hour of work. A job costs £220. For how many hours did the plumber work? Uses: Solving equations

    1. Not all of the £220 is for hours of work. What is part of it for?
    2. Take away the call-out fee, then see how many lots of £36 are left.
    • That looks like 220 ÷ 36. The call-out fee is not charged per hour, so take it off first.
    • That looks like adding the £40 instead of taking it away.

    Let h be the number of hours. Then 40 + 36h = 220.

    36h = 220 − 40 = 180.

    h = 180 ÷ 36 = 5 hours.

    • M1 for 220 − 40 (= 180), or for 40 + 36h = 220
    • M1 for 180 ÷ 36
    • A1 for 5

    [4 marks] Calculator Grade 6 A rectangular worktop has length (x + 5) cm and width (x − 1) cm. Its perimeter is 40 cm. Work out the area of the worktop. Type the number only. Uses: Solving equations Uses: Area and perimeter

    1. The perimeter is the total length of all four sides. Write an expression for it using x.
    2. Put it equal to 40 and solve for x.
    3. Use x to find the length and the width in numbers. Then find the area.
    • That is the value of x. The question asks for the area, so find the length and the width first.
    • That looks like half the perimeter (length + width). Use x to find each side, then multiply.

    Perimeter = 2(x + 5) + 2(x − 1) = 4x + 8.

    4x + 8 = 40, so 4x = 32 and x = 8.

    Length = 8 + 5 = 13 cm and width = 8 − 1 = 7 cm.

    Area = 13 × 7 = 91 cm².

    • M1 for an expression for the perimeter, such as 2(x + 5) + 2(x − 1) = 40
    • M1 for solving to get x = 8
    • M1 for length 13 and width 7, multiplied together
    • A1 for 91

    Geometry and measures

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 4 Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC. Angle A = 48°. Work out the size of angle B. You must show your working. Type the number of degrees. Uses: Shapes and angles

    1. The angles in a triangle add up to a fixed total. What is it?
    2. AB = AC, so the two angles at B and C are equal. How much is left for them to share?
    • That is the total of angles B and C together. They are equal, so share it between them.
    • That looks like treating 48° as one of the two equal angles. The equal angles are at B and C, not at A.

    The angles in a triangle add up to 180°.

    Angle B + angle C = 180 − 48 = 132°.

    The triangle is isosceles with AB = AC, so angle B = angle C = 132 ÷ 2 = 66°.

    • M1 for 180 − 48 (= 132)
    • M1 for dividing by 2 because the base angles are equal
    • A1 for 66

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 5 A 6.5 m ladder leans against a vertical wall. The top of the ladder is 6 m above the ground. Show that the foot of the ladder is 2.5 m from the wall. Uses: Pythagoras

    1. The wall, the ground and the ladder make a right-angled triangle. Which side is the ladder?
    2. The ladder is the longest side. To find a shorter side, subtract the squares.

    The ladder is the longest side (the hypotenuse), so the distance d from the wall satisfies d² + 6² = 6.5².

    d² = 42.25 − 36 = 6.25.

    d = √6.25 = 2.5 m, as required.

    • M1 for 6.5² − 6² (subtracting the squares)
    • M1 for 42.25 − 36 = 6.25
    • C1 for √6.25 = 2.5, stated as the conclusion

    [2 marks] Calculator Grade 4 A circular pond has a diameter of 4.2 m. A gardener wants to put an edging strip all the way round the pond. Work out the length of edging needed. Give your answer in metres, correct to 1 decimal place. Uses: Circles

    1. The distance round a circle has a special name. Which formula uses the diameter?
    2. Multiply the diameter by π, then round to 1 decimal place.
    • That looks like the area of the pond (πr²). The edging goes round the edge, so you need the circumference.
    • That looks like 2πd. The circumference is πd, or 2πr, not both.

    Circumference = π × diameter = π × 4.2 = 13.194… ≈ 13.2 m (to 1 d.p.).

    The edging needed is 13.2 m.

    • M1 for π × 4.2
    • A1 for 13.2

    [4 marks] Calculator Grade 6 A water trough is a prism. Its cross-section is a triangle with base 40 cm and height 30 cm. The trough is 150 cm long. Work out how many litres of water it holds when it is full. 1000 cm³ = 1 litre. Type the number of litres. Uses: Area and perimeter

    1. The volume of a prism is the area of its cross-section times its length. Which face is the cross-section?
    2. Find the area of the triangle first, then multiply by 150.
    3. That gives cm³. How many cm³ are in 1 litre?
    • That is the volume in cm³. Divide by 1000 to change it to litres.
    • That is the area of the triangle. Multiply by the length of the trough to get the volume.

    Area of the triangle = ½ × 40 × 30 = 600 cm².

    Volume = 600 × 150 = 90 000 cm³.

    In litres: 90 000 ÷ 1000 = 90 litres.

    • M1 for ½ × 40 × 30
    • A1 for 600
    • M1 for 600 × 150 (= 90 000)
    • A1 for 90

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 5 An L-shaped room is made from a rectangle 8 m long and 5 m wide, with a square corner measuring 3 m by 3 m cut out of it. Show that the floor area of the room is 31 m². Uses: Area and perimeter

    1. Picture the full rectangle first. What is its area?
    2. Then think about the square that was cut away.

    Whole rectangle: 8 × 5 = 40 m².

    Corner removed: 3 × 3 = 9 m².

    Floor area = 40 − 9 = 31 m², as required.

    Probability and statistics

    [2 marks] Non-calculator Grade 4 A bag has 4 red counters, 7 blue counters and 9 green counters. One counter is taken from the bag at random. Work out the probability that it is not blue. Give your answer as a decimal, like 0.4. Uses: Probability basics

    1. How many counters are there in the bag altogether?
    2. Probabilities of an event and its opposite add up to 1. Or add the red and green together.
    • That is the probability that the counter is blue. The question asks for not blue.
    • That looks like the right probability, but the question asks for a decimal.

    4 + 7 + 9 = 20 counters. P(blue) = 7/20 = 0.35.

    P(not blue) = 1 − 0.35 = 0.65. (Check: 13 counters are not blue, and 13/20 = 0.65.)

    Answer: 0.65.

    • M1 for 13/20, or for 1 − 7/20
    • A1 for 0.65

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 5 The mean of six numbers is 7. Five of the numbers are 4, 9, 6, 10 and 5. Work out the sixth number. Uses: Averages

    1. The mean is the total divided by how many numbers there are. So what is the total of all six?
    2. Add the five numbers you know and compare with the total.
    • That is the mean, which is given. You need the sixth number itself.
    • That is the total of the five numbers you know. Compare it with the total of all six.

    Total of six numbers = 6 × 7 = 42.

    The five known numbers: 4 + 9 + 6 + 10 + 5 = 34.

    Sixth number = 42 − 34 = 8.

    • M1 for 6 × 7 (= 42)
    • M1 for 4 + 9 + 6 + 10 + 5 (= 34) and subtracting from the total
    • A1 for 8

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 5 An electrician checks 20 houses and records how many faults she finds in each. 5 houses had 0 faults, 8 houses had 1 fault, 4 houses had 2 faults and 3 houses had 3 faults. Work out the mean number of faults per house. Uses: Averages

    1. The mean is the total number of faults divided by the number of houses.
    2. To find the total faults, multiply each number of faults by how many houses had that many, then add.
    • That looks like the mean of 0, 1, 2 and 3. More houses had 1 fault than 3 faults, so use the number of houses as well.
    • That is the total number of faults. Divide by the number of houses to get the mean.

    Total faults = 0 × 5 + 1 × 8 + 2 × 4 + 3 × 3 = 0 + 8 + 8 + 9 = 25.

    Number of houses = 5 + 8 + 4 + 3 = 20.

    Mean = 25 ÷ 20 = 1.25 faults per house.

    • M1 for multiplying each number of faults by its frequency
    • M1 for 25 (the total) divided by 20
    • A1 for 1.25

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 4 A spinner has four colours. The probability of red is 0.25 and the probability of blue is 0.4. The probabilities of green and yellow are the same. Show that the probability of green is 0.175. Uses: Probability basics

    1. What do all the probabilities add up to?
    2. Work out how much probability is left after red and blue. Green and yellow share it equally.

    The probabilities add up to 1. Red + blue = 0.25 + 0.4 = 0.65.

    Green + yellow = 1 − 0.65 = 0.35.

    They are equal, so green = 0.35 ÷ 2 = 0.175, as required.

    • M1 for 0.25 + 0.4 = 0.65
    • M1 for 1 − 0.65 = 0.35, then dividing by 2
    • C1 for 0.175, stated as the conclusion

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 5 The bar chart shows how many jobs a plumber did on each day of one week. Work out the mean number of jobs per day. Uses: Averages

    Text version of the picture, for people who cannot see it (it may give away the answer): A bar chart of jobs per day, Monday to Friday. The vertical axis is the number of jobs, from 0 to 10 in steps of 2. Monday 6, Tuesday 9, Wednesday 4, Thursday 7, Friday 3.

    2. The mean is the total number of jobs divided by the number of days.

    Reading the bars: Monday 6, Tuesday 9, Wednesday 4, Thursday 7, Friday 3.

    Mean = 29 ÷ 5 = 5.8 jobs per day.

    • M1 for 29 ÷ 5
    • A1 for 5.8

    Real-life trade and money questions

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 4 An electrician quotes for a job. Parts cost £85. Labour is 4 hours at £45 per hour. VAT at 20% is added to the whole of the quote. Work out the total cost including VAT. Type the number of pounds. Uses: Percentages Uses: Four operations

    1. Work out the cost of the labour first, then add the parts.
    2. VAT is added to the whole amount, so do it last.
    3. Adding 20% is the same as multiplying by 1.2.
    • That is the total before VAT. Add the 20% VAT to it.
    • That is only the VAT. The question asks for the total including VAT.

    Labour = 4 × 45 = £180. Before VAT: 180 + 85 = £265.

    VAT = 20% of 265 = £53, so the total is 265 + 53 = £318.

    (Or 265 × 1.2 = 318.)

    • M1 for 4 × 45 + 85 (= 265)
    • M1 for adding 20% VAT, or multiplying by 1.2
    • A1 for 318

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 4 A tiler is tiling a rectangular floor 5 m long and 4 m wide. Each tile is a square with sides of 50 cm. Show that 80 tiles are needed to cover the floor. Uses: Area and perimeter

    1. Find the area of the floor and the area of one tile. Use the same unit for both.
    2. 50 cm is the same as 0.5 m. How many tile areas fit in the floor area?

    Floor area = 5 × 4 = 20 m².

    One tile = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 m².

    Number of tiles = 20 ÷ 0.25 = 80, as required.

    • M1 for 5 × 4 = 20 m²
    • M1 for 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 m² (same units as the floor)
    • C1 for 20 ÷ 0.25 = 80, stated as the conclusion

    [5 marks] Calculator Grade 6 An electrician wires a new socket circuit. The ring needs 24 m of cable and a spur needs 7.5 m. He buys 10% extra to allow for waste. The cable costs £1.85 per metre before VAT. VAT at 20% is then added. Work out the total cost of the cable including VAT. Give your answer in pounds to the nearest penny, like 12.50. Uses: Percentages Uses: Four operations

    2. Increasing by 10% is the same as multiplying by 1.1. Then find the cost before VAT.
    3. Add the VAT last, which is multiplying by 1.2.
    • That looks like leaving out the 10% extra. He buys 10% more than he needs, so the length is multiplied by 1.1.
    • That is the cost before VAT. Add the 20% on top.

    Length needed = 24 + 7.5 = 31.5 m.

    With 10% extra: 31.5 × 1.1 = 34.65 m.

    Cost before VAT = 34.65 × 1.85 = £64.1025.

    With VAT: 64.1025 × 1.2 = 76.923 ≈ £76.92.

    • M1 for 24 + 7.5 (= 31.5)
    • M1 for 31.5 × 1.1 (= 34.65)
    • M1 for (their 34.65) × 1.85
    • M1 for adding 20% VAT, or multiplying by 1.2
    • A1 for 76.92

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 5 A plumber needs 7 pieces of copper pipe, each 85 cm long. The pipe is sold in 3 m lengths, and each 3 m length costs £8.40. Offcuts that are too short cannot be used. Work out the least cost of the pipe. Give your answer in pounds, like 12.50. Uses: Four operations Uses: Factors and multiples

    1. 3 m is 300 cm. How many whole 85 cm pieces can come out of one length?
    2. Divide the 7 pieces by the number per length. Be careful with a part-length: you cannot buy it.
    3. Then multiply the number of lengths by the price of one.
    • That looks like working out the total pipe (5.95 m) and buying 2 lengths. Each length only gives 3 whole pieces, because the offcut is too short.
    • That is the price of one length. You need more than one length.

    300 ÷ 85 = 3.5…, so only 3 whole pieces come out of each 3 m length (3 × 85 = 255 cm, leaving 45 cm, which is too short).

    7 ÷ 3 = 2.33…, so he needs 3 lengths (2 lengths give only 6 pieces).

    Cost = 3 × 8.40 = £25.20.

    • M1 for 300 ÷ 85 and 3 whole pieces per length
    • M1 for 7 ÷ 3 and rounding up to 3 lengths
    • M1 for 3 × 8.40
    • A1 for 25.20

    [4 marks] Calculator Grade 6 A homeowner gets two quotes to retile a bathroom. Quote A is £1250 including VAT. Quote B is £400 plus £38 for each hour of work, and then 20% VAT is added to the total. Quote B says the job will take 22 hours. Which quote is cheaper? You must show your working, and explain your answer. Uses: Percentages Uses: Solving equations

    1. To compare, both quotes must be worked out with VAT included. Which one already has it?
    2. Work out Quote B before VAT, then add the VAT.

    Labour for B = 22 × 38 = £836. Before VAT: 400 + 836 = £1236.

    With VAT: 1236 × 1.2 = £1483.20.

    Quote A is £1250 including VAT. Since £1250 is less than £1483.20, Quote A is cheaper, by £233.20.

    • M1 for 22 × 38 (= 836)
    • M1 for adding 400 and adding 20% VAT (or × 1.2) to get 1483.20
    • M1 for comparing like with like, both including VAT
    • C1 for stating that Quote A is cheaper (by £233.20), with the figures

    Everyday contexts

    Sport and salon questions: time, tickets, tips, wages and goals.

    Non-calculator Grade 4 A cut and colour takes 1 hour 45 minutes and starts at 10:20. What time does it finish? Type it in 24-hour time, like 12:05. Uses: Four operations

    1. Add the hours first, then the minutes.
    2. 10:20 plus 1 hour is 11:20. Now add the 45 minutes.
    3. Go up to 12:00 first. How many minutes is that from 11:20?
    • There are only 60 minutes in an hour. 65 minutes is 1 hour 5 minutes, so the hour goes up by one.
    • That is 10 minutes too many. From 10:20, count 40 minutes to 11:00, then 1 hour 5 minutes more.

    10:20 + 1 hour = 11:20. 11:20 + 40 minutes = 12:00, and 5 more minutes makes 12:05. So it finishes at 12:05.

    Non-calculator Grade 4 A football club sells 240 tickets for an away match. 3 over 8 of the tickets go to away fans. How many tickets go to away fans? Uses: Fractions

    1. What is the question asking you to find a fraction of?
    2. Divide 240 by the bottom number of the fraction.
    3. Multiply the answer by the top number.
    • That is 1 over 8 of 240. Multiply by 3 to get 3 over 8.
    • That is the tickets that do not go to away fans. The question asks for the away fans.

    240 ÷ 8 = 30 for each eighth. 3 × 30 = 90 tickets.

    Non-calculator Grade 5 Three stylists share a £60 tip pool in the ratio 2 : 3 : 1. How many pounds does the stylist with the biggest share get? Uses: Ratio

    1. How many parts is the pool shared into?
    2. Divide 60 by the total number of parts.
    3. The biggest share is the biggest number in the ratio.
    • That is one part. The biggest share is 3 parts.
    • That is the 2 in the ratio. The biggest number is 3.

    2 + 3 + 1 = 6 parts. 60 ÷ 6 = £10 for one part. The biggest share is 3 × 10 = £30. (The others get £20 and £10, and 20 + 30 + 10 = 60 ✓.)

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 4 A salon charges £28.50 for a cut and blow-dry. A group booking of 3 people gets 10% off the total. How much does the group pay? Type it like 12.50. Uses: Percentages

    1. What is the total before the discount?
    2. Find 10% of that total, then take it off.
    3. Or multiply the total by 0.9.
    • That is the total before the discount. Take off 10%.
    • That is the discount. The question asks how much the group pays.

    3 × 28.50 = £85.50. 10% of 85.50 = 8.55. 85.50 − 8.55 = £76.95.

    • M1 for 3 × 28.50 = 85.50
    • M1 for finding 10% (8.55) or multiplying by 0.9
    • A1 for 76.95

    Calculator Grade 4 Kofi's team scored these goals in 8 matches: 2, 0, 3, 1, 4, 2, 2, 1. Work out the mean number of goals per match. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. Uses: Averages

    1. What is the mean, and how many matches are there?
    2. Add up all the goals first.
    3. Divide the total by the number of matches, then round.
    • That is the total number of goals. Divide by the number of matches, 8.
    • That is the median or the mode. Add all the values and divide by 8 for the mean.

    Total goals: 2 + 0 + 3 + 1 + 4 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 15. Mean = 15 ÷ 8 = 1.875, which is 1.9 to 1 decimal place.

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 5 A stylist is paid £11.40 per hour for the first 35 hours in a week, and time and a half (1.5 times the rate) for every hour after that. Show that she earns £501.60 in a week when she works 41 hours. You must show your working. Uses: Percentages Uses: Four operations

    1. How many hours are paid at the normal rate, and how many at time and a half?
    2. Time and a half is 11.40 × 1.5 per hour.
    3. Add the normal pay and the extra-hours pay.

    Normal pay: 35 × 11.40 = £399. Overtime rate: 11.40 × 1.5 = £17.10 per hour. Overtime hours: 41 − 35 = 6, so 6 × 17.10 = £102.60. Total: 399 + 102.60 = £501.60, as the question says.

    • M1 for 35 × 11.40 (= 399)
    • M1 for 6 × 17.10 (= 102.60), or 6 × 11.40 × 1.5
    • C1 for 399 + 102.60 = 501.60, stated as the answer
