    Grade 7, 8 and 9

    Stretch papers for grades 7, 8 and 9

    Real-paper style questions for Higher tier students. Each is one question worth 3 to 6 marks, with no step-by-step prompts. Choose the method yourself, write your working on paper, then mark it against the mark scheme.

    Where you will use this: the last 20 minutes of revision before a mock exam, and any time you want to know whether a topic is really secure.

    Back to the pyramid
    GCSE Maths Book / Stretch papers

    How it works: read the question, write your answer on paper, then press the button to see the mark scheme. Tick every mark you earned. M marks are for a correct method, A marks for an accurate answer, B marks are independent marks (no method is needed), and C marks are for explaining clearly. A clue is there if you are stuck, but real exams do not give one, so try first. The ticks are counted on screen only.

    Some questions have a box for a final answer. Those mark the answer for you, and the mark scheme still shows where the method marks are. The tag on each question tells you whether a calculator is allowed.

    Number and proof

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 7 A plumber has a length of copper pipe that is 3 3 over 8 m long. She cuts 5 pieces from it, each 5 over 8 m long. Show that the piece left over is 1 over 4 m long. Uses: Fractions

    1. You need the total length of the 5 cut pieces. Then subtract it from the length of the pipe.
    2. It is easier to work with improper fractions than with a mixed number. Change the mixed number first.

    Total cut = 5 × 5 over 8 = 25 over 8 m.

    Length of pipe = 3 3 over 8 = 27 over 8 m.

    Left over = 27 over 8 − 25 over 8 = 2 over 8 = 1 over 4 m. So the piece left over is 1 over 4 m long, as required.

    • M1 for 5 × 5 over 8 = 25 over 8 (or 3 1 over 8)
    • M1 for writing 3 3 over 8 as 27 over 8 and subtracting
    • C1 for 2 over 8 simplified to 1 over 4, stated as the answer

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 9 Prove that the sum of the squares of any two consecutive odd numbers is always 2 more than a multiple of 8. Uses: Algebra basics Uses: Squares and roots

    1. You must prove it for every pair, so use letters, not examples.
    2. If n is a whole number, then 2n + 1 is always odd. What is the next odd number?
    3. Expand both squares, collect like terms, then try to take a multiple of 8 out.

    Let the two odd numbers be 2n + 1 and 2n + 3, where n is a whole number.

    (2n + 1)² + (2n + 3)² = 4n² + 4n + 1 + 4n² + 12n + 9 = 8n² + 16n + 10.

    8n² + 16n + 10 = 8(n² + 2n + 1) + 2.

    n² + 2n + 1 is a whole number, so 8(n² + 2n + 1) is a multiple of 8. The sum is therefore always 2 more than a multiple of 8.

    • B1 for two consecutive odd numbers written as 2n + 1 and 2n + 3 (or similar, such as 2n − 1 and 2n + 1)
    • M1 for expanding both brackets correctly
    • A1 for 8n² + 16n + 10
    • C1 for 8(n² + 2n + 1) + 2 with a conclusion that n² + 2n + 1 is a whole number, so this is 2 more than a multiple of 8

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 8 The highest common factor of two numbers is 6. Their lowest common multiple is 180. One of the numbers is 36. Find the other number. Uses: Factors and multiples

    1. Write 36 and 180 as products of prime factors.
    2. The HCF is made of the primes that both numbers share. The LCM contains every prime that appears in either number, counted the greatest number of times it appears in either one.
    • 5 is the prime that 36 is missing, but the other number also needs a 2 and a 3 to make the HCF 6.
    • That is 6 × 180. Check the HCF and LCM of 36 and 1080: they are not 6 and 180.

    36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 and 180 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5.

    The LCM has a 5, which 36 does not, so the other number has a 5. The HCF is 6 = 2 × 3, so the other number has only one 2 and one 3. It is 2 × 3 × 5 = 30.

    Check: the HCF of 36 and 30 is 6 and their LCM is 180. The other number is 30.

    (Another route: HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers, so 6 × 180 = 1080 and 1080 ÷ 36 = 30.)

    • M1 for prime factors of 36 and 180, or for 6 × 180 = 1080
    • M1 for a clear method: the missing factors are 5 and only one each of 2 and 3, or 1080 ÷ 36
    • A1 for 30

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 9 n is a positive whole number. Prove that n + 1 over n + 2 is always greater than n over n + 1. Uses: Fractions Uses: Algebra basics

    1. Try n = 1, 2, 3 to see the pattern. That is not a proof, but it shows what to aim for.
    2. To compare two fractions, subtract them. What is the common denominator?
    3. Show that the difference is always positive.

    n + 1 over n + 2 − n over n + 1 = (n + 1)(n + 1) − n(n + 2) over (n + 2)(n + 1)

    = n² + 2n + 1 − n² − 2n over (n + 1)(n + 2) = 1 over (n + 1)(n + 2).

    n is positive, so (n + 1)(n + 2) is positive and the difference is positive. So n + 1 over n + 2 is always greater than n over n + 1.

    • M1 for subtracting (or cross-multiplying) with common denominator (n + 1)(n + 2)
    • M1 for a correct numerator, (n + 1)² − n(n + 2), expanded
    • A1 for a difference of 1 over (n + 1)(n + 2)
    • C1 for stating that this is positive because n is positive, so the first fraction is greater

    Percentages and money

    [4 marks] Calculator Grade 7 An electrician's bill is £714 including VAT at 20%. The bill is for parts that cost £130 before VAT, plus labour at £62 an hour. VAT is charged on the whole bill. How many hours did the job take? Uses: Percentage change Uses: Percentages

    1. The £714 is 120% of the bill before VAT. Which number must you divide by?
    2. Once you know the bill before VAT, take away the parts to find what was charged for labour.
    • You took 20% off £714. The £714 already has VAT added, so divide by 1.2 instead.
    • You took the parts off before dealing with VAT. The VAT is on the whole bill, so deal with VAT first.

    Bill before VAT = 714 ÷ 1.2 = £595.

    Labour = 595 − 130 = £465.

    Hours = 465 ÷ 62 = 7.5 hours.

    • M1 for 714 ÷ 1.2
    • A1 for 595
    • M1 for (their 595) − 130, then dividing by 62
    • A1 for 7.5

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 7 A new van loses 15% of its value in its first year. In its second year it loses 10% of its value at the start of that year. After two years it is worth £11 475. Show that the van cost £15 000 when it was new. Uses: Percentage change

    1. Each year has its own multiplier. What are they?
    2. Multiply the two multipliers together to make a single one, then work backwards from the final value.

    Multipliers: 0.85 for the first year and 0.9 for the second.

    0.85 × 0.9 = 0.765, so the value after two years is 0.765 times the original.

    Original = 11 475 ÷ 0.765 = £15 000, as required.

    (Check: 15 000 × 0.765 = 11 475.)

    • M1 for multipliers 0.85 and 0.9 (or 85% and 90%)
    • M1 for 0.85 × 0.9 = 0.765 and dividing 11 475 by it
    • C1 for 15 000 shown, with the check or a clear conclusion

    [4 marks] Calculator Grade 8 £4000 is put into a savings account that pays compound interest at a fixed rate of r% a year. After 2 years the account holds £4410. Work out the value of r. Give a number only, with no % sign. Uses: Percentage change

    1. After 2 years, the money has been multiplied by the same multiplier twice. Call the multiplier m.
    2. Write an equation: 4000 × m² = 4410. Solve for m by dividing, then taking the square root.
    • That is the total interest over 2 years as a percentage of the £4000 (410 out of 4000). The rate is applied each year, so it is smaller.
    • That is half of 10.25, the total interest as a percentage. Compound interest means the second year earns interest on the first year's interest too, so the yearly rate is not exactly half of 10.25.

    4000 × (1 + r over 100)² = 4410.

    (1 + r over 100)² = 4410 ÷ 4000 = 1.1025.

    1 + r over 100 = √1.1025 = 1.05, so r = 5.

    Check: 4000 × 1.05 = 4200 and 4200 × 1.05 = 4410.

    • M1 for 4000 × (1 + r over 100)² = 4410 (or 4000 × m²)
    • M1 for 4410 ÷ 4000 = 1.1025
    • M1 for taking the square root: 1.05
    • A1 for r = 5

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 9 The price of a games console is increased by 20%. A week later the new price is reduced by x% so that the console costs its original price again. Find the exact value of x. Give your answer as a mixed number. Uses: Percentage change Uses: Fractions

    1. You are not told the original price. Call it P, or treat it as 1.
    2. The multiplier for a decrease of x% is 1 − x over 100. Build an equation for "increase then decrease gives back P".
    3. 1.2 is 6 over 5. Work with fractions.

    Let the original price be P. After the increase: 1.2P. After the decrease: 1.2P × (1 − x over 100).

    1.2P × (1 − x over 100) = P, so 1 − x over 100 = 1 over 1.2 = 5 over 6.

    x over 100 = 1 − 5 over 6 = 1 over 6, so x = 100 over 6 = 16 2 over 3.

    The reduction is 16 2 over 3%. (It is less than 20% because the new price is bigger, so a smaller percentage of it takes off the same amount of money.)

    • M1 for an increase multiplier 1.2 and a decrease multiplier (1 − x over 100) multiplied to give 1
    • M1 for 1 − x over 100 = 5 over 6 (or 1 over 1.2)
    • M1 for x over 100 = 1 over 6, so x = 100 over 6
    • A1 for 16 2 over 3

    Ratio and proportion

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 8 Sam and Tia share some sweets in the ratio 3 : 5. Sam then gives 12 of his sweets to Tia. Now Sam and Tia have sweets in the ratio 1 : 3. How many sweets are there altogether? Uses: Ratio Uses: Solving equations

    1. Let one part of the first ratio be k sweets. How many does each person have?
    2. After the gift, write each person's sweets using k. A ratio of 1 : 3 means Tia has 3 times as many as Sam.
    3. Form an equation in k and solve it.
    • 12 is the value of one part, k. There are 8 parts altogether.
    • 36 is how many sweets Sam started with. The question asks for the total.

    Sam starts with 3k sweets and Tia with 5k. After the gift Sam has 3k − 12 and Tia has 5k + 12.

    The ratio is 1 : 3, so 3(3k − 12) = 5k + 12.

    9k − 36 = 5k + 12, so 4k = 48 and k = 12.

    Total = 8k = 96 sweets. (Check: Sam 36 → 24, Tia 60 → 72, and 24 : 72 = 1 : 3.)

    • M1 for 3k and 5k, and 3k − 12 and 5k + 12
    • M1 for an equation such as 3(3k − 12) = 5k + 12
    • M1 for solving to find k = 12
    • A1 for 96

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 8 A decorator mixes paint. Paint A is blue and white in the ratio 2 : 3. Paint B is blue and white in the ratio 3 : 1. He mixes 10 litres of paint A with 20 litres of paint B. Find the ratio of blue paint to white paint in the mixture. Give your answer in its simplest form. Uses: Ratio

    1. Find how many litres of blue and how many of white there are in each paint on its own.
    2. Add the blues together and add the whites together.

    Paint A: 5 parts, so each part is 10 ÷ 5 = 2 litres. Blue = 2 × 2 = 4 litres, white = 3 × 2 = 6 litres.

    Paint B: 4 parts, so each part is 20 ÷ 4 = 5 litres. Blue = 3 × 5 = 15 litres, white = 1 × 5 = 5 litres.

    Total blue = 4 + 15 = 19 litres. Total white = 6 + 5 = 11 litres.

    Blue : white = 19 : 11 (19 is prime, so this cannot be simplified).

    • M1 for 4 litres blue and 6 litres white from paint A (or 10 ÷ 5 = 2)
    • M1 for 15 litres blue and 5 litres white from paint B (or 20 ÷ 4 = 5)
    • M1 for adding the blues and the whites
    • A1 for 19 : 11

    [4 marks] Calculator Grade 7 A builder makes concrete from cement, sand and gravel in the ratio 1 : 2 : 4 by volume. He needs 2.8 m³ of concrete. Cement costs £120 per m³, sand costs £36 per m³ and gravel costs £28 per m³. Work out the total cost of the cement, sand and gravel. Give your answer in pounds, with the pence as a decimal, for example 121.6 for £121.60. Uses: Ratio

    1. How many parts are there altogether? What volume is one part?
    2. Find the volume of each material, then multiply each by its price.
    • That uses 1, 2 and 4 m³ of the materials. That makes 7 m³, not 2.8 m³. Find the volume of one part first.
    • You matched the largest volume with the cement. In the ratio 1 : 2 : 4 the order is cement, sand, gravel.

    1 + 2 + 4 = 7 parts, so one part = 2.8 ÷ 7 = 0.4 m³.

    Cement 0.4 m³, sand 0.8 m³, gravel 1.6 m³.

    Cost = 0.4 × 120 + 0.8 × 36 + 1.6 × 28 = 48 + 28.8 + 44.8 = £121.6, that is £121.60.

    • M1 for 2.8 ÷ 7 (= 0.4)
    • A1 for 0.4, 0.8 and 1.6 m³
    • M1 for multiplying each volume by its cost per m³
    • A1 for 121.6 (£121.60)

    Mixed non-calculator challenge

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 7 3 over 7 of the students in a school are girls. One third of the girls walk to school. One quarter of the boys walk to school. Show that 2 over 7 of all the students walk to school. Uses: Fractions

    1. First find the fraction of the whole school that are boys.
    2. "One third of the girls" means 1 over 3 × 3 over 7. Do the same for the boys.

    Boys = 1 − 3 over 7 = 4 over 7 of the students.

    Girls who walk: 1 over 3 × 3 over 7 = 1 over 7. Boys who walk: 1 over 4 × 4 over 7 = 1 over 7.

    Together: 1 over 7 + 1 over 7 = 2 over 7, as required.

    • M1 for 4 over 7 for boys (1 − 3 over 7)
    • M1 for 1 over 3 × 3 over 7 = 1 over 7 and 1 over 4 × 4 over 7 = 1 over 7
    • C1 for adding to get 2 over 7, stated as the conclusion

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 7 Work out 2 1 over 4 ÷ (1 1 over 2 − 5 over 6). Give your answer as an improper fraction in its simplest form. Uses: Fractions Uses: Order of operations

    1. Brackets come first.
    2. Use a common denominator of 6 inside the bracket. Then change the mixed number to an improper fraction before dividing.
    • That is the value of the bracket. The question asks you to divide 2 1 over 4 by it.

    Bracket: 1 1 over 2 − 5 over 6 = 9 over 6 − 5 over 6 = 4 over 6 = 2 over 3.

    2 1 over 4 = 9 over 4, so 9 over 4 ÷ 2 over 3 = 9 over 4 × 3 over 2 = 27 over 8.

    Answer: 27/8 (which is 3 3 over 8).

    • M1 for the bracket as 9 over 6 − 5 over 6
    • M1 for 9 over 4 × 3 over 2 (multiplying by the reciprocal of the second fraction)
    • A1 for 27/8

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 8 n is the smallest positive whole number for which 360 × n is a square number. Find n. Uses: Factors and multiples Uses: Squares and roots

    1. Write 360 as a product of prime factors. Write each repeated prime out in full, such as 2 × 2 × 3.
    2. A square number has its prime factors in matching pairs. Which prime factors of 360 do not have a partner?
    • 360 × 360 is a square, but it is not the smallest. Only multiply by what is needed to pair up the leftovers.
    • 360 × 5 = 1800, which is not a square number. The prime factor 2 also needs a partner.

    360 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5.

    The 3s are a pair. Two of the 2s are a pair, but one 2 is left over, and the 5 has no partner. So n must include 2 × 5 = 10.

    360 × 10 = 3600 = 60², so n = 10.

    • M1 for 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5
    • M1 for identifying the unpaired 2 and 5
    • A1 for 10

    Algebra and equations

    Choose your own method. Write your working on paper, then check it against the mark scheme.

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 7 Prove that the difference between the squares of any two consecutive integers is always an odd number. Uses: Algebra basics

    1. Any integer can be called n. What is the next integer after it?
    2. Write the difference as the square of the next integer minus n squared, and expand the bracket.
    3. What do all even numbers have as a factor?

    Let the two consecutive integers be n and n + 1.

    (n + 1)² − n² = n² + 2n + 1 − n² = 2n + 1

    2n is a multiple of 2, so it is even. Adding 1 to an even number always gives an odd number, so the difference is always odd.

    • M1 for n and n + 1 (or equivalent) with the difference of squares set up
    • A1 for correct expansion and simplification to 2n + 1
    • C1 for a conclusion: 2n is even, so 2n + 1 is odd

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 8 Prove that the sum of any four consecutive integers is never a multiple of 4. Uses: Algebra basics

    1. Call the first integer n. Write the other three in terms of n.
    2. Add all four and collect like terms.
    3. Try to write your total as 4 × (an expression) + a remainder.

    Let the four consecutive integers be n, n + 1, n + 2 and n + 3.

    Sum = n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) + (n + 3) = 4n + 6

    4n + 6 = 4n + 4 + 2 = 4(n + 1) + 2

    4(n + 1) is a multiple of 4, so the sum is always 2 more than a multiple of 4. A remainder of 2 means it is never a multiple of 4.

    • M1 for four consecutive integers written algebraically, such as n, n + 1, n + 2, n + 3
    • M1 for adding them
    • A1 for 4n + 6, or 4(n + 1) + 2, or equivalent
    • C1 for a conclusion: a multiple of 4 plus 2 is never a multiple of 4

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 7 Plumber A charges a call-out fee of £45 plus £28 for each hour of work. Plumber B charges a call-out fee of £15 plus £33 for each hour of work. For how many hours of work do the two plumbers charge the same total? Type the number of hours. Uses: Solving equations

    1. Let h be the number of hours. Write the total cost for each plumber using h.
    2. The totals are the same, so put the two expressions equal to each other.
    3. Get all the h terms on one side and the numbers on the other.
    • That is 60 ÷ 5. The call-out fees should be subtracted (45 − 15), not added.
    • That is 45 − 15. You still need to divide by the difference in hourly rates.

    Plumber A: 45 + 28h. Plumber B: 15 + 33h.

    45 + 28h = 15 + 33h

    30 = 5h

    h = 6 hours. Check: A costs 45 + 168 = £213 and B costs 15 + 198 = £213.

    • M1 for an expression for each plumber, such as 45 + 28h and 15 + 33h
    • M1 for setting the expressions equal
    • M1 for collecting terms correctly, such as 30 = 5h
    • A1 for 6

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 9 y = 2x + 1 over x − 3. Make x the subject of the formula. Uses: Rearranging formulae

    1. x appears in the numerator and the denominator. First get rid of the fraction.
    2. Multiply out the bracket, then put every term with x on the same side.
    3. Factorise so that x appears only once.

    y(x − 3) = 2x + 1

    xy − 3y = 2x + 1

    xy − 2x = 3y + 1

    x(y − 2) = 3y + 1

    x = 3y + 1 over y − 2

    Check with x = 5: y = 11 ÷ 2 = 5.5, and (3 × 5.5 + 1) ÷ (5.5 − 2) = 17.5 ÷ 3.5 = 5. It works.

    • M1 for multiplying both sides by (x − 3)
    • M1 for expanding and collecting the x terms on one side, such as xy − 2x = 3y + 1
    • M1 for factorising to x(y − 2)
    • A1 for x = (3y + 1) ÷ (y − 2)

    Simultaneous and quadratic

    Work out what to call the unknowns, form the equations, then solve.

    [4 marks] Calculator Grade 7 A decorator buys 4 tins of paint and 3 rollers for £70.75. A week later she buys 2 tins of paint and 5 rollers for £50.25. All prices include VAT. Work out the cost of one tin of paint. Type your answer in pounds, such as 3.20. Uses: Simultaneous equations

    1. Let t be the cost of a tin and r the cost of a roller. Write two equations.
    2. Make the number of tins (or of rollers) the same in both equations, then subtract.
    3. Work out r first, then put it back in to find t.
    • That is the cost of a roller. The question asks for a tin of paint.
    • That is the cost of 4 tins. Divide by 4 to get one tin.

    4t + 3r = 70.75 and 2t + 5r = 50.25

    Double the second equation: 4t + 10r = 100.50

    Subtract the first: 7r = 29.75, so r = 4.25

    4t + 3 × 4.25 = 70.75, so 4t = 58 and t = 14.5, which is £14.50.

    Check in the other equation: 2 × 14.50 + 5 × 4.25 = 29 + 21.25 = 50.25.

    • M1 for two correct equations in two unknowns
    • M1 for a method to eliminate one unknown (equal coefficients, then add or subtract)
    • M1 for substituting back to find the other unknown
    • A1 for 14.5 (£14.50)

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 8 Show that the straight line y = 4x − 4 touches the curve y = x² at exactly one point, and write down the coordinates of that point. Uses: Quadratic equations Uses: Simultaneous equations

    1. A point on both graphs has the same y value on each. Put the two expressions for y equal.
    2. Rearrange to get a quadratic equal to 0 and factorise it.
    3. How many different solutions does it have? What does that tell you?

    Where the graphs meet, x² = 4x − 4.

    x² − 4x + 4 = 0

    (x − 2)(x − 2) = 0, so x = 2 and there is only one solution.

    y = 2² = 4. Check in the line: 4 × 2 − 4 = 4.

    One solution means the line meets the curve at one point only, so it touches the curve at (2, 4).

    • M1 for setting x² = 4x − 4
    • M1 for rearranging to x² − 4x + 4 = 0 and attempting to factorise
    • A1 for (x − 2)² = 0 giving x = 2, and y = 4
    • C1 for stating that one repeated solution means one point of contact, (2, 4)

    [5 marks] Non-calculator Grade 9 A rectangular garden is 12 m long and 9 m wide. A border of the same width, x metres, is dug all the way round the inside edge. The rectangular lawn that is left has an area of 70 m². Work out x. Type your answer in metres. Uses: Quadratic equations

    1. The border is on both sides, so how much shorter is the lawn than the garden in each direction?
    2. Write (length of lawn) × (width of lawn) = 70, then expand and rearrange to a quadratic equal to 0.
    3. Two solutions may come out. Does each one make sense for this garden?
    • That solution is impossible: the garden is only 9 m wide, so a border of 9.5 m cannot fit.
    • That is the number in the factor (2x − 19), not x. Solve 2x − 19 = 0, then think about whether that value fits.

    The lawn is (12 − 2x) m long and (9 − 2x) m wide.

    (12 − 2x)(9 − 2x) = 70

    108 − 42x + 4x² = 70

    4x² − 42x + 38 = 0, so 2x² − 21x + 19 = 0

    (2x − 19)(x − 1) = 0, so x = 9.5 or x = 1.

    x = 9.5 is impossible because the garden is only 9 m wide. So x = 1 m. Check: 10 × 7 = 70.

    • M1 for lawn sides (12 − 2x) and (9 − 2x), and setting their product equal to 70
    • M1 for expanding correctly to 4x² − 42x + 108 = 70
    • M1 for rearranging and factorising (or using the formula) to find x
    • A1 for x = 1 (and x = 9.5)
    • C1 for rejecting 9.5 with a reason (it is wider than the garden) and answering 1

    [4 marks] Calculator Grade 8 A roofer throws a roof tile up to a workmate on a scaffold. The height of the tile above the ground, h metres, after t seconds is given by h = 1.8 + 9t − 5t². The workmate misses it and it falls to the ground. How many seconds after it is thrown does the tile hit the ground? Give your answer to 2 decimal places. Uses: Quadratic equations

    1. What is the value of h when the tile reaches the ground?
    2. Rearrange to the form at² + bt + c = 0, keeping track of the signs, then use the quadratic formula.
    3. You get two answers. Only one makes sense for a time after the throw.
    • A time cannot be negative. Choose the other solution.
    • Check the sign of b in the formula. Here b = −9, so −b = 9.

    On the ground h = 0, so 1.8 + 9t − 5t² = 0, which is 5t² − 9t − 1.8 = 0.

    a = 5, b = −9, c = −1.8

    t = 9 ± √(81 + 36) over 10 = 9 ± √117 over 10

    t = 1.98 or t = −0.18 (to 2 decimal places).

    The time must be positive, so the tile hits the ground after 1.98 seconds.

    • M1 for setting h = 0
    • M1 for rearranging to a correct quadratic equal to 0 (such as 5t² − 9t − 1.8 = 0)
    • M1 for correct use of the quadratic formula with a = 5, b = −9, c = −1.8
    • A1 for 1.98 (the negative solution rejected)

    Angles and Pythagoras

    Look for a right angle before you use Pythagoras. Look for angles with a known total, such as 180° in a triangle, before you form an equation.

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 7 In triangle ABC, angle A = (2x + 10)°, angle B = (3x − 20)° and angle C = (x + 10)°. Show that triangle ABC is isosceles. Uses: Shapes and angles Uses: Solving equations

    1. What do the three angles of any triangle add up to?
    2. Form and solve an equation to find x.
    3. Work out all three angles, then say what you notice.

    Angles in a triangle add up to 180°.

    (2x + 10) + (3x − 20) + (x + 10) = 180

    6x = 180, so x = 30.

    A = 2 × 30 + 10 = 70°, B = 3 × 30 − 20 = 70°, C = 30 + 10 = 40°.

    Two angles are equal (A = B = 70°), so the triangle is isosceles.

    • M1 for adding the three angles and setting the total equal to 180
    • A1 for x = 30
    • M1 for substituting x to find the angles
    • C1 for 70°, 70°, 40° and a conclusion that two equal angles means isosceles

    [5 marks] Non-calculator Grade 8 A landscaper is laying a straight path across a rectangular lawn, from one corner to the opposite corner. The lawn is 24 m long and 10 m wide. He charges £35 for each metre of path, and then adds VAT at 20% to the total. Work out the total cost of the path including VAT. Type your answer in pounds, without the £ sign. Uses: Pythagoras Uses: Percentages

    1. The path is the longest side of a right-angled triangle. Find its length first.
    2. Then multiply by the price per metre. Do the VAT last.
    3. Adding 20% is the same as multiplying by 1.2.
    • That is the cost before VAT. Add 20% on top.
    • That uses 24 + 10 = 34 m for the path. The path goes straight across, so use Pythagoras.
    • That is only the VAT. The question asks for the total including VAT.

    Path² = 24² + 10² = 576 + 100 = 676, so the path is 26 m.

    Cost before VAT = 26 × 35 = £910.

    VAT = 20% of 910 = £182, so the total is 910 + 182 = £1092.

    (Or 910 × 1.2 = 1092.)

    • M1 for 24² + 10² (adding the squares)
    • A1 for 26 m
    • M1 for 26 × 35
    • M1 for adding 20% VAT (or multiplying by 1.2)
    • A1 for 1092

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 8 A triangular sign has two sides of 13 cm and a base of 10 cm. Show that the area of the sign is 60 cm². Uses: Pythagoras

    1. The area of a triangle needs the perpendicular height. You are not given it.
    2. Draw a line from the top straight down to the middle of the base. What kind of triangles do you get?
    3. Use Pythagoras in one of them to find the height.

    The perpendicular height splits the isosceles triangle into two equal right-angled triangles, each with base 5 cm and hypotenuse 13 cm.

    height² = 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144, so height = 12 cm.

    Area = 1 over 2 × 10 × 12 = 60 cm².

    • M1 for 13² − 5² using half the base, 5 cm (subtracting, because 13 is the hypotenuse)
    • A1 for height = 12 cm
    • M1 for 1 over 2 × 10 × 12
    • A1 for area 60 cm², with the units

    [5 marks] Calculator Grade 9 A surveyor measures the angle of elevation of the top of a chimney from point A as 28°. She walks 30 m directly towards the chimney, to point B, and measures the angle of elevation again as 41°. Points A and B and the base of the chimney are on level ground in a straight line. Work out the height of the chimney, in metres, to 1 decimal place. Uses: Trigonometry Uses: Rearranging formulae

    1. Call the height h and the distance from B to the chimney x. Write tan 41° and tan 28° in terms of h and x.
    2. The distance from A to the chimney is x + 30. Rearrange each equation to give a distance in terms of h.
    3. The two distances differ by 30. Form one equation in h only and solve it.
    • That is the distance from B to the chimney, not its height.
    • That treats 30 m as the distance to the chimney from B. It is the gap between A and B.

    Let the height be h. The distance from B to the chimney is h ÷ tan 41°. The distance from A is h ÷ tan 28°.

    The difference is 30: h over tan 28° − h over tan 41° = 30

    h(1.8807... − 1.1503...) = 30, so h × 0.7303... = 30

    h = 41.1 m (to 1 decimal place; the unrounded value is 41.07...).

    • M1 for tan 41° = h ÷ x (or equivalent) in a right-angled triangle
    • M1 for tan 28° = h ÷ (x + 30) (or equivalent)
    • M1 for forming one equation using the 30 m difference in distances
    • M1 for rearranging and solving for h
    • A1 for 41.1

    [4 marks] Calculator Grade 8 A builder's company has a rule: a ramp must make an angle of less than 5° with the ground. A ramp rises 0.45 m and its sloping surface is 4.8 m long. Show that the ramp breaks the rule. Uses: Trigonometry

    1. Label the sides: which is the hypotenuse? Which is opposite the angle?
    2. Which of sin, cos and tan uses opposite and hypotenuse?
    3. Use the inverse function to find the angle, then compare it with 5°.

    Let the angle with the ground be θ. The rise is opposite θ and the sloping surface is the hypotenuse.

    sin θ = 0.45 over 4.8 = 0.09375

    θ = sin−1(0.09375) = 5.4° (5.38° to 2 decimal places).

    5.4° is greater than 5°, so the ramp is too steep and breaks the rule.

    • M1 for sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse with 0.45 and 4.8
    • M1 for using the inverse sine on 0.45 ÷ 4.8
    • A1 for 5.4° (accept 5.38°)
    • C1 for a comparison: 5.4° is more than 5°, so the rule is broken

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 7 A carpenter is making the frame of a roof. The two sloping rafters are equal in length. They meet at the top, each makes an angle of 35° with the horizontal, and the width across the bottom of the frame is 7.2 m. Work out the length of one rafter, in metres, to 2 decimal places. Uses: Trigonometry

    1. Split the frame down the middle into two right-angled triangles. How wide is the base of each?
    2. The rafter is the hypotenuse and the 3.6 m side is adjacent to the 35° angle. Which of sin, cos and tan connects these?
    • That multiplies 3.6 by cos 35°. The rafter is the longest side, so it must be longer than 3.6 m: divide instead.
    • That uses sin 35°, which is the wrong ratio. The 3.6 m side is adjacent to the angle, so use cos.

    Half the width is 7.2 ÷ 2 = 3.6 m. In the right-angled triangle, 3.6 m is adjacent to the 35° angle and the rafter is the hypotenuse.

    cos 35° = 3.6 over rafter

    rafter = 3.6 over cos 35° = 4.39 m (to 2 decimal places).

    • M1 for halving the width to get 3.6 m
    • M1 for cos 35° = 3.6 ÷ rafter (or equivalent)
    • A1 for 4.39

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 7 How many integers satisfy the inequality x² − 3x − 10 < 0? Uses: Inequalities

    1. Start by solving x² − 3x − 10 = 0 to find where the graph crosses the x-axis.
    2. Sketch the graph. Is the part where it is below the axis between the roots or outside them?
    3. List the whole numbers in that range. Check carefully whether the end values are included.
    • That counts −2 and 5 as well. The inequality is < (strictly less than), so the end values are not included.
    • One of the end values has slipped in. Check that neither −2 nor 5 makes x² − 3x − 10 negative.

    x² − 3x − 10 = (x − 5)(x + 2), so the roots are x = 5 and x = −2.

    The graph is a U-shape, so it is below the x-axis between the roots: −2 < x < 5.

    The integers are −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4, which is 6 integers.

    • M1 for factorising (x − 5)(x + 2) or for roots −2 and 5
    • M1 for the inequality −2 < x < 5 (oe)
    • A1 for 6

    [3 marks] Calculator Grade 7 Two bottles are mathematically similar. The small bottle is 12 cm tall and holds 300 ml. The large bottle is 18 cm tall. How much does the large bottle hold, in ml? Uses: Similar shapes

    1. Find the scale factor for length first.
    2. Capacity is a volume. How does a volume change when every length is multiplied by k?
    3. Multiply the small capacity by the volume scale factor.
    • That multiplies by the length scale factor only. Volume scales with the cube of the scale factor.
    • That uses the scale factor squared, which is for areas. A volume needs the cube.

    Length scale factor = 18 ÷ 12 = 1.5.

    Volume scale factor = 1.5³ = 3.375.

    Large bottle = 300 × 3.375 = 1012.5 ml.

    • M1 for a length scale factor of 1.5 (or 3 over 2)
    • M1 for cubing it (3.375) and multiplying by 300
    • A1 for 1012.5 (accept 1013 or 1010 if the working shows 1012.5)

    [3 marks] Non-calculator Grade 7 A bag holds 5 red counters and 3 blue counters. Two counters are taken out at random, one after the other, without being put back. Work out the probability that both counters are the same colour. Type it as a fraction, such as 3/10. Uses: Conditional probability

    1. There are two ways to get the same colour. What are they?
    2. After the first counter is taken, how many counters are left in the bag, and how many of each colour?
    3. Multiply along each branch, then add the two branches.
    • That puts the counter back each time. Without replacement, the second fraction has a denominator of 7.
    • That is only the probability of two reds. Add the probability of two blues.

    P(red, red) = 5 over 8 × 4 over 7 = 20 over 56.

    P(blue, blue) = 3 over 8 × 2 over 7 = 6 over 56.

    P(same colour) = 20/56 + 6/56 = 26/56 = 13/28.

    • M1 for 5 over 8 × 4 over 7 or 3 over 8 × 2 over 7
    • M1 for adding the two products
    • A1 for 13 over 28 (oe, such as 26 over 56)

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 8 Show that 1 + √5 over 3 − √5 can be written as 2 + √5. Uses: Surds

    1. You need to get the surd out of the denominator. Which expression, multiplied by 3 − √5, gives a whole number?
    2. Multiply the numerator and the denominator by 3 + √5.
    3. Expand the numerator and the denominator separately, then simplify.

    Multiply the numerator and the denominator by 3 + √5:

    (1 + √5)(3 + √5) over (3 − √5)(3 + √5)

    Numerator: 3 + √5 + 3√5 + 5 = 8 + 4√5.

    Denominator: 9 − 5 = 4.

    8 + 4√5 over 4 = 2 + √5, as required.

    • M1 for multiplying numerator and denominator by 3 + √5
    • M1 for expanding the numerator to 8 + 4√5 (four terms correct)
    • A1 for a denominator of 9 − 5 = 4
    • C1 for dividing both terms by 4 to reach 2 + √5, stated as the result

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 8 A and B are points on a circle with centre O. The tangents at A and B meet at P and angle APB = 50°. C is a point on the major arc AB. Show that angle ACB = 65°. Give a reason for each step. Uses: Circle theorems

    1. Join O to A and O to B. What do you know about a tangent and the radius at the point where they meet?
    2. OAPB is a quadrilateral. Use its angles to find angle AOB.
    3. Angle AOB is at the centre and angle ACB is at the circumference. What is the connection?

    Angle OAP = angle OBP = 90° (a tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact).

    Angle AOB = 360° − 90° − 90° − 50° = 130° (angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360°).

    Angle ACB = 130° ÷ 2 = 65° (the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference, standing on the same arc AB). So angle ACB = 65°, as required.

    • B1 for angle OAP = angle OBP = 90°
    • M1 for 360 − 90 − 90 − 50
    • A1 for angle AOB = 130°
    • C1 for angle ACB = 65° with the reasons: tangent perpendicular to radius, angles in a quadrilateral add to 360°, and the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference

    [4 marks] Calculator Grade 8 (a) Show that the equation x³ + x − 5 = 0 has a solution between x = 1 and x = 2. (b) Use the iteration formula xn+1 = ∛(5 − xn) with x0 = 1.5 to work out x1 and x2, giving each to 3 decimal places. Uses: Iteration

    1. For part (a), work out the value of x³ + x − 5 at x = 1 and at x = 2. What do you hope to see?
    2. For part (b), put x0 = 1.5 into the formula to get x1.
    3. Use x1 (the full calculator value, not the rounded one) to get x2.

    (a) Let f(x) = x³ + x − 5. f(1) = 1 + 1 − 5 = −3 and f(2) = 8 + 2 − 5 = 5.

    There is a change of sign and f is continuous, so there is a solution between 1 and 2.

    (b) x1 = ∛(5 − 1.5) = ∛3.5 = 1.518 (to 3 decimal places).

    x2 = ∛(5 − 1.5182...) = 1.516 (to 3 decimal places).

    • M1 for evaluating f(1) = −3 and f(2) = 5
    • C1 for a change of sign, and f continuous, so a solution between 1 and 2
    • A1 for x1 = 1.518
    • A1 for x2 = 1.516

    [4 marks] Calculator Grade 8 A sprinter runs 120 m, measured to the nearest 10 m, in 14.2 seconds, measured to 1 decimal place. Work out the upper bound for her average speed, in metres per second, to 2 decimal places. Uses: Bounds and error intervals

    1. Write the lower and upper bounds of the distance and of the time.
    2. To make the speed as large as possible, do you want a large or a small time?
    3. Speed = distance ÷ time, so pair the biggest distance with the smallest time.
    • The distance is right, but the time is the wrong bound. A larger speed needs a smaller time, so use 14.15, not 14.25.
    • That is 120 ÷ 14.2, which is the speed using the numbers as given. The bounds change both values.

    Distance: 115 ≤ d < 125. Time: 14.15 ≤ t < 14.25.

    Upper bound of speed = upper bound of distance ÷ lower bound of time = 125 ÷ 14.15 = 8.83 metres per second (to 2 decimal places; 8.8339...).

    • B1 for lower bound of time 14.15

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 8 Solve 2 over x − 3 + 3 over x + 3 = 1. The equation has two solutions. Type the one that is greater than 0. Uses: Algebraic fractions

    1. Multiply every term by the common denominator (x − 3)(x + 3) to clear the fractions.
    2. Expand and collect everything on one side to get a quadratic equal to 0.
    3. Factorise the quadratic, then pick the positive solution.
    • That is the other solution. The question asks for the one greater than 0.
    • x = 3 would make a denominator zero, so it cannot be a solution. Solve the quadratic you get after clearing the fractions.

    Multiply by (x − 3)(x + 3): 2(x + 3) + 3(x − 3) = (x − 3)(x + 3).

    5x − 3 = x² − 9, so x² − 5x − 6 = 0.

    (x − 6)(x + 1) = 0, so x = 6 or x = −1. The solution greater than 0 is x = 6.

    Check: 2 over 3 + 3 over 9 = 1.

    • M1 for multiplying through by (x − 3)(x + 3), or combining over a common denominator
    • M1 for 2(x + 3) + 3(x − 3) = (x − 3)(x + 3) expanded
    • M1 for x² − 5x − 6 = 0 and factorising (x − 6)(x + 1)
    • A1 for 6

    [5 marks] Non-calculator Grade 9 O is the origin. Vector OA = a and vector OB = b. P is the point on AB with AP : PB = 2 : 1. C is the point with vector OC = a + 2b. Prove that O, P and C lie on a straight line. Uses: Vectors

    1. Write the vector AB in terms of a and b.
    2. AP is two thirds of AB. Then OP = OA + AP.
    3. To show three points are in a line, show that OP is a multiple of OC.

    AB = −a + b = b − a.

    AP = 2 over 3AB = 2 over 3(b − a).

    OP = OA + AP = a + 2 over 3(b − a) = 1 over 3a + 2 over 3b = 1 over 3(a + 2b).

    OC = a + 2b, so OP = 1 over 3OC. The vectors OP and OC are parallel and share the point O, so O, P and C lie on a straight line.

    • M1 for AB = b − a (oe)
    • M1 for AP = 2 over 3AB (oe)
    • A1 for OP = 1 over 3a + 2 over 3b (oe)
    • M1 for factorising to 1 over 3(a + 2b)
    • C1 for OP = 1 over 3OC, parallel with a common point O, so a straight line

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 9 f(x) = 2x + 3 and g(x) = x². Show that the equation fg(x) = gf(x) can be written as x² + 6x + 3 = 0. Uses: Functions and graphs

    1. fg(x) means g first, then f. Which comes first in gf(x)?
    2. Work out each composite function, expanding any bracket carefully.
    3. Put the two expressions equal to each other and collect to one side. Can you divide every term by the same number?

    fg(x) = f(x²) = 2x² + 3.

    gf(x) = g(2x + 3) = (2x + 3)² = 4x² + 12x + 9.

    Set them equal: 2x² + 3 = 4x² + 12x + 9, so 0 = 2x² + 12x + 6.

    Dividing by 2: x² + 6x + 3 = 0, as required.

    • M1 for fg(x) = 2x² + 3
    • M1 for gf(x) = (2x + 3)²
    • A1 for expanding to 4x² + 12x + 9
    • C1 for equating, collecting to 2x² + 12x + 6 = 0 and dividing by 2 to reach x² + 6x + 3 = 0

    [4 marks] Non-calculator Grade 9 A histogram shows the ages of people at a show. The bar for ages 0 to 10 is 1.5 cm tall and stands for 30 people. The bar for ages 10 to 30 is 2 cm tall. How many people are in the bar for ages 10 to 30? Uses: Histograms, cumulative frequency and box plots

    1. In a histogram it is the area of a bar, not its height, that shows the frequency.
    2. Work out the area of the first bar, then how many people each unit of area stands for.
    3. Find the area of the second bar and use the same scale.
    • That is the area of the second bar. You still need to turn area into a number of people using the first bar.
    • That multiplies 30 by the width ratio only (20 ÷ 10). The height is different too, so use areas.

    First bar: width 10, height 1.5, so area = 15. It stands for 30 people, so 1 unit of area is 2 people.

    Second bar: width 20, height 2, so area = 40.

    Number of people = 40 × 2 = 80.

    • M1 for area of first bar 10 × 1.5 = 15, or frequency density 30 ÷ 10 = 3 per year
