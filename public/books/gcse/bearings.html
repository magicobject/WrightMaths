    Bearings

    A bearing is a direction, written as an angle measured clockwise from north. Learn to read and write them, find the way back, draw journeys to scale, and use right-angled triangles to work out where you end up. Then run it backwards to find a bearing from a position.

    Where you will use this: a ship or a walker finding the way, a surveyor setting out the corners of a plot, and reading directions off a site plan.

    Bearing
    A direction, given as an angle measured clockwise from north and written with three figures.
    Back bearing
    The bearing of the way back. It is 180° different from the bearing you travelled on.
    Compass point
    One of the named directions: north, east, south and west, and between them north-east, south-east, south-west and north-west.

    Units and typing

    A bearing is an angle in degrees, measured clockwise from north. It is always written with three figures, so 45° becomes 045° and 5° becomes 005°. You can type either 045 or 45 in the answer box.

    Distances are in km or m. When an answer needs rounding, the question says how many decimal places. A bearing rounded to 1 decimal place looks like 053.1.

    Do not round until the last step. Keep the full number on your calculator, and check it is in degrees mode.

    How many degrees are there in a full turn?

    1. Think of turning all the way round until you face the same way again.
    2. It is the same as four quarter turns of 90°.
    • That is a half turn, a straight line. A full turn is twice as much.
    • That is a quarter turn, a right angle. A full turn is four of those.

    A full turn is 4 quarter turns: 4 × 90 = 360 degrees.

    Quick recap of earlier topics (skip if you are happy)

    Angles on a straight line add up to 180°. One angle is 65°. What is the other angle, in degrees?

    1. Angles on a straight line add up to a fixed total.
    2. Take the angle you know away from 180.
    • That looks like 90 − 65. A straight line is 180°, not 90°.
    • That looks like 180 + 65. Take 65 away from 180.

    The two angles add up to 180°. 180 − 65 = 115°.

    Two lines are parallel. A third line crosses both. One angle is 70°. What is the alternate angle, in degrees?

    1. Alternate angles are in a Z shape, between the parallel lines.
    2. Alternate angles on parallel lines are equal.
    • That is 180 − 70, a co-interior angle. Alternate angles are equal to each other.

    Alternate angles on parallel lines are equal, so the angle is 70°.

    A map has a scale of 1 : 50 000. Two paths are 3 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they in real life, in km?

    1. Multiply the distance on the map by 50 000 to get cm.
    2. Change cm to km: 100 000 cm is 1 km.
    • That is the distance in cm. Divide by 100 000 to change to km.
    • That is the distance in metres. Divide by 1000 to change to km.

    3 × 50 000 = 150 000 cm = 1500 m = 1.5 km.

    1. Compass points

    A compass has four main points: north (N), east (E), south (S) and west (W). Between them are north-east (NE), south-east (SE), south-west (SW) and north-west (NW). Each is a turn from north, going clockwise (the way a clock hand goes).

    • N is 000°, E is 090°, S is 180° and W is 270°. Each step is a quarter turn of 90°.
    • NE is 045°, SE is 135°, SW is 225° and NW is 315°. Each is half way between two main points.

    Key idea: a bearing is measured from north, turning clockwise. North itself is 000°, east is 090°, south is 180° and west is 270°.

    Worked example

    A boat sails due south-west. What is its three-figure bearing?

    1. South is 180° and west is 270°.
    2. South-west is half way between them: (180 + 270) / 2 = 225.
    3. The bearing is 225°.

    Now you finish it

    A walker heads north-west. What is her three-figure bearing?

    1. West is ___° and north is 360° (a full turn, the same as 000°).
    2. Half way between them is (___ + 360) / 2 = ___.
    Check my answer

    West is 270° and north is 360°. (270 + 360) / 2 = 315. The bearing is 315°.

    Check your knowledge: compass points

    What is the three-figure bearing of due west?

    1. Start at north, which is 000°. Turn clockwise. East is one quarter turn.
    2. West is three quarter turns from north.
    • That is east. West is on the other side, three quarter turns from north.
    • That is south, half a turn from north.

    West is three quarter turns clockwise from north: 3 × 90 = 270°.

    Which three-figure bearing is exactly half way between north (000°) and east (090°)?

    1. The bearing half way between two bearings is their average.
    2. Add 000 and 090, then divide by 2.
    • That is east. Half way between north and east is smaller than that.
    • That looks like adding 360 and 90. North is 000 here, so half way is (0 + 90) / 2.

    (0 + 90) / 2 = 45. As a three-figure bearing that is 045°. This is north-east.

    Which compass point is on a bearing of 225°?

    • South-west
    • South-east, because 225 is more than 180
    • North-west, because 225 is close to 270
    1. South is 180° and west is 270°.
    2. 225 is half way between them.

    225 is half way between 180 (south) and 270 (west), so the direction is south-west.

    A ferry sails due south-east. What is its three-figure bearing?

    1. East is 090° and south is 180°.
    2. South-east is half way between them.
    • That is north-west, the opposite way. South-east is between east and south.
    • That is south-west, between south and west. South-east is between east and south.

    (90 + 180) / 2 = 135. The bearing is 135°.

    2. Three-figure bearings

    A Bearing tells you the direction from one point to another. To give a bearing you follow three rules.

    • Start at the point you are travelling from, and draw a north line.
    • Measure the angle clockwise from the north line to the path.
    • Write it with three figures. Put zeros in front if you need them: 70° becomes 070°.

    "The bearing of B from A" means start at A. The words after "from" tell you where to start.

    Key idea: three rules: from north, clockwise, three figures. If the angle you can see is measured anticlockwise, take it away from 360° to get the bearing.

    Worked example: clockwise angle

    Find the bearing of B from A.

    1. The angle is measured clockwise from the north line at A. It is 70°.
    2. Write it with three figures: 070°.

    Worked example: anticlockwise angle

    Find the bearing of B from A.

    1. The angle 50° is measured anticlockwise from north, so it is not the bearing.
    2. A full turn is 360°. The bearing is 360 − 50 = 310°.
    3. Check: B is to the north-west of A, and 310 is between west (270) and north (360). ✓

    Now you finish it

    Find the bearing of B from A when the angle is measured anticlockwise from north and is 35°.

    1. Bearing = 360 − ___ = ___.
    Check my answer

    Bearing = 360 − 35 = 325. The bearing is 325°.

    Check your knowledge: three-figure bearings

    A bearing is always written with…

    • three figures, like 045
    • two figures, like 45 or 08, to save space
    • four figures with a decimal point, like 045.0, every time
    1. Think about how 5° and 45° and 145° would line up.
    2. They all use the same number of figures.

    A bearing is written with three figures: 005°, 045°, 145°. That gives every bearing the same number of figures, so none can be misread.

    Find the bearing of B from A, in degrees. The angle is marked.

    1. Look at where the angle starts and which way it turns.
    2. It starts at north and turns clockwise, so it is already the bearing.
    • That looks like 180 − 128. The marked angle is already measured clockwise from north, so it is the bearing.

    The angle is measured clockwise from north at A, so the bearing is 128°.

    Find the bearing of B from A, in degrees. The angle shown is measured anticlockwise from north.

    1. A bearing is always measured clockwise. Is the marked angle clockwise?
    2. Take the marked angle away from a full turn.
    • That is the anticlockwise angle. A bearing is measured clockwise, so use 360 − 40.
    • That looks like 180 − 40. A full turn is 360°, not 180°.

    360 − 40 = 320. The bearing of B from A is 320°.

    A walker on a hill heads off on a path that is 7° clockwise from north. What is her three-figure bearing? Type it like 007.

    1. The angle is already clockwise from north.
    2. Make it up to three figures by putting zeros in front.
    • That looks like a zero put at the end. Zeros for a three-figure bearing go at the front: 007.

    The angle is 7° clockwise from north. As three figures, that is 007°.

    A boat is 20° south of due west. West is 270° and south is 180°. What is the three-figure bearing of the boat? (Moving from west towards south means the angle gets smaller.)

    1. Picture the compass. Which way does the angle go if you turn from west towards south?
    2. Take 20 away from 270.
    • That is 270 + 20, which is 20° north of west. Going from west towards south makes the bearing smaller.

    Turning from west (270°) towards south (180°) makes the bearing smaller. 270 − 20 = 250°.

    3. Back bearings

    The bearing of B from A is not the same as the bearing of A from B. The way back has a different bearing. It is called the back bearing: you point exactly the opposite way, a half turn of 180°.

    • If the bearing is less than 180°, add 180.
    • If the bearing is 180° or more, subtract 180.

    Either way, the answer must end up between 000° and 360°.

    Key idea: the back bearing is the bearing plus or minus 180°. Add if you are below 180°, subtract if you are 180° or more, so you stay between 000° and 360°.

    Worked example: add 180

    The bearing of B from A is 065°. Find the bearing of A from B.

    1. 65 is less than 180, so add 180.
    2. 65 + 180 = 245.
    3. The bearing of A from B is 245°.

    Worked example: subtract 180

    The bearing of B from A is 285°. Find the bearing of A from B.

    1. 285 is more than 180, so subtract 180.
    2. 285 − 180 = 105.
    3. The bearing of A from B is 105°.

    Now you finish it

    A ship sails on a bearing of 130°. What bearing takes it back to where it started?

    1. 130 is less than 180, so ___ 180.
    2. 130 + 180 = ___.
    Check my answer

    130 is less than 180, so add 180: 130 + 180 = 310. The bearing back is 310°.

    Check your knowledge: back bearings

    The bearing of B from A is 040°. What is the bearing of A from B, in degrees?

    1. The bearing back is a half turn different: 180°.
    2. 040 is less than 180, so add.
    • That looks like 180 − 40. For a back bearing, add 180 to a bearing below 180.
    • That looks like 360 − 40. The back bearing is 180 away.

    40 is less than 180, so add 180: 40 + 180 = 220. The bearing of A from B is 220°.

    The bearing of B from A is 285°. What is the bearing of A from B, in degrees?

    1. 285 is more than 180, so adding 180 would go past 360.
    2. Subtract 180 instead.
    • That is 285 + 180. A bearing cannot be more than 360, so subtract 180 instead.

    285 is more than 180, so subtract 180: 285 − 180 = 105. The bearing of A from B is 105°.

    A ship sails from a port on a bearing of 135°. On what bearing must it sail to return to the port?

    1. The return journey points the opposite way.
    2. 135 is less than 180, so add 180.
    • That is 180 − 135, which is not the back bearing. Add 180 to 135.

    135 + 180 = 315. The ship must sail on a bearing of 315°.

    The bearing of a lighthouse from a boat is 072°. What is the bearing of the boat from the lighthouse?

    • 252°
    • 108°, because 180 − 72 = 108
    • 288°, because 360 − 72 = 288
    1. You want the bearing of the boat from the lighthouse, the way back.
    2. 072 is less than 180, so add 180.

    72 is less than 180, so add 180: 72 + 180 = 252. The bearing of the boat from the lighthouse is 252°.

    4. Bearings and scale drawings

    You can draw a journey to scale, using a ruler and a protractor, and then measure to find the answer. For each leg:

    • Mark the starting point and draw a north line through it.
    • Use the protractor to measure the bearing clockwise from north. Mark it and draw the line.
    • Measure the distance with a ruler, using the scale. Mark the end of the leg.
    • At the end, draw a new north line and repeat for the next leg.

    The scale tells you how long to draw each leg. With a scale of 1 cm to 2 km, a 6 km leg is 6 ÷ 2 = 3 cm on the drawing.

    Key idea: the scale works both ways. Real distance ÷ scale gives the drawing length. Drawing length × scale gives the real distance.

    Worked example

    A walker goes 6 km on a bearing of 040°, then 8 km on a bearing of 130°. Using a scale of 1 cm to 2 km, how long is each leg on the drawing?

    1. First leg: 6 km ÷ 2 = 3 cm, on a bearing of 040° from A.
    2. Second leg: 8 km ÷ 2 = 4 cm, on a bearing of 130° from B.
    3. The drawing is shown. A north line is drawn at A and at B.

    Now you finish it

    A boat goes 10 km on a bearing of 090°. Using a scale of 1 cm to 2 km, how long is the leg on the drawing, and in which direction does it go from the north line?

    1. Length on the drawing = 10 ÷ 2 = ___ cm.
    2. Bearing 090° is a ___ turn clockwise from north, so it goes straight to the east.
    Check my answer

    10 ÷ 2 = 5 cm. A bearing of 090° is a quarter turn clockwise from north, so the leg goes due east.

    Check your knowledge: bearings and scale drawings

    A scale drawing uses 1 cm to 2 km. A path is 7 km long in real life. How long is the path on the drawing, in cm?

    1. Real distance divided by the scale gives the drawing length.
    2. How many 2 km are there in 7 km?
    • That is 7 × 2. The drawing is smaller than real life, so divide by 2, not multiply.

    7 ÷ 2 = 3.5 cm.

    A scale drawing uses 1 cm to 5 km. A ship's path measures 6.4 cm on the drawing. How long is the real path, in km?

    1. Drawing length times the scale gives the real distance.
    2. Multiply 6.4 by 5.
    • That is 6.4 ÷ 5. The real path is bigger than the drawing, so multiply by 5.

    6.4 × 5 = 32 km.

    The scale drawing shows a walk from A to B and then B to C. The scale is 1 cm to 2 km. The path from B to C is 4 cm on the drawing. How long is the real path from B to C, in km?

    1. Find the drawing length of the leg from B to C.
    2. Multiply it by the scale: 2 km for each cm.
    • That is 4 ÷ 2. The real path is bigger than the drawing, so multiply by 2.
    • That is 4 + 2, adding the scale. A scale multiplies.

    The leg from B to C is 4 cm on the drawing. 4 × 2 = 8 km.

    In the same scale drawing, the bearing of C from B is 130° and the bearing of B from A is 040°. What is the bearing of A from B, in degrees?

    1. A from B is the way back along the first leg.
    2. The bearing of B from A is 040, which is less than 180.
    • That is the bearing of C from B, the next leg. You want the way back to A.
    • That looks like 180 − 40. For a back bearing, add 180.

    The back bearing of 040° is 040 + 180 = 220°. So the bearing of A from B is 220°.

    You are drawing a journey with a protractor. What do you do first at the start of each leg?

    • Draw a north line at the starting point
    • Measure the distance with a ruler and mark the end point first, before any angles are drawn
    • Measure the bearing anticlockwise from the east
    1. Bearings are measured from a particular line.
    2. Which compass direction is the starting line for a bearing?

    A bearing is measured clockwise from north. So you first draw a north line through the start point, then measure the angle from it.

    5. Using a bearing to find where you end up

    Suppose you travel a distance on a bearing. How far north have you gone, and how far east? Draw the journey. A line due north from the start and a line due east to the finish make a right-angled triangle.

    For a bearing between 000° and 090°, the angle at the start between the north line and your path is the bearing itself. The path is the hypotenuse. So you can use SOH CAH TOA:

    • The north distance is next to the angle (adjacent). Use cos: north = distance × cos(bearing).
    • The east distance is across from the angle (opposite). Use sin: east = distance × sin(bearing).

    Key idea: from north, cos gives the north distance and sin gives the east distance. Put your calculator in degrees mode, and round only at the end.

    Worked example

    A ship sails 20 km on a bearing of 040°. How far north and how far east has it gone? Give each to 1 decimal place.

    1. The path is the hypotenuse (20 km). The angle at A is 40°.
    2. North (adjacent) = 20 × cos 40° = 15.320… ≈ 15.3 km.
    3. East (opposite) = 20 × sin 40° = 12.855… ≈ 12.9 km.

    Now you finish it

    A walker goes 10 km on a bearing of 060°. How far east does she go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place.

    1. East is opposite the angle, so use sin.
    2. East = 10 × sin ___° = ___ km.
    Check my answer

    East = 10 × sin 60° = 8.660… ≈ 8.7 km.

    Check your knowledge: using a bearing

    A ship sails 50 km on a bearing of 030°. How far north does it go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in km.

    1. The north distance is next to the angle at the start.
    2. Next to the angle and the hypotenuse is given: which ratio uses those two?
    • That is 50 × sin 30°, the east distance. North is next to the angle, so use cos.
    • That looks like 50 ÷ cos 30°. The 50 km is the hypotenuse, so multiply by cos.

    North = 50 × cos 30° = 43.30… ≈ 43.3 km.

    A hiker walks 80 m on a bearing of 025°. How far east does she go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m.

    1. East is across from the angle at the start.
    2. Across from the angle, with the hypotenuse given: use the sine ratio.
    • That is 80 × cos 25°, the north distance. East is opposite the angle, so use sin.
    • That looks like sin 25° on its own. Multiply it by the 80 m.

    East = 80 × sin 25° = 33.809… ≈ 33.8 m.

    A drone flies 120 m on a bearing of 065°. How far east does it go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m.

    1. The east distance is the side across from the bearing angle.
    2. The 120 m is the hypotenuse. Which ratio links opposite and hypotenuse?
    • That is 120 × cos 65°, the north distance. East is opposite the angle, so use sin.

    East = 120 × sin 65° = 108.757… ≈ 108.8 m.

    A cyclist rides 5 km due north and then 12 km due east. How far is she from her start, in a straight line, in km?

    1. Draw the two legs. They meet at a right angle.
    2. The straight line back is the hypotenuse: use Pythagoras.
    • That is 5 + 12. The straight line back is shorter, because it cuts the corner. Use Pythagoras.
    • That is 12 − 5. The straight-line distance is the hypotenuse of the triangle.

    Distance² = 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169. Distance = the square root of 169 = 13 km.

    A ship sails 20 km on a bearing of 040°. Which calculation gives how far north it goes?

    • 20 × cos 40
    • 20 × sin 40, because the north distance is opposite the 40° angle
    • 20 ÷ cos 40, because you always divide by the angle
    1. Draw the north line and the east line to make a right-angled triangle.
    2. Is the north side next to the 40° angle, or across from it?

    The north side is next to the 40° angle, so it is the adjacent side. adjacent = hypotenuse × cos(angle) = 20 × cos 40° = 15.3 km.

    Flip it: run it backwards

    So far you knew the bearing and the distance, and found how far north and east you went. Now go the other way: you know how far north and east you went, and you find the distance and the bearing.

    Forwards

    bearing 040°, distance 20 km, north and east = ?

    Use cos and sin: 15.3 km north and 12.9 km east.

    Backwards

    6 km north, 8 km east, distance and bearing = ?

    Use Pythagoras for the distance and tan−1 for the angle.

    • The distance is the hypotenuse: distance = the square root of (north² + east²).
    • The bearing angle is opposite the east side and next to the north side, so tan(bearing) = east ÷ north. Use tan−1 to find the angle.
    • Write the answer as a three-figure bearing, like 053.1°.

    Key idea: to go from distances back to a bearing, use tan−1(east ÷ north). Put east on top, as the numerator, because east is opposite the angle from north.

    Worked example

    A walker goes 6 km north and then 8 km east. Find the distance from her start to her finish, and the bearing of the finish from the start. Give the bearing to 1 decimal place.

    1. Distance² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100, so the distance is 10 km.
    2. tan(bearing) = east ÷ north = 8 ÷ 6.
    3. bearing = tan−1(8 ÷ 6) = 53.13…° ≈ 53.1°.
    4. As a three-figure bearing: 053.1°.

    Now you finish it

    A hiker goes 5 km north and then 12 km east. Find the distance from her start, and find the bearing of the finish from the start, giving the bearing to 1 decimal place.

    1. Distance² = 5² + 12² = ___, so the distance is ___ km.
    2. bearing = tan−1(___ ÷ ___) = ___°.
    Check my answer

    Distance² = 25 + 144 = 169, so the distance is 13 km. Bearing = tan−1(12 ÷ 5) = 67.38…° ≈ 67.4°, which is 067.4°.

    Check your knowledge: flip it

    A drone flies 7 m north and then 24 m east. How far is it from where it started, in m?

    1. The two legs meet at a right angle. The straight line is the hypotenuse.
    2. Add the squares of the two legs, then take the square root.
    • That is 7 + 24. The straight line cuts the corner, so use Pythagoras.
    • That is 7² + 24², before the square root. Take the square root to get the distance.

    Distance² = 7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625. Distance = the square root of 625 = 25 m.

    The same drone ends 7 m north and 24 m east of its start. Find the bearing of the drone from its start. Give your answer to 1 decimal place, as a three-figure bearing, like 053.1.

    1. East is opposite the angle from north, and north is next to it.
    2. Use tan: tan(bearing) = east ÷ north. Then use tan−1 on your calculator.
    • That is tan−1(7 ÷ 24). The east distance goes on top, because east is opposite the angle from north.

    tan(bearing) = 24 ÷ 7 = 3.428…. Bearing = tan−1(3.428…) = 73.74…° ≈ 73.7°. As a three-figure bearing, 073.7°.

    A cyclist rides 10 km north and then 10 km east. What is the three-figure bearing of her finish from her start?

    1. Work out east ÷ north.
    2. What angle has a tan of that number? Use tan−1 on your calculator.
    • That would be due east. She has gone as far north as east, so the bearing is half way between 000 and 090.

    tan(bearing) = 10 ÷ 10 = 1. tan−1(1) = 45°. As a three-figure bearing, 045°.

    A walker finishes on a bearing of 045° from her start. She went 12 km east. How far north did she go, in km? (Hint: what is tan 45°?)

    1. tan(bearing) = east ÷ north. What is tan 45°?
    2. Rearrange to find north.
    • That is tan 45°. Use it in tan(bearing) = east ÷ north, with east = 12.
    • That looks like 12 × 1.414…, the distance walked in a straight line. The question asks for the north distance.

    tan 45° = 1, so east ÷ north = 1. The north distance equals the east distance, so north = 12 km.

    6. Two-step bearings problems Higher

    For a bearing of more than 090°, the right-angled triangle is the same, but the angle in it is not the bearing. Find the angle from the nearer north or south line first.

    BearingAngle to useDirections
    000° to 090°the bearingnorth and east
    090° to 180°180 − bearingsouth and east
    180° to 270°bearing − 180south and west
    270° to 360°360 − bearingnorth and west

    In a journey with two legs, back bearings and the angles at each corner often show that the triangle has a right angle. Then you can use Pythagoras and trigonometry.

    Key idea: draw the triangle, find the angle inside it (not the bearing itself), then use sin, cos or tan. At the end, turn the angle back into a three-figure bearing.

    Worked example: a bearing over 090

    A ship sails 30 km on a bearing of 130°. How far south and how far east has it gone? Give each to 1 decimal place.

    1. 130° is south-east. The angle from the south line is 180 − 130 = 50°.
    2. South (adjacent) = 30 × cos 50° = 19.28… ≈ 19.3 km.
    3. East (opposite) = 30 × sin 50° = 22.98… ≈ 23.0 km.

    Worked example: a two-leg journey

    A boat sails 6 km on a bearing of 040° from A to B. Then it sails 8 km on a bearing of 130° from B to C. Find the distance AC, and find the bearing of C from A, giving the bearing to 1 decimal place.

    1. The back bearing of A from B is 040 + 180 = 220°. The angle ABC = 220 − 130 = 90°, so the triangle ABC has a right angle at B.
    2. AC² = 6² + 8² = 100, so AC = 10 km.
    3. Angle BAC = tan−1(8 ÷ 6) = 53.13…° ≈ 53.1°.
    4. The bearing of C from A = 040 + 53.1 = 093.1°.

    Now you finish it

    A ship sails 5 km due east (090°) and then 12 km due south (180°). Find the distance from its start, and find the bearing of the finish from the start, giving the bearing to 1 decimal place.

    1. The two legs meet at a right angle. Distance² = 5² + 12² = ___, so the distance is ___ km.
    2. The angle below the east line at A = tan−1(12 ÷ 5) = ___°.
    3. Bearing = 090 + ___ = ___°.
    Check my answer

    Distance² = 25 + 144 = 169, so 13 km. The angle below east is tan−1(12 ÷ 5) = 67.38…° ≈ 67.4°. Bearing = 090 + 67.4 = 157.4°.

    Check your knowledge: two-step problems Higher

    Higher A plane flies 100 km on a bearing of 150°. How far south does it go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in km.

    1. 150° is south-east. Find the angle from the south line.
    2. South is next to that angle, with the hypotenuse given: use cos.
    • That is the east distance, 100 × sin 30°. South uses cos of the angle from the south line.

    The angle from the south line is 180 − 150 = 30°. South = 100 × cos 30° = 86.60… ≈ 86.6 km.

    Higher A ship sails 40 km on a bearing of 250°. How far west does it go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in km.

    1. 250° is south-west. The angle from the south line is the bearing minus 180.
    2. West is opposite that angle: use sin.
    • That is the south distance, 40 × cos 70°. West is opposite the angle from the south line, so use sin.

    The angle from the south line is 250 − 180 = 70°. West = 40 × sin 70° = 37.58… ≈ 37.6 km.

    Higher A walker goes 25 km on a bearing of 310°. How far north does she go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in km.

    1. 310° is north-west. The angle from the north line is 360 minus the bearing.
    2. North is next to that angle: use cos.
    • That is the west distance, 25 × sin 50°. North is next to the angle from the north line, so use cos.

    The angle from the north line is 360 − 310 = 50°. North = 25 × cos 50° = 16.06… ≈ 16.1 km.

    Higher A walker goes 3 km north and then 4 km west. Find the bearing of her finish from her start. Give your answer to 1 decimal place, as a three-figure bearing.

    1. Find the angle between the north line and the path first. It is west of north.
    2. tan of that angle is west ÷ north. Then take the angle away from 360.
    • That is the angle west of north. A bearing is measured clockwise, so take it away from 360°.
    • That is tan−1(3 ÷ 4). The west distance goes on top: west is opposite the angle from north.

    The angle west of north = tan−1(4 ÷ 3) = 53.13…° ≈ 53.1°. Bearing = 360 − 53.1 = 306.9°.

    Spot the mistake

    Finding an error in someone else's working helps you learn. For each one, decide what went wrong.

    Fay says: the angle from north to the path is 40°, measured anticlockwise, so the bearing is 040°. What went wrong?

    • She measured anticlockwise. It is 360 − 40 = 320°
    • Nothing is wrong. It is 40 degrees from north
    • She should have measured from east instead of north, so the bearing is 130°
    1. Which way does a bearing turn from north?
    2. If the angle goes the other way round, take it away from a full turn.

    Bearings are measured clockwise. An angle of 40° anticlockwise from north is 360 − 40 = 320° clockwise, so the bearing is 320°.

    Gus says: the back bearing of 130° is 130° − 180° = −50°. What went wrong?

    • 130 is below 180, so add 180: 310°
    • Nothing is wrong. A bearing of −50° is allowed
    • He should have taken 130 from 360, to get 230°
    1. Could a bearing ever be negative?
    2. Add or subtract 180: which one stays between 000 and 360?

    130 is less than 180, so add 180: 130 + 180 = 310°. Subtracting would give a negative number, which cannot be a bearing.

    Hal says: a ship sailing 20 km on a bearing of 040° goes 20 × sin 40° = 12.9 km north. What went wrong?

    • North is next to the angle, so use cos: 15.3 km
    • Nothing is wrong. Sine always gives the north distance
    • He should have used tan 40°, which gives 16.8 km
    1. Draw the triangle. Which side is next to the 40° angle at the start?
    2. Next to the angle and the hypotenuse is given: which ratio is that?

    The north line is next to the 40° angle (adjacent). adjacent = hypotenuse × cos(angle) = 20 × cos 40° = 15.3 km. The 12.9 km is the east distance.

    Check your knowledge: mixed review

    A walker goes 9 km due east and then 12 km due north. How far is she from her start, in km?

    1. East and north meet at a right angle.
    2. Use Pythagoras. The straight line is the hypotenuse.
    • That is 9 + 12. The straight line back cuts the corner. Use Pythagoras.

    Distance² = 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225. Distance = the square root of 225 = 15 km.

    A map has a scale of 1 : 20 000. A path is 8 cm long on the map. How long is the real path, in km?

    1. Multiply 8 by 20 000 to get cm.
    2. Change cm to km: 100 000 cm is 1 km.
    • That is the length in cm. Divide by 100 000 to change to km.
    • That looks like a slip with the zeros. 160 000 cm is 1.6 km.

    8 × 20 000 = 160 000 cm = 1600 m = 1.6 km.

    From a hilltop A, the bearing of a church B is 070° and the bearing of a farm C is 130°. What is the size of angle BAC, in degrees?

    1. Both bearings are measured clockwise from the same north line at A.
    2. The angle between the two paths is the difference of the bearings.
    • That is 70 + 130. The angle between them is the difference, not the sum.

    Angle BAC = 130 − 70 = 60°.

    A ship has sailed 3 over 5 of a 250 km trip. How many km are left?

    1. If 3 over 5 is done, what fraction is left?
    2. Find that fraction of 250.
    • That is 3 over 5 of 250, the distance already sailed. The question asks what is left.

    2 over 5 is left. 2 over 5 of 250 = 250 ÷ 5 × 2 = 100 km.

    Check your knowledge: on the job

    Surveying. A surveyor stands at point A. A corner of a plot, B, is 45 m away from A on a bearing of 060°. How far east of A is B? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m.

    1. Draw the right-angled triangle with a north line and an east line.
    2. East is opposite the 60° angle, and 45 m is the hypotenuse.
    • That is 45 × cos 60°, the north distance. East is opposite the angle from north, so use sin.

    East = 45 × sin 60° = 38.97… ≈ 39.0 m.

    Electrical. A cable runs in a straight line across a large floor space from a junction box to a new socket. The socket is 18 m north and 24 m east of the junction box. Twin and earth cable costs £1.80 per metre. How much does the cable cost? Give your answer in pounds, like 12.50.

    1. Find the length of the cable: the straight line across the 18 m and 24 m legs.
    2. Multiply the length in metres by £1.80.
    • That is the length of the cable in metres. Multiply by £1.80 to find the cost.
    • That looks like 42 × 1.80, adding the two legs. The cable goes straight across, so use Pythagoras.

    Length² = 18² + 24² = 324 + 576 = 900. Length = 30 m. Cost = 30 × 1.80 = £54.00.

    Plumbing. On a site plan, north is up the page. A pipe run is 12 m long and goes on a bearing of 070°. How far north does the pipe go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m.

    1. Draw the triangle with a north line and an east line.
    2. North is next to the 70° angle, with the pipe as the hypotenuse.
    • That is 12 × sin 70°, the east distance. North is next to the angle, so use cos.

    North = 12 × cos 70° = 4.104… ≈ 4.1 m.

    Setting out. From peg A, peg B is on a bearing of 112°. An engineer at peg B wants to look back at peg A. What bearing should she look on, in degrees?

    1. Looking back is the opposite direction: a half turn, 180°.
    2. 112 is below 180. Add or subtract?
    • That is 180 − 112, which is not the back bearing. Add 180 to a bearing below 180.
    • That looks like 360 − 112. For a back bearing, add 180.

    112 is less than 180, so add 180: 112 + 180 = 292°.

    Navigation. A boat leaves a harbour and sails 15 km north and then 20 km east. What is the bearing of the boat from the harbour? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, as a three-figure bearing, like 053.1.

    1. Draw the triangle. The legs are 15 km north and 20 km east.
    2. tan(bearing) = east ÷ north. Use tan−1 on your calculator.
    • That is tan−1(15 ÷ 20). East goes on top: east is opposite the angle from north.

    tan(bearing) = 20 ÷ 15 = 1.333…. Bearing = tan−1(1.333…) = 53.13…° ≈ 53.1°. As a three-figure bearing, 053.1°.

    Exam style

    Use these like a real paper. The marks tell you how much to write. A calculator is fine here.

    Not sure what a question word means? Question words explained.

    Write your working on paper, then type the answer.

    [3 marks] A ship sails 24 km north and then 7 km east. Work out the straight-line distance from its start to its finish, in km.

    1. The north and east legs meet at a right angle.
    2. Add the squares of the two legs.
    3. Take the square root.
    • That is 24 + 7, the distance along the two legs. The straight line is shorter: use Pythagoras.
    • That is the sum of the squares. Take the square root to find the distance.

    Distance² = 24² + 7² = 576 + 49 = 625.

    Distance = the square root of 625 = 25 km.

    • M1 for 24² + 7² (576 + 49)
    • M1 for the square root of 625
    • A1 for 25

    [4 marks] The same ship sails 24 km north and then 7 km east. Work out the bearing of the ship from its start. Give your answer to 1 decimal place, as a three-figure bearing, like 053.1.

    1. East is opposite the angle from north, and north is next to it.
    2. tan(bearing) = east ÷ north.
    3. Use tan−1 on your calculator, then write it with three figures.
    • That is tan−1(24 ÷ 7). East goes on top: east is opposite the angle from north.

    tan(bearing) = 7 ÷ 24 = 0.2916….

    Bearing = tan−1(0.2916…) = 16.26…° ≈ 16.3°.

    As a three-figure bearing, 016.3°.

    • M1 for tan(bearing) = 7 ÷ 24 (east over north)
    • M1 for tan−1(7 ÷ 24)
    • A1 for 16.3
    • B1 for writing the bearing with three figures, 016.3

    [3 marks] A, B and C are three points. The bearing of B from A is 072°. The bearing of C from B is 162°. Work out the size of angle ABC, in degrees.

    1. Angle ABC is between the line from B back to A and the line from B to C.
    2. Find the bearing of A from B first (a back bearing).
    3. Take the bearing of C from B away from it.
    • That is the bearing of C from B, not the angle ABC. Angle ABC is between the paths B to A and B to C.
    • That is the bearing of B from A. Angle ABC is at B, between the paths to A and to C.

    The bearing of A from B = 072 + 180 = 252°.

    Angle ABC = 252 − 162 = 90°.

    • M1 for the bearing of A from B: 072 + 180 = 252
    • M1 for 252 − 162
    • A1 for 90

    Higher [4 marks] A boat sails 6 km on a bearing of 040° from A to B. It then sails 8 km on a bearing of 130° from B to C. Show that C is 10 km from A.

    1. Find angle ABC first. It uses the back bearing of A from B.
    2. Say what kind of triangle ABC is, then use Pythagoras.

    The bearing of A from B is 040 + 180 = 220°. So angle ABC = 220 − 130 = 90°. Triangle ABC has a right angle at B.

    AC² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100.

    AC = the square root of 100 = 10 km.

    • C1 for the bearing of A from B: 040 + 180 = 220, so angle ABC = 220 − 130
    • M1 for angle ABC = 90°, so AC² = 6² + 8²
    • M1 for AC² = 36 + 64 = 100
    • A1 for AC = 10 km, shown

    Higher [4 marks] The boat in the last question sails 6 km on a bearing of 040° from A to B, then 8 km on a bearing of 130° from B to C. There is a right angle at B. Work out the bearing of C from A. Give your answer to 1 decimal place, as a three-figure bearing.

    1. Angle BAC is in the right-angled triangle. Which sides do you know?
    2. tan(angle BAC) = opposite ÷ adjacent = BC ÷ AB.
    3. The bearing of C is the bearing of B plus angle BAC.
    • That is angle BAC. The bearing of C from A is measured from north, so add the bearing of B (040).
    • That is 040 + tan−1(6 ÷ 8). BC (8 km) is opposite angle BAC, so it goes on top.

    Angle BAC = tan−1(8 ÷ 6) = 53.13…° ≈ 53.1°.

    The bearing of C from A = 040 + 53.1 = 093.1°.

    • M1 for tan(angle BAC) = 8 ÷ 6
    • M1 for angle BAC = 53.1°
    • M1 for 040 + 53.1
    • A1 for 093.1

    Why it works

    • Why three figures? A bearing is a number of degrees from 000 to 360. Always writing three figures keeps them the same length, so 045 can never be mixed up with 450 or 4.5.
    • Why north? Everyone agrees where north is. Measuring from the same line, the same way round (clockwise), means two people give the same bearing for the same journey.
    • Why add or subtract 180 for a back bearing? The two north lines are parallel. The path from A to B crosses them both. The angle at A (the bearing) and the angle at B between north and the path are co-interior angles, so they add to 180°. The back bearing turns that angle into a bearing, which is why it always differs by exactly 180.
    • Why cos for north and sin for east? The bearing angle is measured from the north line. So the north side is next to the angle (cos) and the east side is across from it (sin). If you measured from east instead, it would be the other way round.
    • Why tan−1 backwards? When you know both sides but not the angle, the tan ratio (opposite ÷ adjacent) turns into the angle with the inverse button.

    Common traps

    • Measuring the angle anticlockwise. Bearings go clockwise from north.
    • Forgetting the zeros in front: 45° must be written 045°.
    • Mixing up "bearing of B from A" with "bearing of A from B". Start at the point after the word "from".
    • Adding 180 to a bearing already above 180, and ending up over 360.
    • Using sin for north and cos for east. From the north line, north is cos and east is sin.
    • Putting north on top in tan. The bearing angle is opposite the east side, so use east ÷ north.
    • Using the bearing itself as the angle in the triangle when the bearing is over 090°.

    Going further

    A walker leaves camp A and walks 5 km on a bearing of 090°. She turns right through 120° (so her bearing goes up by 120°) and walks 5 km. She turns right through 120° again and walks 5 km. What is the bearing of her third leg, in degrees? (Where does she end up?)

    1. Her new bearing after a right turn is the old bearing plus the turn.
    2. Do it twice: 090 plus 120, then plus 120 again.
    • That is the bearing of the second leg. She turns right twice, so add 120 again.
    • That is 090 + 120 + 120 + 120. Only two turns have happened before the third leg.

    Leg 1: 090°. After the first turn, leg 2: 090 + 120 = 210°. After the second turn, leg 3: 210 + 120 = 330°. The three legs make an equilateral triangle, so she ends up back at camp.

    Two lighthouses are 5 km apart, with L2 due east of L1. A ship is seen from L1 on a bearing of 045° and from L2 on a bearing of 315°. How far is the ship from L1? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in km.

    1. Find the angles of the triangle L1, L2 and the ship. East from L1 is 090°, and 045° is half way to north.
    2. Work out all three angles. What kind of triangle is it?
    3. The ship is at a right angle. L1 to L2 is the hypotenuse, so use cos 45°.
    • That is half the distance between the lighthouses. The ship is at the top of the triangle, 5 × cos 45° from L1.
    • That is 5 × 1.414…. The 5 km is the hypotenuse, so multiply by cos 45°, which is less than 1.

    At L1 the angle between the direction L1 to L2 (east, 090°) and the direction L1 to the ship (045°) is 45°. At L2 the angle between the direction L2 to L1 (west, 270°) and the direction L2 to the ship (315°) is 45°. The angle at the ship is 180 − 45 − 45 = 90°. L1 to L2 (5 km) is the hypotenuse. L1 to the ship = 5 × cos 45° = 3.535… ≈ 3.5 km.

    Higher A boat leaves a port P and sails 12 km on a bearing of 060° to B. It then sails 5 km on a bearing of 150° to C. Work out the bearing of the port P from C. Give your answer to 1 decimal place.

    1. Look for a right angle at B using the back bearing of P from B.
    2. Find the bearing of C from P first, then think about the way back.
    3. The bearing of C from P is 060 plus the angle at P in the right-angled triangle.
    • That is the bearing of C from P. The question asks for the bearing of P from C, the way back, so add 180.
    • That is the angle at P, tan−1(5 ÷ 12). Add the bearing of B (060) first, then find the back bearing.

    The bearing of P from B is 060 + 180 = 240°. Angle PBC = 240 − 150 = 90°. Angle BPC = tan−1(5 ÷ 12) = 22.62…°. The bearing of C from P = 060 + 22.62 = 82.62°. The bearing of P from C is the back bearing: 82.62 + 180 = 262.62…° ≈ 262.6°.
