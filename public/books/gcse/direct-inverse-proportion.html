    Direct and inverse proportion

    Some things rise together, like the cost of cable and its length. Others pull against each other, like the number of workers and the days a job takes. Learn to tell them apart, find the rule that links them, and use the rule to find a missing value.

    Where you will use this: scaling a recipe or a concrete mix, working out how long a job takes with more or fewer people, and pricing cable or the running cost of a heater.

    Words you need

    Tap a teal word anywhere on the page to see this again.

    Direct proportion
    Two quantities are in direct proportion when one is always the same multiple of the other. When one doubles, so does the other. The rule is y = kx, and the graph is a straight line through the origin.
    Inverse proportion
    Two quantities are in inverse proportion when one is halved as the other is doubled. Their product is always the same. The rule is y = k ÷ x, and the graph is a curve that falls and never touches the axes.
    Constant of proportionality
    The fixed number, usually called k, that links two quantities that are in proportion. In direct proportion k = y ÷ x. In inverse proportion k = x × y.
    Unitary method
    Find the value of one unit first, then multiply to get the amount you want.

    Typing and units

    The letter k stands for the constant of proportionality, the fixed number that links the two quantities.

    Type a rule like y = 4x as 4x (the y = is optional). Type a rule like y = 12 ÷ x as 12/x. Money answers have two decimal places, like 12.50.

    Where a question gives the units, type the number only.

    1. Direct proportion

    Two amounts are in Direct proportion when one is always the same multiple of the other. If one doubles, so does the other. If one is cut in half, so is the other. The cost of cable is a good example: cable at £3 per metre.

    Cable at £3 per metre
    Length x (m)1234
    Cost y (£)36912

    Divide each cost by its length: 3 ÷ 1 = 3, 6 ÷ 2 = 3, 9 ÷ 3 = 3. The answer is always 3. That fixed number is the Constant of proportionality. We call it k. Here k = 3, because every metre costs £3.

    y = k × x    so    k = y ÷ x

    Key idea: in direct proportion, y ÷ x never changes. The rule is y = kx, and k is the value of ONE unit of x.

    That is the Unitary method from the Ratio page: find the value of one, then multiply. In direct proportion, the value of one is k.

    Worked example: using the unitary method

    5 m of cable cost £12. Find the cost of 8 m.

    1. Cost per metre: k = 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4, so 1 m costs £2.40.
    2. Cost of 8 m = 2.4 × 8 = 19.2.
    3. The cost is £19.20.
    4. Check: 8 m is more than 5 m, so the cost should be more than £12. ✓

    Worked example: finding the rule

    y is directly proportional to x. When x = 6, y = 15. Find k, write the rule, then find y when x = 10.

    1. k = y ÷ x = 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5.
    2. The rule is y = 2.5x.
    3. When x = 10, y = 2.5 × 10 = 25.

    Now you finish it

    4 m of pipe cost £14. Find the cost of 10 m.

    1. Cost per metre: k = 14 ÷ 4 = ___.
    2. Cost of 10 m = ___ × 10 = £___.
    Check my answer

    14 ÷ 4 = 3.5, so 1 m costs £3.50. 3.5 × 10 = 35, so 10 m costs £35.00.

    2. The graph and the rule y = kx

    Plot a table of direct proportion and the points lie on a straight line through the Origin, the point (0, 0). That makes sense: 0 metres of cable costs £0. The Gradient of the line is k.

    Key idea: direct proportion gives a straight line through (0, 0). The steeper the line, the bigger k. A straight line that does NOT pass through the origin is not direct proportion.

    Here is a straight line that is not direct proportion. A taxi charges £4 to start, then £2 for each mile. At 0 miles you still pay £4, so the graph starts above the origin. Doubling the miles does not double the fare.

    Worked example: from a graph

    A water butt fills at a steady rate. The graph is a straight line through (0, 0) and (5, 40), where x is minutes and y is litres. Find the rule, then the litres after 12 minutes.

    1. The line goes through the origin, so it is direct proportion.
    2. k = y ÷ x = 40 ÷ 5 = 8 litres per minute.
    3. The rule is y = 8x.
    4. After 12 minutes, y = 8 × 12 = 96 litres.

    Now you finish it

    A straight line through the origin also passes through (3, 18). Write its rule and find y when x = 5.

    1. k = 18 ÷ 3 = ___.
    2. The rule is y = ___x. When x = 5, y = ___ × 5 = ___.
    Check my answer

    k = 18 ÷ 3 = 6. The rule is y = 6x. When x = 5, y = 6 × 5 = 30.

    3. Inverse proportion

    In Inverse proportion, when one amount is doubled the other is halved. More workers means fewer days. A faster tap means less time to fill. In every example on this page, assume everyone works at the same steady speed.

    Tiling a hall: workers and days
    Workers x12346
    Days y2412864

    This time, multiplying is what stays fixed: 1 × 24 = 24, 2 × 12 = 24, 3 × 8 = 24, 4 × 6 = 24, 6 × 4 = 24. The fixed number, 24, is k. It is the total amount of work, measured in worker-days.

    y = k ÷ x    so    k = x × y

    Key idea: direct proportion: y ÷ x is fixed. Inverse proportion: x × y is fixed.

    The graph of inverse proportion is a smooth curve that falls. It never touches either axis, because no number of workers finishes the job in 0 days.

    Worked example: workers and days

    6 workers take 8 days to tile a hotel lobby. How long would 4 workers take?

    1. Find the total work: k = 6 × 8 = 48 worker-days.
    2. Share it between 4 workers: 48 ÷ 4 = 12.
    3. It takes 4 workers 12 days.
    4. Check: fewer workers, so more days. 12 is more than 8. ✓

    Worked example: flow rate

    A hose delivering 15 litres per minute fills a water butt in 12 minutes. How long would a hose delivering 20 litres per minute take?

    1. The butt holds k = 15 × 12 = 180 litres.
    2. Time = 180 ÷ 20 = 9.
    3. It takes 9 minutes. A faster flow gives a shorter time. ✓

    Now you finish it

    5 plumbers take 12 hours to fit all the radiators in a block. How long would 3 plumbers take?

    1. Total work: k = 5 × 12 = ___ plumber-hours.
    2. Time for 3 plumbers = ___ ÷ 3 = ___ hours.
    Check my answer

    5 × 12 = 60 plumber-hours. 60 ÷ 3 = 20, so 3 plumbers take 20 hours.

    Check your knowledge: inverse proportion

    y is inversely proportional to x. When x = 4, y = 6. Work out k.

    2. Work out 4 times 6.
    • That looks like 6 ÷ 4. For inverse proportion, multiply x and y.
    • That looks like 4 + 6. Multiply x and y to get k.

    k = x × y = 4 × 6 = 24.

    y = 36 ÷ x. Work out y when x = 9.

    1. Put x = 9 into the rule.
    2. Work out 36 ÷ 9.
    • That looks like 36 × 9. The rule divides 36 by x.
    • That looks like 36 − 9. The rule divides 36 by x.

    y = 36 ÷ 9 = 4.

    4 painters take 9 days to paint a block of flats. How many days would 6 painters take, working at the same rate?

    2. Then divide by the 6 painters.

    • That is the total painter-days. Divide by 6 to get the number of days.

    Total work = 4 × 9 = 36 painter-days. For 6 painters: 36 ÷ 6 = 6 days.

    4. Telling them apart

    When you are given a table, test both ideas.

    • Divide each y by its x. If you always get the same number, it is direct proportion.
    • Multiply each x by its y. If you always get the same number, it is inverse proportion.
    • If neither is fixed, it is neither.

    When you are given a graph, look at its shape.

    • A straight line through the origin is direct proportion.
    • A smooth falling curve that never touches the axes is inverse proportion.
    • A straight line that misses the origin is neither.

    Worked example: test a table

    Is each table direct proportion, inverse proportion or neither?

    1. x = 2, 4, 5 and y = 10, 20, 25. Divide: 10 ÷ 2 = 5, 20 ÷ 4 = 5, 25 ÷ 5 = 5. Always 5, so direct proportion with k = 5.
    2. x = 2, 4, 8 and y = 12, 6, 3. Multiply: 2 × 12 = 24, 4 × 6 = 24, 8 × 3 = 24. Always 24, so inverse proportion with k = 24.
    3. x = 1, 2, 3 and y = 5, 7, 9. Divide: 5, 3.5, 3. Multiply: 5, 14, 27. Neither is fixed, so it is neither.

    Now you finish it

    x = 3, 6, 9 and y = 12, 6, 4. Is this direct proportion, inverse proportion or neither?

    1. Try dividing: 12 ÷ 3 = ___, 6 ÷ 6 = ___. These are not the same.
    2. Try multiplying: 3 × 12 = ___, 6 × 6 = ___, 9 × 4 = ___.
    Check my answer

    Dividing gives 4, 1 and 0.44…, not fixed. Multiplying gives 36, 36 and 36, so it is inverse proportion with k = 36.

    5. Recipes and scaling

    Scaling a recipe is direct proportion. Work out the scale factor, which is the new number of people divided by the old number. Multiply every ingredient by it.

    • Ingredients such as flour, milk and eggs scale with the number of people.
    • Some things do not scale: the oven temperature, the size of the tin and often the cooking time.
    • You cannot buy half a bag or half a tin. When you need a whole number of something, round up so you have enough.

    Worked example: scaling up

    A recipe for 4 people uses 300 g of flour, 2 eggs and 200 ml of milk. Write the amounts for 10 people.

    1. Scale factor = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5.
    2. Flour: 300 × 2.5 = 750 g.
    3. Eggs: 2 × 2.5 = 5.
    4. Milk: 200 × 2.5 = 500 ml.

    Now you finish it

    The same recipe (300 g of flour and 200 ml of milk for 4 people) is made for 6 people. Find the flour and the milk.

    1. Scale factor = 6 ÷ 4 = ___.
    2. Flour = 300 × ___ = ___ g. Milk = 200 × ___ = ___ ml.
    Check my answer

    Scale factor = 1.5. Flour = 300 × 1.5 = 450 g. Milk = 200 × 1.5 = 300 ml.

    Check your knowledge: recipes and scaling

    A pancake recipe for 6 people uses 240 g of flour. How much flour is needed for 9 people? Answer in grams.

    1. Find the flour for 1 person first, or find the scale factor.
    2. The scale factor is 9 ÷ 6.
    • That is the amount for 4 people. The question asks for 9 people.
    • That looks like 240 × 6. Divide by 6 first to get the amount for 1 person.

    Scale factor = 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5. Flour = 240 × 1.5 = 360 g.

    A recipe for 4 people uses 2 eggs. How many eggs does it need for 10 people?

    1. How many eggs does 1 person need?
    2. Multiply by 10.
    • That looks like adding 6 people, or 2 + 6. Scale up by multiplying.
    • That looks like 2 × 10. First divide by the 4 people to get the eggs for 1 person.

    1 person needs 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5 eggs. 10 people need 0.5 × 10 = 5 eggs.

    A concrete mix needs 7 bags of cement for every 1 m³. A job needs 2.5 m³. How many whole bags must be bought?

    1. First find how many bags 2.5 m³ needs, ignoring the whole-bag problem.
    2. Then decide whether to round up or down. Can you buy part of a bag?
    • That is the exact amount. You cannot buy half a bag, so round up to have enough.
    • That rounds down. 17 bags would be a little short, so round up.

    7 × 2.5 = 17.5 bags. You cannot buy half a bag, and 17 would not be enough, so buy 18 bags.

    A recipe is doubled. Which amount does NOT simply double?

    • The time in the oven
    • The number of eggs
    • The weight of flour in grams
    1. Ingredients scale up. What else is in a recipe that is not an ingredient?
    2. A bigger cake does not usually need twice as long.

    Ingredients are in direct proportion to the number of servings. The oven time depends on the shape and thickness, so it does not simply double.

    1. Each litre covers 12 m². How many lots of 12 are in 78?
    2. Divide 78 by 12.

    • That is 12 ÷ 78, the wrong way round. Divide 78 by 12.

    78 ÷ 12 = 6.5 litres.

    A recipe for 8 people uses 600 ml of stock. You only have 450 ml of stock. How many people can you cook for?

    1. Find how much stock 1 person needs.
    2. Then see how many lots of that fit into 450 ml.
    • That is the number the recipe is written for. You have less stock than the recipe needs.
    • That is the wrong way round. You have less stock than the recipe needs, so you can feed fewer than 8 people.

    1 person needs 600 ÷ 8 = 75 ml. 450 ÷ 75 = 6, so you can cook for 6 people.

    6. Proportional to a square Higher

    Sometimes y is proportional to the square of x. Write it y ∝ x², and the rule is y = kx². If x doubles, x² is 4 times bigger, so y is 4 times bigger. If x is 3 times bigger, y is 9 times bigger.

    A car’s braking distance is a good example. Suppose the braking distance d metres is proportional to the square of the speed v mph. At 20 mph the braking distance is 6 m.

    Key idea: to use y = kx², find k first. Then square the new x BEFORE multiplying by k.

    Worked example: braking distance

    The braking distance d (m) is proportional to v², where v is the speed in mph. When v = 20, d = 6. Find d when v = 40.

    1. The rule is d = kv².
    2. Use the first pair: 6 = k × 20² = 400k, so k = 6 ÷ 400 = 0.015.
    3. When v = 40: d = 0.015 × 40² = 0.015 × 1600 = 24.
    4. The braking distance is 24 m. Check: the speed doubled, so the distance is 4 times as big: 6 × 4 = 24. ✓

    Worked example: finding x

    y is proportional to x². When x = 2, y = 20. Find y when x = 5, and find x when y = 80.

    1. The rule is y = kx². From the first pair, 20 = 4k, so k = 5.
    2. When x = 5: y = 5 × 25 = 125.
    3. When y = 80: 5x² = 80, so x² = 16 and x = 4 (taking the positive value, since x is a length or speed).

    Now you finish it

    y is proportional to x². When x = 3, y = 18. Find y when x = 4.

    1. 18 = k × 3², so k = 18 ÷ ___ = ___.
    2. y = ___ × 4² = ___ × 16 = ___.
    Check my answer

    k = 18 ÷ 9 = 2. y = 2 × 16 = 32.

    Check your knowledge: proportional to a square Higher

    y = 3x². Work out y when x = 4.

    1. Square the x first.
    2. Then multiply by 3.
    • That squares (3 × 4). Square the 4 first, then multiply by 3.
    • That looks like 3 × 4 × 2. Squaring 4 means 4 × 4.

    4² = 16, then 3 × 16 = 48.

    y is proportional to x². When x = 5, y = 50. Work out k.

    1. Put the pair into y = kx².
    2. k = y ÷ x². Square the 5 first.
    • That is 50 ÷ 5. You need to divide by x², which is 25.

    50 = k × 5² = 25k, so k = 50 ÷ 25 = 2.

    y is proportional to x². When x = 2, y = 12. Find y when x = 6.

    1. Find k from the first pair: 12 ÷ 2².
    2. Then work out k × 6².
    • That treats it as direct proportion (y = kx). Here y depends on x².
    • That multiplies by 6² without dividing by 2² first to find k. Find k first.

    k = 12 ÷ 4 = 3. y = 3 × 6² = 3 × 36 = 108.

    y = 2x² and x is positive. Work out x when y = 72.

    1. Put y = 72 into the rule: 72 = 2x².
    2. Divide by 2 to get x², then take the square root.
    • That is x². Take the square root to get x.
    • That looks like 72 × 2. Divide by 2, then take the square root.

    72 = 2x², so x² = 36 and x = 6.

    The cost of a square tile is proportional to the square of its side length. A tile with a side of 10 cm costs £2. How much does a tile with a side of 30 cm cost? Give your answer in pounds, like 18.00.

    1. Write the rule as cost = k × side².
    2. Find k from the 10 cm tile, then use it for 30 cm.
    • That treats cost as direct proportion to the side. It depends on the side squared.
    • That is k. Multiply k by 30² to find the cost.

    k = 2 ÷ 10² = 0.02. Cost = 0.02 × 30² = 0.02 × 900 = 18, so £18.00.

    y is proportional to x². If x is doubled, what happens to y?

    • It is multiplied by 4
    • It is doubled
    • It is multiplied by 8, as it would be for a cube
    1. If x = 3, x² = 9. What is x² if x = 6?
    2. Compare the two squares.

    (2x)² = 4x², so y is multiplied by 4.

    y is inversely proportional to x². When x = 3, y = 4. Find y when x = 6.

    1. The rule is y = k ÷ x². Find k from x = 3 and y = 4.
    2. Then work out k ÷ 6².
    • That halves y because x doubled, but y depends on x². Doubling x makes y four times smaller.
    • That is k. Divide k by 6² to find y.

    k = y × x² = 4 × 9 = 36. When x = 6: y = 36 ÷ 36 = 1.

    Flip it: run it backwards

    So far you knew the rule and used it to find a missing value. Now go the other way. You are given two values, and you have to find the rule first.

    Forwards

    y = 5x, x = 8, y = ?

    Multiply: y = 5 × 8 = 40.

    Backwards

    x = 8, y = 40, rule = ?

    Divide: k = 40 ÷ 8 = 5, so y = 5x.

    • Direct: k = y ÷ x, and the rule is y = kx. To find x from y, divide: x = y ÷ k.
    • Inverse: k = x × y, and the rule is y = k ÷ x. To find x from y, divide k by y: x = k ÷ y.

    Key idea: finding k is always the first step. Then you can use the rule either way round: to find y from x, or to find x from y.

    Worked example: direct proportion

    y is directly proportional to x. When x = 3, y = 18. Find the rule, then y when x = 7, then x when y = 60.

    1. k = 18 ÷ 3 = 6, so the rule is y = 6x.
    2. When x = 7: y = 6 × 7 = 42.
    3. When y = 60: x = 60 ÷ 6 = 10.

    Worked example: inverse proportion

    y is inversely proportional to x. When x = 3, y = 8. Find the rule, then x when y = 2.

    1. k = 3 × 8 = 24, so the rule is y = 24 ÷ x.
    2. When y = 2: 2 = 24 ÷ x, so x = 24 ÷ 2 = 12.
    3. Check: 12 × 2 = 24. ✓

    Now you finish it

    y is directly proportional to x. When x = 4, y = 10. Find the rule, then x when y = 25.

    1. k = 10 ÷ 4 = ___, so y = ___x.
    2. x = 25 ÷ ___ = ___.
    Check my answer

    k = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5, so y = 2.5x. When y = 25: x = 25 ÷ 2.5 = 10.

    Check your knowledge: flip it

    y is directly proportional to x. When x = 7, y = 21. Write the rule for y, like 4x.

    1. Find k = y ÷ x first.
    2. Write the rule as y = kx.
    • That looks like 7 ÷ 21. Divide y by x, not x by y.
    • That is k. Write the rule with x in it, like 3x.

    k = 21 ÷ 7 = 3. The rule is y = 3x.

    y is inversely proportional to x. When x = 8, y = 5. Write the rule for y, like 12/x.

    1. Find k by multiplying x and y.
    2. The rule is y = k ÷ x.
    • That multiplies x by 40. In inverse proportion the rule divides: y = k ÷ x.
    • That is k. Write the rule with x in it, like 40/x.

    k = x × y = 8 × 5 = 40. The rule is y = 40 ÷ x.

    y = 4x. Work out x when y = 36.

    1. Put y = 36 into the rule: 36 = 4x.
    2. Undo the multiplying by 4.
    • That looks like 4 × 36. To find x, divide y by 4.
    • That looks like 36 − 4. The 4 multiplies x, so divide to undo it.

    36 = 4x, so x = 36 ÷ 4 = 9.

    y = 60 ÷ x. Work out x when y = 12.

    1. Put y = 12 into the rule: 12 = 60 ÷ x.
    2. x × y = 60, so x = 60 ÷ y.
    • That looks like 60 × 12. In y = 60 ÷ x, x is 60 ÷ y.
    • That looks like 60 − 12. Divide 60 by y.

    12 = 60 ÷ x, so x = 60 ÷ 12 = 5.

    A 6 m board costs £15. The price is in direct proportion to the length. What length of board costs £22.50? Answer in metres.

    1. Find the cost of 1 metre first.
    2. Then see how many lots of that make £22.50.
    • That multiplies by 2.5. To find the length from the cost, divide by the cost per metre.
    • That is the cost of 1 metre. Divide £22.50 by it to find the length.

    1 m costs 15 ÷ 6 = £2.50. Length = 22.50 ÷ 2.50 = 9 m.

    4 workers take 9 days to resurface a car park. How many workers are needed to finish it in 3 days? Assume everyone works at the same rate.

    1. Work out the total work in worker-days.
    2. Divide by the number of days you want.
    • That looks like 9 × 3. Work out 4 × 9 = 36 worker-days, then divide by 3 days.
    • That is the total worker-days. Divide by 3 days to find the workers.

    Total work = 4 × 9 = 36 worker-days. Workers needed = 36 ÷ 3 = 12.

    Spot the mistake

    Finding an error in someone else’s working helps you learn. For each one, decide what went wrong.

    Dan says: 4 workers take 6 days, so 8 workers take 12 days. What went wrong?

    • He doubled the days, but they should be halved
    • Nothing is wrong, because more workers need more days
    • He should have added 4 days for 4 more workers
    1. If more people share a job, does it take longer or shorter?
    2. Work out the total worker-days, then divide by 8.

    4 × 6 = 24 worker-days. With 8 workers: 24 ÷ 8 = 3 days. Dan used direct proportion, but this is inverse proportion.

    Eve says: y is directly proportional to x, and y = 10 when x = 4. So k = 4 ÷ 10 = 0.4. What went wrong?

    • She divided x by y, not y by x
    • k should be found by multiplying 4 and 10
    • Nothing is wrong, because k = x ÷ y in direct proportion
    1. The rule for direct proportion is y = kx. Rearrange it to get k.
    2. k = y ÷ x.

    k = y ÷ x = 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5. Eve divided the wrong way round.

    Ida says: a taxi charges £3 to start, then £2 for each mile. 5 miles cost £13, so 10 miles cost double, £26. What went wrong?

    • She treated it as direct proportion, but the £3 is a fixed start charge
    • Nothing is wrong, because 10 is double 5
    • She should have found the cost per mile by dividing 13 by 5, then multiplied that by 10
    1. Does 0 miles cost £0?
    2. Work out the fare for 10 miles using £3 plus £2 for each mile.

    10 miles cost 3 + 2 × 10 = £23. The fare is not directly proportional to the miles, because its graph does not pass through the origin.

    Check your knowledge: mixed review

    Mixed up on purpose, like a real paper. Work out which idea fits.

    8 bags of cement cost £68. Which method finds the cost of 12 bags?

    • Find 1 bag first, then times 12
    • Multiply £68 by 8, then divide by 12
    • Add 4 to £68
    1. More bags cost more money, so this is direct proportion.
    2. Find the cost of 1 bag, then multiply.

    This is direct proportion. 1 bag costs 68 ÷ 8 = £8.50, and 12 bags cost 8.50 × 12.

    8 bags of cement cost £68. How much do 12 bags cost? Give your answer in pounds, like 102.00.

    1. Find the cost of 1 bag.
    2. Multiply it by 12.
    • That is the cost of 1 bag. Multiply by 12 to get the cost of 12 bags.
    • That is the wrong way round. More bags cost more, so the answer must be more than £68.

    1 bag costs 68 ÷ 8 = 8.50. 12 bags cost 8.50 × 12 = 102, so £102.00.

    3 bricklayers build a garden wall in 10 days. How many days would 5 bricklayers take, working at the same rate?

    1. More bricklayers means fewer days. Find the total worker-days.
    2. Divide by 5.
    • That is the wrong way round. More bricklayers should take fewer days.
    • That is the total bricklayer-days. Divide by 5 to find the days.

    Total = 3 × 10 = 30 bricklayer-days. 30 ÷ 5 = 6 days.

    y = 2.5x. Work out y when x = 12.

    1. Put x = 12 into the rule.
    2. Work out 2.5 × 12.
    • That looks like 12 ÷ 2.5. The rule multiplies x by 2.5.
    • That looks like 2.5 + 12. The 2.5 multiplies x.

    y = 2.5 × 12 = 30.

    x and y are in inverse proportion, and x × y = 60. Work out y when x = 15.

    1. x × y is fixed at 60.
    2. Divide 60 by 15.
    • That looks like 60 × 15. Since x × y = 60, y = 60 ÷ x.
    • That looks like 60 + 15. Divide 60 by x.

    y = 60 ÷ x = 60 ÷ 15 = 4.

    A recipe for 8 people uses 600 g of potatoes. How many grams are needed for 5 people?

    1. Find the amount for 1 person.
    2. Multiply by 5.
    • That is the amount for 1 person. Multiply by 5.
    • That is the wrong way round. Fewer people need less, so the answer must be below 600.

    600 ÷ 8 = 75 g for 1 person. 75 × 5 = 375 g.

    Check your knowledge: on the job

    Real jobs from the building trades: plumbing, electrics and carpentry. Prices are typical for 2026, so check them with your own supplier.

    Two identical pumps empty a flooded cellar in 90 minutes. How long would three of the same pumps take? Answer in minutes.

    1. More pumps means less time. Find the total in pump-minutes.
    2. Divide by 3 pumps.
    • That is the wrong way round. More pumps should take less time, so the answer must be below 90.
    • That looks like 90 × 3. Work out 2 × 90 = 180 pump-minutes and divide by 3.

    Total = 2 × 90 = 180 pump-minutes. 180 ÷ 3 = 60 minutes.

    The voltage drop along a cable is directly proportional to its length. Over 25 m of cable the drop is 1.8 V. What is the drop over 40 m of the same cable? Answer in volts.

    1. Find the drop for 1 metre first.
    2. Multiply by 40.
    • That is the wrong way round. A longer cable has a bigger drop, so the answer must be more than 1.8.
    • That looks like 1.8 × 40. First divide by the 25 m to find the drop per metre.

    Drop per metre = 1.8 ÷ 25 = 0.072 V. Over 40 m: 0.072 × 40 = 2.88 V.

    A 2 kW heater costs 56p an hour to run. At the same price per unit of electricity, how much does a 3 kW heater cost to run for an hour? Answer in pence.

    1. The cost is in direct proportion to the power. Find the cost for 1 kW.
    2. Multiply by 3.
    • That is the wrong way round. A more powerful heater costs more to run.
    • That looks like 56 + 3. Find the cost per kW, then multiply by 3.

    Cost per kW = 56 ÷ 2 = 28p. For 3 kW: 28 × 3 = 84p.

    6 bags of cement make 0.9 m³ of concrete. How many bags are needed for 2.1 m³?

    1. Find how many bags make 1 m³: 6 ÷ 0.9.
    2. Multiply that by 2.1.
    • That is 6 × 2.1 on its own. First divide by 0.9 to find the bags for 1 m³.
    • That is the wrong way round. A bigger job needs more bags, not fewer.

    Bags for 1 m³ = 6 ÷ 0.9 = 6.67…. For 2.1 m³: 6 ÷ 0.9 × 2.1 = 14 bags. Check: 2.1 ÷ 0.9 = 2.33… lots of 6, and 6 × 2.33… = 14.

    4 plasterers take 9 days to plaster a house. The deadline is 6 days. How many plasterers are needed, working at the same rate?

    1. Work out the total plasterer-days.
    2. Divide by 6 days.
    • That is the total plasterer-days. Divide by 6 days to find the number of plasterers.
    • That is the wrong way round. A shorter deadline needs more plasterers, so the answer must be more than 4.

    Total = 4 × 9 = 36 plasterer-days. 36 ÷ 6 = 6 plasterers.

    Decking boards are in direct proportion to the area covered. 9 boards cover 3.24 m². How many boards are needed to cover 10.8 m²?

    1. Find the area one board covers: 3.24 ÷ 9.
    2. Then see how many of those fit into 10.8 m².
    • That looks like 9 × 10.8. Work out the area of 1 board, then divide 10.8 by it.
    • That is the area 9 boards cover, not the number of boards for 10.8 m².

    One board covers 3.24 ÷ 9 = 0.36 m². Boards = 10.8 ÷ 0.36 = 30.

    [3 marks] A 1.5 kW heater costs 42p to run for one hour. A 2.5 kW heater is used for 4 hours at the same price per unit. How much does it cost in total? Give your answer in pounds, like 3.10.

    1. Find the cost of 1 kW for 1 hour.
    2. Multiply by 2.5 kW and by 4 hours.
    3. Change pence to pounds at the end.
    • That is the cost of 1 hour in pence. Multiply by 4 hours, then change to pounds.
    • That is the cost in pence. Divide by 100 to change to pounds.

    Cost per kW per hour = 42 ÷ 1.5 = 28p. For 2.5 kW and 4 hours: 28 × 2.5 × 4 = 280p, which is £2.80.

    • M1 for 42 ÷ 1.5 = 28p per kW per hour
    • M1 for 28 × 2.5 × 4 = 280p
    • A1 for £2.80

    [3 marks] y is directly proportional to x. When x = 8, y = 20. Find the value of y when x = 14.

    1. Find k, the value of y for each 1 of x.
    2. Use y = kx with x = 14.
    • That looks like 20 × 14. Divide by 8 first to find k.
    • That looks like adding 6 to 20 because x went up by 6. Multiply by k instead.

    k = 20 ÷ 8 = 2.5. y = 2.5 × 14 = 35.

    • M1 for k = 20 ÷ 8 (= 2.5) or equivalent
    • M1 for 2.5 × 14 or 20 ÷ 8 × 14
    • A1 for 35

    [3 marks] 6 workers take 10 days to build a wall. How many days would 4 workers take to build the same wall? Assume that all workers work at the same rate.

    1. More workers means fewer days, so this is inverse proportion.
    2. Find the total in worker-days, then divide by 4.
    • That is the total in worker-days. Divide by 4 to find the days.
    • That is the wrong way round. Fewer workers should take more days, so the answer must be more than 10.

    Total work = 6 × 10 = 60 worker-days. For 4 workers: 60 ÷ 4 = 15 days.

    • M1 for 6 × 10 = 60 (worker-days)
    • M1 for 60 ÷ 4
    • A1 for 15

    [4 marks] 4 tilers take 15 hours to tile a school corridor. Show that 10 tilers, working at the same rate, would take 6 hours.

    1. More tilers means less time. What stays the same?
    2. The total work stays the same: tilers times hours.
    3. Work out 4 × 15, then divide by 10.

    The work is fixed, so tilers × hours is fixed (inverse proportion). 4 × 15 = 60 tiler-hours. With 10 tilers: 60 ÷ 10 = 6 hours, as required. More tilers means less time, so the answer is sensible: 6 is less than 15.

    • M1 for 4 × 15 = 60 (tiler-hours)
    • M1 for 60 ÷ 10
    • A1 for 6 hours
    • C1 for stating that this is inverse proportion (more tilers, less time), so 6 is sensible and less than 15

    [3 marks] The cost of grout is directly proportional to its weight. 4.5 kg of grout costs £7.20. Work out the cost of 7 kg of grout. Give your answer in pounds, like 11.20.

    1. Find the cost of 1 kg first.
    2. Multiply by 7.
    • That looks like 7.20 × 4.5. Divide by the weight to find the cost of 1 kg.
    • That is the cost of 1 kg. Multiply by 7 kg to get the cost of 7 kg.

    1 kg costs 7.20 ÷ 4.5 = £1.60. 7 kg cost 1.60 × 7 = 11.20, so £11.20.

    • M1 for 7.20 ÷ 4.5 (= 1.60 per kg)
    • M1 for 1.60 × 7
    • A1 for £11.20

    [4 marks] x and y are in inverse proportion. The table shows some values. (a) Show that k = 60, where x × y = k. (b) Find the value of y when x = 5. Type your answer to part (b).

    Values of x and y
    x41020
    y1563
    1. Multiply each x by its y. What do you notice?
    2. The rule is y = k ÷ x.
    3. Put x = 5 into y = 60 ÷ x.
    • That looks like 60 × 5. In inverse proportion, y = 60 ÷ x.
    • That looks like 60 − 5. Divide 60 by x.

    (a) 4 × 15 = 60, 10 × 6 = 60 and 20 × 3 = 60. Every pair multiplies to 60, so k = 60. (b) y = 60 ÷ x = 60 ÷ 5 = 12.

    • M1 for 4 × 15 = 60 and 10 × 6 = 60
    • C1 for stating that x × y is always 60, so k = 60
    • M1 for y = 60 ÷ x

    [4 marks] Higher y is directly proportional to the square of x. When x = 4, y = 48. Find the value of y when x = 7.

    1. Write the rule as y = kx² and find k from x = 4 and y = 48.
    2. Square the 4 when you find k.
    3. Square the new x before multiplying by k.
    • That treats it as direct proportion to x (y = kx). Here y is proportional to x².
    • That looks like 12 × 49. First divide by x² = 16 to find k, not by 4.

    y = kx². 48 = k × 4² = 16k, so k = 3. When x = 7: y = 3 × 7² = 3 × 49 = 147.

    • M1 for y = kx²
    • M1 for k = 48 ÷ 16 (= 3)
    • M1 for 3 × 7²
    • A1 for 147

    Why it works

    • Direct proportion is the unitary method. If 5 m cost £12, then 1 m costs £2.40, and every length costs 2.4 times its number of metres. That 2.4 is k.
    • The graph passes through the origin because 0 of x gives 0 of y. The gradient of the line is the cost of one unit, k.
    • Inverse proportion shares out a fixed total. If 48 worker-days of work are shared out between more workers, each has less to do, so the days go down. x × y = 48 stays the same.
    • Squaring changes the scale. If x is doubled, a square with that side has 4 times the area, which is why y ∝ x² grows so quickly.
    • Finding k is the key step. Whatever the problem, find k from the pair you know, write the rule, then use the rule.

    Common traps

    • Using direct proportion for a job with workers. More workers means fewer days.
    • Dividing x by y instead of y by x when finding k in direct proportion.
    • Saying a straight-line graph is always direct proportion. It must go through the origin.
    • Scaling a recipe’s oven temperature or cooking time as if they were ingredients.
    • Rounding down when you need enough whole bags, boards or tins. Round up.
    • Forgetting to square x in y = kx², or squaring k instead.

    Going further

    5 machines make 5 widgets in 5 minutes. Every machine works at the same steady rate. How many minutes would 100 machines take to make 100 widgets?

    1. How long does 1 machine take to make 1 widget?
    2. If 100 machines each make 1 widget, how many widgets is that, and how long does it take?
    • That matches the numbers, but each machine still makes one widget in the same time as before.
    • That divides by 5 machines too often. Each machine takes 5 minutes to make one widget.

    5 machines make 5 widgets in 5 minutes, so each machine makes 1 widget in 5 minutes. 100 machines each make 1 widget at the same time, so 100 widgets take 5 minutes.

    Two gears are meshed together. The first gear has 20 teeth and turns 30 times a minute. The second gear has 12 teeth. How many times a minute does the second gear turn?

    1. Meshed gears pass the same number of teeth per minute. How many is that for the first gear?
    2. The number of teeth times the turns per minute is fixed. Divide by 12.
    • That is the wrong way round. A smaller gear turns faster, so the answer must be more than 30.
    • That looks like 30 × 12. First work out 20 × 30 teeth per minute.

    Teeth per minute = 20 × 30 = 600. The second gear has 12 teeth, so it turns 600 ÷ 12 = 50 times a minute. Gears are in inverse proportion: fewer teeth means more turns.

    y is directly proportional to x. Show that if x is increased by 20%, then y is also increased by 20%.

    1. Write the rule as y = kx. What is the new x after a 20% increase?
    2. The new x is 1.2x. Put it into the rule.

    y = kx. After a 20% increase, the new x is 1.2x. The new y = k × 1.2x = 1.2 × kx = 1.2y. So the new y is 1.2 times the old y, which is an increase of 20%. This works for any k and any percentage.
