Rearranging formulae

A formula is a rule that works in both directions. Put numbers in to get an answer, or turn the formula round so a different letter is on its own.

Where you will use this: an electrician finds the current from V = IR, a plumber works out how many hours a bill pays for, and a cook turns a roasting-time rule round to plan the day.

Words you need

Tap a teal word anywhere on the page to see this again.

Formula
A rule written with letters that links quantities. The plural is formulae.
Subject (of a formula)
The letter on its own on one side of a formula, usually the left. Making a letter the subject means rearranging the formula so that letter is alone.
Substitute
Replace a letter with a number, then work out the answer.
Inverse operation
The operation that undoes another. Adding undoes subtracting, and multiplying undoes dividing.

Warm-up: what do you already know?

Have a go before you read anything. Wrong answers are welcome: they show where to look.

I think of a number, double it, then take away 3. My answer is 11. What was my number?

  1. Work backwards from 11. What was the last thing I did?
  2. Undo it by adding 3. Then undo the doubling.
  3. 11 + 3 = 14. What undoes doubling 14?
  • That is 11 + 3. I also doubled my number at the start, so there is one more step to undo.
  • You took 3 away again. The last thing I did was take 3 away, so undo it by adding 3.

11 + 3 = 14, then 14 ÷ 2 = 7. You just solved 2x − 3 = 11, so the number was 7.

Solve 5x = 40.

  1. 5x means 5 × x. What undoes multiplying by 5?
  2. Divide both sides by 5.
  • You took 5 away. 5x is a multiplication, so divide.
  • You multiplied. Undo a multiplication by dividing.

x = 40 ÷ 5 = 8. Check: 5 × 8 = 40.

Solve x − 6 = 10.

  1. 6 has been taken away from x.
  2. Undo it by adding 6 to both sides.
  • You took 6 away again. Undo a subtraction by adding.

x = 10 + 6 = 16. Check: 16 − 6 = 10.

Work out 4 + 3 × 5.

  1. Which comes first, the adding or the multiplying?
  2. Multiply first, then add.
  • You worked left to right. Multiplication comes before addition.

3 × 5 = 15, then 4 + 15 = 19.

1. Formulae and putting numbers in

A Formula is a rule written with letters, such as A = l × w for the area of a rectangle. Each letter stands for something that changes from job to job: the length, the width, the hours worked.

An equation like 3x + 5 = 20 has one unknown to find, once. A formula is used again and again with different numbers. To Substitute is to swap each letter for its number and work out the answer.

Key idea: a formula is a recipe. Put the numbers in the right places, follow the order of operations (multiply before you add), and write the unit.

Try it: a taxi charges C = 3 + 2m pounds for m miles. Find C when m = 5.

Worked example

A plumber charges C = 40 + 25h pounds for a job of h hours. Find the charge for a 4-hour job.

  1. Swap h for 4: C = 40 + 25 × 4.
  2. Multiply first: 25 × 4 = 100.
  3. Then add: C = 40 + 100 = 140.
  4. The charge is £140.

Now you finish it

Use the same formula, C = 40 + 25h. Find the charge for a 6-hour job.

  1. Swap h for 6: C = 40 + 25 × 6.
  2. Multiply first: 25 × 6 = ___.
  3. Then add 40 to get C = ___.
Check my answer

25 × 6 = 150, and 40 + 150 = £190.

Check your knowledge: putting numbers in

Speed s = d ÷ t, where d is the distance and t is the time. A train goes 150 km in 3 hours. What is its speed in km/h?

  1. Which numbers go in place of d and t?
  2. Swap them in: s = 150 ÷ 3.
  • You multiplied. The formula says divide the distance by the time.
  • That is 3 ÷ 150, the wrong way round. Distance goes on top.

s = 150 ÷ 3 = 50 km/h.

The area of a rectangle is A = lw. Find A in cm² when l = 8 cm and w = 5 cm.

  1. lw means l × w.
  2. Swap in the numbers: 8 × 5.
  • You added. lw means l times w.
  • That is the perimeter, 2 × (8 + 5). Area is length times width.

A = 8 × 5 = 40 cm².

An electrician uses V = IR, where V is the voltage in volts, I is the current in amps and R is the resistance in ohms. Find V when I = 4 and R = 6.

  1. IR means I × R.
  2. Swap in: V = 4 × 6.
  • You added. IR means I times R.
  • You divided 6 by 4. The formula multiplies.

V = 4 × 6 = 24 volts.

The distance round a circle is C = 2πr. To keep the sums simple, use π = 3. Find C when r = 5.

  1. 2πr means 2 × π × r.
  2. With π = 3 that is 2 × 3 × 5.
  • You left out π. It is 3 here, so multiply by it too.
  • You used π × r but left out the 2.

C = 2 × 3 × 5 = 30.

T = 5 − 2n. Find T when n = 4.

  1. 2n means 2 × n. Work that out first.
  2. Then take it away from 5. The answer is below zero.
  • You worked out (5 − 2) × 4. Do 2 × 4 first, then take it from 5.
  • That is 8 − 5, the wrong way round. The sum is 5 − 8.

2 × 4 = 8, so T = 5 − 8 = −3.

A cook roasts a joint of beef for T = 40w + 20 minutes, where w is its weight in kg. How many minutes does a 2.5 kg joint need?

  1. Swap w for 2.5.
  2. Multiply 40 × 2.5 first, then add 20.
  • You worked out (40 + 20) × 2.5. Multiply before you add.
  • You stopped early. Do not forget to add the 20.

40 × 2.5 = 100, then 100 + 20 = 120 minutes.

2. Making a letter the subject

The Subject (of a formula) of a formula is the letter on its own, usually on the left. In A = l × w the subject is A. To make l the subject means to rearrange the formula until l is on its own.

You do it the way you solved equations. Use the inverse operation on both sides, but now the other side stays as letters.

Key idea: a formula is a balance. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Pick the operation that undoes what was done to the letter you want.

Try it: if p = q + 9, what is q on its own?

Worked example: one step

Make x the subject of y = x + 5.

  1. 5 has been added to x. The inverse is to take away 5.
  2. Do it to both sides: y − 5 = x + 5 − 5.
  3. So y − 5 = x. Write it the usual way round: x = y − 5.
  4. Check with numbers: if x = 3, then y = 8, and 8 − 5 = 3.

Worked example: a product

Make I the subject of V = IR. (Here R is not zero.)

  1. I has been multiplied by R. The inverse is to divide by R.
  2. Divide both sides by R: V over R = I.
  3. So I = V over R.
  4. Check: if I = 2 and R = 3, then V = 6, and 6 ÷ 3 = 2.

Now you finish it

Make x the subject of y = x − 3.

  1. 3 has been taken away from x. The inverse is to add 3.
  2. Add 3 to both sides: y + 3 = x.
  3. So x = ___.
Check my answer

x = y + 3. Check: if x = 10, then y = 7, and 7 + 3 = 10.

Typing algebra: use / for divide and * for times, and use brackets when a whole top line is divided, like (y-1)/2. You can type just the right-hand side, such as y+3.

Check your knowledge: one step

p = q − 4. Make q the subject. Type your answer starting q =.

  1. What has been done to q to get p?
  2. Undo it on both sides.
  3. Add the amount that was taken away to p.
  • You copied the minus sign. 4 was taken away from q, so undo it by adding 4.
  • The letters are the wrong way round. Add 4 to p.

Add 4 to both sides: p + 4 = q. So q = p + 4. Check: if q = 14, then p = 10, and 10 + 4 = 14.

A = lw. Make w the subject. Type your answer like w = A/l.

  1. w has been multiplied by l.
  2. What undoes multiplying by l?
  3. Divide both sides by l.
  • You multiplied. To undo a multiplication, divide.
  • You took l away. lw is a multiplication, so divide by l instead.

Divide both sides by l: A over l = w. So w = A/l. Check: if l = 4 and w = 3, then A = 12, and 12 ÷ 4 = 3.

An oven uses energy E = Pt, where P is its power and t is the time it is on. Make P the subject. Type your answer like P = E/t.

  1. P has been multiplied by t.
  2. Undo it by dividing both sides.
  3. Divide both sides by t.
  • You multiplied. Pt is a multiplication, so divide by t to undo it.
  • That is upside down. The letter you are dividing by goes underneath.

Divide both sides by t: E over t = P. So P = E/t.

F = ma (force = mass × acceleration). Which is the formula with a as the subject?

  • a = F − m, taking m away
  • a = F ÷ m
  • a = m ÷ F
  • a = F × m
  1. What has been done to a?
  2. Undo it on both sides.

a is multiplied by m, so divide both sides by m: a = F ÷ m.

y = x over 4. Make x the subject. Type your answer like x = 3y.

  1. x has been divided by 4.
  2. What undoes dividing by 4?
  3. Multiply both sides by 4.
  • You divided again. To undo a division, multiply.
  • You added 4. The formula divides by 4, so multiply by 4 to undo it.

Multiply both sides by 4: 4y = x. So x = 4y. Check: if x = 8, then y = 2, and 4 × 2 = 8.

s = d over t (speed = distance ÷ time). Make d the subject. Type your answer like d = ab.

  1. d has been divided by t.
  2. Undo a division by multiplying.
  3. Multiply both sides by t.
  • You divided. d was divided by t, so undo it by multiplying by t.
  • That is upside down. Multiply s by t.

Multiply both sides by t: st = d. So d = st. Check: at 4 m/s for 3 s you go 12 m, and 4 × 3 = 12.

3. Two steps

Most formulae have two things done to the letter. Undo them in the reverse order, just as with equations: first the adding or subtracting, then the multiplying or dividing.

Key idea: a fraction line works like brackets. y − 2 over 3 means (y − 2) ÷ 3, so the whole top line is divided, not just the last bit.

Worked example

Make x the subject of y = 3x + 2.

  1. Take 2 from both sides: y − 2 = 3x.
  2. Divide both sides by 3: y − 2 over 3 = x.
  3. So x = y − 2 over 3.
  4. Check: if x = 4, then y = 14, and (14 − 2) ÷ 3 = 4.

Worked example: a trade formula

A plumber charges C = 40 + 25h. Make h the subject.

  1. Take 40 from both sides: C − 40 = 25h.
  2. Divide both sides by 25: h = C − 40 over 25.
  3. Check: for 4 hours C = 140, and (140 − 40) ÷ 25 = 4.

Now you finish it

Make n the subject of T = 5n − 7.

  1. 7 has been taken away, so add 7 to both sides: T + 7 = 5n.
  2. Divide both sides by 5: n = ___.
Check my answer

n = T + 7 over 5. Check: if n = 3, then T = 8, and (8 + 7) ÷ 5 = 3.

Check your knowledge: two steps

y = 2x + 1. Make x the subject. Type your answer like x = (y-5)/3, with brackets.

  1. Which step was done last to x? Undo that one first.
  2. Take 1 from both sides, then divide.
  3. After taking 1 away you have y − 1 = 2x. Now divide the whole of the left side.
  • That divides only the 1 by 2. Put brackets round y − 1, because the whole top line is divided.
  • That takes the whole 1 away after halving. Take 1 from y first, then halve all of it.

y − 1 = 2x, so x = y − 1 over 2, typed (y-1)/2. Check: if x = 4, then y = 9, and (9 − 1) ÷ 2 = 4.

A skip costs C = 30 + 12d pounds to hire for d days. Make d the subject.

  1. What is done to d first, and what is done after?
  2. Undo the adding first, then the multiplying.
  3. Take 30 from both sides to get C − 30 = 12d.
  • That divides only the 30. Put brackets round C − 30, because the whole top line is divided by 12.
  • You divided C by 12 but not the 30. Divide everything on that side.

C − 30 = 12d, so d = C − 30 over 12, typed (c-30)/12.

p = 4q − 3. Make q the subject.

  1. q is multiplied by 4, then 3 is taken away.
  2. Undo the taking away first. Add 3 to both sides.
  3. You have p + 3 = 4q. Now divide the whole left side by 4.
  • You took 3 away again. The formula takes 3 away, so add 3 to undo it.
  • That divides only the 3. Put brackets round p + 3.

p + 3 = 4q, so q = p + 3 over 4, typed (p+3)/4. Check: if q = 2, then p = 5, and (5 + 3) ÷ 4 = 2.

You have the formula x = (y − 1) ÷ 2. Find x when y = 9.

  1. Swap y for 9.
  2. Work out the brackets first.
  • That is 9 − 1. You still need to divide by 2.
  • You halved 9 first, then took 1 away. Work out 9 − 1 first, then halve it.

(9 − 1) ÷ 2 = 8 ÷ 2 = 4.

A scaffold tower costs C = 55 + 20d pounds for d days. Ali pays £215. For how many days did he hire it?

  1. You know C. You want d.
  2. Rearrange to d = (C − 55) ÷ 20, or solve 55 + 20d = 215.
  3. Take 55 from 215 first.
  • You divided 215 by 20 but forgot to take away the 55 first.
  • That is 215 − 55, the cost of the days. Divide by 20 to get the number of days.

215 − 55 = 160, and 160 ÷ 20 = 8 days. Check: 55 + 20 × 8 = 215.

y = x over 3 + 2. Make x the subject. Use brackets if you need them.

  1. x is divided by 3, then 2 is added.
  2. Undo in reverse: take 2 away first, then multiply by 3.
  3. You have y − 2 = x over 3. Multiply the whole left side by 3.
  • You multiplied y by 3 but not the 2. Take 2 from y first, then multiply all of it by 3.
  • The formula adds 2, so take 2 away to undo it. Subtract, do not add.

y − 2 = x over 3, so x = 3(y − 2) = 3y − 6. Check: if x = 6, then y = 4, and 3 × (4 − 2) = 6.

P = 2r + 6. Which is the formula with r as the subject?

  • r = P − 3, because 6 ÷ 2 = 3
  • r = (P − 6) ÷ 2
  • r = (P + 6) ÷ 2, adding 6 first
  • r = P ÷ 2 − 6
  1. Undo the adding first.
  2. Then undo the multiplying by 2.

Take 6 from both sides: P − 6 = 2r. Divide both sides by 2: r = (P − 6) ÷ 2.

4. Squares and roots Higher

When the letter you want is squared, the last step is a Square root. The square root undoes squaring. For lengths and other positive quantities, take the positive root.

Key idea: undo in reverse order again. Get the squared letter on its own first, then take the square root of both sides.

Worked example

The area of a circle is A = πr². Make r the subject. (r is a length, so it is positive.)

  1. r² has been multiplied by π. Divide both sides by π: A over π = r².
  2. Take the square root of both sides: r = √(A over π).
  3. Check: if r = 2, then A = 4π, and 4π ÷ π = 4, and the root of 4 is 2.

Check your knowledge: squares and roots Higher

Type a square root as sqrt( ), like sqrt(A). Take x, s and v to be positive.

The area of a square with side s is A = s². Make s the subject.

  1. s is squared. What undoes squaring?
  2. Take the square root of both sides.
  • You divided by 2. Squaring is not doubling. Take the square root instead.
  • That squares again. Undo a square with a square root.

The square root of both sides gives s = √A, which you type as sqrt(a). Check: if s = 5, then A = 25, and the root of 25 is 5.

y = x² + 3. Make x the subject.

  1. First get x² on its own.
  2. Take 3 from both sides, then take the square root.
  3. You have y − 3 = x². Take the square root of the whole left side.
  • That takes the root of y alone. Take 3 from y first, then the root of the whole thing.
  • The formula adds 3, so take 3 away. Subtract, do not add.

y − 3 = x², so x = √(y − 3), typed sqrt(y-3). Check: if x = 4, then y = 19, and the root of 16 is 4.

A circular pond has area A = πr². Use π = 3.14. The area is 78.5 m². Find r in metres.

  1. Rearrange first, or solve 3.14 × r² = 78.5.
  2. Divide 78.5 by 3.14 to find r².
  3. 78.5 ÷ 3.14 = 25. What is the square root of 25?
  • That is r². Take the square root to get r.
  • You halved 25. The opposite of squaring is the square root.

r² = 78.5 ÷ 3.14 = 25, so r = 5 metres.

A moving object has kinetic energy E = 1 over 2mv². Make v the subject.

  1. v² has been multiplied by m and by a half.
  2. Undo in reverse: multiply by 2, divide by m, then take the square root.
  3. 2E = mv², so 2E over m = v².
  • Doubling is the inverse of halving. Multiply both sides by 2.
  • You stopped before the square root. That is v², not v.

2E = mv², so 2E over m = v², so v = √(2E over m), typed sqrt(2e/m).

Flip it: run it backwards

A formula can be used in two directions.

Forwards

C = 40 + 25h. When h = 4, what is C?

Substitute the numbers, then work it out.

Backwards

C = 40 + 25h. When C = 190, what is h?

Rearrange (or solve), then substitute.

Key idea: substituting asks “what comes out?”. Rearranging asks “what must go in?”. If you will need the answer many times with different numbers, rearrange once, then substitute each time.

Worked example: both directions

v = u + at, where u is the starting speed, a the acceleration and t the time.

  1. Forwards: u = 5, a = 2, t = 6 gives v = 5 + 2 × 6 = 17.
  2. Backwards: make t the subject. Take u from both sides: v − u = at. Divide by a: t = v − u over a.
  3. Use it with v = 17, u = 5, a = 2: t = (17 − 5) ÷ 2 = 6.
  4. That is the t we started with, so the two directions agree.

Check your knowledge: flip it

Distance d = vt, where v is speed and t is time. A cyclist rides 36 km at 12 km/h. How many hours does it take?

  1. You know d and v. Which letter do you want?
  2. Make t the subject: t = d ÷ v.
  3. Swap in 36 and 12.
  • You multiplied. To find the time, divide the distance by the speed.
  • That is 12 ÷ 36, upside down. The distance goes on top.

t = d ÷ v = 36 ÷ 12 = 3 hours.

An electrician uses V = IR. A heater has V = 230 volts and R = 46 ohms. Find the current I in amps.

  1. You know V and R. You want I.
  2. Make I the subject: I = V ÷ R.
  3. Swap in 230 and 46.
  • You multiplied. To undo I × R, divide by R.
  • That is 46 ÷ 230, upside down. V goes on top.

I = 230 ÷ 46 = 5 amps.

A rectangular patio has area 24 m² and is 6 m long. How wide is it, in metres?

  1. The area formula is A = lw.
  2. Make w the subject, or ask: 6 times what is 24?
  3. w = A ÷ l.
  • You multiplied 24 by 6. Undo a multiplication by dividing.
  • You took 6 from 24. A = lw is a multiplication, so divide.

w = A ÷ l = 24 ÷ 6 = 4 m. Check: 6 × 4 = 24.

The distance round a circle is C = 2πr. Use π = 3. A circle has C = 36. Find r.

  1. With π = 3, the formula is C = 2 × 3 × r.
  2. That is C = 6r.
  3. Divide both sides by 6.
  • You divided by 3 only. C = 2πr has a 2 as well, so divide by 2 × 3 = 6.
  • You divided by 2 only. π is 3 here, so divide by 2 × 3 = 6.
  • You multiplied. Undo a multiplication by dividing.

36 = 6r, so r = 36 ÷ 6 = 6. Check: 2 × 3 × 6 = 36.

v = u + at. Make u the subject. Type your answer like u = v-at.

  1. What has been added to u?
  2. Undo it on both sides.
  3. Take at away from both sides.
  • You added at. In the formula at is added to u, so take it away to undo it.
  • That is the wrong way round. Start with v, then take at away.

Take at from both sides: v − at = u. So u = v − at. Check: 17 − 2 × 6 = 5, the u we used before.

v = u + at. Make t the subject. Use brackets where you need them.

  1. t is multiplied by a, and u is added. Undo the adding first.
  2. Take u from both sides to get v − u = at.
  3. Now divide the whole left side by a.
  • That divides only u by a. Put brackets round v − u.
  • You added u. In the formula u is added, so take it away to undo it.

v − u = at, so t = v − u over a, typed (v-u)/a.

A plumber charges C = 45 + 30h pounds for h hours. A bill is £165. How many hours was the job?

  1. You know C and want h.
  2. Take 45 from the bill, then divide by 30.
  3. 165 − 45 = 120.
  • You divided 165 by 30 and forgot to take away the 45 first.
  • That is 165 − 45, the cost of the hours. Divide by 30 to get the number of hours.

(165 − 45) ÷ 30 = 120 ÷ 30 = 4 hours.

Spot the mistake

Finding an error in someone else's working helps you learn. For each one, decide what went wrong.

Ben says: if y = 3x + 2, then x = y over 3 − 2. What went wrong?

  • He should have added 2, not taken it away.
  • He divided only y by 3.
  • Nothing: it is right.
  1. Test it with a number. If x = 4, what is y? What does Ben's formula give?

The whole of y − 2 must be divided by 3: x = y − 2 over 3. Ben's version gives about 2.7 when it should give 4.

Priya says: if d = vt, then t = v over d. What went wrong?

  • She should have multiplied v and d.
  • Nothing: it is right.
  • She turned it upside down.
  1. Test with v = 2 and t = 3. What is d? What does her formula give for t?

Divide both sides by v: t = d over v. With d = 6 and v = 2 that gives 3, as it should.

Ali says: if P = 2l + 2w, then l = P over 2 − 2w. What went wrong?

  • He should have written P ÷ 2 + 2w.
  • He halved P but not the 2w.
  • Nothing: it is right.
  1. Take 2w from both sides. Then divide the whole left side by 2.

P − 2w = 2l, so l = P − 2w over 2 = P over 2 − w. Both parts are halved.

Check your knowledge: mixed review

Mixed up on purpose, like a real paper. Work out which method fits.

Temperatures: F = 1.8C + 32 turns Celsius into Fahrenheit. A summer day is 20°C. What is that in °F?

  1. Swap C for 20.
  2. Multiply 1.8 × 20 first, then add 32.
  • You worked out (1.8 + 32) × 20. Multiply before you add.
  • You stopped before adding the 32.

1.8 × 20 = 36, then 36 + 32 = 68°F.

Make C the subject of F = 1.8C + 32. Use brackets where you need them.

  1. Which step was done to C last?
  2. Take 32 from both sides, then divide by 1.8.
  3. F − 32 = 1.8C. Divide the whole left side.
  • That divides only the 32. Put brackets round F − 32.
  • The formula adds 32, so take 32 away to undo it.

F − 32 = 1.8C, so C = F − 32 over 1.8, typed (f-32)/1.8.

Use C = (F − 32) ÷ 1.8 to change 86°F to degrees Celsius.

  1. Swap F for 86.
  2. Work out the brackets first: 86 − 32.
  • You put 86 into the first formula. Here 86 is F, so use the one with C on its own.
  • That is 86 − 32. Now divide by 1.8.

86 − 32 = 54, and 54 ÷ 1.8 = 30°C.

A cake recipe uses f = 75s + 50 grams of flour for s servings. You have 500 g of flour. How many servings can you make?

  1. You know f and want s.
  2. Take 50 from 500, then divide by 75.
  • You added 50. The formula adds 50 to the flour, so take it away to undo it.
  • You divided 500 by 75 and forgot to take away the 50.

(500 − 50) ÷ 75 = 450 ÷ 75 = 6 servings.

In a game, you need P = 150L − 50 points to finish level L. Zed has 700 points. What level is that?

  1. You know P and want L.
  2. The formula takes 50 away, so add 50 to undo it, then divide by 150.
  • You took 50 away again. Undo a subtraction by adding.
  • You divided 700 by 150 and forgot the 50.

(700 + 50) ÷ 150 = 750 ÷ 150 = 5. Check: 150 × 5 − 50 = 700.

The area of a triangle is A = 1 over 2bh. Which formula has h as the subject?

  • h = A − b
  • h = A ÷ b ÷ 2
  • h = 2A ÷ b
  • h = 2b ÷ A
  1. First get rid of the half: multiply both sides by 2.
  2. That gives 2A = bh. Now divide by b.

Multiply both sides by 2: 2A = bh. Divide both sides by b: h = 2A ÷ b.

A runner holds 4 m/s over 600 m. Using t = d ÷ s, how many seconds does she run?

  1. Which numbers are d and s?
  2. Swap in: t = 600 ÷ 4.
  • You multiplied. To find a time, divide the distance by the speed.

t = 600 ÷ 4 = 150 seconds.

2y + 3x = 12. Make y the subject. Use brackets where you need them.

  1. You want y on its own on the left.
  2. Take 3x from both sides, then divide by 2.
  3. You have 2y = 12 − 3x. Divide the whole right side by 2.
  • That halves only the 3x. Put brackets round 12 − 3x.
  • You added 3x. In the formula 3x is added to 2y, so take it away to undo it.

2y = 12 − 3x, so y = 12 − 3x over 2, typed (12-3x)/2. Check: x = 2 gives y = 3, and 6 + 6 = 12.

Exam style

Use these like a real paper. The marks tell you how much to write.

Not sure what a question word means? Question words explained.

Write your working on paper, then type the answer.

  • A plumber charges C pounds for a job of h hours.
  • The formula is C = 35 + 28h.

(a) [2 marks] Work out the charge for a job of 3 hours. Answer in pounds.

  1. Swap h for 3.
  2. Multiply 28 × 3 before you add 35.
  • You worked out (35 + 28) × 3. Multiply before you add.
  • That is the cost of the hours only. Add the 35 too.

C = 35 + 28 × 3 (multiply first). 28 × 3 = 84 and 35 + 84 = £119.

  • M1 for C = 35 + 28 × 3, multiplying first
  • A1 for 35 + 84 = 119

(b) [3 marks] Make h the subject of C = 35 + 28h. Use brackets where you need them.

  1. Undo the adding first, then the multiplying.
  2. Take 35 from both sides, then divide both sides by 28.
  • That divides only the 35. Put brackets round C − 35.
  • You divided C by 28 but not the 35. Divide everything on that side.

Subtract 35 from both sides: C − 35 = 28h. Divide both sides by 28: h = C − 35 over 28, typed (c-35)/28.

  • M1 for subtracting 35 from both sides: C − 35 = 28h
  • M1 for dividing both sides by 28
  • A1 for h = C − 35 over 28

(c) [2 marks] A customer pays £175. For how many hours did the plumber work?

  1. Use your answer to part (b).
  2. Swap C for 175.
  • You divided 175 by 28 and forgot to take away the 35 first.
  • That is 175 − 35. Now divide by 28.

h = (175 − 35) ÷ 28 = 140 ÷ 28 = 5 hours.

  • M1 for h = (175 − 35) ÷ 28 = 140 ÷ 28
  • A1 for 5 hours

[3 marks] Ali says: if y = 4x − 6, then x = (y + 6) ÷ 4. Show that Ali is right when y = 14. Type the value of x you get.

  1. Use Ali's formula. Swap y for 14.
  2. Then check your x in the original formula.
  • That is 14 ÷ 4. The formula says add 6 to y first, then divide.
  • That is 14 + 6 and you have not divided by 4 yet.

x = (14 + 6) ÷ 4 = 20 ÷ 4 = 5. Put x = 5 in the original formula: 4 × 5 − 6 = 14. It gives y = 14 again, so the two formulae agree, and x = 5. One example is a check, not a proof; the proof is to rearrange.

  • M1 for x = (14 + 6) ÷ 4 = 20 ÷ 4 = 5
  • M1 for putting x = 5 in the original formula: 4 × 5 − 6 = 14
  • A1 for saying it gives y = 14 again, so the formulae agree and x = 5
  • A gym charges a joining fee of J pounds and then m pounds a month.
  • After n months you have paid T = J + mn pounds in total.
  • J = 30, n = 12 and T = 210.

[4 marks] Work out m, the monthly price, in pounds.

  1. Put the numbers in: 210 = 30 + 12m, or rearrange first.
  2. Take the joining fee away, then divide by the number of months.
  • You divided 210 by 12 and forgot the joining fee.
  • That is 210 − 30, the total for all the months. Divide it by 12.

Rearrange: m = (T − J) ÷ n, or write 210 = 30 + 12m. Substitute: (210 − 30) ÷ 12 = 180 ÷ 12 = 15, so m = £15 a month.

  • M1 for rearranging, m = (T − J) ÷ n, or writing 210 = 30 + 12m
  • M1 for substituting: (210 − 30) ÷ 12
  • M1 for 180 ÷ 12
  • A1 for m = 15

Why it works

  • Same on both sides. A formula says two things are equal. Doing the same to both sides keeps them equal, which is why every step is allowed.
  • Reverse order. To undo a chain of steps you go backwards, like taking off your shoes before your socks.
  • The fraction line is a bracket. y − 2 over 3 divides all of y − 2. That is why brackets appear when you type it on one line.
  • Rearrange once, use many times. A rearranged formula is a ready-made tool: put different numbers in as often as you like.

Common traps

  • Dividing only one term when the whole side should be divided.
  • Copying a sign instead of reversing it: the inverse of + is −, and of − is +.
  • Turning a division upside down, so t = v over d instead of d over v.
  • Multiplying out of order when substituting: multiply before you add.
  • Forgetting the units when you give a numerical answer.

Going further

F = 1.8C + 32 changes Celsius to Fahrenheit. At one temperature the number on both scales is the same. What is that temperature?

  1. If F and C are the same number, call it t and write t in both places.
  2. That gives t = 1.8t + 32. Get all the t terms on one side.
  3. Take 1.8t from both sides. Then divide.
  • That is the number added. Collect the t terms on one side first.
  • That is the number in front of t after collecting. Divide 32 by it.

t = 1.8t + 32, so t − 1.8t = 32, so −0.8t = 32, so t = 32 ÷ (−0.8) = −40. Check: 1.8 × (−40) + 32 = −72 + 32 = −40.

Stretch: the letter on both sides Higher

ax + b = cx + d. Make x the subject. Assume a is not equal to c. Use brackets where you need them.

  1. x is on both sides. Collect the x terms on one side and the rest on the other.
  2. Take cx and b from both sides: ax − cx = d − b.
  3. Factorise the left side: x(a − c) = d − b. Then divide by (a − c).
  • The whole of a − c must be on the bottom. Put it in brackets.
  • You added b. b was added on the left, so take it away to undo it.

ax − cx = d − b, so x(a − c) = d − b, so x = d − b over a − c, typed (d-b)/(a-c). This works only when a is not equal to c, because you cannot divide by zero. Check: 3x + 1 = x + 9 gives x = 4, and (9 − 1) ÷ (3 − 1) = 4.

y = 2x + 1 and x = (y − 1) ÷ 2 say the same thing. Put x = (y − 1) ÷ 2 into 2x + 1 and simplify. What do you get?

  1. Replace x by (y − 1) ÷ 2 in 2x + 1.
  2. 2 × (y − 1) ÷ 2: the 2s cancel.
  • That is 2 times the bracket. Do not forget the +1 at the end.

2 × y − 1 over 2 + 1 = (y − 1) + 1 = y. A formula and its rearrangement undo each other, so you get back to y.

n is any whole number and R = 2n + 7. Is R always odd? Explain why.

  • No. It depends on n: odd values of n give an odd R but even values of n give an even R.
  • Yes: 2n is even, and even + 7 is odd.
  • Yes, but only when n is small.
  1. Try n = 1, 2, 3. What do you notice?
  2. Is 2n ever odd?

2n is two lots of n, so it is always even. An even number plus 7 (odd) is always odd. So R is always odd. Rearranged, n = (R − 7) ÷ 2, which is a whole number only when R is odd.

Check your knowledge: on the job

Real jobs from the building trades: plumbing, electrics and carpentry. Prices are typical, so check them with your own supplier.

Speed, distance and time are linked by s = d ÷ t. Rearrange it to make t the subject, then find how long a fitter takes to drive 78 miles at an average speed of 52 mph. Give your answer in minutes.

  1. Which letter do you want on its own?
  2. Multiply both sides by t, then divide both sides by s: t = d ÷ s.
  3. Work out 78 ÷ 52 in hours, then change to minutes.
  • That is in hours. The question asks for minutes: multiply by 60.
  • You divided 52 by 78. The rearranged formula is t = d ÷ s, so divide the distance by the speed.

s = d ÷ t, so t = d ÷ s. t = 78 ÷ 52 = 1.5 hours = 1.5 × 60 = 90 minutes.

The area of the inside of a round pipe is A = 3.14r2, where r is the radius in mm. A pipe has A = 314 mm2. Rearrange the formula and find r, in mm.

  1. What is multiplying the r2? Which operation undoes it?
  2. Divide both sides by 3.14 to get r2 on its own, then take the square root.
  3. Work out 314 ÷ 3.14, then the square root of the answer.
  • That is r2. Take the square root to get r.
  • You took the square root of 314 and then did not divide by 3.14. Divide by 3.14 first, then take the square root.

A = 3.14r2, so r2 = A ÷ 3.14 = 314 ÷ 3.14 = 100. Take the square root: r = 10 mm. (Check: 3.14 × 102 = 314 ✓.)

A quote is worked out with C = a + bn, where a is the call-out fee, b is the price per hour and n is the number of hours. Which is the formula made with n as the subject?

  • n = C − (a ÷ b)
  • n = (C + a) ÷ b
  • n = (C − a) ÷ b
  1. First get the term with n on its own: bn.
  2. Subtract a from both sides, then divide everything on that side by b.

C = a + bn. Subtract a: C − a = bn. Divide by b: n = (C − a) ÷ b. The bracket matters because all of C − a is divided by b.

[3 marks] A quote is C = 65 + 48h, where C is the cost in pounds and h is the hours of work. A customer can spend at most £300. Rearrange the formula to make h the subject, then find the number of hours, to 1 decimal place.

  1. Which letter do you want on its own? What is added to the 48h?
  2. Subtract 65 from both sides, then divide both sides by 48.
  3. Work out (300 − 65) ÷ 48.
  • You divided 300 by 48 without taking off the 65 first.
  • That is 48h, the labour cost. Divide by 48 to get the hours.

C = 65 + 48h. Subtract 65: C − 65 = 48h. Divide by 48: h = (C − 65) ÷ 48. When C = 300, h = 235 ÷ 48 = 4.896..., so 4.9 hours (1 d.p.).

  • M1 for subtracting 65: C − 65 = 48h
  • M1 for dividing by 48: h = (C − 65) ÷ 48
  • A1 for h = 235 ÷ 48 = 4.9 hours (1 d.p.)

Using C = a + bn, make b the subject. Then find b when C = 345, a = 75 and n = 6. Give b in pounds per hour.

  1. Which letter do you want on its own? What is it multiplied by?
  2. Subtract a from both sides, then divide by n.
  3. b = (C − a) ÷ n. Put the numbers in.
  • You divided 345 by 6 without taking off the 75 first.
  • That is bn, which is 345 − 75. Divide by n = 6 to get b.

C = a + bn. Subtract a: C − a = bn. Divide by n: b = (C − a) ÷ n. b = (345 − 75) ÷ 6 = 270 ÷ 6 = 45. (Check: 75 + 45 × 6 = 345 ✓.)

Ohm’s law is V = IR, where V is the voltage in volts, I is the current in amps and R is the resistance in ohms. Rearrange it to make I the subject, then find I when V = 230 and R = 46. Give your answer in amps.

  1. Which letter do you want on its own? What is it multiplied by?
  2. Divide both sides by R to get I = V ÷ R.
  3. Work out 230 ÷ 46.
  • You multiplied 230 by 46. I = V ÷ R, so divide.
  • You divided 46 by 230. Put the voltage on the top: V ÷ R.

V = IR. Divide both sides by R: I = V ÷ R. I = 230 ÷ 46 = 5 A. (Check: 5 × 46 = 230 ✓.)

Power, voltage and current are linked by P = IV, where P is the power in watts, V is the voltage in volts and I is the current in amps. Rearrange it to make I the subject, then find I for a 3 kW heater on a 230 V supply. Give your answer in amps, to 1 decimal place. (3 kW = 3000 W.)

  1. Which letter do you want on its own? What is it multiplied by?
  2. Divide both sides by V to get I = P ÷ V. Use P in watts.
  3. Work out 3000 ÷ 230.
  • You used 3 instead of 3000. P must be in watts: 3 kW = 3000 W.
  • You multiplied 3000 by 230. I = P ÷ V, so divide.

P = IV. Divide both sides by V: I = P ÷ V. I = 3000 ÷ 230 = 13.04..., so I = 13.0 A (1 d.p.).
