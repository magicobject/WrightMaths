Pythagoras' theorem

One rule about right-angled triangles that finds missing lengths, and runs both ways: find the long side, find a short side, or test whether a triangle has a right angle at all.

GCSE Maths Book / Pythagoras' theorem

Before you start: what this stands on

Maths is built like a pyramid, each topic standing on the ones below. This topic needs:

  • Squares and square roots Number
  • Shapes and angles Geometry

The "Last time" questions below are a quick check of that base. If they feel shaky, build the base first.

Warm-up: what do you already know?

Have a go before you read anything. A guess is fine.

A 5 m ladder leans against a vertical wall. Its foot is 3 m from the wall. How high up the wall does the ladder reach, in metres? (Hint: it is a nice whole number.)

  1. The ladder, the wall and the ground make a right-angled triangle. Which side is the ladder?
  2. Try 3² + ?² = 5². What square number added to 9 makes 25?
  • That is 3 + 5. The ladder is the longest side, so the wall must be shorter than 5 m.
  • That is 5 − 3. Lengths do not subtract like that in a right-angled triangle: work with squares.
  • That is √(9 + 25). But the ladder (5 m) is the longest side, so the answer must be shorter than 5 m: subtract the squares.

9 + ?² = 25, so ?² = 16 and ? = 4. The ladder reaches 4 m up the wall. You just used Pythagoras.

Last time: the base this stands on

Work out 7².

  1. 7 × 7.
  • That is 7 × 2. Squaring means multiplying a number by itself.

7² = 7 × 7 = 49.

Work out 12².

  1. 12 × 12. Ten twelves are 120, then add two more twelves.

12 × 12 = 144.

Work out √81.

  1. Which number multiplied by itself gives 81?
  • That is 81 ÷ 2. A square root asks "what times itself makes 81?".

9 × 9 = 81, so √81 = 9. (Squaring and square-rooting undo each other.)

Work out √169.

  1. It is a little more than 12, because 12² = 144.

13 × 13 = 169, so √169 = 13.

1. The theorem

A triangle with one right angle is a right-angled triangle. Its longest side, always opposite the right angle, is the Hypotenuse. Pythagoras' theorem says the squares on the two shorter sides add up to the square on the hypotenuse.

a² + b² = c²

Key idea: it only works for right-angled triangles, and the c must be the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle.

Worked example: find the hypotenuse

A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 6 cm and 8 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

  1. a² + b² = c², so 6² + 8² = c².
  2. 36 + 64 = 100, so c² = 100.
  3. c = √100 = 10 cm.
  4. Sense check: 10 is longer than 6 and 8 ✓

Many triangles do not give a whole number. Then use a calculator and round as asked. A set of three whole numbers that fits the rule, like 3, 4, 5 or 5, 12, 13, is a Pythagorean triple.

Check your knowledge: the hypotenuse

Triangle PQR has a right angle at Q. Which side is the hypotenuse?

  • PQ
  • QR
  • PR
  1. The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. Which side is opposite Q?

PR is opposite Q, so PR is the hypotenuse (and the longest side).

Find the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with shorter sides 5 cm and 12 cm. Answer in cm.

  1. c² = 5² + 12².
  2. 25 + 144 = ?
  • That is 5 + 12. Square first, add, then take the square root.
  • That is c². Take the square root to find c.

c² = 25 + 144 = 169, so c = 13 cm.

Find the hypotenuse when the shorter sides are 8 cm and 15 cm.

  1. 64 + 225 = ?
  • That is 8 + 15. Square first.
  • That is c². Take the square root.

c² = 64 + 225 = 289, so c = 17 cm.

Find the hypotenuse when the shorter sides are 4 cm and 6 cm. Give your answer to 1 decimal place.

  1. 16 + 36 = 52.
  2. Use √52 on your calculator, then round to 1 d.p.
  • That is 4 + 6. Square, add, then square root.
  • That is c². Take the square root.

c² = 52, so c = √52 = 7.211… = 7.2 cm (1 d.p.). Sense check: longer than 6, shorter than 4 + 6 ✓

Find the hypotenuse when the shorter sides are 5 cm and 7 cm, to 1 d.p.

  1. 25 + 49 = 74.
  • That is 5 + 7. Square, add, square root.
  • That is c². Take the square root.

√74 = 8.602… = 8.6 cm.

2. Finding a shorter side

If you know the hypotenuse and one other side, rearrange the rule: a² = c² − b². You subtract the squares.

Key idea: the hypotenuse is always the longest side. So when you find a short side, your answer must be smaller than the hypotenuse. If it is bigger, you added when you should have subtracted.

Worked example

The hypotenuse is 13 cm and one shorter side is 5 cm. Find the other side.

  1. a² + 5² = 13², so a² = 13² − 5².
  2. a² = 169 − 25 = 144.
  3. a = √144 = 12 cm.
  4. Sense check: 12 is shorter than 13 ✓

Now you finish it

The hypotenuse is 10 cm and one shorter side is 6 cm.

  1. a² = 10² − 6² = 100 − 36 = ___.
  2. a = √___ = ___.

a² = 64 and a = 8 cm. (A 6, 8, 10 triangle: double the 3, 4, 5.)

Check your knowledge: a shorter side

The hypotenuse is 17 cm and one shorter side is 8 cm. Find the other side.

  1. Subtract: 17² − 8² = 289 − 64.
  • You added the squares. A short side must be shorter than the 17 cm hypotenuse, so subtract.
  • That is the square of the answer. Take the square root.

289 − 64 = 225, so the side is √225 = 15 cm.

The hypotenuse is 25 cm and one shorter side is 7 cm. Find the other side.

  1. 625 − 49 = ?
  • You added the squares. The other side must be shorter than 25 cm.

625 − 49 = 576, so the side is √576 = 24 cm.

The hypotenuse is 9 cm and one shorter side is 5 cm. Find the other side to 1 decimal place.

  1. 81 − 25 = 56.
  • You added the squares, but a short side cannot be longer than the 9 cm hypotenuse. Subtract.
  • That is the square of the answer. Take the square root.

√56 = 7.483… = 7.5 cm.

The hypotenuse is 12 cm and one shorter side is 7 cm. Find the other side to 1 d.p.

  1. 144 − 49 = ?
  • You added. The other side must be shorter than 12 cm.
  • That is the square of the answer. Take the square root.

144 − 49 = 95, and √95 = 9.746… = 9.7 cm.

Flip it: the theorem backwards

So far you have used the rule to find a length. Flip it, and use it to test a triangle.

Forwards

"The triangle is right-angled, so a² + b² = c²."

Find the missing length.

Backwards

"a² + b² = c², so the triangle is right-angled."

Decide whether the angle is 90°.

Key idea: the backwards statement is called the Converse. It is not true for every theorem, but it is true for Pythagoras. Builders use it: lay out a 3, 4, 5 triangle and the corner is a perfect right angle.

Worked example

Is a triangle with sides 7, 24 and 25 right-angled?

  1. Test the longest side as c: 7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625.
  2. 25² = 625. They match, so yes.

If the two sides of the test do not match, the triangle is not right-angled.

Check your knowledge: flip it

A triangle has sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 8 cm. Is it right-angled?

  • Yes
  • No, because 5² + 6² = 61 but 8² = 64
  • It is almost right-angled, so it counts
  1. Put the longest side (8) as c and test a² + b² against c².

25 + 36 = 61, which is not 64, so the triangle is not right-angled.

A triangle has sides 9 cm, 12 cm and 15 cm. Is it right-angled?

  • Yes: 81 + 144 = 225 = 15²
  • No, because 9 + 12 = 21, not 15
  • Cannot tell
  1. It is a 3, 4, 5 triangle multiplied by 3.

81 + 144 = 225 and 15² = 225. Yes, right-angled.

3, 4, 5 and 6, 8, ___ are both triples (and the second is the first doubled). What is the missing number?

  1. Double the 5.
  2. Or check: 36 + 64 = 100.

Doubling 3, 4, 5 gives 6, 8, 10. Check: 36 + 64 = 100 = 10² ✓

One more triple: 5, ___, 13. Find the missing shorter side.

  1. Flip it: this is finding a shorter side. 169 − 25.

169 − 25 = 144 and √144 = 12. So 5, 12, 13.

3. Using it in the real world

Look for a right angle hiding in the problem. Sketch it, mark the right angle, and decide whether you are finding the hypotenuse or a shorter side.

Worked example: distance on a grid

Point A is (1, 2) and point B is (5, 5). Find the distance AB.

  1. Across: 5 − 1 = 4. Up: 5 − 2 = 3.
  2. These are the shorter sides of a right-angled triangle, and AB is the hypotenuse.
  3. AB² = 4² + 3² = 25, so AB = 5.

Worked example: a hidden right angle

An isosceles triangle has two sides of 13 cm and a base of 10 cm. Find its height.

  1. The height splits the base in half, so one half is 5 cm.
  2. Each half makes a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 13 and a shorter side 5.
  3. h² = 13² − 5² = 144, so h = 12 cm.

Check your knowledge: in context

Find the distance between (0, 0) and (3, 4).

  1. Across 3, up 4, then the hypotenuse.
  • That is 3 + 4, the distance if you could only travel along the grid. The straight-line distance is shorter.

√(9 + 16) = √25 = 5.

A rectangle is 9 cm by 12 cm. How long is its diagonal, in cm?

  1. The diagonal splits the rectangle into two right-angled triangles. It is the hypotenuse.
  • That is 9 + 12. Square, add, then square root.

81 + 144 = 225, and √225 = 15 cm.

A 6.5 m ladder leans against a vertical wall with its foot 2.5 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach, in m?

  1. The ladder is the hypotenuse. You want a shorter side, so subtract.
  2. 6.5² = 42.25 and 2.5² = 6.25.
  • You added the squares. The wall is shorter than the ladder, so subtract.

42.25 − 6.25 = 36 and √36 = 6 m.

Spot the mistake

Dev says: "The shorter sides are 6 and 8, so the hypotenuse is 6 + 8 = 14." What went wrong?

  • He added the lengths. Pythagoras adds the squares: 36 + 64 = 100, so it is 10.
  • 14 is correct because the hypotenuse is the longest side
  • The sides cannot make a triangle
  1. Sense check: in a triangle, the hypotenuse is shorter than the sum of the other two sides, but it is never equal to it. A 6, 8, 10 triangle fits.

Correct: √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10.

Eve says: "6² + 8² = 100, so the hypotenuse is 100." What went wrong?

  • She added wrongly
  • She stopped at c². She needed to take the square root: √100 = 10.
  • Pythagoras does not apply here
  1. Check the size: could the hypotenuse of a triangle with sides 6 and 8 really be 100?

c² = 100 means c = 10.

Check your knowledge: mixed review

Hypotenuse or shorter side? Add or subtract? Choosing is part of the job.

The shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are 20 cm and 21 cm. How long is the hypotenuse, in cm?

  1. 400 + 441 = ?
  • That is 20 + 21. Square, add, square root.
  • That is c². Take the square root.

400 + 441 = 841 and √841 = 29 cm.

The hypotenuse is 15 cm and one shorter side is 9 cm. Find the other side, in cm.

  1. You are finding a shorter side, so subtract: 225 − 81.
  • You added. The other side must be shorter than 15 cm.

225 − 81 = 144 and √144 = 12 cm. (A 3, 4, 5 triangle times 3.)

Which of these is a Pythagorean triple?

  • 6, 7, 8
  • 8, 15, 17
  • 9, 10, 11
  1. Test each: do the two smaller squares add to the biggest square?

64 + 225 = 289 = 17².

The shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are 1.5 m and 2 m. Find the hypotenuse, in m.

  1. 1.5² = 2.25 and 2² = 4.
  • That is 1.5 + 2. Square, add, square root.
  • That is c². Take the square root.

2.25 + 4 = 6.25 and √6.25 = 2.5 m. (It is a 3, 4, 5 triangle halved.)

The two shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are in the ratio 3 : 4, and the longer of them is 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse, in cm.

  1. The 4 parts are 12 cm, so each part is 3 cm. The shorter of the two sides is 3 parts.
  2. Then it is a 9, 12, ? triangle.
  • 12 and 5 would give 13, but the sides are in the ratio 3 : 4, so the other side is 9, not 5.

The other side is 9 cm. 81 + 144 = 225 and √225 = 15 cm.

Exam style

Show your working. The marks tell you how many steps to write.

(a) [3 marks] A television screen is 40 cm wide and 30 cm tall. Work out the length of its diagonal, in cm.

  1. The diagonal is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle.
  2. 1600 + 900 = ?
  • That is 40 + 30. Square each, add, then take the square root.
  • That is the square. Take the square root.

c² = 40² + 30² = 1600 + 900 = 2500, so c = 50 cm. (A 3, 4, 5 triangle times 10.)

(b) [3 marks] A flagpole is held upright by a wire 13 m long from the top to a point on the ground 5 m from the base of the pole. How tall is the pole, in m?

  1. The wire is the hypotenuse. You want a shorter side: subtract.
  • You added the squares. The pole is shorter than the wire.
  • That is 13 − 5. Lengths do not subtract: work with squares.

13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144, so the pole is 12 m tall.

(c) [3 marks] Work out the area of the right-angled triangle formed by the pole, the ground and the wire, in m².

  1. The pole and the ground meet at a right angle, so they are the base and the height.
  2. Area of a triangle = 1 over 2 × base × height. Use 5 and your answer to (b).
  • That is 5 × 12, the area of a rectangle. A triangle is half of that.
  • That uses the wire (13) as a base. Use the two sides that meet at the right angle: 5 and 12.

1 over 2 × 5 × 12 = 30 m².

Why it works

  • Squares are areas. The figure shows a square on each side. The two smaller areas add up to the big one: 9 + 16 = 25.
  • Adding vs subtracting. The hypotenuse is the biggest, so it stands alone on one side. Finding it, you add. Finding anything else, you subtract.
  • Square root undoes square. The theorem gives c², and the square root undoes the squaring to give c.

Common traps

  • Adding the lengths instead of the squares.
  • Stopping at c² and forgetting the square root.
  • Adding when you are finding a shorter side.
  • Using Pythagoras on a triangle with no right angle.
  • Rounding too early.

Going further

A right-angled triangle has two shorter sides, both 3 cm. Find the exact length of the hypotenuse as a Surd. (Type it like 3√2 or 3sqrt2.)

  1. c² = 9 + 9 = 18, so c = √18.
  2. Simplify: √18 = √9 × √2.
  • Correct, but it can be simplified: √18 = 3√2.

√18 = √(9 × 2) = 3√2 cm, about 4.24 cm.

A square has sides of 5 cm. Find the exact length of its diagonal. (Type it like 5√2 or 5sqrt2.)

  1. The diagonal is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with shorter sides 5 and 5.

c² = 25 + 25 = 50, so c = √50 = 5√2 cm.
