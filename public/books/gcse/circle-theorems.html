    Circle theorems

    A circle theorem is a fact that is always true about angles, tangents and chords in a circle. There are eight to learn. Use each one to find an angle, and write the reason for it in the words an examiner wants. Then run it backwards: look at a diagram and say which theorem it is using.

    Where you will use this: checking a corner is square with the semicircle trick, fitting a strap to a round pipe, and working out the radius of an arch from its width and height.

    Words you need

    Tap a teal word anywhere on the page to see this again.

    Angle at the centre
    The circle theorem: the angle at the centre of a circle is twice the angle at the circumference when both are made from the same arc (the same two points on the circle).
    Cyclic quadrilateral
    A four-sided shape with all four corners on a circle. Its opposite angles add up to 180°.
    Tangent
    A straight line that touches a circle at exactly one point. It is perpendicular to the radius at that point.
    Angle in a semicircle
    The circle theorem: an angle drawn from the two ends of a diameter to any point on the circle is 90°.

    How to write a reason

    In an exam, the answer and the reason are marked separately. The reason is usually a C1 mark: it is lost if you write only the angle.

    Write the angle you found, then a short reason in words, using the standard wording. Not “it is a circle rule” or “same arc”. Say the whole fact.

    You will also use simpler reasons: angles in a triangle add up to 180°, angles on a straight line add up to 180°, angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360°, and the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal (two radii make an isosceles triangle).

    Angles can be written with three letters. Angle ACB is the angle at C between the lines to A and to B.

    An isosceles triangle has an angle of 50° at the top. What is each base angle? Answer in degrees.

    1. An isosceles triangle has two equal angles, the base angles.
    2. The three angles add up to 180°. Take away the 50 first, then share what is left between the two equal angles.
    • That is 180 − 50, but there are two equal base angles to share it between. Halve it.
    • That is the top angle. Take it from 180 and share the rest between the two base angles.

    1. The longest side is opposite the right angle.
    2. Use Pythagoras: square the short sides, add, then take the square root.
    • That adds 5 + 12. Square the sides first, add the squares, then square root.
    • That subtracts. The longest side comes from adding the squares of the other two.

    1. The language of circles Higher

    Circle theorems use some words you have met, and a few new ones.

    • A Chord joins two points on the circle. The longest chord is a Diameter.
    • An Arc is part of the edge of the circle.
    • A Segment (of a circle) is the region between a chord and the arc it cuts off. The chord splits the circle into two segments. Unless the chord is a diameter, one segment is small and the other is big.
    • A Tangent is a straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point. That point is the point of contact.
    • The angle at the centre has its corner at O. The angle at the circumference has its corner on the circle itself.
    • An angle stands on the arc between the two points where its lines meet the circle. Two angles stand on the same arc when their lines go to the same two points on the circle. Two angles at the circumference that stand on the same arc also have their corners on the same side of the line joining those two points.

    Key idea: almost every theorem is about two things: which points are on the circle, and which arc the angle stands on. Always find the arc first.

    2. The angle at the centre Higher

    Take two points A and B on a circle with centre O, and a third point C on the longer arc AB, the arc on the same side of AB as O. The angle at the centre, AOB, and the angle at the circumference, ACB, both stand on the arc AB.

    Theorem 1: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference, when both stand on the same arc.

    This works both ways. Double the angle at the circumference to get the angle at the centre. Halve the angle at the centre to get the angle at the circumference.

    OA and OB are both radii, so triangle OAB is isosceles. You can use that to find the base angles.

    Worked example

    A, B and C are on a circle with centre O. Angle ACB = 35°. Work out angle AOB. Give a reason.

    1. Angle ACB is at the circumference and angle AOB is at the centre. Both stand on the arc AB.
    2. Angle AOB = 2 × 35 = 70°.
    3. Reason: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.

    Worked example: with an isosceles triangle

    A and B are points on a circle with centre O. Angle AOB = 130°. Work out angle OAB.

    1. OA and OB are radii, so triangle OAB is isosceles with OA = OB.
    2. The angles in a triangle add up to 180°, so the two base angles share 180 − 130 = 50°.
    3. Each base angle is 50 ÷ 2 = 25°. Angle OAB = 25°.

    Now you finish it

    A and B are points on a circle with centre O. Angle AOB = 110°. Work out angle ACB, where C is on the long arc AB.

    1. The angle at the circumference is ___ the angle at the centre.
    2. 110 ÷ 2 = ___°.
    Check my answer

    It is half of the angle at the centre. 110 ÷ 2 = 55, so angle ACB = 55°.

    Key idea: the corner of the angle must be on the circle (for the circumference) or at O (for the centre). If C is on the short arc AB, the angle at the centre it goes with is the reflex angle, 360° minus AOB.

    3. Angles in the same segment Higher

    Two points A and B on a circle cut it into two segments. Pick any two points C and D on the circle, both on the same side of the line AB. Then the angle ACB, made by the lines from C to A and to B, is equal to the angle ADB, made by the lines from D to A and to B.

    Theorem 2: angles in the same segment are equal. (Angles at the circumference that stand on the same arc are equal.)

    Think of A and B as two lights and C and D as two people on the edge of the circle. Each person sees the two lights at the same angle.

    Worked example

    Angle ACB = 42°. C and D are in the same segment. Find angle ADB.

    1. Both angles stand on the arc AB, with their corners on the same side of AB.
    2. Angle ADB = angle ACB = 42°.
    3. Reason: angles in the same segment are equal.

    Now you finish it

    Angle ACB = 55°. D is in the same segment as C. Find angle ADB.

    1. Angles in the same ___ are equal.
    2. Angle ADB = ___°.
    Check my answer

    Angles in the same segment are equal, so angle ADB = 55°.

    Check your knowledge: angles in the same segment

    C and D are on the circle. Angle ACB = 42°. Work out angle ADB. Answer in degrees.

    1. Look at where C and D are. Are they on the same side of AB?
    2. Angles in the same segment are equal.
    • That doubles 42. That would be the angle at the centre. Here both angles are at the circumference, so they are equal.
    • That is 180 − 42. This rule says the angles are equal, not that they add to 180.

    Both angles stand on the arc AB from the same side, so angle ADB = 42°. Reason: angles in the same segment are equal.

    1. C and D are in the same segment, so the two angles have a special relationship.
    2. The angles are equal: set 2x + 5 equal to 4x − 21 and solve.
    • That adds the angles to make 180. Angles in the same segment are equal, so set them equal instead.

    2x + 5 = 4x − 21. So 26 = 2x and x = 13. Check: 2 × 13 + 5 = 31 and 4 × 13 − 21 = 31.

    • Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°, because all four corners are on the circle
    1. Where are the corners of the two angles?
    2. Both stand on the arc AB, from the same side.

    C and D are in the same segment, so the reason is: angles in the same segment are equal.

    Angle ACB = 3x° and angle ADB = (x + 40)°, where C and D are in the same segment. Work out x.

    1. Angles in the same segment are equal.
    2. Write 3x = x + 40.
    • That adds the two angles to make 180. They should be equal.

    3x = x + 40, so 2x = 40 and x = 20. The angles are both 60°.

    Angle ACB = 62°. Which angle must also be 62°?

    • Angle AEB
    • Angle ADB

    1. Look at which side of the chord AB each point is on.
    2. Equal angles need corners in the same segment.

    C and D are on the same side of AB, so ADB = ACB = 62°. E is on the other side, so AEB is a different size (180 − 62 = 118°).

    4. The angle in a semicircle Higher

    If AB is a Diameter and C is any other point on the circle, then angle ACB is a right angle. This is the angle in a semicircle.

    Theorem 3: the angle in a semicircle is 90°. (The angle at the circumference, standing on a diameter.)

    This is a special case of Theorem 1. The diameter AB is a straight line through the centre, so the angle at the centre standing on that arc is 180°. Half of 180° is 90°.

    It also works the other way. If a triangle with all three corners on a circle has a right angle, then the side opposite the right angle is a diameter.

    Worked example

    AB is a diameter of a circle and C is another point on the circle. Angle CAB = 35°. Work out angle ABC.

    1. Angle ACB is in a semicircle, so it is 90°.
    2. The angles in triangle ABC add up to 180°: 90 + 35 = 125.
    3. Angle ABC = 180 − 125 = 55°.

    Now you finish it

    AB is a diameter of a circle and C is another point on the circle. Angle ABC = 62°. Work out angle CAB.

    1. Angle ACB = ___°, because it is in a ___.
    2. Angle CAB = 180 − 90 − 62 = ___°.
    Check my answer

    Angle ACB = 90°, because it is in a semicircle. Angle CAB = 180 − 90 − 62 = 28°.

    5. Cyclic quadrilaterals Higher

    A Cyclic quadrilateral is a four-sided shape with all four corners on a circle.

    Theorem 4: the opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.

    Opposite means across the shape, not next to each other: A and C, and B and D. Angles at neighbouring corners do not follow this rule.

    An extra fact follows. If you extend one side, the exterior angle at that corner is equal to the interior angle at the opposite corner.

    Worked example

    ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle ABC = 88°. Work out angle ADC.

    1. B and D are opposite corners.
    2. Angle ADC = 180 − 88 = 92°.
    3. Reason: opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.

    6. Tangents Higher

    Two facts about a Tangent come up again and again.

    Theorem 5: a tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact. The angle between them is 90°.

    Theorem 6: two tangents drawn to a circle from the same outside point are equal in length.

    The shape OATB is a kite, with two right angles at A and B. The two tangents TA and TB are equal, so triangle TAB is isosceles. The tangent TA, the radius OA and the line OT make a right-angled triangle, so Pythagoras’ theorem works.

    Worked example: tangent and radius

    TA is a tangent at A to a circle with centre O. Angle OTA = 28°. Work out angle AOT.

    1. Angle OAT = 90°, because a tangent is perpendicular to the radius.
    2. Angle AOT = 180 − 90 − 28 = 62°.

    Worked example: two tangents

    TA and TB are tangents to a circle from the point T. Angle ATB = 50°. Work out angle TAB.

    1. TA = TB (tangents from the same point are equal), so triangle TAB is isosceles.
    2. The base angles share 180 − 50 = 130°, so each is 65°.
    3. Angle TAB = 65°.

    Now you finish it

    2. Use Pythagoras with 130 as the longest side.
    • That adds 130 + 50. Use Pythagoras: the strap and the radius meet at a right angle.
    • That subtracts 130 − 50. Square the lengths first.

    The tangent TA is perpendicular to the radius OA. TA² = 130² − 50² = 16900 − 2500 = 14400, so TA = 120 mm.

    An electrician sets up a circular lighting track with centre O. Lights are at A and B, and angle AOB = 110°. A camera is on the track at C. Work out the angle ACB that the camera sees. C is on the long arc AB. Answer in degrees.

    1. O is the centre. A and B are on the track and the camera C is on the track too.
    2. The angle at the circumference is half of the angle at the centre.
    • That doubles 110. The camera angle at the circumference is the smaller one: halve it.

    110 ÷ 2 = 55, so angle ACB = 55°. Reason: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.

    A carpenter tests a corner with a semicircular piece of plywood. Its straight edge is a diameter, 1.2 m long. A and B are the two ends of the straight edge, and C is a point on the curved edge. AC = 0.72 m. How long is BC? Answer in m.

    1. AB is a diameter, so what is the angle at C?
    2. Use Pythagoras with AB = 1.2 as the longest side.
    • That is 1.2 − 0.72. Use Pythagoras: square the lengths first.
    • That adds the squares of 0.72 and 1.2. AB is the longest side, so subtract.

    Angle ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle). BC² = 1.2² − 0.72² = 1.44 − 0.5184 = 0.9216, so BC = 0.96 m.

    A plumber checks the level of water in a round pipe with a radius of 25 mm. The surface of the water makes a chord that is 48 mm long. How far is the surface of the water from the centre of the pipe? Answer in mm.

    1. The line from the centre to the middle of the chord is perpendicular to it.
    2. Half of the chord is 24 mm. Use Pythagoras with the radius as the longest side.
    • That subtracts 25 − 24. Square the lengths first, then subtract.
    • That adds the squares of 24 and 25. The radius is the longest side, so subtract.

    Half the chord = 24 mm. OM² = 25² − 24² = 625 − 576 = 49, so OM = 7 mm.

    Higher [4 marks] The arch in the first question (span 2.4 m, radius 1.69 m) has a curved oak trim fixed along the whole curve. The trim costs £18.50 per metre. How much does the trim cost? Use the full calculator value until the end, then give your answer to 2 decimal places, like 12.50.

    1. Half of the span is 1.2 m. In the triangle with the centre, sin of the half-angle at the centre = 1.2 ÷ 1.69.
    2. The angle at the centre for the whole arch is double that half-angle. The arc is that fraction of the circumference.
    3. Multiply the arc length by £18.50.
    • That is the length of the curve in m. Multiply by £18.50 to get the cost.
    • That uses the half-angle (45.2°) for the arc, which gives half the curve. The whole arch uses the full angle at the centre.

    Half the angle at O: sin θ = 1.2 ÷ 1.69, so θ = 45.2397…°. The whole angle = 90.4794…°. Arc = (90.4794 ÷ 360) × 2 × π × 1.69 = 2.6688… m. Cost = 2.6688… × 18.50 = 49.3725…, so £49.37.

    • M1 sin θ = 1.2 ÷ 1.69, so θ = 45.2…°
    • M1 arc = (90.48 ÷ 360) × 2 × π × 1.69 (= 2.66…)
    • M1 × 18.50

    [3 marks] O is the centre of the circle. Angle AOB = 118°. Work out the size of angle ACB. Give a reason for your answer.

    1. Which angle is at the centre and which is at the circumference?
    2. Halve the angle at the centre.
    3. Your reason must be the whole fact.
    • That doubles 118. The angle at the circumference is half of the angle at the centre.
    • That looks like 180 − 118. This question uses the centre and circumference theorem.

    Angle ACB = 118 ÷ 2 = 59°. Reason: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.

    • M1 for 118 ÷ 2
    • A1 for 59
    • C1 for the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference

    [4 marks] TA and TB are tangents to a circle with centre O. Angle ATB = 64°. Work out the size of angle OAB. Give reasons for your working.

    1. Triangle TAB is isosceles. Why?
    2. Find angle TAB, then use the right angle OAT.
    3. Give a reason each time: tangents from a point, and tangent and radius.
    • That is angle TAB. Angle OAB is what is left of the right angle OAT.
    • That is 90 − 64. Find angle TAB first, then take it from 90.

    TA = TB (tangents from a point are equal), so angle TAB = (180 − 64) ÷ 2 = 58°. Angle OAT = 90° (tangent perpendicular to the radius). Angle OAB = 90 − 58 = 32°.

    • M1 for angle TAB = (180 − 64) ÷ 2 = 58
    • M1 for 90 − 58
    • A1 for 32
    • C1 for tangents from a point are equal AND a tangent is perpendicular to the radius

    [4 marks] TS is a tangent to the circle at A. Angle SAB = 48° and angle CAB = 62°. Work out the size of angle ABC. Give a reason for each step.

    1. Find angle ACB first. Which theorem links it to the tangent?
    2. Then use the angles in a triangle.
    3. Give a reason for each step.
    • That adds 48 + 62. Take the total from 180.
    • That is angle ACB. You are asked for angle ABC.

    Angle ACB = angle SAB = 48° (the angle between a tangent and a chord is equal to the angle in the alternate segment). Angle ABC = 180 − 48 − 62 = 70° (angles in a triangle add up to 180°).

    • M1 for angle ACB = 48
    • M1 for 180 − 48 − 62
    • A1 for 70
    • C1 for the alternate segment theorem stated as a reason

    [3 marks] A circle has a radius of 17 cm. The chord AB is 16 cm long. Work out the distance from the centre of the circle to the chord AB. Give your answer in cm.

    1. The line from the centre to the chord meets it at right angles, at the middle.
    2. Half the chord is 8 cm.
    3. Use Pythagoras with 17 as the longest side.
    • That is 17 − 16. Use half the chord and Pythagoras.
    • That adds the squares of 17 and 8. The radius is the hypotenuse, so subtract.

    Half the chord = 8 cm. OM² = 17² − 8² = 289 − 64 = 225, so OM = 15 cm.

    • M1 for 16 ÷ 2 = 8 (the line from the centre bisects the chord)
    • M1 for 17² − 8²
    • A1 for 15

    [3 marks] O is the centre of the circle. Angle AOB = 130°. Work out angle OAB. Give reasons for your answer.

    1. OA and OB are radii.
    2. Triangle OAB is isosceles. Find the total of the two equal angles.
    3. Halve that total.
    • That is the total of the two base angles. Each is half of it.

    OA = OB (radii), so triangle OAB is isosceles. Angle OAB = (180 − 130) ÷ 2 = 25°.

    • M1 for (180 − 130) ÷ 2
    • A1 for 25
    • C1 for OA = OB (radii) so base angles equal, or angles in a triangle add up to 180°

    Why it works

    • One idea runs through them all. The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference. The semicircle (180° at the centre gives 90°), the same-segment fact (two angles that are each half of the same angle at the centre) and the cyclic quadrilateral (two arcs that make the whole circle) all follow from it.
    • Radii are all the same length. That is why triangles made from two radii are isosceles, and why equal tangents come out of Pythagoras.
    • A tangent is the limit of a chord. Slide one end of a chord round the circle towards its other end, and the chord gets closer and closer to a tangent. That is why the tangent and chord angle equals the angle in the alternate segment.
    • A line from the centre cuts a chord in half. The two right-angled triangles it makes have the same hypotenuse (a radius) and a common side.

    Common traps

    • Doubling when you should halve: the angle at the centre is the BIGGER one.
    • Using the ordinary angle at the centre when the point C is on the short arc: use the reflex angle instead.
    • Adding adjacent angles of a cyclic quadrilateral. Only the OPPOSITE corners add up to 180°.
    • Finding the angle but forgetting the reason, which loses the C1 mark. Write the whole fact.
    • Forgetting that radii are equal, so a triangle with two radii as sides is isosceles.
    • Adding lengths when you should use Pythagoras: the tangent, radius and OT make a right-angled triangle.

    Higher Two tangents are drawn to a circle of radius 4 cm from a point T. The angle between the tangents is 60°. How far is T from the centre O? Answer in cm.

    1. OT splits the angle at T in half. So the angle OTA is 30°.
    2. Angle OAT = 90°, so OA is opposite the 30° angle. Use sin 30° = OA ÷ OT.
    • That is the radius. OT is the hypotenuse, which is longer.
    • That is 4 ÷ tan 30°, the tangent length TA. OT is the hypotenuse.

    Angle OTA = 30° (OT bisects the angle between the tangents). Angle OAT = 90°. sin 30° = 4 ÷ OT, and sin 30° = 0.5, so OT = 4 ÷ 0.5 = 8 cm.

    Higher ABCD is a parallelogram with all four corners on a circle. Angle ABC = x°. Work out x.

    1. In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal.
    2. In a cyclic quadrilateral, opposite angles add up to 180°.
    • That is the total of the pair. The two equal angles share it, so each is half of it.
    • 45 is half of a right angle. Equal opposite angles that add to 180 are each 90.

    Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal, so angle ADC = angle ABC = x. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°, so x + x = 180, giving x = 90. A parallelogram on a circle is a rectangle.

    Higher A circular arc has a chord of 12 cm and a rise (height of the arc above the chord) of 2 cm. What is the radius of the circle? Answer in cm.

    1. Draw the line from the centre to the middle of the chord. It is perpendicular, and it carries on to the top of the arc.
    2. The centre is (r − 2) from the chord. Write r² = (r − 2)² + 6².
    • That is half of the chord. The radius is the longer line from the centre to the edge.
    • That is the rise doubled. Form a right-angled triangle using the radius.

    Half the chord = 6 cm. r² = (r − 2)² + 6² = r² − 4r + 4 + 36, so 4r = 40 and r = 10 cm.
