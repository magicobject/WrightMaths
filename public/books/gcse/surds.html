    Surds

    A surd is a root like √2 whose decimal never ends and never repeats (it is irrational). Keep it as a root and your answer is exact: no rounding, no error.

    Where you will use this: the exact length of a diagonal brace, cable run or pipe offset before you turn it into a cutting length, and any exam question that says “give your answer exactly”.

    Words you need

    Tap a teal word anywhere on the page to see this again.

    Surd
    A root that cannot be written as a whole number or a fraction. A surd is an exact answer, so keep it as a root.
    Rational number
    A number that can be written as a fraction with a whole number on the top and a whole number on the bottom. Its decimal either stops or repeats.
    Rationalising the denominator
    Rewriting a fraction so there is no surd on the bottom. Multiply top and bottom by a suitable surd.
    Conjugate (of a surd expression)
    The same two terms with the sign in the middle swapped. Multiplying an expression by its conjugate removes the surds.

    How to type surds on this page: type 3sqrt2 (or 3√2) for 3√2. Put the number in front, then sqrt, then the number under the root, like 5sqrt3 or sqrt7. Where an answer has more than one part, such as 4+2sqrt3, type it in the order the question shows, with no spaces needed. A fraction goes like sqrt3/3.

    What is the square root of 81?

    1. What number multiplied by itself makes 81?
    2. 8 × 8 = 64 is too small. Try the next number up.
    • That is half of 81. A square root is a number that multiplies by itself.
    • That doubles it. A square root is the number that multiplies by itself to make 81.

    9 × 9 = 81, so the square root of 81 is 9.

    What is 12²?

    1. 12² means 12 × 12.
    2. Try 12 × 10 = 120, then add 12 × 2.
    • That doubles 12. Squaring is a number times itself.

    12 × 12 = 120 + 24 = 144.

    What is the biggest square number that is a factor of 50? (The square numbers are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36 and so on.)

    1. A factor divides into 50 exactly.
    2. Try the square numbers from the top: does 36 divide into 50? Does 25?
    • 50 is not a square number. Look for a square number that divides into 50.
    • 5 is a factor, but it is not a square number. You want 25.

    50 ÷ 25 = 2, so 25 is a factor of 50. It is a square number (5 × 5), and no bigger square number divides 50.

    Which square number is the biggest factor of 48?

    1. List the square numbers up to 48: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36.
    2. Check from the biggest: does 36 go into 48? Does 25?
    • 4 is a square factor of 48, but a bigger one divides in as well.
    • 24 is a factor of 48, but it is not a square number.

    48 ÷ 16 = 3, so 16 is a square factor. 25 and 36 do not divide 48, so 16 is the biggest.

    1. What is a surd?

    Some square roots are friendly: √16 = 4 and √81 = 9. They are whole numbers. A whole number, a fraction or a decimal that stops is a rational number.

    Other roots are not friendly. Try √2 on a calculator. You get 1.41421356… and the digits go on for ever with no pattern. That is an Irrational number number. A root like this, left as a root, is called a Surd.

    Key idea: a surd is exact. 1.414 is only close. When a question says “give your answer exactly” or “in surd form”, leave the root in, like √2 or 3√5.

    How to tell: if the whole number under the root is a Square number (1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100…), the root is a whole number. If a whole number under the root is not a square number, the root is a surd. So √25 = 5 is not a surd, but √26 is.

    Typing tip: the root sign is √. You can type sqrt instead, so √7 is sqrt7.

    Worked example

    Which of √36, √20 and √2.25 are surds?

    1. √36: 36 is a square number (6 × 6), so √36 = 6. Not a surd.
    2. √20: 20 is not a square number (4 × 4 = 16 and 5 × 5 = 25). So √20 is a surd.
    3. √2.25: 1.5 × 1.5 = 2.25, so √2.25 = 1.5. It is a decimal that stops. Not a surd.

    Now you finish it

    Which of √49 and √50 is a surd?

    1. Is 49 a square number? 7 × 7 = ___.
    2. Is 50 a square number? 7 × 7 = 49 and 8 × 8 = ___, so 50 sits between them.
    Check my answer

    7 × 7 = 49, so √49 = 7 and is not a surd. 8 × 8 = 64, so 50 is not a square number and √50 is a surd.

    Check your knowledge: what is a surd?

    Which of these is a surd?

    • √64
    • √7
    • √0.09
    1. A surd is a root that does not come out as a whole number or a stopping decimal.
    2. Check which number under the root is a square number.

    64 is a square number and 0.09 = 0.3 × 0.3, so those roots are 8 and 0.3. 7 is not a square number, so √7 is a surd.

    How many of these are surds? √4, √5, √9, √10, √16, √20

    1. Check each number under the root: is it a square number?
    2. The square numbers up to 20 are 1, 4, 9 and 16.
    • That counts every root. Count only the ones that are not whole numbers.

    √4 = 2, √9 = 3 and √16 = 4 are whole numbers. √5, √10 and √20 are surds. That is 3.

    Work out √9 + √16.

    1. Find each root first, then add.
    2. What are √9 and √16 as whole numbers?
    • That is √25. You cannot add the numbers under the roots. Find √9 and √16 first.

    √9 = 3 and √16 = 4, so √9 + √16 = 3 + 4 = 7.

    √50 is a surd. It sits between two whole numbers. What is the smaller one?

    1. Find two square numbers that 50 sits between.
    2. 49 = 7 × 7 and 64 = 8 × 8.
    • That is the larger whole number. The smaller one is below √50.
    • 5 × 5 is only 25. Find the square number just below 50.

    49 < 50 < 64, so 7 < √50 < 8. The smaller whole number is 7. (The calculator gives √50 = 7.0710…)

    2. Simplifying a surd

    Some surds can be written in a tidier way. The trick is that a root of a product splits: √(a × b) = √a × √b. (Here a and b are not negative.)

    So to simplify √18: find a square number that goes into 18, split it off, and take its root.

    √18 = √(9 × 2) = √9 × √2 = 3√2

    Key idea: look for the biggest Square number that is a Factor of the number. If you pick a smaller one you will have to do it again. A surd is fully simplified when the number under the root has no square factor except 1.

    Learn these square numbers: 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100.

    Worked example

    Simplify √48.

    1. The square factors of 48: 4 and 16. The biggest is 16, and 48 = 16 × 3.
    2. √48 = √16 × √3 = 4√3.
    3. Check: 3 has no square factor, so 4√3 is fully simplified.

    Worked example: a number in front

    Simplify 5√8.

    1. 8 = 4 × 2, so √8 = 2√2.
    2. 5√8 = 5 × 2√2 = 10√2. (Multiply the numbers in front.)

    Now you finish it

    Simplify √75.

    1. The biggest square factor of 75 is ___, and 75 = ___ × 3.
    2. √75 = √25 × √3 = ___√3.
    Check my answer

    75 = 25 × 3, so √75 = 5√3.

    Check your knowledge: simplifying surds

    Type answers like 3sqrt2.

    Write √12 as a simplified surd.

    1. Which square number goes into 12?
    2. 12 = 4 × 3. Split the root.
    • The 4 comes out as its root, √4 = 2, not 4.
    • √12 is not 12 ÷ 2. Find a square factor and take its root.

    √12 = √4 × √3 = 2√3.

    Write √45 as a simplified surd.

    1. Is 4 a factor of 45? Is 9?
    2. 45 = 9 × 5.
    • The 9 comes out as its root, √9 = 3, not 9.
    • It is the 9 that comes out of the root, not the 5.

    √45 = √9 × √5 = 3√5.

    Write √32 as a simplified surd.

    1. Find the biggest square number that divides 32.
    2. 32 = 16 × 2.
    • That is true, but 8 still has a square factor (4). Use the biggest square factor, 16, to finish in one go.
    • 16 comes out as √16 = 4, not 8.

    √32 = √16 × √2 = 4√2.

    Write √72 as a simplified surd.

    1. The biggest square factor is bigger than 10.
    2. 72 = 36 × 2.
    • 3√8 is true, but 8 still has a square factor (4). Use 36 instead.
    • 2√18 is true, but 18 still has a square factor (9). Use 36 instead.

    √72 = √36 × √2 = 6√2.

    Which is √98 written as a simplified surd?

    • 49√2
    • 7√2
    • 2√7
    1. Which square number is a factor of 98?
    2. 98 = 49 × 2.

    √98 = √49 × √2 = 7√2.

    Write 5√8 as a simplified surd.

    1. Simplify √8 first.
    2. √8 = 2√2. Then multiply by the 5 in front.
    • You still need to multiply by the 2 that comes out of √8.
    • You cannot multiply the 5 into the root like that. Simplify √8 first.

    5√8 = 5 × 2√2 = 10√2.

    A square tile has an area of 27 cm². Write the side length as a simplified surd, in cm.

    1. The area of a square is side × side, so the side is the square root of the area.
    2. Simplify √27. Which square number goes into 27?
    • That is right but not simplified: 27 has a square factor, 9.
    • The 9 comes out as √9 = 3.

    Side = √27 = √9 × √3 = 3√3 cm.

    3. Multiplying and dividing surds

    The splitting rule works backwards, so you can multiply roots:

    • √a × √b = √(ab)
    • √a ÷ √b = √(a ÷ b) (b is more than zero)
    • √a × √a = a. For example √7 × √7 = 7, because squaring undoes a square root.

    If there are numbers in front, multiply those together, and multiply the roots together: 2√3 × 4√5 = (2 × 4)√(3 × 5) = 8√15.

    Key idea: multiply the numbers outside, multiply the numbers inside, then check whether the answer can be simplified.

    Worked example: multiply

    Work out √6 × √10 and simplify.

    1. √6 × √10 = √60.
    2. 60 = 4 × 15, so √60 = 2√15.

    Worked example: divide

    Work out 12√10 ÷ 4√2.

    1. Divide the numbers in front: 12 ÷ 4 = 3.
    2. Divide the roots: √10 ÷ √2 = √5.
    3. So the answer is 3√5.

    Now you finish it

    Work out √2 × √8.

    1. √2 × √8 = √___.
    2. That is a square number, so the root is a whole number: ___.
    Check my answer

    √2 × √8 = √16 = 4.

    Check your knowledge: multiplying and dividing

    Work out √3 × √5. Give your answer as a surd.

    1. Multiply the numbers under the roots.
    2. √a × √b = √(ab).
    • That looks like adding. For multiplying, multiply the numbers under the roots.

    √3 × √5 = √15.

    Work out √7 × √7.

    1. What happens when you multiply a square root by itself?
    2. Squaring and square rooting undo each other.
    • That is 7 × 7. Here you multiply √7 by itself, which brings you back to 7.

    √7 × √7 = √49 = 7.

    Work out √2 × √8.

    1. Multiply the numbers under the roots.
    2. Is the answer a square number?
    • √16 is a whole number. What is it?
    • That looks like adding. Multiply the numbers under the roots.

    √2 × √8 = √16 = 4.

    Work out 3√2 × 4√3.

    1. Multiply the numbers in front, then multiply the roots.
    2. Work out 3 × 4. Then work out √2 × √3.
    • That looks like adding 3 and 4. Multiply the numbers in front.
    • That looks like adding 2 and 3 under the roots. Multiply them.

    3√2 × 4√3 = (3 × 4) √(2 × 3) = 12√6.

    Work out √50 ÷ √2.

    1. Divide the numbers under the roots.
    2. 50 ÷ 2 = ?
    • That is 50 ÷ 2, but you need its square root.
    • √25 is a whole number. What is it?

    √50 ÷ √2 = √(50 ÷ 2) = √25 = 5.

    Work out (2√3)².

    1. (2√3)² means 2√3 × 2√3.
    2. Square the 2 and square the √3 separately.
    • That looks like squaring √3 but not the 2. (2√3)² = 2² × (√3)².
    • That looks like squaring the 2 but not the √3.

    (2√3)² = 2² × (√3)² = 4 × 3 = 12.

    Work out √6 × √10 and simplify your answer.

    1. Multiply the numbers under the roots first.
    2. Does 60 have a square factor?
    • That is right but not simplified: 60 has a square factor, 4.

    √6 × √10 = √60 = √4 × √15 = 2√15.

    Work out 12√10 ÷ 4√2.

    1. Divide the numbers in front, then divide the roots.
    2. Work out 12 ÷ 4. Then work out √10 ÷ √2.
    • That looks like subtracting the numbers under the roots. Divide them: 10 ÷ 2.

    12√10 ÷ 4√2 = (12 ÷ 4)√(10 ÷ 2) = 3√5.

    A rectangle is 2√6 cm long and 3√2 cm wide. Find its area as a simplified surd, in cm².

    1. Area of a rectangle = length × width.
    2. Multiply the numbers in front, multiply the roots, then simplify.
    • That is right but not simplified: 12 has a square factor, 4.
    • That looks like adding. For an area, multiply.

    Area = 2√6 × 3√2 = 6√12 = 6 × 2√3 = 12√3 cm².

    4. Adding and subtracting surds

    Surds behave like Like terms. 3x + 5x = 8x, and in the same way 3√2 + 5√2 = 8√2. The √2 is like the x.

    You cannot join √2 + √3 into one surd, just as you cannot join x + y. And √2 + √3 is not √5.

    Key idea: simplify each surd first. Surds that look different, such as √8 and √2, may turn out to be like terms: √8 = 2√2.

    Worked example

    Simplify √12 + √27.

    1. √12 = 2√3 and √27 = 3√3.
    2. Now they are like terms: 2√3 + 3√3 = 5√3.

    Now you finish it

    Simplify √50 − √8.

    1. √50 = ___√2 and √8 = 2√2.
    2. Subtract: ___√2 − 2√2 = ___√2.
    Check my answer

    √50 = 5√2 and √8 = 2√2, so 5√2 − 2√2 = 3√2.

    Check your knowledge: adding and subtracting

    Simplify 3√5 + 4√5.

    1. Think of √5 as a letter, like x.
    2. 3x + 4x = 7x.
    • That looks like adding the numbers under the roots. They stay the same: √5 is like the x.
    • That looks like multiplying. For adding, add the numbers in front.

    3√5 + 4√5 = 7√5.

    Simplify √2 + √8.

    1. Simplify √8 first.
    2. √8 = 2√2.
    • You cannot add the numbers under the roots. Simplify √8 first.

    √8 = 2√2, so √2 + √8 = √2 + 2√2 = 3√2.

    Simplify √18 + √50.

    1. Simplify both surds first.
    2. √18 = 3√2. What is √50?
    • You cannot add the numbers under the roots. Simplify each surd first.

    √18 = 3√2 and √50 = 5√2, so the sum is 8√2.

    Simplify √75 − √12.

    1. Simplify both surds first.
    2. √75 = 5√3. What is √12?
    • You cannot subtract the numbers under the roots. Simplify each surd first.

    √75 = 5√3 and √12 = 2√3, so the difference is 3√3.

    Which is √12 + √3 written as simply as possible?

    • √15
    • 3√3
    • 3√6
    1. Simplify √12 first.
    2. √12 = 2√3, so you have 2√3 + √3.

    √12 = 2√3, so √12 + √3 = 2√3 + √3 = 3√3.

    A rectangle has sides of √20 cm and √45 cm. Find its perimeter as a simplified surd, in cm.

    1. The perimeter is 2 lengths plus 2 widths.
    2. Simplify √20 and √45 first.
    • That is half of the perimeter. There are two of each side.

    √20 = 2√5 and √45 = 3√5. Half the perimeter is 5√5, so the perimeter is 2 × 5√5 = 10√5 cm.

    5. Brackets with surds

    Expand brackets with surds exactly like brackets with letters: multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second. The only new fact is √a × √a = a.

    (2 + √3)(4 + √3) = 8 + 2√3 + 4√3 + 3 = 11 + 6√3

    Key idea: the answer is a whole-number part plus a surd part. Collect them separately, just like numbers and x.

    Do you remember the grid from double brackets? It works here too: write the four boxes, then add the boxes up.

    Worked example

    Expand and simplify (4 + √2)(1 + √2).

    1. Multiply every pair: 4 × 1 = 4, 4 × √2 = 4√2, √2 × 1 = √2, √2 × √2 = 2.
    2. Whole numbers: 4 + 2 = 6. Surds: 4√2 + √2 = 5√2.
    3. The answer is 6 + 5√2.

    Worked example: a special pair

    Expand and simplify (3 + √5)(3 − √5).

    1. The four pieces: 9, − 3√5, +3√5 and − 5.
    2. The two surd pieces cancel. 9 − 5 = 4.
    3. The answer is 4, with no surd left. This is the Difference of two squares pattern: (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b².

    Now you finish it

    Expand and simplify (2 + √5)(3 + √5).

    1. The pieces: 6, 2√5, 3√5 and ___.
    2. Whole numbers: 6 + ___ = ___. Surds: 2√5 + 3√5 = ___√5.
    Check my answer

    The last piece is √5 × √5 = 5. Whole numbers: 6 + 5 = 11. Surds: 5√5. The answer is 11 + 5√5.

    Check your knowledge: brackets with surds

    Type answers like 3+2sqrt5, with the whole number first.

    Expand √3(√3 + 4).

    1. Multiply √3 by each term in the bracket.
    2. √3 × √3 = 3.
    • The first term is √3 × √3, which is 3, not √3.

    √3 × √3 = 3 and √3 × 4 = 4√3, so √3(√3 + 4) = 3 + 4√3.

    Expand and simplify (2 + √3)(1 + √3).

    1. Multiply every pair, to get four pieces.
    2. The pieces are 2, 2√3, √3 and 3.
    • Count the surd pieces again: 2√3 and √3 make 3√3.

    (2 + √3)(1 + √3) = 2 + 2√3 + √3 + 3 = 5 + 3√3.

    Expand and simplify (3 + √2)(2 − √2).

    1. Multiply every pair. Watch the minus sign.
    2. The pieces are 6, − 3√2, +2√2 and − 2.
    • Check the signs: − 3√2 + 2√2 = − √2, not + √2.
    • The last piece is +√2 × (− √2) = − 2. Take it away from 6.

    (3 + √2)(2 − √2) = 6 − 3√2 + 2√2 − 2 = 4 − √2.

    Expand and simplify (√2 + 1)².

    1. Squaring means a bracket times itself: (√2 + 1)(√2 + 1).
    2. There are four pieces, and two of them are the same.
    • That looks like missing the two middle pieces. (√2 + 1)² is not √2² + 1².
    • Check the first piece: √2 × √2 = 2, and it joins the 1.

    (√2 + 1)(√2 + 1) = 2 + √2 + √2 + 1 = 3 + 2√2.

    Expand and simplify (5 + √2)(5 − √2).

    1. Look at the two brackets. What do you notice about them?
    2. It is the difference of two squares: (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b².
    • The last piece is − √2 × √2 = − 2, so you take 2 away from 25.
    • That looks like squaring the 5 but not the √2. 25 − (√2)² = 25 − 2.

    (5 + √2)(5 − √2) = 25 − 2 = 23.

    A rectangle has a length of (4 + √3) cm and a width of (2 + √3) cm. Find its area as a simplified expression, in cm².

    1. Area = length × width, so expand (4 + √3)(2 + √3).
    2. The pieces are 8, 4√3, 2√3 and 3.
    • That is missing the two surd pieces, 4√3 and 2√3.
    • The surd pieces are 4√3 and 2√3. Add them: how many √3?

    (4 + √3)(2 + √3) = 8 + 4√3 + 2√3 + 3 = 11 + 6√3 cm².

    6. Rationalising the denominator

    A fraction with a surd on the bottom, like 1 over √2, is awkward to work with. Rationalising means rewriting it so the bottom is a whole number. Multiply top and bottom by the same surd.

    1 over √2 = 1 × √2 over √2 × √2 = √2 over 2

    This is allowed because √2 over √2 = 1, so the value does not change. On the bottom, √2 × √2 = 2, a whole number.

    Typing tip: type √2 over 2 as sqrt2/2, and 3√2 over 2 as 3sqrt2/2.

    Key idea: multiply top and bottom by the surd that is on the bottom. Then simplify if you can: 6 over √3 = 6√3 over 3 = 2√3.

    Worked example

    Rationalise the denominator of 10 over √5.

    1. Multiply top and bottom by √5: 10 × √5 over √5 × √5 = 10√5 over 5.
    2. Divide 10 by 5: the answer is 2√5.

    Now you finish it

    Rationalise the denominator of 4 over √3.

    1. Multiply top and bottom by √3: 4√3 over ___.
    2. Can 4 and the bottom be simplified?
    Check my answer

    4 over √3 = 4√3 over 3. The 4 and 3 share no factor, so this is the answer. In surd form it is 4√3 over 3.

    Two-part bottoms Higher

    What about a bottom like (1 + √2)? Multiplying by √2 does not help. Instead multiply top and bottom by its Conjugate (of a surd expression): the same two numbers with the sign in the middle swapped, (1 − √2). The surds then cancel, by the difference of two squares.

    (1 + √2)(1 − √2) = 1 − 2 = − 1

    Worked example

    Rationalise the denominator of 3 over 1 + √2.

    1. The conjugate of 1 + √2 is 1 − √2. Multiply top and bottom by it.
    2. Top: 3(1 − √2) = 3 − 3√2. Bottom: (1 + √2)(1 − √2) = 1 − 2 = − 1.
    3. So the fraction is 3 − 3√2 over − 1 = − 3 + 3√2, or 3√2 − 3.

    Now you finish it

    Rationalise the denominator of 1 over 3 + √2.

    1. Multiply top and bottom by 3 − √2.
    2. Bottom: (3 + √2)(3 − √2) = 9 − ___ = ___.
    Check my answer

    Bottom: 9 − 2 = 7. Top: 3 − √2. So the answer is 3 − √2 over 7.

    Check your knowledge: rationalising

    Type fractions like sqrt3/3 or 3sqrt2/2.

    Rationalise the denominator of 1 over √2.

    1. Multiply top and bottom by the same surd.
    2. Which surd is on the bottom?
    • You cannot just drop the surd. Multiply the top and the bottom by √2.
    • The bottom becomes √2 × √2 = 2. Keep the 2 on the bottom.

    1 over √2 = 1 × √2 over √2 × √2 = √2 over 2.

    Rationalise the denominator of 6 over √3 and simplify.

    1. Multiply top and bottom by √3.
    2. Then divide 6 by the 3 on the bottom.
    • That is right but not fully simplified. 6 ÷ 3 = 2.
    • The √3 on the top stays. 6 ÷ 3 = 2, so the answer has a √3 in it.

    6 over √3 = 6√3 over 3 = 2√3.

    Rationalise the denominator of 3 over √2.

    1. Multiply top and bottom by √2.
    2. The top is 3 × √2.
    • You cannot drop the surd. Multiply the top and the bottom by √2.
    • The bottom becomes √2 × √2 = 2. Keep the 2 on the bottom.

    3 over √2 = 3√2 over √2 × √2 = 3√2 over 2.

    What should you multiply the top and bottom of 5 over √7 by?

    • √7
    • 7, the number under the root
    • 5, the number on the top
    1. You want the bottom to become a whole number.
    2. What do you multiply √7 by to get a whole number?

    √7 × √7 = 7, a whole number, so multiply by √7. The answer is 5√7 over 7.

    Higher Rationalise the denominator of 1 over 1 + √2. Type it like 2+sqrt3.

    1. Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate, 1 − √2.
    2. The bottom becomes 1 − 2.
    • The bottom is 1 − 2 = − 1. Divide the top by − 1: both signs change.

    1 over 1 + √2 = 1 − √2 over (1 + √2)(1 − √2) = 1 − √2 over − 1 = √2 − 1.

    Higher Rationalise the denominator of 2 over 3 − √5. Type it like (1+sqrt3)/2.

    1. Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate, 3 + √5.
    2. The bottom is 3² − (√5)².
    • The conjugate of 3 − √5 is 3 + √5. Swap the middle sign.
    • That is right but not simplified. Divide top and bottom by 2.

    2 over 3 − √5 = 2(3 + √5) over (3 − √5)(3 + √5) = 2(3 + √5) over 9 − 5 = 2(3 + √5) over 4 = 3 + √5 over 2.

    7. Exact answers in diagrams

    In Pythagoras questions the answer is often a surd. If the question says “exact”, do not use a calculator for the last step. Stop at the root and simplify it.

    Legs 4 and 6: c² = 16 + 36 = 52, so c = √52 = 2√13

    Key idea: an exact answer is what you get before rounding. You can check it by typing it into a calculator, but write the surd.

    The same idea is behind the exact values in trigonometry: sin 60° = √3 over 2 and tan 60° = √3 are exact, and the 3 and 2 come from a triangle with sides 1, √3 and 2.

    Worked example

    A right-angled triangle has short sides of 4 cm and 6 cm. Find the long side exactly.

    1. Pythagoras: c² = 4² + 6² = 16 + 36 = 52.
    2. c = √52. The biggest square factor of 52 is 4, and 52 = 4 × 13.
    3. So c = 2√13 cm. (Check: 2√13 ≈ 7.2, and 4 and 6 give about 7.2.)

    Now you finish it

    A right-angled triangle has short sides of 2 cm and 6 cm. Find the long side exactly.

    1. c² = 4 + ___ = ___.
    2. c = √40 = ___√10.
    Check my answer

    c² = 4 + 36 = 40, so c = √40 = √4 × √10 = 2√10 cm.

    Check your knowledge: exact answers

    Give every answer exactly, as a simplified surd. Type it like 5sqrt2.

    A right-angled triangle has two short sides of 1 cm each. Find the length of the long side, exactly, in cm.

    1. Use Pythagoras: c² = a² + b².
    2. 1² + 1² = ?
    • That is the decimal. The question asks for an exact answer: leave the surd.
    • That is c². Take the square root to find c.

    c² = 1² + 1² = 2, so c = √2 cm.

    A right-angled triangle has short sides of 3 cm and 5 cm. Find the long side exactly, in cm.

    1. Square both short sides and add.
    2. Check whether 34 has a square factor before you finish.
    • That is c². Take the square root to find c.
    • That looks like adding the sides. Pythagoras needs the squares: 9 + 25.

    c² = 3² + 5² = 9 + 25 = 34. 34 has no square factor, so c = √34 cm.

    A right-angled triangle has short sides of 2 cm and 4 cm. Find the long side exactly and simplified, in cm.

    1. c² = 2² + 4².
    2. Simplify the root: 4 is a square factor of 20.
    • That is right but not simplified: 20 has the square factor 4.
    • √4 = 2, so the 4 comes out of the root as a 2.

    c² = 4 + 16 = 20, so c = √20 = √4 × √5 = 2√5 cm.

    In a right-angled triangle the long side is 6 cm and one short side is 3 cm. Find the other short side exactly, in cm.

    1. The long side is the hypotenuse. Which side is it?
    2. Subtract: a² = 6² − 3².
    • That adds 36 and 9. When you look for a short side you subtract.
    • That is right but not simplified: 27 has the square factor 9.

    a² = 36 − 9 = 27, so a = √27 = √9 × √3 = 3√3 cm.

    A square has sides of 5 cm. Find the length of its diagonal exactly, in cm.

    1. A diagonal cuts the square into two right-angled triangles.
    2. The diagonal is the long side. Use 5² + 5².
    • That is right but not simplified: 50 has the square factor 25.
    • That is 5 + 5. A diagonal is shorter than going round two sides: use Pythagoras.

    d² = 25 + 25 = 50, so d = √50 = √25 × √2 = 5√2 cm.

    A triangle has a base of 2√3 cm and a perpendicular height of 3√3 cm. Find its area exactly, in cm².

    1. Area of a triangle = 1 over 2 × base × height.
    2. Multiply the surds: √3 × √3 = 3.
    • That looks like the halving step is missing. A triangle is half of a rectangle.
    • That looks like adding. For area, multiply base and height.

    Area = 1 over 2 × 2√3 × 3√3 = 1 over 2 × 6 × 3 = 9 cm².

    A 6 m ladder leans against a wall at 60° to the ground. Its height up the wall is 6 × sin 60°, and sin 60° = √3 over 2 exactly. Find the height exactly, in m.

    1. Replace sin 60° with √3 over 2.
    2. Work out 6 × √3 over 2.
    • That is the rounded decimal. The question asks for an exact answer.
    • That is right but not simplified: 6 ÷ 2 = 3.

    Height = 6 × √3 over 2 = 3√3 m.

    Flip it: run it backwards

    So far you took a root and made it simpler: √18 became 3√2. Now go the other way: take 3√2 and put the 3 back under the root.

    Forwards

    √18 = ?

    Split off the square: √9 × √2 = 3√2.

    Backwards

    3√2 = √?

    Square the 3 and multiply: √(9 × 2) = √18.

    • To write a√b as one root: square the number in front, then multiply by b. a√b = √(a²b).
    • To write a whole number as a root: 5 = √25, because 5² = 25.

    Key idea: to compare surds, write them all as single roots. The bigger number under the root is the bigger value.

    Worked example

    Write 4√3 as a single square root.

    1. Square the 4: 4² = 16.
    2. Multiply by 3: 16 × 3 = 48.
    3. So 4√3 = √48. Check: √48 = √16 × √3 = 4√3.

    Worked example: compare

    Which is bigger, 3√6 or 4√3?

    1. 3√6 = √(9 × 6) = √54.
    2. 4√3 = √(16 × 3) = √48.
    3. 54 > 48, so 3√6 is bigger.

    Now you finish it

    Write 2√7 as a single square root.

    1. Square the 2: ___. Multiply by 7: ___.
    Check my answer

    2² = 4 and 4 × 7 = 28, so 2√7 = √28.

    Check your knowledge: flip it

    Type answers like sqrt18.

    Write 4√2 as a single square root.

    1. Square the number in front.
    2. 4² = 16. Then multiply by the 2 under the root.
    • That looks like multiplying 4 by 2. The 4 must be squared first: 4² = 16.

    4² = 16 and 16 × 2 = 32, so 4√2 = √32.

    Write 2√5 as a single square root.

    1. Square the 2.
    2. 2² = 4. Then multiply by 5.
    • That looks like multiplying by 2 instead of squaring it. 2² = 4.

    2² = 4 and 4 × 5 = 20, so 2√5 = √20.

    Write 3√7 as a single square root.

    1. Square the 3.
    2. 3² = 9. Then multiply by 7.
    • That looks like multiplying 3 by 7. Square the 3 first: 3² = 9.

    3² = 9 and 9 × 7 = 63, so 3√7 = √63.

    Write 5 as a square root.

    1. Which number, when you take its square root, gives 5?
    2. Square 5.
    • √10 is not 5. You need a number that is 5 times itself.

    5² = 25, so 5 = √25.

    √n = 6√3. What is the whole number n?

    1. Write 6√3 as a single root.
    2. Square the 6, then multiply by 3.
    • That looks like multiplying 6 by 3. Square the 6 first: 36 × 3.
    • That looks like multiplying 6 by 3 twice (6 × 3 × 3). Square the 6 once: 6² = 36.

    6√3 = √(36 × 3) = √108, so n = 108.

    Which is the smallest: 2√3, √11 or 3√2?

    • 2√3
    • √11
    • 3√2
    1. Write each one as a single root.
    2. Then the smallest number under the root is the smallest value.

    2√3 = √12, 3√2 = √18 and the other is √11. 11 is the smallest, so √11 is the smallest.

    Spot the mistake

    Finding an error in someone else's working helps you learn. For each one, decide what went wrong.

    Ali says: √8 + √2 = √10. What went wrong?

    • Nothing is wrong
    • You cannot add the numbers under the roots
    • He should have multiplied them to get √16
    1. Try it on a calculator: is √8 + √2 the same as √10?
    2. Surds behave like letters. Do 2x + x work out as x²?

    You cannot add the numbers under the roots. √8 = 2√2, so √8 + √2 = 2√2 + √2 = 3√2.

    Cal says: (2 + √3)² = 4 + 3 = 7. What went wrong?

    • He left out the middle term 4√3
    • He should have used 4 × 3 = 12
    • Nothing is wrong, because squaring a sum squares each part
    1. Write the bracket twice: (2 + √3)(2 + √3).
    2. How many pieces do you get when you multiply out?

    (2 + √3)(2 + √3) = 4 + 2√3 + 2√3 + 3 = 7 + 4√3. Cal missed the two middle pieces.

    Dee says: 6 over √3 = 6 over 3 = 2. What went wrong?

    • She changed the surd on the bottom but not on the top
    • Nothing is wrong. It does equal 2.
    • She should have multiplied by 3 on the top and bottom
    1. Rationalising multiplies top and bottom by the same thing.
    2. Did Dee multiply the top by √3 as well?

    She must multiply the top by √3 as well: 6 × √3 over √3 × √3 = 6√3 over 3 = 2√3.

    Check your knowledge: mixed review

    Mixed up on purpose, like a real paper. Work out which idea fits.

    Write √200 as a simplified surd.

    1. Find the biggest square factor of 200.
    2. 200 = 100 × 2.
    • 2√50 is true, but 50 still has a square factor (25). Use 100.
    • Take the root of the square factor: √100 = 10.

    √200 = √100 × √2 = 10√2.

    Work out 2√3 × √6 and simplify your answer.

    1. Multiply the numbers in front and the numbers under the roots.
    2. √18 has a square factor.
    • That is right but not simplified: 18 has the square factor 9.

    2√3 × √6 = 2√18 = 2 × 3√2 = 6√2.

    Expand and simplify (√2 − 1)².

    1. Write it as (√2 − 1)(√2 − 1).
    2. The four pieces: 2, − √2, − √2 and 1.
    • Check the signs of the two middle pieces: both are negative.

    (√2 − 1)(√2 − 1) = 2 − √2 − √2 + 1 = 3 − 2√2.

    Rationalise the denominator of 10 over √2.

    1. Multiply top and bottom by √2.
    2. Then divide 10 by 2.
    • That is right but not simplified: 10 ÷ 2 = 5.

    10 over √2 = 10√2 over 2 = 5√2.

    Which of these is not a surd?

    • √12
    • √81
    • √30
    1. Check which number under the root is a square number.
    2. The square numbers go 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81.

    81 = 9 × 9, so √81 = 9, a whole number. The other two are surds.

    Exam style

    Use these like a real paper. The marks tell you how much to write. No calculator is needed.

    Write your working on paper, then type the answer. Type surds like 7sqrt3.

    [3 marks] Write √75 + √12 in the form a√3, where a is a whole number.

    1. Simplify each surd on its own first.
    2. √75 = 5√3. What is √12?
    • You cannot add the numbers under the roots. Simplify each surd first.
    • Check √12 again: 12 = 4 × 3, so √12 = 2√3.

    √75 = √25 × √3 = 5√3. √12 = √4 × √3 = 2√3. Sum = 5√3 + 2√3 = 7√3.

    • M1 for √75 = 5√3
    • M1 for √12 = 2√3
    • A1 for 7√3

    [3 marks] Expand and simplify (3 + √5)(2 − √5). Type it like 4+sqrt3.

    1. Multiply every pair to get four pieces.
    2. Remember √5 × √5 = 5.
    • Check the signs: the last piece is − √5 × √5 = − 5, which you take away.
    • The two surd pieces are − 3√5 and +2√5. They add to − √5.

    (3 + √5)(2 − √5) = 6 − 3√5 + 2√5 − 5 = 1 − √5.

    • M1 for a correct expansion of all four pieces: 6, − 3√5, +2√5 and − 5
    • M1 for using √5 × √5 = 5 and collecting the surd terms
    • A1 for 1 − √5

    [3 marks] Rationalise the denominator of 14 over √7 and simplify. Type it like 3sqrt2.

    1. Multiply top and bottom by √7.
    2. Then divide 14 by 7.
    • That is right but not simplified: 14 ÷ 7 = 2.
    • The √7 on the top stays. The answer has a √7 in it.

    14 over √7 = 14 × √7 over √7 × √7 = 14√7 over 7 = 2√7.

    • M1 for multiplying top and bottom by √7
    • M1 for 14√7 over 7
    • A1 for 2√7

    [4 marks] Show that (2 + √3)² + (2 − √3)² = 14.

    1. Expand each bracket separately. Squaring a bracket needs four pieces.
    2. The middle terms of the two brackets should cancel when you add.

    (2 + √3)² = 4 + 2√3 + 2√3 + 3 = 7 + 4√3. (2 − √3)² = 4 − 2√3 − 2√3 + 3 = 7 − 4√3. Adding: 7 + 4√3 + 7 − 4√3 = 14, as required.

    • M1 for expanding (2 + √3)² to 7 + 4√3
    • M1 for expanding (2 − √3)² to 7 − 4√3
    • M1 for adding the two results
    • C1 for the surd terms cancelling and the total 14 stated

    [4 marks] A rectangle has an area of 24 cm² and a length of 4√3 cm. Work out the width exactly, as a simplified surd, in cm.

    1. Width = area ÷ length.
    2. Write it as 24 over 4√3, simplify the numbers, then rationalise.
    • That still has a surd on the bottom. Rationalise it.
    • That looks like multiplying by √3 without dividing by the 3 on the bottom.

    Width = 24 ÷ 4√3 = 6 over √3 = 6√3 over 3 = 2√3 cm.

    • M1 for 24 ÷ 4√3
    • M1 for 6 over √3
    • M1 for multiplying top and bottom by √3
    • A1 for 2√3

    Higher [4 marks] A triangle has a base of (4 + √2) cm and a perpendicular height of (4 − √2) cm. Work out its area, in cm².

    1. Area = 1 over 2 × base × height.
    2. Multiply (4 + √2)(4 − √2) first. It is the difference of two squares.
    • That is the product of the base and height. A triangle is half of that.
    • That is half of 16. You forgot to take away (√2)² = 2 before halving.

    (4 + √2)(4 − √2) = 16 − 2 = 14. Area = 1 over 2 × 14 = 7 cm².

    • M1 for (4 + √2)(4 − √2) or 4² − (√2)²
    • M1 for 16 − 2 = 14
    • M1 for 1 over 2 × 14
    • A1 for 7

    Why it works

    • The product rule. √(a × b) = √a × √b. Check with numbers: √(4 × 9) = √36 = 6 and √4 × √9 = 2 × 3 = 6.
    • There is no matching rule for adding. √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5, but √9 + √16 = 3 + 4 = 7. Adding is different.
    • Rationalising multiplies by 1. √3 over √3 is 1, so the value is the same. Only the way it is written changes.
    • The conjugate works by the difference of two squares. (a + √b)(a − √b) = a² − b. The surd terms cancel, so nothing under a root is left.
    • Why bother with exact answers? Rounding early loses accuracy. Keep the surd, and round once at the very end if you have to.

    Common traps

    • Adding under the root: √a + √b is not √(a + b).
    • Taking a square factor out without taking its root: √12 = 2√3, not 4√3.
    • Stopping too early: √72 = 3√8 is not fully simplified; 6√2 is.
    • Squaring a bracket as if it were two terms: (2 + √3)² is not 4 + 3.
    • Rationalising the bottom only. Multiply the top by the same surd.
    • Typing a decimal when the question says “exactly”.

    Going further

    x = √3 + √2. Work out x² and give it in the form a + b√c. Then think: is x² bigger or smaller than 10?

    1. Write x² as (√3 + √2)(√3 + √2).
    2. The two middle pieces are √3 × √2, which is √6.
    • That looks like missing the middle pieces. (a + b)² is not a² + b².
    • There are two middle pieces, each √6.

    x² = 3 + √6 + √6 + 2 = 5 + 2√6. So x² is less than 10, because 2√6 is less than 5: 2√6 = √24 and 24 is less than 25 = 5².

    Show that 1 over √3 − √2 = √3 + √2.

    1. Rationalise by multiplying top and bottom by the conjugate, √3 + √2.
    2. The bottom is the difference of two squares: (√3)² − (√2)².

    Multiply top and bottom by √3 + √2. The bottom is (√3 − √2)(√3 + √2) = 3 − 2 = 1. The top is √3 + √2. So the fraction equals √3 + √2 over 1 = √3 + √2, as required.

    k is a positive whole number and (k + √3)(k − √3) = 13. Find k.

    1. Expand the brackets. The surd terms cancel.
    2. You get k² − 3 = 13. Solve for k.
    • That is k². Take the square root to find k.
    • k² − 3 = 13, so k² = 16, not 10.

    (k + √3)(k − √3) = k² − 3 = 13, so k² = 16. k is positive, so k = 4. Check: (4 + √3)(4 − √3) = 16 − 3 = 13.

    2. 250² + 250² = 125 000. Then take the square root.

    Check your knowledge: stretch, is √2 a fraction?

    A proof for Higher students who want to see why a surd really never ends.

    [4 marks] Higher Prove that √2 is irrational. (Start by supposing √2 = a/b, where a and b are whole numbers with no common factor.)

    1. What does irrational mean? Which kind of number can be written as a/b?
    2. Square both sides of √2 = a/b and rearrange to get a² = 2b².
    3. If a² is even, what must a be? Write a = 2c and see what happens to b.

    Suppose √2 = a/b with a and b whole numbers that have no common factor. Squaring gives 2 = a²/b², so a² = 2b². So a² is even, which means a is even (the square of an odd number is odd). Write a = 2c. Then 4c² = 2b², so b² = 2c², so b² is even and b is even. Now a and b are both even, so they share the factor 2. That contradicts the choice of a and b with no common factor. So √2 cannot be written as a fraction: it is irrational.

    • M1 for supposing √2 = a/b in lowest terms and squaring to get a² = 2b²
    • A1 for a² even, so a is even (a = 2c)
    • M1 for 4c² = 2b², so b² = 2c² and b is even
    • C1 for a and b both even, which contradicts no common factor, so √2 is irrational
