1In a right-angled triangle the hypotenuse is 10 cm and one angle is 30°. How long is the side opposite the 30° angle? Give your answer in centimetres. [Figure: Right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 10 and a 30 degree angle]5
Working: sin 30° = x/10, so x = 10 × sin 30° = 10 × 0.5 = 5 cm.
2Two angles of a triangle are 48° and 71°. What is the third angle, in degrees?61
Working: 48 + 71 = 119. 180 − 119 = 61°.
3A triangle has a base of 9 cm and a perpendicular height of 6 cm. What is its area, in square centimetres?27
Working: 1/2 × 9 × 6 = 27 cm².
4Solve 6/x = 1.5.4
Working: 6 = 1.5x, so x = 6 ÷ 1.5 = 4.
5Work out √52, and give your answer to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Triangle ABC with its sides and angles named]7.2
Working: √52 = 7.2111… ≈ 7.2.
6In triangle ABC, which side is opposite angle B? Type one small letter, like a. [Figure: Triangle ABC]b (also accept: B)
Working: Side b joins A and C, so it does not touch B. Side b is opposite angle B.
7In triangle ABC, side c is 7 cm long. Which angle is opposite side c? Type one capital letter, like A.C (also accept: c)
Working: Side c joins A and B. The corner it does not touch is C, so angle C is opposite side c.
8Which of these is a matching pair, which means a side and the angle opposite it?Side a and angle A
Working: A matching pair uses the same letter: a with A, b with B, c with C. The side is across from the angle.

Working: 34 + 105 = 139. 180 − 139 = 41°. Angle C = 41°.
10In this triangle, angle C = 40° is known and so is its matching side. Which side is it? Type one small letter, like a. [Figure: Triangle with angle C and one side marked]c (also accept: C)
Working: The side across from C joins A and B. It is side c, and it is marked 9 cm.
11Angle A = 40°, side a = 12 cm and angle B = 65°. You need side b. Which rule? [Figure: Triangle with angles A and B and side a known]Sine rule
Working: a and A are a matching pair, and you want b, which goes with B. That is the sine rule.
12Sides b = 7 cm and c = 9 cm, with angle A = 52° between them. You need side a. Which rule? [Figure: Triangle with two sides and the angle between them known]Cosine rule
Working: The known angle A is between sides b and c, and the unknown a is opposite it. There is no matching pair, so use the cosine rule.
13A triangle has sides 7 cm, 8 cm and 9 cm. You want to find the biggest angle. Type the name of the rule, sine or cosine. [Figure: Triangle with three sides known]cosine (also accept: cosine rule, cos, the cosine rule)
Working: No angle is given, so there is no matching pair. All three sides are known, so use the cosine rule.
14A surveyor knows two angles of a triangle and one side. She works out the third angle first. Which rule does she use next? Type sine or cosine.sine (also accept: sine rule)
Working: Two angles give the third (they add to 180°). The known side then has its opposite angle, which is a matching pair. Use the sine rule.
15You want to find side c in this triangle. Do you need the sine rule or the cosine rule? [Figure: Right-angled triangle]No. It has a right angle, so SOH CAH TOA is enough.
Working: It has a right angle at B, so SOH CAH TOA and Pythagoras solve it. The sine rule would also work here, but it is not needed. Here, tan 36° = 8/c.
16In triangle ABC, angle A = 48°, angle B = 71° and side a = 7.5 cm. Find side b. Give your answer in centimetres, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Triangle with angles 48 and 71 and one side]9.5
Working: Sine rule: b/(sin 71°) = 7.5/(sin 48°). b = 7.5 × sin 71° ÷ sin 48° = 7.5 × 0.9455… ÷ 0.7431… = 9.5424… ≈ 9.5 cm.
17In triangle ABC, angle A = 35°, angle B = 82° and side a = 9.4 cm. Find side c, in centimetres, to 1 decimal place. (Find angle C first.) [Figure: Triangle with angles 35 and 82]14.6
Working: Angle C = 180° − 35° − 82° = 63°. Sine rule: c/(sin 63°) = 9.4/(sin 35°). c = 9.4 × sin 63° ÷ sin 35° = 9.4 × 0.8910… ÷ 0.5735… = 14.6021… ≈ 14.6 cm.
18In triangle ABC, angle A = 78°, angle B = 31° and side a = 15 cm. Find side c, in centimetres, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Triangle with angles 78 and 31]14.5
Working: Sine rule: c/(sin 71°) = 15/(sin 78°). c = 15 × sin 71° ÷ sin 78° = 15 × 0.9455… ÷ 0.9781… = 14.4996… ≈ 14.5 cm. (c is shorter than a because 31° is smaller than 78°. That is a good check.)
19In triangle ABC, A = 52°, B = 40° and a = 9 cm. Which line finds b? [Figure: Triangle with A 52, B 40 and side a 9]b = 9 × sin 40° ÷ sin 52°
Working: b goes with B = 40° and a goes with A = 52°. So b/(sin 40°) = 9/(sin 52°), and b = 9 × sin 40° ÷ sin 52° = 7.3413… ≈ 7.3 cm.
20A surveyor wants the distance from point A to a tree at C across a river. She measures a baseline AB = 140 m on her side. The angle at A between the baseline and the tree is 64°. The angle at B is 71°. Find the distance AC, to the nearest metre. [Figure: Surveyor baseline across a river]187
Working: Angle C = 180° − 64° − 71° = 45°. AB = 140 m is opposite C and AC is opposite B. AC/(sin 71°) = 140/(sin 45°), so AC = 140 × sin 71° ÷ sin 45° = 187.2031… ≈ 187 m.
21A roof truss is a triangle ABC. The span BC is 8.4 m. Angle B = 32° and angle C = 41°. Find the length of rafter AB, in metres, to 2 decimal places. [Figure: Roof truss triangle]5.76
Working: Angle A = 180° − 32° − 41° = 107°. The span BC = 8.4 m is opposite A. AB is opposite C. AB/(sin 41°) = 8.4/(sin 107°), so AB = 8.4 × sin 41° ÷ sin 107° = 5.7626… ≈ 5.76 m.
22In triangle ABC, a = 15 cm is opposite A = 72°, and b = 11 cm. Find angle B, in degrees, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Triangle with a, A and b known]44.2
Working: Sine rule: (sin B)/11 = (sin 72°)/15. So sin B = 11 × sin 72° ÷ 15 = 0.6974…. B = sin^−1(0.6974…) = 44.2220… ≈ 44.2°.
23In triangle ABC, A = 58°, a = 13 cm and b = 9 cm. Find angle C, in degrees, to 1 decimal place. (Find angle B first, and keep all the digits on your calculator.) [Figure: Triangle with A, a and b known, finding C]86.0
Working: sin B = 9 × sin 58° ÷ 13 = 0.5871…, so B = sin^−1(0.5871…) = 35.9522…°. Then C = 180° − 58° − 35.9522…° = 86.0477… ≈ 86.0°.
24For a triangle drawn with an obtuse angle B, the sine rule gives sin B = 0.5, and your calculator says sin^−1(0.5) = 30°. What is B?150°
Working: Both 30° and 150° have sine 0.5. The diagram shows an obtuse angle, so B = 180° − 30° = 150°.
25A triangular plot has corners A, B and C. AB = 64 m, AC = 48 m and the angle at C is 75°. Find angle B, in degrees, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Triangular plot ABC]46.4
Working: Sine rule: (sin B)/48 = (sin 75°)/64. So sin B = 48 × sin 75° ÷ 64 = 0.7244…. B = sin^−1(0.7244…) = 46.4226… ≈ 46.4°.
26A carpenter fits a brace into a triangle. A side of 3.1 m is opposite an angle of 52°. Another side is 2.5 m. Find the angle opposite the 2.5 m side, in degrees, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Brace triangle]39.5
Working: Sine rule: (sin B)/2.5 = (sin 52°)/3.1. So sin B = 2.5 × sin 52° ÷ 3.1 = 0.6354…. B = sin^−1(0.6354…) = 39.4565… ≈ 39.5°.
27In triangle ABC, b = 5.4 cm, c = 7.1 cm and angle A = 38°. Find side a, in centimetres, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Triangle with two sides and the angle between them]4.4
Working: Cosine rule: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A = 5.4² + 7.1² − 2 × 5.4 × 7.1 × cos 38° = 19.1453…. a = √19.1453… = 4.3755… ≈ 4.4 cm.
28In triangle ABC, b = 6 cm, c = 9 cm and angle A = 115°. Find side a, in centimetres, to 1 decimal place. (Notice that cos 115° is negative.) [Figure: Triangle with an obtuse angle]12.8
Working: Cosine rule: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A = 6² + 9² − 2 × 6 × 9 × cos 115° = 162.6427…. a = √162.6427… = 12.7531… ≈ 12.8 cm. (The obtuse angle makes a the longest side, which matches the picture.)
29In this triangle b = 6 cm, c = 9 cm and A = 115°. Which line is the start of the cosine rule for a? [Figure: Triangle with b, c and A]a² = 6² + 9² − 2 × 6 × 9 × cos 115°
Working: The rule is a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A. With b = 6, c = 9 and A = 115° that is a² = 6² + 9² − 2 × 6 × 9 × cos 115°.
30A surveyor stands at A. Posts B and C are on opposite sides of a pond. AB = 110 m, AC = 85 m and angle BAC = 72°. Find the distance BC across the pond, in metres, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Surveying across a pond]116.4
Working: Cosine rule: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A = 85² + 110² − 2 × 85 × 110 × cos 72° = 13546.3822…. a = √13546.3822… = 116.3889… ≈ 116.4 m.
31In triangle ABC, b = 8 m, c = 6 m and A = 60°. What is side a, in metres, to 1 decimal place? (cos 60° = 0.5 exactly.)7.2
Working: a² = 8² + 6² − 2 × 8 × 6 × 0.5 = 64 + 36 − 48 = 52. a = √52 = 7.2111… ≈ 7.2 m.
32A triangle has sides a = 9 cm, b = 12 cm and c = 15 cm. Find angle C, in degrees. What do you notice? [Figure: Triangle with sides 9, 12 and 15]90
Working: cos C = (9² + 12² − 15²)/(2 × 9 × 12) = 0/216 = 0, so C = cos^−1(0) = 90°. It is a right angle: 9² + 12² = 15², so this is Pythagoras again.
33A roof truss is a triangle ABC. The span is BC = 6.8 m and each rafter, AB and AC, is 4.1 m. Find the angle between the span and rafter AB at B, in degrees, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Symmetrical roof truss]34.0
Working: Cosine rule: cos B = (a² + c² − b²)/2ac = (6.8² + 4.1² − 4.1²)/(2 × 6.8 × 4.1) = 46.24/55.76 = 0.8292…. B = cos^−1(0.8292…) = 33.9763… ≈ 34.0°.
34A triangle has sides a = 11 cm, b = 5 cm and c = 7 cm. Find angle A, in degrees, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Triangle with a long side a]132.2
Working: Cosine rule: cos A = (b² + c² − a²)/2bc = (5² + 7² − 11²)/(2 × 5 × 7) = (-47)/70 = -0.6714…. A = cos^−1(-0.6714…) = 132.1774… ≈ 132.2°. The cosine is negative, so the angle is obtuse, as the picture shows.
35You use the cosine rule and get cos A = −0.35. What does this tell you?Angle A is obtuse, bigger than 90°.
Working: cos 90° = 0, and angles bigger than 90° have a negative cosine. cos^−1(−0.35) = 110.5…°, an obtuse angle.

42The gable end of a roof is a triangle ABC with base BC = 9 m. The angle at B is 42° and the angle at C is 58°. Find the perpendicular height of the gable from A to BC, in metres, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Gable end with its height]5.2
Working: Angle A = 180° − 42° − 58° = 80°. Sine rule: AB = 9 × sin 58° ÷ sin 80° = 7.7501…. In the right-angled triangle on the left, h = AB × sin 42° = 7.7501… × 0.6691… = 5.1858… ≈ 5.2 m.
43A field is made of two triangles, ABC and ACD. In triangle ABC, AB = 9 m, BC = 12 m and angle ABC = 70°. Triangle ACD has a right angle at C, and CD = 5 m. Find the length AD, in metres, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Field made of two triangles]13.3
Working: In triangle ABC (cosine rule): AC² = 9² + 12² − 2 × 9 × 12 × cos 70° = 151.1236…. In triangle ACD (Pythagoras): AD² = AC² + 5² = 151.1236… + 25 = 176.1236…. AD = √176.1236… = 13.2711… ≈ 13.3 m.
44A walker leaves A and walks 5 km on a bearing of 060° to B. She then walks 7 km on a bearing of 150° to C. Find the distance AC, in kilometres, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: A walk in two legs]8.6
Working: The back bearing from B to A is 060° + 180° = 240°. The bearing from B to C is 150°. Angle ABC = 240° − 150° = 90°. Right-angled: AC² = 5² + 7² = 74, so AC = √74 = 8.6023… ≈ 8.6 km.
45Use the same walk: A to B is 5 km on a bearing of 060°, and B to C is 7 km on a bearing of 150°. The angle at B is 90°. Find the bearing of C from A, to the nearest degree. Type it as a number, like 075.114
Working: tan(BAC) = 7/5, so BAC = tan^−1(1.4) = 54.4623…°. The bearing of C from A is 060° + 54.4623…° = 114.4623…°, which is 114° to the nearest degree.

49A triangle has an area of 24 cm². One side is 6 cm and the angle between that side and the unknown side is 50°. Find the length of the unknown side, in centimetres, to 1 decimal place.10.4

51In the worked example for the cosine rule (b = 7, c = 9, A = 52°) side a came out as 7.2 cm to 1 decimal place. Run it backwards: a triangle has sides 7 cm, 9 cm and 7.2 cm. Working: cos A = (9² + 7² − 7.2²)/(2 × 9 × 7) = 78.16/126 = 0.6203…. A = cos^−1(0.6203…) = 51.6606… ≈ 51.7°. That is close to the original 52°. It is not exact because 7.2 was rounded.
52Jo says: a triangle has sides 8 cm and 6 cm with a 30° angle between them. The area is 1/2 × 8 × 6 × sin 30°, and my calculator says −23.7. What went wrong?The calculator was in radian mode, not degrees.
Working: In degree mode sin 30° = 0.5, so the area is 24 × 0.5 = 12 cm². In radian mode sin 30 = −0.988…, which gives −23.7. Always check D or DEG on the screen, and check an area is positive.
53Sam says: to use the cosine rule with b = 7, c = 9 and A = 52°, I typed (7² + 9² − 2 × 7 × 9) × cos 52° and got a² = 2.5. That cannot be right. What went wrong?The cos 52° should only multiply the 2 × 7 × 9 part.
Working: Sam multiplied the whole of (49 + 81 − 126) by cos 52°, which gives 4 × 0.6157 = 2.46…. The correct sum is 49 + 81 − (126 × cos 52°) = 130 − 77.57… = 52.43…, so a = 7.2…. Work out 2bc cos A first.
54Ana says: a triangle has sides 7 cm and 9 cm, and the 40° angle is opposite the 7 cm side. The area is 1/2 × 7 × 9 × sin 40°. Is she right? [Figure: Triangle with a 40 degree angle opposite the 7 centimetre side]No. The 40° angle is not between the 7 cm and 9 cm sides.

73Show that when angle A = 90°, the cosine rule a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A becomes Pythagoras’ theorem.Written answer: see the mark scheme
  1. M1 for cos 90° = 0, so 2bc cos A = 0
  2. C1 for a² = b² + c² − 0 = b² + c², which is Pythagoras

  3. C1 for C = 120°, because cos 120° = −1/2
