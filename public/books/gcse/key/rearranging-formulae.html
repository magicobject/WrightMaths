    Rearranging formulae: answers

    61 questions, 6 of them marked (17 marks in all). Question numbers match the page. Use your browser's Print for a copy.

    Answers for Rearranging formulae
    No.QuestionAnswerMarks
    1I think of a number, double it, then take away 3. My answer is 11. What was my number? [Figure: Number puzzle flow chart]7
    Working: 11 + 3 = 14, then 14 ÷ 2 = 7. You just solved 2x − 3 = 11, so the number was 7.
    2Solve 5x = 40.8
    Working: x = 40 ÷ 5 = 8. Check: 5 × 8 = 40.
    3Solve x − 6 = 10.16
    Working: x = 10 + 6 = 16. Check: 16 − 6 = 10.
    4Work out 4 + 3 × 5.19
    Working: 3 × 5 = 15, then 4 + 15 = 19.
    5Speed s = d ÷ t, where d is the distance and t is the time. A train goes 150 km in 3 hours. What is its speed in km/h?50
    Working: s = 150 ÷ 3 = 50 km/h.
    6The area of a rectangle is A = lw. Find A in cm² when l = 8 cm and w = 5 cm. [Figure: A rectangle 8 cm by 5 cm]40
    Working: A = 8 × 5 = 40 cm².
    7An electrician uses V = IR, where V is the voltage in volts, I is the current in amps and R is the resistance in ohms. Find V when I = 4 and R = 6.24
    Working: V = 4 × 6 = 24 volts.
    8The distance round a circle is C = 2πr. To keep the sums simple, use π = 3. Find C when r = 5.30
    Working: C = 2 × 3 × 5 = 30.
    9T = 5 − 2n. Find T when n = 4.-3
    Working: 2 × 4 = 8, so T = 5 − 8 = −3.
    10A cook roasts a joint of beef for T = 40w + 20 minutes, where w is its weight in kg. How many minutes does a 2.5 kg joint need? [Figure: Bar model for y = x + 5]120
    Working: 40 × 2.5 = 100, then 100 + 20 = 120 minutes.
    11p = q − 4. Make q the subject. Type your answer starting q =. [Figure: A bar q with a piece taken away]p+4 (also accept: 4+p)
    Working: Add 4 to both sides: p + 4 = q. So q = p + 4. Check: if q = 14, then p = 10, and 10 + 4 = 14.
    12A = lw. Make w the subject. Type your answer like w = A/l.a/l
    Working: Divide both sides by l: A/l = w. So w = A/l. Check: if l = 4 and w = 3, then A = 12, and 12 ÷ 4 = 3.
    13An oven uses energy E = Pt, where P is its power and t is the time it is on. Make P the subject. Type your answer like P = E/t.e/t
    Working: Divide both sides by t: E/t = P. So P = E/t.
    14F = ma (force = mass × acceleration). Which is the formula with a as the subject?a = F ÷ m
    Working: a is multiplied by m, so divide both sides by m: a = F ÷ m.
    15y = x/4. Make x the subject. Type your answer like x = 3y.4y (also accept: y4)
    Working: Multiply both sides by 4: 4y = x. So x = 4y. Check: if x = 8, then y = 2, and 4 × 2 = 8.
    16s = d/t (speed = distance ÷ time). Make d the subject. Type your answer like d = ab. [Figure: Flow chart for y = 3x + 2]st (also accept: ts)
    Working: Multiply both sides by t: st = d. So d = st. Check: at 4 m/s for 3 s you go 12 m, and 4 × 3 = 12.
    17y = 2x + 1. Make x the subject. Type your answer like x = (y-5)/3, with brackets. [Figure: Flow chart for y = 2x + 1](y-1)/2 (also accept: y/2-1/2)
    Working: y − 1 = 2x, so x = (y − 1)/2, typed (y-1)/2. Check: if x = 4, then y = 9, and (9 − 1) ÷ 2 = 4.
    18A skip costs C = 30 + 12d pounds to hire for d days. Make d the subject.(c-30)/12 (also accept: c/12-30/12, c/12-5/2, c/12-2.5)
    Working: C − 30 = 12d, so d = (C − 30)/12, typed (c-30)/12.
    19p = 4q − 3. Make q the subject.(p+3)/4 (also accept: p/4+3/4)
    Working: p + 3 = 4q, so q = (p + 3)/4, typed (p+3)/4. Check: if q = 2, then p = 5, and (5 + 3) ÷ 4 = 2.
    20You have the formula x = (y − 1) ÷ 2. Find x when y = 9.4
    Working: (9 − 1) ÷ 2 = 8 ÷ 2 = 4.
    21A scaffold tower costs C = 55 + 20d pounds for d days. Ali pays £215. For how many days did he hire it?8
    Working: 215 − 55 = 160, and 160 ÷ 20 = 8 days. Check: 55 + 20 × 8 = 215.
    22y = x/3 + 2. Make x the subject. Use brackets if you need them.3(y-2) (also accept: 3y-6, (y-2)3, y3-6)
    Working: y − 2 = x/3, so x = 3(y − 2) = 3y − 6. Check: if x = 6, then y = 4, and 3 × (4 − 2) = 6.
    23P = 2r + 6. Which is the formula with r as the subject?r = (P − 6) ÷ 2
    Working: Take 6 from both sides: P − 6 = 2r. Divide both sides by 2: r = (P − 6) ÷ 2.
    24The area of a square with side s is A = s². Make s the subject.sqrt(a) (also accept: √a)
    Working: The square root of both sides gives s = √A, which you type as sqrt(a). Check: if s = 5, then A = 25, and the root of 25 is 5.
    25y = x² + 3. Make x the subject.sqrt(y-3) (also accept: √(y-3))
    Working: y − 3 = x², so x = √(y − 3), typed sqrt(y-3). Check: if x = 4, then y = 19, and the root of 16 is 4.
    26A circular pond has area A = πr². Use π = 3.14. The area is 78.5 m². Find r in metres.5
    Working: r² = 78.5 ÷ 3.14 = 25, so r = 5 metres.
    27A moving object has kinetic energy E = 1/2mv². Make v the subject. [Figure: Forwards: h = 4 and C = unknown]sqrt(2e/m) (also accept: √(2e/m), sqrt((2e)/m), sqrt(e2/m))
    Working: 2E = mv², so 2E/m = v², so v = √(2E/m), typed sqrt(2e/m).
    28Distance d = vt, where v is speed and t is time. A cyclist rides 36 km at 12 km/h. How many hours does it take?3
    Working: t = d ÷ v = 36 ÷ 12 = 3 hours.
    29An electrician uses V = IR. A heater has V = 230 volts and R = 46 ohms. Find the current I in amps.5
    Working: I = 230 ÷ 46 = 5 amps.
    30A rectangular patio has area 24 m² and is 6 m long. How wide is it, in metres? [Figure: A rectangular patio]4
    Working: w = A ÷ l = 24 ÷ 6 = 4 m. Check: 6 × 4 = 24.
    31The distance round a circle is C = 2πr. Use π = 3. A circle has C = 36. Find its radius, r. [Figure: A circle with its radius]6
    Working: 36 = 6r, so r = 36 ÷ 6 = 6. Check: 2 × 3 × 6 = 36.
    32v = u + at. Make u the subject. Type your answer like u = v-at.v-at
    Working: Take at from both sides: v − at = u. So u = v − at. Check: 17 − 2 × 6 = 5, the u we used before.
    33v = u + at. Make t the subject. Use brackets where you need them.(v-u)/a (also accept: v/a-u/a)
    Working: v − u = at, so t = (v − u)/a, typed (v-u)/a.
    34A plumber charges C = 45 + 30h pounds for h hours. A bill is £165. How many hours was the job?4
    Working: (165 − 45) ÷ 30 = 120 ÷ 30 = 4 hours.
    35Ben says: if y = 3x + 2, then x = y/3 − 2. What went wrong?He divided only y by 3.
    Working: The whole of y − 2 must be divided by 3: x = (y − 2)/3. Ben's version gives about 2.7 when it should give 4.
    36Priya says: if d = vt, then t = v/d. What went wrong?She turned it upside down.
    Working: Divide both sides by v: t = d/v. With d = 6 and v = 2 that gives 3, as it should.
    37Ali says: if P = 2l + 2w, then l = P/2 − 2w. What went wrong?He halved P but not the 2w.
    Working: P − 2w = 2l, so l = (P − 2w)/2 = P/2 − w. Both parts are halved.
    38Temperatures: F = 1.8C + 32 turns Celsius into Fahrenheit. A summer day is 20°C. What is that in °F?68
    Working: 1.8 × 20 = 36, then 36 + 32 = 68°F.
    39Make C the subject of F = 1.8C + 32. Use brackets where you need them.(f-32)/1.8 (also accept: f/1.8-32/1.8, (f-32)5/9, 5(f-32)/9)
    Working: F − 32 = 1.8C, so C = (F − 32)/1.8, typed (f-32)/1.8.
    40Use C = (F − 32) ÷ 1.8 to change 86°F to degrees Celsius.30
    Working: 86 − 32 = 54, and 54 ÷ 1.8 = 30°C.
    41A cake recipe uses f = 75s + 50 grams of flour for s servings. You have 500 g of flour. How many servings can you make?6
    Working: (500 − 50) ÷ 75 = 450 ÷ 75 = 6 servings.
    42In a game, you need P = 150L − 50 points to finish level L. Zed has exactly the 700 points needed to finish one level. Which level is it?5
    Working: (700 + 50) ÷ 150 = 750 ÷ 150 = 5. Check: 150 × 5 − 50 = 700.
    43The area of a triangle is A = 1/2bh. Which formula makes h the subject? [Figure: A triangle with base b and height h]h = 2A ÷ b
    Working: Multiply both sides by 2: 2A = bh. Divide both sides by b: h = 2A ÷ b.
    44A runner keeps a steady speed of 4 m/s for a distance of 600 m. Using t = d ÷ s, how many seconds does she run for?150
    Working: t = 600 ÷ 4 = 150 seconds.
    452y + 3x = 12. Make y the subject. Use brackets where you need them.(12-3x)/2 (also accept: 6-3x/2, 6-1.5x)
    Working: 2y = 12 − 3x, so y = (12 − 3x)/2, typed (12-3x)/2. Check: x = 2 gives y = 3, and 6 + 6 = 12.
    46(a) [2 marks] Work out the charge for a job of 3 hours. Answer in pounds.119
    1. M1 for C = 35 + 28 × 3, multiplying first
    2. A1 for 35 + 84 = 119
    Working: C = 35 + 28 × 3 (multiply first). 28 × 3 = 84 and 35 + 84 = £119.
    		2
    47(b) [3 marks] Make h the subject of C = 35 + 28h. Use brackets where you need them.(c-35)/28 (also accept: c/28-35/28, c/28-5/4)
    1. M1 for subtracting 35 from both sides: C − 35 = 28h
    2. M1 for dividing both sides by 28
    3. A1 for h = (C − 35)/28
    Working: Subtract 35 from both sides: C − 35 = 28h. Divide both sides by 28: h = (C − 35)/28, typed (c-35)/28.
    		3
    48(c) [2 marks] A customer pays £175. For how many hours did the plumber work?5
    1. M1 for h = (175 − 35) ÷ 28 = 140 ÷ 28
    2. A1 for 5 hours
    Working: h = (175 − 35) ÷ 28 = 140 ÷ 28 = 5 hours.
    		2
    49Ali says: if y = 4x − 6, then x = (y + 6) ÷ 4. Show that Ali is right when y = 14. Type the value of x you get.5
    1. M1 for x = (14 + 6) ÷ 4 = 20 ÷ 4 = 5
    2. M1 for putting x = 5 in the original formula: 4 × 5 − 6 = 14
    3. A1 for saying it gives y = 14 again, so the formulae agree and x = 5
    Working: x = (14 + 6) ÷ 4 = 20 ÷ 4 = 5. Put x = 5 in the original formula: 4 × 5 − 6 = 14. It gives y = 14 again, so the two formulae agree, and x = 5. One example is a check, not a proof; the proof is to rearrange.
    		3
    50Work out m, the monthly price, in pounds.15
    1. M1 for rearranging, m = (T − J) ÷ n, or writing 210 = 30 + 12m
    2. M1 for substituting: (210 − 30) ÷ 12
    3. M1 for 180 ÷ 12
    4. A1 for m = 15
    Working: Rearrange: m = (T − J) ÷ n, or write 210 = 30 + 12m. Substitute: (210 − 30) ÷ 12 = 180 ÷ 12 = 15, so m = £15 a month.
    		4
    51F = 1.8C + 32 changes Celsius to Fahrenheit. At one temperature the number on both scales is the same. What is that temperature?-40
    Working: t = 1.8t + 32, so t − 1.8t = 32, so −0.8t = 32, so t = 32 ÷ (−0.8) = −40. Check: 1.8 × (−40) + 32 = −72 + 32 = −40.
    52ax + b = cx + d. Make x the subject. Assume a is not equal to c. Use brackets where you need them.(d-b)/(a-c) (also accept: (b-d)/(c-a))
    Working: ax − cx = d − b, so x(a − c) = d − b, so x = (d − b)/(a − c), typed (d-b)/(a-c). This works only when a is not equal to c, because you cannot divide by zero. Check: 3x + 1 = x + 9 gives x = 4, and (9 − 1) ÷ (3 − 1) = 4.
    53y = 2x + 1 and x = (y − 1) ÷ 2 say the same thing. Put x = (y − 1) ÷ 2 into 2x + 1 and simplify. What do you get?y
    Working: 2 × (y − 1)/2 + 1 = (y − 1) + 1 = y. A formula and its rearrangement undo each other, so you get back to y.
    54n is any whole number and R = 2n + 7. Is R always odd? Explain why.Yes: 2n is even, and even + 7 is odd.
    Working: 2n is two lots of n, so it is always even. An even number plus 7 (odd) is always odd. So R is always odd. Rearranged, n = (R − 7) ÷ 2, which is a whole number only when R is odd.
    55Speed, distance and time are linked by s = d ÷ t. Rearrange it to make t the subject, then find how long a fitter takes to drive 78 miles at an average speed of 52 mph. Give your answer in minutes.90
    Working: s = d ÷ t, so t = d ÷ s. t = 78 ÷ 52 = 1.5 hours = 1.5 × 60 = 90 minutes.
    56The area of the circular opening of a round pipe is A = 3.14r^2, where r is the radius in mm. A pipe has A = 314 mm^2. Rearrange the formula and find r, in mm.10
    Working: A = 3.14r^2, so r^2 = A ÷ 3.14 = 314 ÷ 3.14 = 100. Take the square root: r = 10 mm. (Check: 3.14 × 10^2 = 314 ✓.)
    57A quote is worked out with C = a + bn, where a is the call-out fee, b is the price per hour and n is the number of hours. Which formula has n as the subject?n = (C − a) ÷ b
    Working: C = a + bn. Subtract a: C − a = bn. Divide by b: n = (C − a) ÷ b. The bracket matters because all of C − a is divided by b.
    58A quote is C = 65 + 48h, where C is the cost in pounds and h is the hours of work. A customer can spend at most £300. Rearrange the formula to make h the subject, then find the number of hours, to 1 decimal place.4.9
    1. M1 for subtracting 65: C − 65 = 48h
    2. M1 for dividing by 48: h = (C − 65) ÷ 48
    3. A1 for h = 235 ÷ 48 = 4.9 hours (1 d.p.)
    Working: C = 65 + 48h. Subtract 65: C − 65 = 48h. Divide by 48: h = (C − 65) ÷ 48. When C = 300, h = 235 ÷ 48 = 4.896..., so 4.9 hours (1 d.p.).
    		3
    59Using C = a + bn, make b the subject. Then find b when C = 345, a = 75 and n = 6. Give b in pounds per hour.45
    Working: C = a + bn. Subtract a: C − a = bn. Divide by n: b = (C − a) ÷ n. b = (345 − 75) ÷ 6 = 270 ÷ 6 = 45. (Check: 75 + 45 × 6 = 345 ✓.)
    60Ohm’s law is V = IR, where V is the voltage in volts, I is the current in amps and R is the resistance in ohms. Rearrange it to make I the subject, then find I when V = 230 and R = 46. Give your answer in amps.5
    Working: V = IR. Divide both sides by R: I = V ÷ R. I = 230 ÷ 46 = 5 A. (Check: 5 × 46 = 230 ✓.)
    61Power, voltage and current are linked by P = IV, where P is the power in watts, V is the voltage in volts and I is the current in amps. Rearrange it to make I the subject, then find I for a 3 kW heater on a 230 V supply. Give your answer in amps, to 1 decimal place. (3 kW = 3000 W.)13.0
    Working: P = IV. Divide both sides by V: I = P ÷ V. I = 3000 ÷ 230 = 13.04..., so I = 13.0 A (1 d.p.).
