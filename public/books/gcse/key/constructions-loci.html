    Constructions and loci: answers

    82 questions, 9 of them marked (30 marks in all). Question numbers match the page. Use your browser's Print for a copy.

    Open the questions Back to the teacher page
    Answers for Constructions and loci
    No.QuestionAnswerMarks
    1Two angles of a triangle are 50° and 60°. What is the third angle? Answer in degrees.70
    Working: 50 + 60 = 110, and 180 − 110 = 70, so the third angle is 70°.
    2A circle has a radius of 3.5 cm. What is its diameter? Answer in cm.7
    Working: 2 × 3.5 = 7, so the diameter is 7 cm.
    3Two angles on a straight line are 115° and x. Find x. Answer in degrees.65
    Working: 180 − 115 = 65, so x = 65°.
    4What is the area of a quarter of a circle with a radius of 4 cm? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in cm². [Figure: A protractor measuring an angle]12.6
    Working: π × 4² = 50.2655…. A quarter of that is 12.5664… ≈ 12.6 cm².
    5The zero line of this protractor is along the arm on the right. What angle does the slanting arm make with it? Answer in degrees. [Figure: A protractor with a slanting arm]40
    Working: The arm points to 40 on the scale that starts at 0 on the right, so the angle is 40°. It is acute, which fits.
    6This time the zero line is the arm on the LEFT. What angle does the slanting arm make with it? Answer in degrees. [Figure: A protractor measured from the left]50
    Working: Measured from the left, the arm points to 50 on the inner scale (which starts at 0 on the left). 130 + 50 = 180. The angle is 50°, and it is acute.
    7Which tool do you use to draw an arc?A pair of compasses
    Working: Compasses fix one point and swing a pencil round it, which draws an arc or a circle.
    8You measure an angle that looks acute, but your protractor reading says 125. What has probably gone wrong?You read the wrong scale
    Working: 125 + 55 = 180, so the other scale reads 55°. An acute angle fits 55°, so you read the wrong scale.
    9A line is 7.3 cm long. How many millimetres is that?73
    Working: 7.3 × 10 = 73, so the line is 73 mm.
    10You are going to draw a triangle with angles of 50° and 72°. Work out the third angle, so you can check your drawing at the end. Answer in degrees. [Figure: A triangle built from three sides]58
    Working: 50 + 72 = 122, and 180 − 122 = 58, so the third angle is 58°.
    11You know all three sides of a triangle (SSS). Which is the correct order?Draw one side, then draw arcs from its ends, then join the crossing to each end
    Working: Draw one side first. Then arcs from each end, using the other two lengths. Where they cross is the third corner. Join it to both ends.
    12You are drawing a triangle with sides 7 cm, 5 cm and 4 cm. You have drawn the 7 cm side AB. You draw the 4 cm arc from B. What radius do you set on the compasses for the other arc, from A?5
    Working: The arcs use the two sides that are not drawn yet, 5 cm and 4 cm. The arc from B is 4 cm, so the arc from A is 5 cm.
    13Can you construct a triangle with sides of 3 cm, 4 cm and 8 cm?No, the arcs never meet
    Working: 3 + 4 = 7, which is less than 8. The two short sides together cannot reach across the longest side, so the arcs never meet.
    14A triangle has sides of 6 cm and 4 cm with an angle of 50° between them (SAS). Which two tools do you need most?A protractor and a ruler
    Working: You measure the 50° angle with a protractor and the two sides with a ruler.
    15A triangle has a side of 8 cm. The angles at the two ends of that side are 45° and 65°. What is the angle at the third corner? Answer in degrees.70
    Working: 45 + 65 = 110, and 180 − 110 = 70, so the angle at the third corner is 70°.
    16A triangle is drawn with sides of 6 cm and 8 cm and a right angle between them. How long is the third side? Answer in cm. [Figure: A triangle with a right angle]10
    Working: 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100. The square root of 100 is 10, so the third side is 10 cm.
    17Which information gives only one possible triangle (in size and shape)? [Figure: Perpendicular bisector of a line]Three sides
    Working: Three sides fix the triangle completely (SSS). Three angles give the same shape but any size.
    18AB is 9 cm long. Its perpendicular bisector cuts AB at M. How long is AM? Answer in cm.4.5
    Working: 9 ÷ 2 = 4.5, so AM = 4.5 cm.
    19AB is 8 cm. A student sets the compasses to 3 cm and draws arcs from A and from B. What happens?They do not cross
    Working: Half of 8 cm is 4 cm. The radius must be more than 4 cm. With 3 cm each arc is too short and they do not meet.
    20Arcs of radius 6 cm are drawn from A and from B. They cross at X. How far is X from B? Answer in cm. [Figure: Arcs from both ends of a line]6
    Working: X lies on the arc drawn from B with radius 6 cm, so X is 6 cm from B. It is also 6 cm from A.
    21X is on the perpendicular bisector of AB. X is 6.5 cm from A. How far is X from B? Answer in cm.6.5
    Working: A point on the perpendicular bisector is equidistant from A and B. So X is 6.5 cm from B too.
    22A plumber marks the point on a pipe run that is the same distance from two connectors A and B. A and B are 3.6 m apart on a straight line. How far from A is the point? Answer in m.1.8
    Working: 3.6 ÷ 2 = 1.8, so the point is 1.8 m from A.
    23An angle of 84° is bisected. How big is each of the two new angles? Answer in degrees.42
    Working: 84 ÷ 2 = 42, so each angle is 42°.
    24An angle has been bisected and each half is 27°. How big was the whole angle? Answer in degrees.54
    Working: 2 × 27 = 54, so the whole angle was 54°.
    25In the angle bisector construction, you first draw an arc with the compass point on the corner O that cuts both arms. Why?It marks equal distances from O on the arms
    Working: The first arc marks one point on each arm, both the same distance from O. Equal distances are what make the later crossing sit exactly between the arms.
    26Which is the correct order to bisect an angle? Steps: (P) arcs of the same radius from the two points where the first arc meets the arms; (Q) line from the corner through where the new arcs cross; (R) an arc across both arms with the point on the corner.R, then P, then Q
    Working: First the arc across both arms (R). Then equal arcs from the two marks (P). Last, the line through the crossing (Q).
    27P is 4.5 cm from the line AB. The perpendicular from P meets the line at F. G is another point on the line, 6 cm from F. How far is P from G? Answer in cm. [Figure: A right-angled triangle on a line]7.5
    Working: 4.5² + 6² = 20.25 + 36 = 56.25. The square root of 56.25 is 7.5, so PG = 7.5 cm. (PG is longer than PF, as the perpendicular is the shortest way.)
    28To drop a perpendicular from a point P to a line, what do you do first? [Figure: The four standard loci]With the point on P, draw an arc that cuts the line twice
    Working: First, an arc centred on P cuts the line at two points. Then arcs of equal radius from those two points cross on the other side of the line. The line through P and the crossing is perpendicular.
    29What is the locus of points exactly 3 cm from a point P?A circle with radius 3 cm and centre P
    Working: All the points exactly 3 cm from P make a circle with centre P and radius 3 cm.
    30All the points exactly 5 m from a post make a circle. What is the diameter of that circle? Answer in m.10
    Working: 2 × 5 = 10, so the diameter is 10 m.
    31What is the locus of points exactly 2 cm from a line segment AB (not beyond its ends)?A track shape with curved ends
    Working: Beside AB the points make two parallel lines, 2 cm away. Round each end they make half circles of radius 2 cm.
    32A point is the same distance from A and from B. Where can it be?On the perpendicular bisector of AB
    Working: Points the same distance from A and B lie on the perpendicular bisector of AB.
    33A point is the same distance from two lines that meet at a corner. Where can it be?On the angle bisector
    Working: Points the same distance from both lines lie on the bisector of the angle between them.
    34A sprinkler waters every point within 4 m of it. What is the area watered? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m².50.3
    Working: π × 4² = π × 16 = 50.2655… ≈ 50.3 m².
    35A guard dog is on a 6 m lead tied to a post in the middle of an open field. What is the length of the curved edge of the area the dog can reach? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m. [Figure: A garden with two posts]37.7
    Working: 2 × π × 6 = 37.6991… ≈ 37.7 m.
    36Look at the garden picture. Which region is closer to A than to B, and further than 4 m from A? [Figure: A garden with two posts]Region R
    Working: Closer to A: P or R. Further than 4 m from A: outside the curve, which is R. Region R.
    37In the same garden, which region is closer to B than to A, and within 4 m of A?Region Q
    Working: B's side: Q or S. Inside the curve: P or Q. Both: Q.
    38In the garden picture each square is 1 m. How many metres is it from post A to post B?5
    Working: A is 4 squares from the left and B is 9 squares from the left. 9 − 4 = 5 squares, so A to B is 5 m.
    39A fence is 12 m long. It is drawn on a plan with a scale of 1 cm to 2 m. How long is the fence on the plan? Answer in cm.6
    Working: 12 ÷ 2 = 6, so the fence is 6 cm on the plan.
    40On a plan, 1 cm stands for 3 m. A tree is 2.4 cm from the fence on the plan. How far is it from the fence in real life? Answer in m.7.2
    Working: 2.4 × 3 = 7.2, so the tree is 7.2 m from the fence.
    41A sprinkler stands at the corner S of a rectangular lawn. It waters every point within 4 m of S, and 4 m is less than the sides of the lawn. How much of the lawn is watered? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m². [Figure: A lawn with a sprinkler in one corner]12.6
    Working: Whole circle = π × 4² = 50.2655…. A quarter = 12.5664… ≈ 12.6 m².
    42A cylinder stands on its circular base. What is its plan (the view from above)? [Figure: A cylinder]A circle
    Working: From above you see the circular top, so the plan is a circle.
    43What is the front elevation of the same cylinder?A rectangle
    Working: From the front you see a rectangle as wide as the circle (the diameter) and as tall as the cylinder.
    44What is the front elevation of a square-based pyramid? [Figure: A square-based pyramid]A triangle
    Working: From the front the two sloping edges meet at the top, so you see a triangle.
    45What is the plan of the same square-based pyramid?A square crossed by its diagonals
    Working: From above the base is a square. The edges from the top point to the four corners are seen as lines joining each corner to the middle, which are the diagonals.
    46A cuboid is 5 cm long, 3 cm wide and 2 cm high. It lies flat on a table. What is the area of its plan? Answer in cm². [Figure: A cuboid with its sizes]15
    Working: The plan shows length and width: 5 × 3 = 15 cm².
    47For the same cuboid, with its length across the front, what is the area of the front elevation? Answer in cm².10
    Working: The front elevation shows length and height: 5 × 2 = 10 cm².
    48A cylinder has a radius of 3 cm and a height of 8 cm. What is the area of its front elevation? Answer in cm².48
    Working: The diameter is 2 × 3 = 6 cm. The rectangle is 6 cm by 8 cm, so its area is 48 cm².
    49A triangular prism has a triangular end with a base of 6 cm and a height of 4 cm. It is 10 cm long. The triangular end faces the front. What is the area of the front elevation? Answer in cm². [Figure: Forwards: a rule, 3 cm from P, to a drawing]12
    Working: 1/2 × 6 × 4 = 12, so the area is 12 cm².
    50What construction has been drawn here? [Figure: Arcs and a line]The perpendicular bisector of a line
    Working: The arcs are centred on the two ends of a line and the new line cuts it in half at right angles. It is the perpendicular bisector.
    51What construction has been drawn here? [Figure: Arcs between two arms]The bisector of an angle
    Working: Two lines meet at a corner and a line from the corner splits the angle into equal parts. It is the angle bisector.
    52What construction has been drawn here? [Figure: A line and a point]A perpendicular from a point
    Working: The first arc is centred on the point P and the final line goes from P to the line at a right angle. It is a perpendicular from a point to a line.
    53A locus is a circle with radius 6.5 m centred on a tree T. Which rule makes this locus?Exactly 6.5 m from T
    Working: The circle is the set of points exactly 6.5 m from T.
    54A circular lawn watered by a sprinkler has an area of 78.5 m². The sprinkler is in the middle. How far does the water reach? Give your answer in m, to 1 decimal place.5.0 (also accept: 5)
    Working: r² = 78.5 ÷ π = 24.9873…. r = 4.9987… ≈ 5.0 m.
    55The plan of a solid is a circle and its front elevation is a triangle. Which solid is it?A cone
    Working: A cone has a circular plan and a triangular front elevation.
    56Arcs of the same radius are drawn from A and B and cross at X and Y. Which fact is NOT used to show triangles AXY and BXY are congruent?The radius is a whole number of cm
    Working: The sides AX, BX, AY, BY are all radii of the same size and XY is shared, so the triangles are congruent (SSS). The size of the radius is not needed: any equal radii work.
    57Triangles AXY and BXY are congruent. What reason do you give?SSS: three pairs of equal sides
    Working: All three pairs of sides are equal, so the reason is SSS (side, side, side).
    58Higher X is on the perpendicular bisector of AB, which is 10 cm long. X is 13 cm from A. How far is X from the line AB? Answer in cm.12
    Working: AM = 5 cm. In the right-angled triangle AMX, 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144. The square root is 12, so X is 12 cm from AB.
    59Higher [4 marks] Show that triangles AXY and BXY are congruent. Arcs with the same radius are drawn from A and B and cross at X and Y.Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. B1 AX = BX (equal radii)
    2. B1 AY = BY (equal radii)
    3. B1 XY is a common side
    4. C1 SSS, so the triangles are congruent
    Working: Triangles AXY and BXY: AX = BX, AY = BY, and XY is common. So they are congruent (SSS).
    		4
    60Dan says: to bisect the line AB, which is 8 cm, I set the compasses to 3 cm and drew arcs from A and from B. Why is that wrong?3 cm is too small, so the arcs do not cross
    Working: Half of AB is 4 cm. A radius of 3 cm is too small, so the arcs from A and B never meet. Use a radius bigger than 4 cm.
    61Eve says: to build a triangle from three sides, I drew both arcs from the same end of the base. What went wrong?The arcs need different centres, one at each end
    Working: Arcs from the same centre never meet. The second arc has to be centred on the other end of the base, so the crossing is the third corner.
    62Ida says: the points exactly 2 cm from a line segment AB are two straight lines, one each side. What went wrong?She left out the half circles at the two ends
    Working: Round each end of AB the points 2 cm away make a half circle. The full locus is a track shape: two straight lines and two half circles.
    63Which of these is drawn with compasses?An arc 4 cm from a point
    Working: An arc a fixed distance from a point is drawn with compasses.
    64An angle of 76° is bisected. How big is each part? Answer in degrees.38
    Working: 76 ÷ 2 = 38, so each part is 38°.
    65On a plan, 1 cm stands for 2 m. A wall is 9 m long. How long is the wall on the plan? Answer in cm.4.5
    Working: 9 ÷ 2 = 4.5, so the wall is 4.5 cm on the plan.
    66Which standard locus describes all the points the same distance from two fixed points?A perpendicular bisector
    Working: Points equidistant from two fixed points lie on the perpendicular bisector of the line joining them.
    67Point X is on the perpendicular bisector of a line AB, and AB is 14 cm. How far along AB from A is the foot of the bisector? Answer in cm.7
    Working: 14 ÷ 2 = 7, so the foot of the bisector is 7 cm from A.
    68An electrician fits a socket on a wall. A lamp has a 2.4 m lead, so the lamp can stand anywhere on the floor within 2.4 m of the socket. The wall is straight, so that region is half a circle. What is its area? Give your answer to 2 decimal places, in m².9.05
    Working: Whole circle = π × 2.4² = 18.0956… m². Half of that is 9.0478… ≈ 9.05 m².
    69A CCTV camera is fixed in the corner of a car park, so it covers a quarter of a circle of radius 12 m. What area does it cover? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m².113.1
    Working: Whole circle = π × 12² = 452.3893…. A quarter = 113.0973… ≈ 113.1 m².
    70A plumber runs a pipe along the line that is the same distance from two walls that meet at a right angle. At one point the pipe is 40 cm from each wall. How far is that point from the corner where the walls meet? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in cm.56.6
    Working: 40² + 40² = 1600 + 1600 = 3200. The square root of 3200 is 56.5685… ≈ 56.6 cm.
    71A carpenter cuts a mitre joint where two boards meet at a corner of 120°. Each board is cut at half the corner angle. At what angle is each board cut? Answer in degrees.60
    Working: 120 ÷ 2 = 60, so each board is cut at 60°.
    72A garden fence goes in a circle round a tree, exactly 2 m from the trunk all the way round. Fencing costs £14.50 per metre. How much does the fencing cost? Give your answer to 2 decimal places, like 12.50.182.21
    1. M1 for 2 × π × 2 (the circumference)
    2. M1 for × 14.50
    3. A1 for £182.21
    Working: Length = 2 × π × 2 = 12.5663… m. Cost = 12.5663… × 14.50 = 182.2124…, so £182.21.
    		3
    73A gardener waters a circular patch with a sprinkler that reaches 4 m. Turf for the patch costs £5.20 per m². How much is the turf? Use the full calculator value until the end, then give your answer to 2 decimal places, like 12.50.261.38
    1. M1 for π × 4²
    2. M1 for × 5.20
    3. A1 for £261.38
    Working: Area = π × 4² = 50.2655… m². Cost = 50.2655… × 5.20 = 261.3805…, so £261.38.
    		3
    74A rectangular lawn is 10 m by 8 m. A sprinkler is fixed in one corner and waters every point within 6 m of it. Work out the area of lawn that is watered. Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m².28.3
    1. M1 for π × 6² (= 113.09…)
    2. M1 for dividing by 4 (or × 1/4)
    3. A1 for 28.3
    Working: Whole circle = π × 6² = 113.0973… m². A quarter = 28.2743… ≈ 28.3 m².
    		3
    75A tree T has a security light. The light shines on all points more than 3 m from T but not more than 5 m from T. Work out the area lit. Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m².50.3
    1. M1 for π × 5²
    2. M1 for π × 3²
    3. A1 for 50.3 (the subtraction)
    Working: Big circle = π × 5² = 78.5398…. Small circle = π × 3² = 28.2743…. Lit area = 50.2655… ≈ 50.3 m².
    		3
    76A triangle is drawn accurately. Two of its sides are 5 cm and 12 cm, with an angle of 90° between them. Work out the perimeter of the triangle. Give your answer in cm.30
    1. M1 for 5² + 12² = 169
    2. M1 for the square root, 13
    3. A1 for 30
    Working: 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169. The third side is the square root of 169, which is 13 cm. Perimeter = 5 + 12 + 13 = 30 cm.
    		3
    77A security light is at a point P. It lights every point within 8 m of P. A straight path passes 5 m from P at its closest point. Show that the length of the path that is lit is 12.5 m, correct to 1 decimal place.Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 8 and a short side 5
    2. M1 for 8² − 5² = 39 and √39 (= 6.24…)
    3. M1 for doubling (2 × 6.24… = 12.49…)
    4. C1 for stating 12.49… ≈ 12.5 as required
    Working: Let M be the closest point on the path. PM = 5 m and the light reaches 8 m, so half the lit length is √(8² − 5²) = √39 = 6.2449… m. Both sides: 2 × 6.2449… = 12.4899… ≈ 12.5 m, as required.
    		4
    78A tent is a triangular prism lying on one of its long rectangular faces. Its front end is an isosceles triangle with a base of 8 cm and a height of 3 cm. The tent is 12 cm long. The triangle is at the front. Work out the total of the area of the plan and the area of the side elevation. Give your answer in cm².132
    1. M1 for plan 8 by 12 (= 96)
    2. M1 for side elevation 12 by 3 (= 36)
    3. M1 for adding the two areas
    4. A1 for 132
    Working: Plan: 8 × 12 = 96 cm². Side elevation: 12 × 3 = 36 cm². Total = 96 + 36 = 132 cm².
    		4
    79Higher [3 marks] A path is a straight line AB, 6 m long. A fence runs exactly 1.5 m from the path all the way round it (a track shape with straight sides and half circles at the ends). Work out the area inside the fence. Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m².25.1
    1. M1 for the rectangle 6 × 3 = 18
    2. M1 for π × 1.5² (= 7.06…)
    3. A1 for 25.1
    Working: Rectangle = 6 × 3 = 18 m². Two half circles make a circle of radius 1.5: π × 1.5² = 7.0686… m². Total = 25.0686… ≈ 25.1 m².
    		3
    80Two trees, A and B, are 12 m apart. A treasure chest is buried the same distance from both trees, and exactly 10 m from A. How far is the chest from the straight line AB? Answer in m.8
    Working: The chest is on the perpendicular bisector of AB, so it is above the middle M. AM = 6 m. In the right-angled triangle, 10² − 6² = 100 − 36 = 64, and the square root is 8. The chest is 8 m from the line.
    81A goat is tied by a 6 m rope to the outside corner of a square shed with sides of 4 m. Find the total area of grass the goat can reach. Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m².91.1
    Working: Three-quarter circle: 3/4 × π × 6² = 84.8230…. Two quarter circles of radius 2: 2 × 1/4 × π × 2² = 6.2832…. Total = 91.1062… ≈ 91.1 m².
    82A radio mast must be the same distance from three villages A, B and C. The villages form a right-angled triangle with the right angle at B, AB = 6 km and BC = 8 km. How far must the mast be from each village? Answer in km.5
    Working: AC = √(6² + 8²) = √100 = 10 km. The mast is at the middle of AC, which is the centre of the circle through A, B and C. Its distance from each village is the radius, 10 ÷ 2 = 5 km.
