    Answers for Bounds and error intervals
    No.QuestionAnswerMarks
    1Round 3.46 to 1 decimal place.3.5
    Working: The digit after the 4 is 6, which is 5 or more, so the 4 rounds up: 3.46 ≈ 3.5.
    2Round 3862 to the nearest 100.3900
    Working: The tens digit is 6, so round the 8 up to 9: 3862 ≈ 3900.
    3Round 0.0367 to 2 significant figures.0.037
    Working: The significant figures start at 3. Keep 3 and 6: 0.036. The next digit is 7, so round up to 0.037.
    4x is an integer and 2 ≤ x < 6. List the values of x. Put commas between them, like 1,2,3. [Figure: All the values that round to 7]2,3,4,5 (also accept: 2, 3, 4, 5)
    Working: 2 is allowed and 6 is not: 2, 3, 4, 5.
    5A mass is 62 kg, to the nearest kg. What is its lower bound, in kg?61.5
    Working: Half of 1 kg is 0.5 kg. The lower bound is 62 − 0.5 = 61.5 kg.
    6A crowd is 3400 to the nearest 100. What is its upper bound?3450
    Working: Half of 100 is 50. The upper bound is 3400 + 50 = 3450.
    7A pipe is 1.8 m long, to 1 decimal place. What is its lower bound, in m?1.75
    Working: Half of 0.1 is 0.05. The lower bound is 1.8 − 0.05 = 1.75 m.
    8A reading is 12.45 volts, to 2 decimal places. What is its upper bound, in volts?12.455
    Working: Half of 0.01 is 0.005. The upper bound is 12.45 + 0.005 = 12.455 volts.
    9A distance is 4300 m, to 2 significant figures. What is its lower bound, in m?4250
    Working: The last significant figure is the 3, in the hundreds place. The unit is 100, so half the unit is 50. The lower bound is 4300 − 50 = 4250 m.
    10A length is 9.0 cm to 1 decimal place. What is its upper bound? [Figure: The error interval 2.35 up to 2.45]9.05
    Working: The unit is 0.1, so half the unit is 0.05. The upper bound is 9.0 + 0.05 = 9.05 cm.
    11x = 7, to the nearest whole number. Write the error interval for x. Type it like 2.5<=x<3.5. [Figure: The number 7 on a number line]6.5<=x<7.5 (also accept: 6.5≤x<7.5)
    Working: Half the unit is 0.5. Lower bound 6.5, upper bound 7.5. The error interval is 6.5 ≤ x < 7.5.
    12A length is l = 2.4 m, to 1 decimal place. Write the error interval for l.2.35<=l<2.45 (also accept: 2.35≤l<2.45)
    Working: Half the unit is 0.05. Lower bound 2.35, upper bound 2.45. The error interval is 2.35 ≤ l < 2.45.
    13n = 560, to 2 significant figures. Write the error interval for n.555<=n<565 (also accept: 555≤n<565)
    Working: The last significant figure is in the tens place, so the unit is 10 and half of it is 5. 555 ≤ n < 565.
    14t = 0.085, to 2 significant figures. Write the error interval for t.0.0845<=t<0.0855 (also accept: 0.0845≤t<0.0855)
    Working: The 5 is in the thousandths place, so the unit is 0.001 and half of it is 0.0005. Lower bound 0.0845, upper bound 0.0855. 0.0845 ≤ t < 0.0855.
    15A mass is m = 15 kg, to the nearest 5 kg. Write the error interval for m.12.5<=m<17.5 (also accept: 12.5≤m<17.5)
    Working: The unit is 5, so half the unit is 2.5. Lower bound 12.5, upper bound 17.5. 12.5 ≤ m < 17.5.
    16A cable is cut to l = 3.60 m, correct to the nearest centimetre. Write the error interval for l, in metres. [Figure: Numbers that truncate to 4.6]3.595<=l<3.605 (also accept: 3.595≤l<3.605)
    Working: 1 cm = 0.01 m, so half the unit is 0.005 m. Lower bound 3.595, upper bound 3.605. 3.595 ≤ l < 3.605.
    17x = 5.2, truncated to 1 decimal place. Write the error interval for x.5.2<=x<5.3 (also accept: 5.2≤x<5.3)
    Working: A truncated number is at least 5.2 and below 5.2 + 0.1. So 5.2 ≤ x < 5.3.
    18A number is 12 after it has been truncated to a whole number. What is the smallest it could be?12
    Working: Cutting the decimals off 12.0 gives 12, and any smaller number gives 11 or less. So the smallest is 12.
    19A number is 12 after it has been truncated to a whole number. What is its upper bound?13
    Working: 12.9, 12.99 and 12.999 all truncate to 12. The first number that does not is 13, so the upper bound is 13.
    20A number is 3.4 when truncated to 1 decimal place. Which error interval is correct?3.4 ≤ x < 3.5
    Working: Truncated to 1 decimal place, the number is at least 3.4 and below 3.5.
    21A stopwatch shows 14.8 seconds, truncated to 1 decimal place. Write the error interval for the real time t.14.8<=t<14.9 (also accept: 14.8≤t<14.9)
    Working: The real time is at least 14.8 and below 14.8 + 0.1. So 14.8 ≤ t < 14.9.
    22a = 8 and b = 5, both to the nearest whole number. What is the greatest possible value of a + b?14
    Working: Upper bounds: a = 8.5 and b = 5.5. Greatest a + b = 8.5 + 5.5 = 14.
    23a = 8 and b = 5, both to the nearest whole number. What is the smallest possible value of a + b?12
    Working: Lower bounds: a = 7.5 and b = 4.5. Smallest a + b = 7.5 + 4.5 = 12.
    24p = 20.4 and q = 7.9, both to 1 decimal place. What is the greatest possible value of p − q?12.6
    Working: Upper bound of p = 20.45. Lower bound of q = 7.85. Greatest p − q = 20.45 − 7.85 = 12.6.
    25p = 20.4 and q = 7.9, both to 1 decimal place. What is the smallest possible value of p − q?12.4
    Working: Lower bound of p = 20.35. Upper bound of q = 7.95. Smallest p − q = 20.35 − 7.95 = 12.4.
    26Two lengths of conduit are 1.2 m, to 1 decimal place, and 0.85 m, to 2 decimal places. What is the greatest possible total length, in m?2.105
    Working: Upper bounds: 1.2 → 1.25 and 0.85 → 0.855. Greatest total = 1.25 + 0.855 = 2.105 m.
    27A pipe is 2.5 m long, to 1 decimal place. A piece 0.8 m long, to 1 decimal place, is cut off. What is the smallest possible length left, in m?1.6
    Working: Lower bound of the pipe = 2.45 m. Upper bound of the piece = 0.85 m. Smallest left = 2.45 − 0.85 = 1.6 m.
    28A tile is 20 cm by 10 cm, both to the nearest cm. What is the greatest possible area, in cm²? [Figure: A rectangular tile]215.25
    Working: Upper bounds: 20.5 and 10.5. Greatest area = 20.5 × 10.5 = 215.25 cm².
    29A tile is 20 cm by 10 cm, both to the nearest cm. What is the smallest possible area, in cm²?185.25
    Working: Lower bounds: 19.5 and 9.5. Smallest area = 19.5 × 9.5 = 185.25 cm².
    30A rectangle is 6.4 cm by 3.2 cm, both to 1 decimal place. What is the smallest possible perimeter, in cm? [Figure: A rectangle]19
    Working: Lower bounds: 6.35 and 3.15. Smallest perimeter = 2 × (6.35 + 3.15) = 2 × 9.5 = 19 cm.
    31x = 15 and y = 4, both to the nearest whole number. What is the greatest possible value of x ÷ y? Give your answer to 2 decimal places.4.43
    Working: Upper bound of x = 15.5. Lower bound of y = 3.5. Greatest x ÷ y = 15.5 ÷ 3.5 = 4.4285… ≈ 4.43.
    32x = 15 and y = 4, both to the nearest whole number. What is the smallest possible value of x ÷ y? Give your answer to 2 decimal places.3.22
    Working: Lower bound of x = 14.5. Upper bound of y = 4.5. Smallest x ÷ y = 14.5 ÷ 4.5 = 3.2222… ≈ 3.22.
    33A 24.5 m cable, to 1 decimal place, is cut into pieces of 3.5 m, to 1 decimal place. What is the greatest possible value of 24.5 ÷ 3.5? Give your answer to 2 decimal places.7.12
    Working: Upper bound of the cable = 24.55 m. Lower bound of a piece = 3.45 m. Greatest value = 24.55 ÷ 3.45 = 7.1159… ≈ 7.12.
    34A runner covers 100 m, to the nearest metre, in 12.5 s, to 1 decimal place. What is the greatest possible speed? Give your answer to 2 decimal places, in m/s.8.07
    Working: Upper bound of distance = 100.5 m. Lower bound of time = 12.45 s. Greatest speed = 100.5 ÷ 12.45 = 8.0722… ≈ 8.07 m/s.
    35A runner covers 100 m, to the nearest metre, in 12.5 s, to 1 decimal place. What is the smallest possible speed? Give your answer to 2 decimal places, in m/s.7.93
    Working: Lower bound of distance = 99.5 m. Upper bound of time = 12.55 s. Smallest speed = 99.5 ÷ 12.55 = 7.9282… ≈ 7.93 m/s.
    36The density of a metal is mass ÷ volume. A block has mass 480 g, to the nearest 10 g, and volume 60 cm³, to the nearest cm³. What is the greatest possible density? Give your answer to 2 decimal places, in g/cm³.8.15
    Working: Upper bound of mass = 485 g. Lower bound of volume = 59.5 cm³. Greatest density = 485 ÷ 59.5 = 8.1512… ≈ 8.15 g/cm³.
    37A square has sides of 4.5 cm, to 1 decimal place. What is the upper bound of its area, in cm²?20.7025
    Working: Upper bound of the side = 4.55 cm. Upper bound of the area = 4.55 × 4.55 = 20.7025 cm².
    38A journey is 150 km, to the nearest 10 km, at 60 km/h, to the nearest 10 km/h. What is the greatest possible time? Give your answer to 2 decimal places, in hours.2.82
    Working: Upper bound of distance = 155 km. Lower bound of speed = 55 km/h. Greatest time = 155 ÷ 55 = 2.8181… ≈ 2.82 hours.
    39A bike travels 320 m, to the nearest metre, in 16.0 s, to 1 decimal place. The bounds of its speed are 19.906… and 20.094… m/s. What is the speed to a degree of accuracy you can justify, in m/s?20
    Working: To 2 s.f. both bounds round to 20 (19.9… ≈ 20 and 20.09… ≈ 20). To 3 s.f. they are 19.9 and 20.1, which differ. So the speed is 20 m/s to 2 s.f.
    40A drone flies 300 m, to the nearest metre, in 12.5 s, to 1 decimal place. Work out the upper and lower bounds of its speed, then give the speed to a degree of accuracy you can justify, in m/s.24
    Working: Upper bound = 300.5 ÷ 12.45 = 24.1365…. Lower bound = 299.5 ÷ 12.55 = 23.8645…. To 3 s.f. they are 24.1 and 23.9, which differ. To 2 s.f. both are 24. So the speed is 24 m/s to 2 s.f.
    41A delivery robot moves 24 m, to the nearest metre, in 4.8 minutes, to 1 decimal place. Its speed is 24 ÷ 4.8 = 5 m/min. The upper and lower bounds are 5.158… and 4.845… m/min. What speed can you justify? Answer to the number of significant figures the bounds allow.5
    Working: To 2 s.f. the bounds are 5.2 and 4.8, which differ. To 1 s.f. both are 5. So the speed is 5 m/min to 1 s.f.
    42A metal block has mass 480 g, to the nearest 10 g, and volume 60 cm³, to the nearest cm³. The bounds of its density are 7.85… and 8.15… g/cm³. What density can you justify?8
    Working: To 2 s.f. the bounds are 7.9 and 8.2, which differ. To 1 s.f. both are 8. So the density is 8 g/cm³ to 1 s.f.
    43The bounds of a speed are 19.9… and 20.09… m/s. Why is giving the speed as “20.0 m/s” not justified? [Figure: Forwards: 7 to the nearest whole number to its limits]The bounds differ at 3 s.f. (19.9 and 20.1)
    Working: To 3 s.f. the bounds are 19.9 and 20.1. They are different, so the true speed could round to either, and 3 s.f. is not justified. At 2 s.f., both give 20.
    44A number has the error interval 3.45 ≤ x < 3.55. It was rounded to 1 decimal place. What is the number?3.5
    Working: (3.45 + 3.55) ÷ 2 = 3.5. Check: the gap is 0.1, so it is 1 decimal place.
    45A number has a lower bound of 2450 and an upper bound of 2550. It was rounded to the nearest 100. What is the number?2500
    Working: (2450 + 2550) ÷ 2 = 2500. The gap is 100, so it is the nearest 100.
    46A number has the error interval 0.175 ≤ x < 0.185. What is the number, rounded to 2 decimal places?0.18
    Working: (0.175 + 0.185) ÷ 2 = 0.18. The gap is 0.01, so it is 2 decimal places.
    47The upper bound of a number is 8.35. It was rounded to 1 decimal place. What is the number?8.3
    Working: Half of 0.1 is 0.05. The number is 8.35 − 0.05 = 8.3.
    48x is a number rounded to 1 decimal place. The upper bound of 2x is 9.1. What is x?4.5
    Working: Upper bound of x = 9.1 ÷ 2 = 4.55. Half a unit (0.05) below that is 4.55 − 0.05 = 4.5. So x = 4.5. Check: the upper bound of 2x is 2 × 4.55 = 9.1.
    49A length has the error interval 14.5 ≤ l < 15.5, in metres. How was it rounded?To the nearest metre
    Working: 15.5 − 14.5 = 1, so the rounding unit is 1 metre. The length was rounded to the nearest metre (to 15 m).
    50Tom says: a length of 5.4 cm, to 1 decimal place, has an upper bound of 5.5 cm. What went wrong?He added the whole unit (0.1), not half of it
    Working: The unit is 0.1, so half the unit is 0.05. The upper bound is 5.4 + 0.05 = 5.45 cm.
    51Ava says: the greatest possible value of a − b is the upper bound of a minus the upper bound of b. What went wrong?She should take away the lower bound of b
    Working: Greatest a − b = upper bound of a − lower bound of b. You take away the smallest b possible.
    52Ben says: the error interval for 12, to the nearest whole number, is 11.5 < x ≤ 12.5. What went wrong?The signs are the wrong way round: it should be 11.5 ≤ x < 12.5
    Working: 11.5 rounds up to 12, so it is included. 12.5 rounds up to 13, so it is not. The error interval is 11.5 ≤ x < 12.5.
    53x = 0.6, to 1 decimal place. Write the error interval for x.0.55<=x<0.65 (also accept: 0.55≤x<0.65)
    Working: Half the unit is 0.05. Lower bound 0.55, upper bound 0.65. 0.55 ≤ x < 0.65.
    54a = 3.5 and b = 2.1, both to 1 decimal place. What is the greatest possible value of a + b?5.7
    Working: Upper bounds: 3.55 and 2.15. Greatest a + b = 3.55 + 2.15 = 5.7.
    55a = 3.5 and b = 2.1, both to 1 decimal place. What is the smallest possible value of a × b?7.0725
    Working: Lower bounds: 3.45 and 2.05. Smallest a × b = 3.45 × 2.05 = 7.0725.
    56A number is 9.4 after being truncated to 1 decimal place. What is its upper bound?9.5
    Working: The upper bound is one whole unit above 9.4: 9.4 + 0.1 = 9.5.
    57a = 3.5 and b = 2.1, both to 1 decimal place. What is the greatest possible value of a ÷ b? Give your answer to 2 decimal places.1.73
    Working: Upper bound of a = 3.55. Lower bound of b = 2.05. Greatest a ÷ b = 3.55 ÷ 2.05 = 1.7317… ≈ 1.73.
    58a = 3.5 and b = 2.1, both to 1 decimal place. What is the smallest possible value of a − b?1.3
    Working: Lower bound of a = 3.45. Upper bound of b = 2.15. Smallest a − b = 3.45 − 2.15 = 1.3.
    59A cable is 12.5 m long, correct to 1 decimal place. Three of these cables are laid end to end. Work out the upper bound of the total length, in m.37.65
    1. M1 for the upper bound 12.55
    2. M1 for 3 × 12.55
    3. A1 for 37.65
    Working: Upper bound of one cable = 12.5 + 0.05 = 12.55 m. Three cables: 3 × 12.55 = 37.65 m.

    60A rectangle is 6.4 cm long and 3.2 cm wide, both to 1 decimal place. Work out the lower bound of its area. Give your answer to 2 decimal places, in cm².20.00
    1. M1 for the lower bound 6.35
    2. M1 for the lower bound 3.15 and multiplying
    3. A1 for 20.00 (20.0025 before rounding)
    Working: Lower bounds: 6.35 and 3.15. Lower bound of the area = 6.35 × 3.15 = 20.0025 ≈ 20.00 cm².

    61A runner covers 400 m, to the nearest 10 m, in 52 s, to the nearest second. Work out the speed to a degree of accuracy that is justified. Give your answer in m/s.8
    1. M1 for 405 ÷ 51.5 (the upper bound of the speed)
    2. M1 for 395 ÷ 52.5 (the lower bound of the speed)
    3. A1 for 7.86… and 7.52…
    4. C1 for both bounds rounding to 8 at 1 s.f. (they differ at 2 s.f.), so 8 m/s
    Working: Upper bound = 405 ÷ 51.5 = 7.8640…. Lower bound = 395 ÷ 52.5 = 7.5238…. To 2 s.f. these are 7.9 and 7.5, which differ. To 1 s.f. both are 8. So the speed is 8 m/s to 1 s.f.
    		4
    62A rod is 2.4 m long, to 1 decimal place. Pieces of 0.35 m, to 2 decimal places, are cut from it. Show that it might be possible to cut 7 pieces from the rod. [Figure: A rod and one cut piece]Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for the upper bound of the rod, 2.45
    2. M1 for the lower bound of a piece, 0.345, and 2.45 ÷ 0.345
    3. C1 for 7.10… is more than 7, so 7 pieces might fit
    Working: Longest rod = 2.45 m. Shortest piece = 0.345 m. 2.45 ÷ 0.345 = 7.10…, which is more than 7. So it might be possible to cut 7 pieces, as required.
    		3
    63Higher p = 4.8, to 1 decimal place, and q = 0.25, to 2 decimal places. Work out the upper bound of p ÷ q. Give your answer to 3 significant figures.19.8
    1. M1 for the upper bound of p, 4.85
    2. M1 for the lower bound of q, 0.245
    3. M1 for 4.85 ÷ 0.245
    4. A1 for 19.8 (19.7959… before rounding)
    Working: Upper bound of p = 4.85. Lower bound of q = 0.245. Upper bound of p ÷ q = 4.85 ÷ 0.245 = 19.7959… ≈ 19.8.
    		4
    64A square tile has sides of 15 cm, to the nearest cm. Show that the area of the tile cannot be less than 210 cm². [Figure: A square tile]Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for the lower bound of the side, 14.5
    2. M1 for 14.5² = 210.25
    3. C1 for 210.25 is more than 210, as required
    Working: Lower bound of the side = 14.5 cm. Smallest area = 14.5 × 14.5 = 210.25 cm². This is more than 210, so the area cannot be less than 210 cm², as required.
    		3
    65A length is 5 m, to the nearest metre. Maya says: “Twice the length must be 10 m, to the nearest metre.” Show that Maya might be wrong.Written answer: see the mark scheme
    Working: The length is between 4.5 and 5.5 m. Double the length is between 9 and 11 m. A length of 4.5 m doubles to 9 m, which is 9 to the nearest metre, not 10. So Maya might be wrong. (Doubling an uncertain number doubles the uncertainty.)
    66A number x is 4.5 when rounded to 1 decimal place, and 5 when rounded to 1 significant figure. Write the error interval for all the values of x that fit both facts. Type it like 1<=x<2.4.5<=x<4.55 (also accept: 4.5≤x<4.55)
    Working: 1 d.p.: 4.45 ≤ x < 4.55. 1 s.f.: 4.5 ≤ x < 5.5. The numbers in both are 4.5 ≤ x < 4.55.
    67Higher a = 7.2 and b = 3.1, both to 1 decimal place. Work out the upper bound of a ÷ (a − b). Give your answer to 3 significant figures. (Take care: a appears twice, so the usual rule does not work.)1.79
    Working: Write it as 1 ÷ (1 − b ÷ a). It gets bigger when b ÷ a gets bigger, so use the biggest b and smallest a. Upper bound of b = 3.15. Lower bound of a = 7.15. a ÷ (a − b) = 7.15 ÷ (7.15 − 3.15) = 7.15 ÷ 4 = 1.7875 ≈ 1.79.
    68An electrician measures a cable run as 24 m, to the nearest metre. Cable costs £1.90 per metre. What is the greatest the cable could cost? Give your answer to 2 decimal places, like 12.50.46.55
    Working: Upper bound of the length = 24.5 m. Greatest cost = 24.5 × 1.90 = £46.55.
    69A copper pipe has an outside diameter of d = 15 mm, to the nearest millimetre. Write the error interval for d.14.5<=d<15.5 (also accept: 14.5≤d<15.5)
    Working: Half the unit is 0.5 mm. Lower bound 14.5, upper bound 15.5. 14.5 ≤ d < 15.5.
    70A carpenter has a board 2.40 m long, to the nearest centimetre. She cuts shelves 0.60 m long, to the nearest centimetre. What is the smallest possible value of the board length ÷ the shelf length? Give your answer to 3 significant figures. Can she be certain of getting 4 shelves?3.96
    1. M1 for the lower bound of the board, 2.395
    2. M1 for the upper bound of a shelf, 0.605, and dividing
    3. A1 for 3.96 (so she cannot be certain of 4 shelves)
    Working: Lower bound of the board = 2.395 m. Upper bound of a shelf = 0.605 m. Smallest value = 2.395 ÷ 0.605 = 3.9587… ≈ 3.96. This is less than 4, so she cannot be certain of getting 4 shelves.
    		3
    71A bolt has diameter 11.8 mm, to 1 decimal place, and fits in a hole of diameter 12.0 mm, to 1 decimal place. What is the smallest possible gap between the bolt and the hole, in mm?0.1
    Working: Lower bound of the hole = 11.95 mm. Upper bound of the bolt = 11.85 mm. Smallest gap = 11.95 − 11.85 = 0.1 mm.
    72The current in a circuit is I = V ÷ R. The voltage V is 230 V, to the nearest 10 V, and the resistance R is 46 ohms, to the nearest ohm. Work out the current to a degree of accuracy that is justified, in amps.5
    1. M1 for 235 ÷ 45.5 (the upper bound of the current)
    2. M1 for 225 ÷ 46.5 (the lower bound of the current)
    3. A1 for 5.16 and 4.84 (to 2 decimal places)
    4. C1 for both bounds rounding to 5 at 1 s.f., so 5 A
    Working: Upper bound = 235 ÷ 45.5 = 5.1648…. Lower bound = 225 ÷ 46.5 = 4.8387…. To 2 s.f.: 5.2 and 4.8, which differ. To 1 s.f.: both 5. So the current is 5 A to 1 s.f.
    		4
    73A plumber fills a tank of 150 litres, to the nearest 10 litres, using a pump that delivers 12 litres per minute, to the nearest litre per minute. What is the greatest possible time to fill the tank? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in minutes.13.5
    1. M1 for the upper bound of the volume, 155
    2. M1 for the lower bound of the pump rate, 11.5, and dividing
    3. A1 for 13.5 (13.478… before rounding)
    Working: Upper bound of the volume = 155 litres. Lower bound of the rate = 11.5 litres per minute. Greatest time = 155 ÷ 11.5 = 13.478… ≈ 13.5 minutes.
    		3
