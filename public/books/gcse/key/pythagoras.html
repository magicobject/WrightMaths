1A 5 m ladder leans against a vertical wall. Its foot is 3 m from the wall. How high up the wall does the ladder reach, in metres? (Hint: it is a nice whole number.) [Figure: A ladder against a wall]4
Working: 9 + ?² = 25, so ?² = 16 and ? = 4. The ladder reaches 4 m up the wall. You just used Pythagoras.
2Work out 7².49
Working: 7² = 7 × 7 = 49.
3Work out 12².144
Working: 12 × 12 = 144.
4Work out √81.9
Working: 9 × 9 = 81, so √81 = 9. (Squaring and square-rooting undo each other.)
5Work out √169. [Figure: Squares on the sides of a 3, 4, 5 triangle]13
Working: 13 × 13 = 169, so √169 = 13.
6Triangle PQR has a right angle at Q. Which side is the hypotenuse? [Figure: Triangle PQR with a right angle at Q]PR
Working: PR is opposite Q, so PR is the hypotenuse (and the longest side).
7Find the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with shorter sides 5 cm and 12 cm. Answer in cm. [Figure: Right-angled triangle with shorter sides 12 cm and 5 cm]13
Working: c² = 25 + 144 = 169, so c = 13 cm.
8Find the hypotenuse when the shorter sides are 8 cm and 15 cm. [Figure: Right-angled triangle with shorter sides 15 cm and 8 cm]17
Working: c² = 64 + 225 = 289, so c = 17 cm.
9Find the hypotenuse when the shorter sides are 4 cm and 6 cm. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Right-angled triangle with shorter sides 6 cm and 4 cm]7.2
Working: c² = 52, so c = √52 = 7.211… ≈ 7.2 cm (1 d.p.). Sense check: longer than 6, shorter than 4 + 6 ✓
10Find the hypotenuse when the shorter sides are 5 cm and 7 cm, to 1 d.p. [Figure: Right-angled triangle with shorter sides 7 cm and 5 cm]8.6
Working: √74 = 8.602… ≈ 8.6 cm.
11A big square has side 7 cm. Four right-angled triangles with short sides 3 cm and 4 cm sit inside it, leaving a tilted square in the middle. Find the area of the tilted square, in cm². [Figure: A square with four triangles inside]25
Working: Big square: 7 × 7 = 49. One triangle: 1/2 × 3 × 4 = 6, so four triangles = 24. Tilted square: 49 − 24 = 25 cm².
12What is the side length of the tilted square in the last question, in cm? [Figure: A square with four triangles inside]5
Working: √25 = 5 cm. This is the hypotenuse of the 3, 4, 5 triangle, as the theorem says: 3² + 4² = 5².
13Which statement is the key reason that a ² + b ² = c ² for every right-angled triangle?The big square has the same area in both pictures, and both use the same four triangles
Working: The big square and the four triangles are the same in both pictures, so what is left over must have the same area: a ² + b ² = c ².
14Four triangles with short sides 5 cm and 12 cm sit inside a big square of side 17 cm, leaving a tilted square. Find the area of the tilted square, in cm². [Figure: A square with four triangles inside]169
Working: 17² = 289. One triangle: 1/2 × 5 × 12 = 30, so four triangles = 120. Tilted square: 289 − 120 = 169 cm², so its side is 13 cm: the 5, 12, 13 triple.
15The hypotenuse is 17 cm and one shorter side is 8 cm. Find the other side. [Figure: Right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 17 cm and a side of 8 cm]15
Working: 289 − 64 = 225, so the side is √225 = 15 cm.
16The hypotenuse is 25 cm and one shorter side is 7 cm. Find the other side. [Figure: Right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 25 cm and a side of 7 cm]24
Working: 625 − 49 = 576, so the side is √576 = 24 cm.
17The hypotenuse is 9 cm and one shorter side is 5 cm. Find the other side to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 9 cm and a side of 5 cm]7.5
Working: √56 = 7.483… ≈ 7.5 cm.
18The hypotenuse is 12 cm and one shorter side is 7 cm. Find the other side to 1 d.p. [Figure: Right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 12 cm and a side of 7 cm]9.7
Working: 144 − 49 = 95, and √95 = 9.746… ≈ 9.7 cm.
19A triangle has sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 8 cm. Is it right-angled?No, because 5² + 6² = 61 but 8² = 64
Working: 25 + 36 = 61, which is not 64, so the triangle is not right-angled.
20A triangle has sides 9 cm, 12 cm and 15 cm. Is it right-angled?Yes: 81 + 144 = 225 = 15²
Working: 81 + 144 = 225 and 15² = 225. Yes, right-angled.
21A triangle has sides 4 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm. What type of triangle is it?Acute, because 4² + 5² = 41 is more than 6² = 36
Working: 16 + 25 = 41, which is greater than 36, so the triangle is acute.
22A triangle has sides 2 cm, 3 cm and 4 cm. What type of triangle is it?Obtuse, because 2² + 3² = 13 is less than 4² = 16
Working: 4 + 9 = 13, which is less than 16, so the triangle is obtuse.
233, 4, 5 and 6, 8, ___ are both triples (and the second is the first doubled). What is the missing number?10
Working: Doubling 3, 4, 5 gives 6, 8, 10. Check: 36 + 64 = 100 = 10² ✓
24One more triple: 5, ___, 13. Find the missing shorter side. [Figure: A triple with sides 5, ? and 13]12
Working: 169 − 25 = 144 and √144 = 12. So 5, 12, 13.
25Find the distance between (0, 0) and (3, 4). [Figure: Two points on a grid joined by a line]5
Working: √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5.
26A rectangle is 9 cm by 12 cm. How long is its diagonal, in cm? [Figure: A rectangle with its diagonal]15
Working: 81 + 144 = 225, and √225 = 15 cm.
27A 6.5 m ladder leans against a vertical wall with its foot 2.5 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach, in m? [Figure: A ladder against a wall]6
Working: 42.25 − 6.25 = 36 and √36 = 6 m.
28A cuboid measures 2 cm by 3 cm by 6 cm. Find the length of the longest straight line inside it, in cm. [Figure: A cuboid with its longest diagonal]7
Working: Base diagonal: √(4 + 9) = √13. Longest line: √(13 + 36) = √49 = 7 cm.
29A cuboid measures 4 cm by 5 cm by 7 cm. Find the length of the longest straight line inside it, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: A cuboid with its longest diagonal]9.5
Working: 16 + 25 + 49 = 90, and √90 = 9.486… ≈ 9.5 cm.
30A room is 4 m long, 5 m wide and 3 m high. What is the longest straight pole that fits inside it, in m, to 1 decimal place? [Figure: A room as a cuboid with its longest diagonal]7.1
Working: 16 + 25 + 9 = 50, and √50 = 7.071… ≈ 7.1 m.
31The base of a cuboid has a diagonal of 10 cm. The cuboid is 24 cm high. Find the longest line inside it, in cm. [Figure: A cuboid with a base diagonal and its longest diagonal]26
Working: 100 + 576 = 676, and √676 = 26 cm. (A 5, 12, 13 triple doubled.)
32Dev says: "The shorter sides are 6 and 8, so the hypotenuse is 6 + 8 = 14." What went wrong? [Figure: Right-angled triangle with shorter sides 8 and 6]He added the lengths. Pythagoras adds the squares : 36 + 64 = 100, so it is 10.
Working: Correct: √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10.
33Eve says: "6² + 8² = 100, so the hypotenuse is 100." What went wrong? [Figure: Right-angled triangle with shorter sides 8 and 6]She stopped at c². She needed to take the square root: √100 = 10.
Working: c² = 100 means c = 10.
34The shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are 20 cm and 21 cm. How long is the hypotenuse, in cm? [Figure: Right-angled triangle with shorter sides 21 cm and 20 cm]29
Working: 400 + 441 = 841 and √841 = 29 cm.
35The hypotenuse is 15 cm and one shorter side is 9 cm. Find the other side, in cm. [Figure: Right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 15 cm and a side of 9 cm]12
Working: 225 − 81 = 144 and √144 = 12 cm. (A 3, 4, 5 triangle times 3.)
36Which of these is a Pythagorean triple?8, 15, 17
Working: 64 + 225 = 289 = 17².
37The shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are 1.5 m and 2 m. Find the hypotenuse, in m. [Figure: Right-angled triangle with shorter sides 2 m and 1.5 m]2.5
Working: 2.25 + 4 = 6.25 and √6.25 = 2.5 m. (It is a 3, 4, 5 triangle halved.)
38The two shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are in the ratio 3 : 4, and the longer of them is 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse, in cm.15
Working: The other side is 9 cm. 81 + 144 = 225 and √225 = 15 cm.
39(a) [3 marks] A television screen is 40 cm wide and 30 cm tall. Work out the length of its diagonal, in cm. [Figure: A television screen with its diagonal]50
  1. M1 for using Pythagoras: c² = 40² + 30²
  2. M1 for 1600 + 900 = 2500
  3. A1 for c = 50
Working: c² = 40² + 30² = 1600 + 900 = 2500, so c = 50 cm. (A 3, 4, 5 triangle times 10.)

40(b) [3 marks] A flagpole is held upright by a wire 13 m long from the top to a point on the ground 5 m from the base of the pole. How tall is the pole, in m? [Figure: A flagpole held up by a wire]12
  1. M1 for using Pythagoras to find a shorter side: 13² − 5²
  2. M1 for 169 − 25 = 144
  3. A1 for 12
Working: 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144, so the pole is 12 m tall.

41(c) [3 marks] Work out the area of the right-angled triangle formed by the pole, the ground and the wire, in m². [Figure: The triangle made by a pole, the ground and a wire]30
  1. M1 for using the pole (12 m) and the ground (5 m) as the base and height
  2. M1 for 1/2 × 5 × 12
  3. A1 for 30
Working: 1/2 × 5 × 12 = 30 m².

42A right-angled triangle has two shorter sides, both 3 cm. Find the exact length of the hypotenuse as a surd. (Type it like 3√2 or 3sqrt2.) [Figure: Right-angled triangle with two shorter sides of 3 cm]3√2 (also accept: 3sqrt2, 3sqrt(2))
Working: √18 = √(9 × 2) = 3√2 cm, about 4.24 cm.
43A square has sides of 5 cm. Find the exact length of its diagonal. (Type it like 5√2 or 5sqrt2.) [Figure: A square with its diagonal]5√2 (also accept: 5sqrt2, 5sqrt(2))
Working: c² = 25 + 25 = 50, so c = √50 = 5√2 cm.
44To check that a corner of a room is square, a fitter marks a point 60 cm along one wall and a point 80 cm along the other wall. For the corner to be a right angle, how far apart, in centimetres, should the two marks be? [Figure: Checking a corner is square]100
Working: 60² + 80² = 3600 + 6400 = 10 000. The square root of 10 000 is 100, so the marks should be 100 cm apart. (This is a 3-4-5 triangle made 20 times bigger: 60 : 80 : 100.)
45A pipe has to run diagonally across a rectangular floor, from one corner to the opposite corner. The floor is 2.4 m long and 1.8 m wide. How long is the diagonal pipe, in metres?3
Working: 2.4² + 1.8² = 5.76 + 3.24 = 9. The square root of 9 is 3, so the pipe is 3 m long. (Sense check: 3 is longer than 2.4 and shorter than 2.4 + 1.8 = 4.2 ✓.)
46A 4 m ladder leans against a vertical wall. Its foot is 1.2 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach? Give your answer in metres, to 2 decimal places.3.82
Working: height² = 4² − 1.2² = 16 − 1.44 = 14.56. The square root of 14.56 is 3.8157..., so the ladder reaches 3.82 m (2 d.p.).
47A copper pipe runs along a wall, then goes diagonally to a higher run, then carries on along the wall. The diagonal piece goes 320 mm along and 240 mm up. Copper pipe costs £7.50 per metre. How much does the diagonal piece cost, in pounds? Type it like 42.75. [Figure: A pipe offset]3.00
  1. M1 for 320² + 240² = 160 000
  2. M1 for 400 mm = 0.4 m and multiplying by 7.50
  3. A1 for £3.00
Working: 320² + 240² = 102 400 + 57 600 = 160 000. The square root of 160 000 is 400 mm = 0.4 m. Cost = 0.4 × 7.50 = £3.00.
3
48A shed roof has a rafter that runs from the top of the wall up to the ridge. It goes 3.6 m across and rises 2.7 m. How long is the rafter, in metres?4.5
Working: 3.6² + 2.7² = 12.96 + 7.29 = 20.25. The square root of 20.25 is 4.5, so the rafter is 4.5 m long. (It is a 3-4-5 triangle: 2.7 : 3.6 : 4.5 is 3 : 4 : 5 times 0.9 ✓.)
49An electrician fits a steel brace diagonally across a rectangular cable-tray frame, from the bottom left corner to the top right corner. The frame is 3.2 m wide and 2.4 m high. How long is the brace, in metres?4
Working: 3.2² + 2.4² = 10.24 + 5.76 = 16. The square root of 16 is 4, so the brace is 4 m long. (It is a 3-4-5 triangle: 2.4 : 3.2 : 4 is 3 : 4 : 5 times 0.8 ✓.)
50A 3.5 m length of conduit is fixed diagonally up a wall. It reaches 2.8 m up the wall. How far along the wall does it go, in metres?2.1
Working: distance² = 3.5² − 2.8² = 12.25 − 7.84 = 4.41. The square root of 4.41 is 2.1, so the conduit goes 2.1 m along the wall. (Sense check: 2.1 is shorter than the 3.5 m conduit ✓.)
