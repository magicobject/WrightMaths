    Answers for Probability
    No.QuestionAnswerMarks
    1Simplify 8/20. Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.2/5
    Working: 8 ÷ 4 = 2 and 20 ÷ 4 = 5, so 8/20 = 2/5.
    2Work out 1 − 3/8. Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.5/8
    Working: 1 = 8/8, so 1 − 3/8 = 5/8.
    3Work out 1/4 + 1/2. Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.3/4
    Working: 1/2 = 2/4, so 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4.
    4Work out 2/5 of 30. [Figure: The probability scale]12
    Working: 30 ÷ 5 = 6 and 6 × 2 = 12.
    5Which of these could not be a probability?6/5
    Working: 6/5 = 1.2, which is bigger than 1. A probability can only go from 0 to 1.
    6A bag holds only red counters. You take one without looking. What is the probability that it is red? Type a number.1
    Working: Every counter is red, so picking a red one is certain: probability 1.
    7A forecast says there is a 35% chance of rain. Write this probability as a decimal.0.35
    Working: 35 ÷ 100 = 0.35.
    8A normal dice has faces 1 to 6. What is the probability that it shows a 7? Type a number.0
    Working: There is no 7 on the dice, so it is impossible: probability 0.
    9The marker shows the probability that a train is on time. What decimal does it show? [Figure: A marker on the probability scale]0.8
    Working: There are 10 equal gaps, so each is 0.1. The marker is 8 gaps from 0, and 8 × 0.1 = 0.8.
    10Which of these probabilities shows the most likely outcome? [Figure: A fair spinner with eight sectors]1/2
    Working: 1/2 = 0.5, which is bigger than 0.2, 0.45 and 0.05.
    11One counter is taken at random from the bag shown. What is the probability that it is blue? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5. [Figure: Ten counters in a bag]1/2
    Working: 5 of the 10 counters are blue, so P(blue) = 5/10 = 1/2.
    12A fair dice is rolled. What is the probability that the score is a prime number? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.1/2
    Working: The primes on a dice are 2, 3 and 5. That is 3 out of 6, so P(prime) = 3/6 = 1/2.
    13The spinner shown is fair. What is the probability that it lands on 4? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5. [Figure: A spinner with six sectors]1/3
    Working: There are 6 equal sectors and 2 show a 4, so P(4) = 2/6 = 1/3.
    14A raffle (a prize draw where people buy numbered tickets) sells 200 tickets. Dev buys 8 of them. One ticket is drawn at random to win. What is the probability that Dev wins? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.1/25
    Working: 8/200: divide the numerator and denominator by 8 to get 1/25.
    15A standard pack has 52 cards in 4 suits of 13 cards each (hearts, diamonds, clubs and spades). One card is picked at random. What is the probability that it is a heart? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.1/4
    Working: 13 hearts out of 52 cards: 13/52 = 1/4.
    16A spinner has three colours, so Sam says P(red) = 1/3. When is Sam right?When the three sectors are the same size
    Working: Dividing by 3 works only when all the outcomes are equally likely, so the three sectors must be the same size.
    17The probability that a train is late is 0.15. What is the probability that it is on time?0.85
    Working: P(on time) = 1 − 0.15 = 0.85.
    18The probability that Mia wins a game is 3/7. What is the probability that she does not win? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.4/7
    Working: P(not win) = 1 − 3/7 = 7/7 − 3/7 = 4/7.
    19The probability that a tile in a box is cracked is 6%. What is the probability it is not cracked? Give your answer like 12%.94% (also accept: 94)
    Working: P(not cracked) = 100% − 6% = 94%.
    20The table gives the probability of each colour that a spinner can land on. The probabilities add up to 1. What is the probability that it lands on green? Give a decimal.0.25
    Working: 0.2 + 0.4 + 0.15 = 0.75, so P(green) = 1 − 0.75 = 0.25.
    21A bag has 4 red, 6 yellow and 5 white counters. One counter is taken at random. What is the probability that it is not yellow? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.3/5
    Working: There are 4 + 6 + 5 = 15 counters. Not yellow: 4 + 5 = 9. P = 9/15 = 3/5. Check: 1 − 6/15 = 9/15.
    22Aled says that for Saturday’s match P(win) = 0.6, P(draw) = 0.3 and P(lose) = 0.2. Why cannot this be right?They add up to 1.1, not 1
    Working: 0.6 + 0.3 + 0.2 = 1.1. The probabilities of all the possible results must add to exactly 1.
    23Two coins are tossed. List every possible pair, like HH and HT. How many different outcomes are there? Count HT and TH as different.4
    Working: HH, HT, TH, TT: 4 outcomes.
    24Two fair coins are tossed. What is the probability of two heads? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.1/4
    Working: Only HH is two heads, out of 4 outcomes: 1/4.
    25A cafe meal deal is one sandwich (cheese, ham or egg) and one drink (tea or juice). How many different meal deals are there?6
    Working: Cheese-tea, cheese-juice, ham-tea, ham-juice, egg-tea, egg-juice: 6 meal deals. (3 × 2 = 6.)
    26Two fair dice are rolled and the scores are added. Use the table above. What is the probability that the total is 9? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.1/9
    Working: The pairs that total 9 are (3,6), (4,5), (5,4) and (6,3): 4 pairs. P = 4/36 = 1/9.
    27A fair spinner has three equal sectors A, B and C. The spinner is spun once and a fair coin is tossed once. What is the probability that the spinner lands on B and the coin lands on tails? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.1/6
    Working: The pairs are AH, AT, BH, BT, CH, CT. Only BT is B and tails, so P = 1/6.
    28Two fair dice are rolled and the scores are added. What is the probability that the total score is less than 5? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.1/6
    Working: Total 2: 1 pair. Total 3: 2 pairs. Total 4: 3 pairs. That is 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 pairs, so P = 6/36 = 1/6.
    29A fair dice is rolled 120 times. How many times do you expect it to show a 5?20
    Working: P(5) = 1/6. Expected number = 1/6 × 120 = 20.
    30The probability of rain on a day in April is 0.3. April has 30 days. How many rainy days do you expect?9
    Working: 0.3 × 30 = 9 rainy days.
    31Dev drops a drawing pin 50 times. It lands point up 18 times. What is the relative frequency of “point up”? Give a decimal.0.36
    Working: 18 ÷ 50 = 0.36.
    32A factory tests 800 phone screens and finds that 24 are faulty. Use this to estimate the probability that a screen is faulty. Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.3/100
    Working: 24/800: divide the numerator and denominator by 8 to get 3/100.
    33The factory makes 5000 screens. Using the estimate P(faulty) = 3/100, how many faulty screens do you expect?150
    Working: 3/100 × 5000 = 150 faulty screens.
    34Three students each toss the same coin and count the heads. Ali: 7 heads in 10 tosses. Bea: 46 heads in 100 tosses. Cat: 512 heads in 1000 tosses. Whose result gives the best estimate of P(heads)?Cat, who tossed it the most times
    Working: The more trials, the better the estimate. Cat did 1000 tosses, so her relative frequency (0.512) is the most reliable.
    35A biased spinner has P(red) = 0.4 and P(blue) = 0.35. The only other colour is green. The spinner is spun 200 times. How many greens do you expect?50
    Working: P(green) = 1 − 0.4 − 0.35 = 0.25. Expected greens = 0.25 × 200 = 50.
    36A fair dice is rolled. What is the probability that it shows a 2 or a 5? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.1/3
    Working: 1/6 + 1/6 = 2/6 = 1/3.
    37A student picks one flavour of crisps at random. P(salt) = 0.35, P(cheese) = 0.25 and P(other) = 0.4. What is P(salt or cheese)?0.6
    Working: 0.35 + 0.25 = 0.6.
    38A bag has 4 red, 7 blue and 9 green counters. One counter is picked at random. What is the probability that it is a red or a green counter? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.13/20
    Working: There are 4 + 7 + 9 = 20 counters. Red or green: 4 + 9 = 13. P = 13/20.
    39A fair dice is rolled once. Which pair of outcomes is mutually exclusive?Rolling a factor of 5, and rolling a 6
    Working: The factors of 5 are 1 and 5. Neither is a 6, so they cannot happen together. The other pairs share a score (2 and 5).
    40A card is picked at random from a standard pack of 52 cards. There are 4 kings and 4 queens. What is the probability it is a king or a queen? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.2/13
    Working: 4 + 4 = 8 cards out of 52: 8/52 = 2/13.
    41A fair dice is rolled. What is the probability of an even number or a number bigger than 3? List the scores that fit. Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.2/3
    Working: Even: 2, 4, 6. Bigger than 3: 4, 5, 6. Together, counting each once: 2, 4, 5, 6. That is 4 scores, so P = 4/6 = 2/3.
    42A fair coin is tossed and a fair dice is rolled. What is the probability of heads and a 6? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.1/12
    Working: 1/2 × 1/6 = 1/12.
    43Zoe is late for school with probability 0.1 on any day, independently. What is the probability that she is late on both Monday and Tuesday?0.01
    Working: 0.1 × 0.1 = 0.01.
    44A seed grows with probability 0.8. Two seeds are planted and they grow independently. What is the probability that both grow?0.64
    Working: 0.8 × 0.8 = 0.64.
    45Which pair of events is independent?Tossing a coin, then rolling a dice
    Working: The coin does not affect the dice, so these are independent. In the other pairs the first event changes the second.
    46Use the table of 40 students above. One student is chosen at random. What is the probability that it is a boy who goes by bus? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.11/40
    Working: 11 boys go by bus out of 40 students: 11/40.
    47Use the same table. One of the girls is chosen at random. What is the probability that she walks? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.3/5
    Working: 12 of the 20 girls walk: 12/20 = 3/5.
    48A part from machine A is faulty with probability 0.02. A part from machine B is faulty with probability 0.05. The machines work independently. What is the probability that both parts are faulty? [Figure: Tree diagram for Saturday and Sunday]0.001
    Working: 0.02 × 0.05 = 0.001.
    49A biased coin lands on heads with probability 0.6. The tree shows two tosses. What probability goes in place of the question mark? [Figure: Tree for two tosses of a biased coin]0.4
    Working: 1 − 0.6 = 0.4.
    50Two seeds are planted. Each grows (G) with probability 0.8, independently. What is the probability that exactly one seed grows? [Figure: Tree for two seeds]0.32
    Working: G then N: 0.8 × 0.2 = 0.16. N then G: 0.2 × 0.8 = 0.16. Add: 0.16 + 0.16 = 0.32.
    51A seed fails to grow with probability 0.2. Two seeds are planted independently. What is the probability that neither grows?0.04
    Working: 0.2 × 0.2 = 0.04.
    52A factory tests two parts. Each part is faulty with probability 0.1, independently. What is the probability that at least one part is faulty? (Try finding P(none faulty) first.)0.19
    Working: P(fine) = 0.9, so P(none faulty) = 0.9 × 0.9 = 0.81. P(at least one faulty) = 1 − 0.81 = 0.19.
    53How do you find the probability of a whole path on a tree diagram, such as “first this, then that”?Multiply the probabilities along the branches
    Working: A path means the first thing and then the second thing, so multiply the probabilities along the branches.
    54A bus is late with probability 1/5 each day, independently. What is the probability that it is late on exactly one of two days? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.8/25
    Working: L then N: 1/5 × 4/5 = 4/25. N then L: 4/5 × 1/5 = 4/25. Add: 8/25.
    55On a two-stage tree diagram, what do the probabilities of all the end results add up to?1
    Working: The paths cover every possible outcome once each, so their probabilities add up to 1.
    56A bag has 4 red and 6 yellow counters. Two counters are taken at random without replacement. What is the probability that both are red? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.2/15
    Working: 4/10 × 3/9 = 12/90 = 2/15.
    57The tree shows two counters taken without replacement from a bag of 3 red and 2 blue. What fraction goes where the question mark is? [Figure: Tree for two counters taken without replacement]1/2
    Working: After a red is taken, 4 counters are left and 2 of them are red, so the missing probability is 2/4 = 1/2.
    58A bag has 3 red and 2 blue counters. Two counters are taken at random without replacement. What is the probability of one red and one blue, in either order? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.3/5
    Working: Red then blue: 3/5 × 2/4 = 6/20. Blue then red: 2/5 × 3/4 = 6/20. Add: 12/20 = 3/5.
    59A box has 8 screws and 3 of them are damaged. Two screws are taken at random without replacement. What is the probability that neither is damaged? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.5/14
    Working: 5/8 × 4/7 = 20/56 = 5/14.
    60Two counters are taken from a bag without replacement. Why is the second probability different from the first?One counter has been taken out
    Working: Without replacement, one counter is no longer in the bag, so the denominator of the fraction is smaller, and the numerator may be smaller too.
    61A pack has 10 cards and 4 of them are rare. Two cards are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that at least one is rare? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5. (Try finding P(no rare card) first.) [Figure: A Venn diagram of two sets]2/3
    Working: P(no rare card) = 6/10 × 5/9 = 30/90 = 1/3. So P(at least one rare) = 1 − 1/3 = 2/3.
    62The Venn diagram shows 20 people. A is the set of people who own a bike and B is the set who own a skateboard. How many people are in set B altogether? [Figure: Venn diagram of bike and skateboard owners]10
    Working: The B circle holds the overlap and B only: 4 + 6 = 10.
    63In the same Venn diagram (20 people), one person is chosen at random. What is the probability that they are in neither set? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5. [Figure: Venn diagram of bike and skateboard owners]1/10
    Working: 2 people are outside both circles out of 20: 2/20 = 1/10.
    64In a group of 40 pupils, 22 own a bike, 15 own a skateboard and 8 own both. How many own neither?11
    Working: At least one: 22 + 15 − 8 = 29. Neither: 40 − 29 = 11.
    65In a class of 25, 14 study French and 9 study Spanish. The diagram shows that 3 study both. How many study only French? [Figure: Venn diagram of French and Spanish]11
    Working: French only = 14 − 3 = 11.
    66The letters p, q, r and s mark the four regions of a Venn diagram. Which letter marks the region that is in both A and B? Type the letter. [Figure: Venn diagram with lettered regions]q
    Working: The overlap of the two circles is in A and in B. It is marked q.
    67In a class of 30, 17 like pasta, 14 like pizza and 6 like both. One student is chosen at random. What is the probability that they like neither? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5. [Figure: Forwards: counters to a probability]1/6
    Working: At least one: 17 + 14 − 6 = 25. Neither: 30 − 25 = 5. P = 5/30 = 1/6.
    68A bag has red and blue counters. P(red) = 1/4 and there are 5 red counters. How many counters are in the bag?20
    Working: 5 ÷ 1/4 = 5 × 4 = 20 counters.
    69The probability that a part is faulty is 0.04. In a batch you expect 25 faulty parts. How many parts are in the batch?625
    Working: 25 ÷ 0.04 = 625 parts.
    70A bag has 24 counters. P(green) = 3/8. How many green counters are in the bag?9
    Working: 24 ÷ 8 = 3 and 3 × 3 = 9 green counters.
    71A bag has 3 red counters, 5 blue counters and some green counters. P(green) = 1/3. How many green counters are there?4
    Working: Red and blue make 3 + 5 = 8 counters. They are the 2/3 that are not green, so one third is 4. There are 4 green counters. Check: 4/12 = 1/3.
    72A bag has N counters. The probability that a counter taken at random is red is p. Which expression gives the number of red counters in the bag?N × p
    Working: The number of red counters is the total times the probability: N × p. For example 10 × 0.5 = 5.
    73The probability that a bus is on time is 0.85. Out of 40 buses, how many do you expect not to be on time?6
    Working: P(not on time) = 1 − 0.85 = 0.15. Expected = 0.15 × 40 = 6 buses.
    74Jo says: when I roll a fair dice there are three outcomes: “a 6”, “an even number” and “something else”, so P(6) = 1/3. What is wrong?The three outcomes she lists are not equally likely, and they overlap
    Working: The three outcomes are not equally likely (“something else” covers 1, 3 and 5, and a 6 is also even). P(6) = 1/6.
    75Kit says: I tossed a fair coin 5 times and got heads every time, so the next toss is more likely to be tails. What is wrong?Each toss is independent, so P(tails) is still 1/2
    Working: Tosses are independent. Past results do not change the next one, so P(tails) is 1/2 again.
    76Mia says: the probability of two heads when I toss two fair coins is 1/2 + 1/2 = 1. What went wrong?She added, but “and” means multiply
    Working: “And” multiplies: 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4. Adding gave 1, which would mean certain.
    77The probability that a switch is faulty is 3/20. Write this probability as a percentage. Give your answer like 12%.15% (also accept: 15)
    Working: 3 ÷ 20 = 0.15, and 0.15 × 100 = 15%.
    78A fair dice is rolled. What is the probability that the score is a multiple of 3? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.1/3
    Working: The multiples of 3 are 3 and 6. P = 2/6 = 1/3.
    79The probability of a frost on a winter night is 0.12. How many frosts do you expect in 250 nights?30
    Working: 0.12 × 250 = 30 frosts.
    80A fair coin is tossed three times. What is the probability of three heads? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.1/8
    Working: 1/2 × 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/8.
    81A and B are mutually exclusive events. P(A) = 0.3 and P(B) = 0.5. What is P(A or B)?0.8
    Working: 0.3 + 0.5 = 0.8.
    82In a class of 30, 14 are boys. 6 of the boys and 5 of the girls wear glasses. A student is chosen at random. What is the probability that they wear glasses? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.11/30
    Working: 6 + 5 = 11 students wear glasses out of 30: 11/30.
    83An electrician has a box of 50 fuses and 3 of them are faulty. She picks one at random. What is the probability that it is faulty? Type it as a fraction, like 3/10.3/50
    Working: 3 faulty out of 50 fuses: 3/50.
    84A valve fails a pressure test with probability 0.04. A plumber fits 150 of these valves. Each failed valve costs £38 to replace. What is the expected cost, in pounds? Type it like 42.75.228.00 (also accept: 228)
    1. M1 for 0.04 × 150 = 6
    2. A1 for £228.00
    Working: 0.04 × 150 = 6 failed valves. 6 × 38 = £228.00.

    85A carpenter has a stack of 20 planks and 4 of them are warped. She picks one at random. What is the probability that it is not warped? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.4/5
    Working: 20 − 4 = 16 planks are fine. P = 16/20 = 4/5.
    86An electrician tests two RCDs. Each passes the test with probability 0.98, independently. What is the probability that both pass? Give a decimal.0.9604
    1. M1 for 0.98 × 0.98
    2. A1 for 0.9604
    Working: 0.98 × 0.98 = 0.9604.
    		2
    87A merchant finds that 1 in every 40 taps sold is returned. A plumber orders 400 taps. How many returns would you expect?10
    Working: 1/40 × 400 = 10 returns.
    88A roofer needs two dry days in a row. The probability of rain on any day is 0.25, and the days are independent. What is the probability that both days are dry?0.5625
    1. M1 for P(dry) = 0.75
    2. A1 for 0.75 × 0.75 = 0.5625
    Working: P(dry) = 1 − 0.25 = 0.75. P(both dry) = 0.75 × 0.75 = 0.5625.
    		2
    89Two parts are taken at random. Work out the probability that exactly one of them is faulty. [Figure: Tree for two parts]0.095
    1. M1 for 0.05 × 0.95 (or 0.95 × 0.05)
    2. M1 for adding the two paths, or doubling 0.0475
    3. A1 for 0.095
    Working: Faulty then fine: 0.05 × 0.95 = 0.0475. Fine then faulty: 0.95 × 0.05 = 0.0475. Add: 0.0475 + 0.0475 = 0.095.
    		3
    90Work out how many of the counters are green.15
    1. M1 for 1 − 0.3 − 0.45 = 0.25
    2. M1 for 0.25 × 60
    3. A1 for 15
    Working: P(green) = 1 − 0.3 − 0.45 = 0.25. Green counters = 0.25 × 60 = 15.
    		3
    91In a class of 30 students, 17 play football, 12 play tennis and 7 play both. Show that the probability that a student chosen at random plays neither sport is 4/15.Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for 17 + 12 − 7 = 22 (or 10, 7 and 5 in a Venn diagram)
    2. M1 for 30 − 22 = 8
    3. C1 for 8/30 simplified to 4/15 stated as the answer
    Working: Football only = 17 − 7 = 10 and tennis only = 12 − 7 = 5, so the number playing at least one sport is 10 + 7 + 5 = 22 (or 17 + 12 − 7 = 22). Neither: 30 − 22 = 8, so P(neither) = 8/30 = 4/15, as required.
    		3
    92Higher [4 marks] A bag has 6 red and 4 blue counters. Two counters are taken at random without replacement. Work out the probability that the two counters are the same colour. Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5.7/15
    1. M1 for 6/10 × 5/9
    2. M1 for 4/10 × 3/9
    3. M1 for adding the two products
    4. A1 for 7/15
    Working: Both red: 6/10 × 5/9 = 30/90. Both blue: 4/10 × 3/9 = 12/90. Add: 30/90 + 12/90 = 42/90 = 7/15.
    		4
    93A factory tests 250 light switches and 10 of them fail. Use this to estimate how many of the next 5000 switches will fail.200
    1. M1 for 10/250 or 0.04
    2. M1 for 0.04 × 5000
    3. A1 for 200
    Working: Relative frequency = 10/250 = 0.04. Expected number = 0.04 × 5000 = 200.
    		3
    94Two fair dice are rolled. What is the probability that the first dice shows a bigger number than the second? Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form, like 3/5. (Hint: how many of the 36 pairs have the same number on both?)5/12
    Working: There are 36 pairs. In 6 of them the numbers are equal. In the other 30 pairs, the first dice is bigger in exactly as many as the second dice is, so 15 each. P = 15/36 = 5/12.
    95A bag has red and blue counters. A counter is taken, its colour is noted, and it is put back. This is done twice. The probability of two reds is 9/25. What is the probability of a red on a single pick? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5.3/5
    Working: With replacement the picks are independent, so P(two reds) = p × p = 9/25. The square root of 9 is 3 and the square root of 25 is 5, so p = 3/5. Check: 3/5 × 3/5 = 9/25.
    96A fair dice is rolled three times. What is the probability of at least one six? Give your answer as a fraction, like 3/5. (A tempting wrong answer is 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 1/2. Why does it fail?)91/216
    Working: P(no sixes) = 5/6 × 5/6 × 5/6 = 125/216. So P(at least one six) = 1 − 125/216 = 91/216. Adding 1/6 three times fails because a roll sequence with two sixes is counted more than once: the events are not mutually exclusive.
