    Answers for Circle theorems
    No.QuestionAnswerMarks
    1An isosceles triangle has an angle of 50° at the top. What is each base angle? Answer in degrees.65
    Working: 180 − 50 = 130. The two base angles are equal, so each is 130 ÷ 2 = 65°.
    2Three angles of a quadrilateral are 90°, 105° and 85°. What is the fourth angle? Answer in degrees.80
    Working: 90 + 105 + 85 = 280. 360 − 280 = 80, so the fourth angle is 80°.
    3A right-angled triangle has two short sides of 5 cm and 12 cm. How long is the longest side? Answer in cm.13
    Working: 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169. The square root of 169 is 13, so the longest side is 13 cm.
    4A circle has a diameter of 18 cm. What is its radius? Answer in cm. [Figure: Parts of a circle]9
    Working: 18 ÷ 2 = 9, so the radius is 9 cm.
    5A straight line touches a circle at exactly one point. What is it called?A tangent
    Working: A line that touches a circle at one point is a tangent.
    6What is the region between a chord and the arc it cuts off called?A segment
    Working: The region between a chord and an arc is a segment.
    7Look at the picture. Which angle is the angle at the centre? [Figure: A circle with two angles on the same arc]Angle AOB
    Working: Angle AOB has its corner at the centre O, so it is the angle at the centre. Angle ACB has its corner at C on the circle.
    8Which is the longest chord of a circle? [Figure: The angle at the centre and the circumference]The diameter
    Working: A chord through the centre is the diameter, and it is the longest chord.
    9O is the centre of the circle. Angle ACB = 38°. Work out angle AOB. Answer in degrees. [Figure: A circle with two angles on the same arc]76
    Working: 2 × 38 = 76, so angle AOB = 76°. Reason: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.
    10O is the centre of the circle. Angle AOB = 130°. Work out angle ACB. Answer in degrees. [Figure: A circle with two angles on the same arc]65
    Working: 130 ÷ 2 = 65, so angle ACB = 65°. Reason: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.
    11Angle AOB = 124° and angle ACB = 62°. Give a reason why angle ACB = 62°. [Figure: A circle with two angles on the same arc]The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference
    Working: Angle AOB is at the centre and angle ACB is at the circumference, both on the arc AB. The reason is: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.
    12O is the centre of the circle. Angle AOB = 130°. Work out angle OAB. Answer in degrees. [Figure: A triangle made of two radii and a chord]25
    Working: Triangle OAB is isosceles (OA = OB, radii). 180 − 130 = 50, and 50 ÷ 2 = 25, so angle OAB = 25°.
    13O is the centre of the circle. Angle AOB = (5x + 10)° and angle ACB = (2x + 15)°. Work out x. [Figure: A circle with two angles on the same arc]20
    Working: 5x + 10 = 2(2x + 15) = 4x + 30, so x = 20. Check: AOB = 110, ACB = 55, and 110 = 2 × 55.
    14The angle at the circumference is 41°. What is the angle at the centre when both stand on the same arc? Answer in degrees.82
    Working: 2 × 41 = 82, so the angle at the centre is 82°.
    15Higher O is the centre. Angle AOB = 100°. The point C is on the SHORT arc AB. Work out angle ACB. Answer in degrees. [Figure: A circle with two angles on the same arc]130
    Working: The reflex angle at O is 360 − 100 = 260°. The angle at the circumference is half of that: 130°.
    16C and D are on the circle. Angle ACB = 42°. Work out angle ADB. Answer in degrees. [Figure: Angles on the same arc]42
    Working: Both angles stand on the arc AB from the same side, so angle ADB = 42°. Reason: angles in the same segment are equal.
    17C and D are in the same segment. Angle ACB = (2x + 5)° and angle ADB = (4x − 21)°. Work out x. [Figure: Angles on the same arc]13
    Working: 2x + 5 = 4x − 21. So 26 = 2x and x = 13. Check: 2 × 13 + 5 = 31 and 4 × 13 − 21 = 31.
    18Angle ACB = 55° and angle ADB = 55°. Give a reason why these two angles are equal. [Figure: Angles on the same arc]Angles in the same segment are equal
    Working: C and D are in the same segment, so the reason is: angles in the same segment are equal.

    Working: C and D are on the same side of AB, so ADB = ACB = 62°. E is on the other side, so AEB is a different size (180 − 62 = 118°).
    21AB is a diameter of the circle. Angle CAB = 35°. Work out angle ABC. Answer in degrees. [Figure: A triangle in a semicircle]55
    Working: Angle ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle). Angle ABC = 180 − 90 − 35 = 55°.
    22AB is a diameter of a circle and is 10 cm long. The point C is on the circle and AC = 6 cm. How long is BC? Answer in cm. [Figure: A triangle in a semicircle]8

    23AB is a diameter. Give a reason why angle ACB = 90°. [Figure: A triangle in a semicircle]The angle in a semicircle is 90°

    Working: Angle ABC = 180 − 72 = 108° (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral). Angle CBE = 180 − 108 = 72° (angles on a straight line). The exterior angle equals the interior angle opposite it.
    30Angle ABC = 88° and angle ADC = 92°. Give a reason why these two angles add up to 180°. [Figure: A four-sided shape with its corners on a circle]Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°
    Working: B and D are opposite corners of a cyclic quadrilateral, so the reason is: opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.

    33A circle has a radius of 5 cm. T is 13 cm from the centre O. TA is a tangent to the circle at A. How long is TA? Answer in cm. [Figure: A tangent from a point]12

    36TA and TB are tangents to the circle. Give a reason why TA = TB. [Figure: Two tangents from a point]Tangents from an external point are equal in length

    37TA and TB are tangents to a circle from T. TA = (3x + 2) cm and TB = (5x − 6) cm. How long is TA? Answer in cm.14

    67AB is a diameter of a circle with centre O, and C is a point on the circle. Prove that angle ACB = 90°. [Figure: A triangle in a semicircle]Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. B1 OA = OB = OC (radii), so triangles OAC and OBC are isosceles
    2. M1 angle OCA = a and angle OCB = b (base angles of isosceles triangles)
    3. M1 a + b + (a + b) = 180 (angles in triangle ABC)
    4. C1 so a + b = 90, angle ACB = 90°
    Working: OA = OC = OB (radii). Triangle OAC is isosceles, so angle OCA = a. Triangle OBC is isosceles, so angle OCB = b. In triangle ABC: a + b + (a + b) = 180, so 2(a + b) = 180 and a + b = 90. So angle ACB = a + b = 90°.
    		4
    68ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral in a circle with centre O. Let angle BAD = x. Prove that angle BCD = 180° − x. (Take x at most 90°. If x is more than 90°, swap the names A and C, because then angle BCD is the one less than 90°. Use the theorem that the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.)Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. B1 angle BOD = 2x (the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference)
    2. B1 reflex angle BOD = 360 − 2x (angles at a point)
    3. M1 angle BCD = (360 − 2x) ÷ 2 = 180 − x
    4. C1 so angle BAD + angle BCD = 180°
    Working: Take x at most 90°, so 2x is at most 180° and is the angle BOD on the short arc. (If x is more than 90°, swap A and C and the same working applies.) Angle BOD = 2x (the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference). The reflex angle BOD = 360 − 2x (angles at a point). Angle BCD = half of the reflex angle = 180 − x (the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference). So x + (180 − x) = 180: opposite angles add up to 180°.
    		4
    69TA and TB are tangents to a circle with centre O. Prove that TA = TB. (Use Pythagoras.)Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. B1 angles OAT and OBT are 90° (tangent perpendicular to radius)
    2. M1 TA² = OT² − OA² and TB² = OT² − OB²
    3. C1 OA = OB (radii), so TA = TB
    Working: Angles OAT and OBT are 90° (tangent and radius). TA² = OT² − OA² and TB² = OT² − OB². OA = OB (radii), so TA² = TB² and TA = TB.

    77A bricklayer builds an arch with a span (width) of 2.4 m and a rise (height) of 0.5 m. The curve is part of a circle. Work out the radius of the circle. Give your answer in m. [Figure: An arch]1.69

    2. M1 r² = (r − 0.5)² + 1.2² and expanded
    3. A1 r = 1.69
    Working: The centre O is r − 0.5 below the base, and half the span is 1.2 m. r² = (r − 0.5)² + 1.2² = r² − r + 0.25 + 1.44. So r = 1.69 m.

    80A carpenter tests a corner with a semicircular piece of plywood. Its straight edge is a diameter, 1.2 m long. A and B are the two ends of the straight edge, and C is a point on the curved edge. AC = 0.72 m. How long is BC? Answer in m.0.96

    86A circle has a radius of 17 cm. The chord AB is 16 cm long. Work out the distance from the centre of the circle to the chord AB. Give your answer in cm. [Figure: A chord and the line from the centre]15
    1. M1 for 16 ÷ 2 = 8 (the line from the centre bisects the chord)
    2. M1 for 17² − 8²
    3. A1 for 15
    Working: Half the chord = 8 cm. OM² = 17² − 8² = 289 − 64 = 225, so OM = 15 cm.

    87O is the centre of the circle. Angle AOB = 130°. Work out angle OAB. Give reasons for your answer. [Figure: A triangle made of two radii and a chord]25
    1. M1 for (180 − 130) ÷ 2
    2. A1 for 25
    3. C1 for OA = OB (radii) so base angles equal, or angles in a triangle add up to 180°
    Working: OA = OB (radii), so triangle OAB is isosceles. Angle OAB = (180 − 130) ÷ 2 = 25°.

    88Higher Two tangents are drawn to a circle of radius 4 cm from a point T. The angle between the tangents is 60°. How far is T from the centre O? Answer in cm.8
    Working: Angle OTA = 30° (OT bisects the angle between the tangents). Angle OAT = 90°. sin 30° = 4 ÷ OT, and sin 30° = 0.5, so OT = 4 ÷ 0.5 = 8 cm.
    89Higher ABCD is a parallelogram with all four corners on a circle. Angle ABC = x°. Work out x.90
    Working: Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal, so angle ADC = angle ABC = x. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°, so x + x = 180, giving x = 90. A parallelogram on a circle is a rectangle.
    90Higher A circular arc has a chord of 12 cm and a rise (height of the arc above the chord) of 2 cm. What is the radius of the circle? Answer in cm.10
    Working: Half the chord = 6 cm. r² = (r − 2)² + 6² = r² − 4r + 4 + 36, so 4r = 40 and r = 10 cm.
