23Which is the formula for the area of a circle?A = πr²
Working: The area of a circle is π × r². The other two formulas give the circumference.

25This is the same semicircle. What is its perimeter? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in cm. [Figure: The same semicircle]20.6

26What is the area of this quarter circle with a radius of 10 cm? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in cm². [Figure: A quarter circle]78.5

27What is the perimeter of that quarter circle with a radius of 10 cm? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in cm.35.7

28A semicircle has a radius r. What is its perimeter?πr + 2r
Working: The curved edge is half of 2πr, which is πr. The straight edge is the diameter, 2r. So the perimeter is πr + 2r.
29A window is a rectangle with a semicircle on top. The rectangle part is 1.4 m wide and 1.2 m high. The semicircle has the 1.4 m width as its diameter. What is the area of glass? Give your answer to 2 decimal places, in m². [Figure: A window with a curved top]2.45

  2. M1 for π × 3² (= 28.27…)
  3. M1 for the subtraction 50.27… − 28.27…

68A piece of string 40 cm long makes the outline of a square with sides of 10 cm. The string is then reshaped into a circle. What is the area of the circle? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in cm². Is it bigger than the area of the square?127.3
Working: C = 40, so r = 40 ÷ (2π) = 6.3662… cm. A = π × 6.3662…² = 127.3240… ≈ 127.3 cm². That is bigger than the square (100 cm²). Of all shapes with the same perimeter, the circle holds the most area.
69The radius of a circle is doubled. How many times bigger is the new area? (Try it with a radius of 3 cm, then of 5 cm. Does the answer change?)4
Working: Radius 3: A = π × 9 = 9π. Radius 6: A = π × 36 = 36π. 36π ÷ 9π = 4. For any radius r, doubling gives π(2r)² = 4πr², which is always 4 times the original. A circle with twice the radius has 4 times the area.
70A rope is pulled tight round a circle (a ball, or the Earth). The rope is then made exactly 1 m longer, and held at the same height above the circle all the way round. How far is the rope above the surface? Give your answer to 2 decimal places, in m. (The answer is the same for any size of circle.)0.16
Working: Old circumference = 2πr. New circumference = 2πr + 1 = 2πR. So 2π(R − r) = 1, and R − r = 1 ÷ (2π) = 0.1592… ≈ 0.16 m. The gap is about 16 cm, and the radius of the circle does not matter.
71A plumber uses a hole saw to cut a round hole for a 110 mm waste pipe. The hole has a diameter of 110 mm. What is the area of the hole? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in mm².9503.3
Working: Radius = 55 mm. A = π × 55² = π × 3025 = 9503.3178… ≈ 9503.3 mm².
72An electrician winds a cable round the core of a drum 60 times. The core has a diameter of 25 cm. Ignore the thickness of the cable. How many metres of cable are on the drum? Give your answer to 1 decimal place.47.1
Working: One turn: π × 25 = 78.5398… cm. 60 turns: 60 × 78.5398… = 4712.389… cm. In metres: 4712.389… ÷ 100 = 47.1239… ≈ 47.1 m.
