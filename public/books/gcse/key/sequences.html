    Answers for Sequences
    No.QuestionAnswerMarks
    1Work out 4n − 1 when n = 6.23
    Working: 4 × 6 = 24, then 24 − 1 = 23.
    2Work out 3 − 5.-2
    Working: 3 − 5 = −2.
    3Work out −2 − 4.-6
    Working: −2 − 4 = −6.
    4Work out 5 × 6 − 3.27
    Working: 5 × 6 = 30, then 30 − 3 = 27.
    5Work out 2 × 2 × 2 × 2. [Figure: A sequence with a rule]16
    Working: 2 × 2 = 4, 4 × 2 = 8, 8 × 2 = 16.
    6Here are the first four terms of a sequence. What is the 5th term? [Figure: The start of a sequence]21
    Working: 9 − 5 = 4, 13 − 9 = 4 and 17 − 13 = 4. The rule is add 4, so 17 + 4 = 21.
    7What is the term-to-term rule for 5, 9, 13, 17 …?Add 4
    Working: The gap is 9 − 5 = 4, then 13 − 9 = 4 again. The rule is add 4.
    8Carry on the sequence 40, 34, 28, 22 … Write the next two terms with a comma between them, like 8, 6.16,10 (also accept: 16 and 10)
    Working: The gap is 40 − 34 = 6, so the rule is subtract 6. 22 − 6 = 16 and 16 − 6 = 10. The next two terms are 16, 10.
    9Write the next term of 6, 2, −2, −6 …-10
    Working: The rule is subtract 4. −6 − 4 = −10.
    10A carpenter cuts shelf supports. The first is 30 cm long and each one after it is 5 cm longer than the one before. How long is the 6th support, in cm?55
    Working: 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 55. The 6th support is 55 cm long.
    11The nth term of a sequence is 2n + 5. What is the 1st term?7
    Working: Put n = 1: 2 × 1 + 5 = 7.
    12The nth term of a sequence is 3n − 2. What is the 10th term?28
    Working: 3 × 10 = 30, then 30 − 2 = 28.
    13The nth term of a sequence is 4n − 10. What is the 1st term?-6
    Working: 4 × 1 − 10 = 4 − 10 = −6.
    14Each riser of a staircase is 18 cm high. The top of step n is 18n cm above the floor. How high above the floor is the top of step 14? Answer in cm. [Figure: A staircase of five steps]252
    Working: 18 × 14 = 252, so the top of step 14 is 252 cm above the floor.
    15The nth term of a sequence is 7n − 3. What is the 5th term? [Figure: Rows of matchstick squares]32
    Working: 7 × 5 = 35, then 35 − 3 = 32.
    16Find the nth term of the sequence 4, 7, 10, 13 …3n+1 (also accept: 1+3n)
    Working: The gap is 3, so start with 3n: 3, 6, 9, 12. The sequence is 1 more each time: 4, 7, 10, 13. The nth term is 3n + 1.
    17Find the nth term of the sequence 2, 8, 14, 20 …6n-4 (also accept: -4+6n)
    Working: The gap is 6, so start with 6n: 6, 12, 18, 24. The sequence is 4 less each time: 2, 8, 14, 20. The nth term is 6n − 4.
    18Find the nth term of the sequence 9, 7, 5, 3 …-2n+11 (also accept: 11-2n)
    Working: The gap is −2, so start with −2n: −2, −4, −6, −8. To reach 9, 7, 5, 3 you add 11 each time. The nth term is −2n + 11 (or 11 − 2n).
    19Matchsticks make a row of triangles. Pattern n has n triangles. Write the nth term for the number of matchsticks. [Figure: Rows of matchstick triangles]2n+1 (also accept: 1+2n)
    Working: Patterns 1, 2, 3 and 4 use 3, 5, 7 and 9 matchsticks. The gap is 2, so start with 2n: 2, 4, 6, 8. Add 1 to reach 3, 5, 7, 9. The nth term is 2n + 1.
    20Pattern n of a row of matchstick triangles uses 2n + 1 matchsticks. How many matchsticks make a row of 30 triangles?61
    Working: 2 × 30 + 1 = 61 matchsticks.
    21A path is laid in sections. One section needs 8 slabs, two sections need 12 slabs and three sections need 16 slabs. It carries on the same way. Write the nth term for the number of slabs.4n+4 (also accept: 4+4n)
    Working: 8, 12, 16 goes up by 4, so start with 4n: 4, 8, 12. Add 4 to reach 8, 12, 16. The nth term is 4n + 4.
    22Which term of the sequence with nth term 3n + 2 is equal to 50? Give the position n.16
    Working: 3n + 2 = 50. Subtract 2: 3n = 48. Divide by 3: n = 16. So 50 is the 16th term.
    23Is 70 a term of the sequence with nth term 4n − 2?Yes, it is term 18
    Working: 4n − 2 = 70, so 4n = 72 and n = 18. That is a whole number, so 70 is the 18th term.
    24Is 45 a term of the sequence with nth term 6n − 1? Type yes or no.no
    Working: 6n − 1 = 45 gives 6n = 46, so n = 46 ÷ 6 = 7.67 … That is not a whole number, so 45 is not in the sequence. The answer is no.
    25The sequence 7, 12, 17, 22 … carries on in the same way. Will 100 be in it? Type yes or no.no
    Working: The gap is 5 and 5n gives 5, 10, 15, so the nth term is 5n + 2. Then 5n + 2 = 100 gives 5n = 98 and n = 19.6, which is not whole. So 100 is not in the sequence: no.
    26What is the first term of the sequence with nth term 7n − 3 that is bigger than 100? [Figure: A sequence that multiplies]102
    Working: 7n − 3 = 100 gives 7n = 103, so n = 14.7 … Try n = 14: 7 × 14 − 3 = 95, which is too small. Try n = 15: 7 × 15 − 3 = 102. The first term over 100 is 102.
    27Carry on the sequence 3, 6, 12, 24 … Write the next two terms with a comma between them, like 8, 6.48,96 (also accept: 48 and 96)
    Working: Each term is 2 times the one before. 24 × 2 = 48 and 48 × 2 = 96. The next two terms are 48, 96.
    28Write the next term of the sequence 81, 27, 9, 3 …1
    Working: 81 ÷ 3 = 27, 27 ÷ 3 = 9 and 9 ÷ 3 = 3. The rule is divide by 3, so the next term is 3 ÷ 3 = 1.
    29In this sequence each term after the first two is the sum of the two terms before it: 4, 5, 9, 14, 23 … What is the next term?37
    Working: 4 + 5 = 9, 5 + 9 = 14, 9 + 14 = 23. So the next term is 14 + 23 = 37.
    30Which sequence multiplies by the same number each time to get the next term?1, 3, 9, 27
    Working: 1 × 3 = 3, 3 × 3 = 9, 9 × 3 = 27. It multiplies by 3 each time.
    31A plumber cuts a 640 mm length of pipe in half. She keeps cutting the piece in half again. The lengths are 640, 320, 160, 80 … mm. What is the 6th length, in mm?20
    Working: 640, 320, 160, 80, 40, 20. The 6th length is 20 mm.
    32Find the nth term of the quadratic sequence 2, 6, 12, 20 … Type it like n^2 + 3n.n^2+n (also accept: n^2+n+0, n(n+1))
    Working: First differences: 4, 6, 8. Second differences: 2, 2. Half of 2 is 1, so start with n²: 1, 4, 9, 16. Take these from the sequence: 1, 2, 3, 4, which is n. The nth term is n² + n.
    33Find the nth term of the quadratic sequence 5, 11, 21, 35 … Type it like 3n^2 + n.2n^2+3 (also accept: 3+2n^2)
    Working: First differences: 6, 10, 14. Second differences: 4, 4. Half of 4 is 2, so start with 2n²: 2, 8, 18, 32. Take these from the sequence: 3, 3, 3, 3. The nth term is 2n² + 3.
    34Pattern n is a square of n rows of n dark dots, plus 2 pink dots. Write the nth term for the total number of dots. Type it like n^2 + 3n. [Figure: Dot patterns]n^2+2 (also accept: 2+n^2)
    Working: The square has n × n = n² dark dots and there are always 2 pink dots. The nth term is n² + 2. (Check: 3, 6, 11 for patterns 1, 2, 3.)
    35Write the first three terms of the sequence with nth term 4n + 3. Type them with commas, like 2, 4, 6.7,11,15 (also accept: 7, 11 and 15)
    Working: n = 1: 4 + 3 = 7. n = 2: 8 + 3 = 11. n = 3: 12 + 3 = 15. The terms are 7, 11, 15.
    36A sequence starts 2, 6, 10, 14 … Write the nth term.4n-2 (also accept: -2+4n)
    Working: The gap is 4, so start with 4n: 4, 8, 12, 16. The sequence is 2 smaller each time: 2, 6, 10, 14. The nth term is 4n − 2.
    37The 2nd term of a linear sequence is 9 and the 5th term is 18. Write the nth term.3n+3 (also accept: 3+3n)
    Working: From the 2nd term to the 5th is 3 steps and the terms rise by 18 − 9 = 9, so each step is 9 ÷ 3 = 3. The nth term starts 3n. When n = 2, 3n = 6 but the term is 9, so add 3. The nth term is 3n + 3. Check: the 5th term is 3 × 5 + 3 = 18.
    38A linear sequence starts at 5 and goes up by 6 each time. Write the nth term.6n-1 (also accept: -1+6n)
    Working: The gap is 6, so start with 6n. When n = 1, 6n = 6 but the first term is 5, so take away 1. The nth term is 6n − 1.
    39The nth term of a sequence is 5n − 4. What is its term-to-term rule?Add 5
    Working: The first three terms are 1, 6, 11. Each is 5 more than the one before, so the rule is add 5.
    40Lee says: the nth term of 5, 8, 11, 14 … is n + 3, because the sequence goes up by 3. What went wrong?He should have used 3n and fixed the start.
    Working: n + 3 gives 4, 5, 6, which goes up by 1. The gap of 3 must multiply n: 3n gives 3, 6, 9, 12, and the sequence is 2 more each time. The nth term is 3n + 2.
    41Zoe says: the 10th term of 5, 7, 9, 11 … is 5 + 10 × 2 = 25. What went wrong?There are only 9 steps up to the 10th term.
    Working: The 10th term is the 1st term plus 9 steps of 2: 5 + 9 × 2 = 23. Check with the nth term 2n + 3: 2 × 10 + 3 = 23.
    42Work out the 8th term of the sequence with nth term 9n − 20.52
    Working: 9 × 8 = 72, then 72 − 20 = 52.
    43Find the nth term of the sequence 12, 9, 6, 3 …-3n+15 (also accept: 15-3n)
    Working: The terms go down by 3, so start with −3n: −3, −6, −9, −12. To reach 12, 9, 6, 3 add 15. The nth term is −3n + 15.
    44Which is the term-to-term rule for 2, 6, 18, 54 …?Multiply by 3
    Working: 6 ÷ 2 = 3, 18 ÷ 6 = 3 and 54 ÷ 18 = 3. Each term is 3 times the one before.
    45Which term of the sequence with nth term 6n − 5 is equal to 91?16
    Working: 6n − 5 = 91, so 6n = 96 and n = 16. So 91 is the 16th term.
    46Each term of this sequence is the sum of the two before it: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 … What are the next two terms? Type them with a comma, like 8, 6.13,21 (also accept: 13 and 21)
    Working: 5 + 8 = 13 and 8 + 13 = 21. The next two terms are 13, 21.
    47(a) [2 marks] Write an expression for the nth term.6n+5 (also accept: 5+6n)
    1. M1 for 6n (a difference of 6)
    2. A1 for 6n + 5
    Working: The terms go up by 6, so start with 6n: 6, 12, 18, 24. The sequence is 5 more each time. The nth term is 6n + 5.
    		2
    48(b) [3 marks] Is 200 a term of this sequence? Type yes or no, then show your working on paper.no
    1. M1 for setting 6n + 5 = 200
    2. M1 for solving to get n = 32.5
    3. C1 for concluding that n is not a whole number, so 200 is not a term
    Working: 6n + 5 = 200, so 6n = 195 and n = 32.5. A position must be a whole number, so 200 is not a term. The answer is no.
    		3
    49Pattern n of a row of matchstick squares uses 3n + 1 matchsticks. Mia has 100 matchsticks. She wants to make the biggest pattern she can. What is the biggest value of n she can make?33
    1. M1 for 3n + 1 = 100 (or at most 100)
    2. M1 for 3n = 99
    3. A1 for 33
    Working: 3n + 1 = 100, so 3n = 99 and n = 33. Pattern 33 uses 3 × 33 + 1 = 100 matchsticks, so Mia can make pattern 33.
    		3
    50Show that 96 is a term of the sequence 6, 11, 16, 21 …Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for the nth term 5n + 1
    2. M1 for solving 5n + 1 = 96 to get n = 19
    3. C1 for concluding that n is a whole number, so 96 is a term
    Working: The gap is 5, so the nth term starts 5n: 5, 10, 15, 20. The sequence is 1 more, so the nth term is 5n + 1. Then 5n + 1 = 96 gives 5n = 95 and n = 19. That is a whole number, so 96 is the 19th term.
    		3
    51In which week does Ava first save more in that week than Tom does? Give the week number.5
    1. M1 for Tom: 12n + 18
    2. M1 for Ava: 20n − 15
    3. M1 for comparing them, giving n > 4.125
    4. A1 for week 5
    Working: Tom: the terms go up by 12, so the nth term is 12n + 18. Ava: they go up by 20, so the nth term is 20n − 15. Ava is ahead when 20n − 15 > 12n + 18, so 8n > 33 and n > 4.125. The first whole week is 5. Check: Tom saves 78 and Ava saves 85 in week 5.
    		4
    52Show that the sum of any two consecutive terms of the sequence with nth term 4n − 1 is never a multiple of 4.Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for the next term 4(n + 1) − 1 = 4n + 3
    2. M1 for adding the terms to get 8n + 2
    3. C1 for 4(2n) + 2, so always 2 more than a multiple of 4
    Working: Two consecutive terms are 4n − 1 and 4(n + 1) − 1 = 4n + 3. Their sum is 8n + 2. And 8n + 2 = 4(2n) + 2, which is always 2 more than a multiple of 4. A number that is 2 more than a multiple of 4 is never a multiple of 4.
    		3
    53In a sequence of whole numbers, each term from the 3rd onwards is the sum of the two terms before it. The 3rd term is 10 and the 5th term is 26. What are the 1st and 2nd terms? Type them with a comma, like 8, 6.4,6 (also accept: 4 and 6)
    Working: Let the first two terms be a and b. The 3rd term is a + b = 10. The 4th is a + 2b. The 5th is (a + b) + (a + 2b) = 2a + 3b = 26. Double the first equation: 2a + 2b = 20. Subtract: b = 6, so a = 4. The sequence is 4, 6, 10, 16, 26.
    54Sequence A has nth term 4n + 3. Sequence B has nth term 54 − 4n. Is there a number that is in both sequences? Type yes or no.no
    Working: Every term of A is 3 more than a multiple of 4, so it leaves remainder 3 when divided by 4. And 54 − 4n = 4(13 − n) + 2, so every term of B leaves remainder 2. A number cannot leave remainder 3 and remainder 2 together, so no number is in both sequences. The answer is no.
    55The risers of a staircase are 19 cm high, so the top of step n is 19n cm above the floor. The top of the staircase must be 2.66 m above the floor. How many steps does it need?14
    Working: 2.66 m = 266 cm. 19n = 266, so n = 266 ÷ 19 = 14. The staircase needs 14 steps.
    56A tiler lays wall tiles in rows that get longer towards a corner. Row 1 has 12 tiles, row 2 has 14, row 3 has 16, and so on. How many tiles are in row 9?28
    Working: The rows go up by 2, so the nth term starts 2n: 2, 4, 6. Add 10 to get 12, 14, 16, so the nth term is 2n + 10. For n = 9: 2 × 9 + 10 = 28 tiles. Check: 12 + 8 × 2 = 28.
    57An electrician fixes cable clips along a wall. The 1st clip is 25 cm from the corner and the clips are 40 cm apart, so the distances are 25, 65, 105, 145 … cm. How far from the corner is the 12th clip? Answer in cm.465
    Working: The gap is 40, so start with 40n: 40, 80, 120. The terms are 15 smaller, so the nth term is 40n − 15. For n = 12: 40 × 12 − 15 = 480 − 15 = 465 cm.
    58A plumber charges a £60 call-out fee plus £18 for each metre of pipe fitted. The costs of fitting 1, 2, 3 … metres are £78, £96, £114 … Write the nth term, then work out the cost of fitting 11 metres. Answer in pounds.258
    1. M1 for the nth term 18n + 60 (a difference of 18)
    2. M1 for putting n = 11 into it
    3. A1 for 258
    Working: The costs go up by 18, so the nth term starts 18n: 18, 36, 54. Add 60 to reach 78, 96, 114. The nth term is 18n + 60. For 11 metres: 18 × 11 + 60 = 198 + 60 = 258. The cost is £258.
    		3
    59A carpenter fits joists. The 1st joist is 40 cm from the wall and the joists are 45 cm apart, so the distances are 40, 85, 130 … cm. Could there be a joist exactly 400 cm from the wall? Type yes or no.yes
    Working: The gap is 45, so the nth term starts 45n: 45, 90, 135. That is 5 too big, so the nth term is 45n − 5. Then 45n − 5 = 400 gives 45n = 405 and n = 9. That is a whole number, so yes: it is the 9th joist.
    60A plumber stacks pipes in rows. The bottom row has 12 pipes and each row above has one pipe fewer: 12, 11, 10 … The nth term is 13 − n. Which row has 5 pipes? Give the row number.8
    Working: 13 − n = 5, so n = 13 − 5 = 8. Row 8 has 5 pipes. Check: the rows go 12, 11, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, so 5 is the 8th number.
