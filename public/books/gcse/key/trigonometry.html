    No.QuestionAnswerMarks
    1A right-angled triangle has short sides of 6 cm and 8 cm. How long is the longest side, in centimetres? [Figure: Right-angled triangle with sides 6 and 8]10
    Working: 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100. The square root of 100 is 10 cm.
    2Simplify the ratio 12 : 18. Type it like 3:4.2:3
    Working: 12 ÷ 6 = 2 and 18 ÷ 6 = 3, so the ratio is 2:3.
    3Fill the gap: 3 : 4 = 9 : ?. What number goes where the ? is?12
    Working: 3 × 3 = 9, so 4 × 3 = 12.
    4Round 7.456 to 1 decimal place.7.5
    Working: 7.456 has 4 in the first decimal place and 5 after it. 5 rounds up, so 7.5.
    5Solve 20 ÷ x = 4. [Figure: Right-angled triangle with sides named from angle x]5
    Working: 20 ÷ 5 = 4, so x = 5.
    6Triangle ABC has a right angle at B. Which side is the hypotenuse? Type two letters, like PQ. [Figure: Triangle ABC with the right angle at B]AC (also accept: CA)
    Working: The right angle is at B, so the side opposite it is AC. The hypotenuse is AC.
    7Same triangle, with the angle x at C. Which side is opposite x? Type two letters, like PQ. [Figure: Triangle ABC with angle x at C]AB (also accept: BA)
    Working: x is at C. The side that does not touch C is AB, so AB is opposite x.
    8Same triangle, with the angle x at C. Which side is adjacent to x? Type two letters, like PQ. [Figure: Triangle ABC with angle x at C]BC (also accept: CB)
    Working: Two sides touch C: AC and BC. AC is the hypotenuse, so the adjacent side is BC.
    9Triangle ABC again, but this time the angle y is at A. Which side is opposite y? Type two letters, like PQ. [Figure: Triangle ABC with angle y at A]BC (also accept: CB)
    Working: y is at A. The side that does not touch A is BC, so BC is opposite y. (AB is adjacent to y. Last time AB was opposite: the names change with the angle.)
    10A ladder leans against a wall. The angle x is between the ladder and the ground. Which side is opposite x? [Figure: Ladder against a wall as a triangle]The wall
    Working: x is where the ladder meets the ground. The wall does not touch that corner, so the wall is opposite x. The ground is adjacent and the ladder is the hypotenuse.
    11In a right-angled triangle, what is the sine of an angle?opposite ÷ hypotenuse
    Working: SOH: sin = opposite ÷ hypotenuse. CAH: cos = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse. TOA: tan = opposite ÷ adjacent.
    12Use the triangle to find sin x. Give your answer as a fraction, like 2/3. [Figure: Triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5]3/5
    Working: sin x = opposite ÷ hypotenuse = 3/5.
    13Use the same triangle to find cos x. Give your answer as a fraction, like 2/3. [Figure: Triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5]4/5
    Working: cos x = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse = 4/5.
    14Use the same triangle to find tan x. Give your answer as a fraction, like 2/3. [Figure: Triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5]3/4
    Working: tan x = opposite ÷ adjacent = 3/4.
    15Use your calculator to work out sin 40°. Give your answer to 3 decimal places.0.643
    Working: sin 40° = 0.6427876…, which is 0.643 to 3 decimal places.
    16Use your calculator to work out tan 50°. Give your answer to 3 decimal places.1.192
    Working: tan 50° = 1.19175…, which is 1.192 to 3 decimal places. (tan can be bigger than 1, but sin and cos never are.)
    17Your calculator says cos 60 = −0.952. You know cos 60° should be 0.5. What is wrong? [Figure: Triangle with angle 35 degrees and hypotenuse 12 centimetres]The calculator is in radian mode, so change it to degrees.
    Working: The calculator is measuring in radians. Switch it to degrees (DEG) and cos 60 gives 0.5.
    18Find the length x. The angle is 30° and the hypotenuse is 10 m. Give your answer in metres. [Figure: Triangle with angle 30 degrees and hypotenuse 10 metres]5
    Working: sin 30° = x/10, so x = 10 × sin 30° = 10 × 0.5 = 5 m.
    19Find the length x to 1 decimal place. The angle is 20° and the side adjacent to the angle is 15 m. [Figure: Triangle with angle 20 degrees and adjacent side 15 metres]5.5
    Working: tan 20° = x/15, so x = 15 × tan 20° = 15 × 0.3640… = 5.459…, which is 5.5 m to 1 decimal place.
    20Find the length x to 1 decimal place. The angle is 62° and the hypotenuse is 9.5 cm. [Figure: Triangle with angle 62 degrees and hypotenuse 9.5 centimetres]4.5
    Working: cos 62° = x/9.5, so x = 9.5 × cos 62° = 9.5 × 0.4695… = 4.46…, which is 4.5 cm.
    21Find the length x to 1 decimal place. The angle is 28° and the side opposite the angle is 7 cm. The side x is the hypotenuse. [Figure: Triangle with angle 28 degrees and opposite side 7 centimetres]14.9
    Working: sin 28° = 7/x, so x = 7 ÷ sin 28° = 7 ÷ 0.4695… = 14.91…, which is 14.9 cm. (The hypotenuse is longest, and 14.9 is longer than 7. ✓)
    22The hypotenuse is 15 and the angle is 40°. The side x is adjacent to the angle. Which equation gives the length x? [Figure: Triangle with angle 40 degrees and hypotenuse 15]x = 15 cos 40°
    Working: cos 40° = x/15, so x = 15 × cos 40°.
    23A 4 m ladder leans against a wall. It makes an angle of 70° with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach? Give your answer in metres, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Ladder against a wall]3.8
    Working: sin 70° = h/4, so h = 4 × sin 70° = 4 × 0.9397… = 3.76…, which is 3.8 m.
    24sin x = 0.5. Use your calculator to find the angle x, in degrees.30
    Working: x = sin^−1(0.5) = 30°.
    25Find the angle x to 1 decimal place. The side opposite x is 3 and the side adjacent to x is 4. [Figure: Triangle with opposite 3 and adjacent 4]36.9
    Working: tan x = 3/4 = 0.75, so x = tan^−1(0.75) = 36.87…°, which is 36.9°.
    26A wheelchair ramp is 6 m long (the sloping surface) and rises 0.5 m. What angle does it make with the ground? Give your answer in degrees, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Wheelchair ramp as a triangle]4.8
    Working: sin x = 0.5/6 = 0.0833…, so x = sin^−1(0.0833…) = 4.78…°, which is 4.8°.
    27Find the angle x to the nearest degree. The side adjacent to x is 8 cm and the hypotenuse is 13 cm. [Figure: Triangle with adjacent 8 centimetres and hypotenuse 13 centimetres]52
    Working: cos x = 8/13 = 0.6153…, so x = cos^−1(0.6153…) = 52.02…°, which is 52° to the nearest degree.
    28Maya types sin^−1(9 ÷ 6) into her calculator and gets an error. Why?The opposite side cannot be longer than the hypotenuse.
    Working: sin x = opposite ÷ hypotenuse, and the hypotenuse is the longest side. So the ratio is always less than 1. 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5 means the sides are wrong, so check them again.
    29A roof rises 2.1 m over a horizontal distance of 4.5 m. What is the angle of the roof (its pitch) to the horizontal? Give your answer in degrees, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Roof pitch as a triangle]25
    Working: tan x = 2.1/4.5 = 0.4666…, so x = tan^−1(0.4666…) = 25.0° to 1 decimal place. The roof pitch is 25.0°, so type 25.
    30From the top of a cliff you look down at a boat. The angle of depression of the boat is 20°. Which angle is also 20°?The angle of elevation of the cliff top, seen from the boat
    Working: The horizontal at the top of the cliff is parallel to the sea, so the angle of depression equals the angle of elevation seen from the boat (alternate angles).
    31Sam stands 20 m from a tree. The angle of elevation of the top of the tree is 40°. How tall is the tree to 1 decimal place? [Figure: Angle of elevation to the top of a tree]16.8
    Working: tan 40° = h/20, so h = 20 × tan 40° = 20 × 0.8390… = 16.78…, which is 16.8 m.
    32A drone is 60 m above the ground. From the drone, the angle of depression of a person on the ground is 32°. How far is the person from the point directly below the drone? Give your answer in metres, to the nearest metre. [Figure: Angle of depression from a drone]96
    Working: The angle at the person is 32°. tan 32° = 60/d, so d = 60 ÷ tan 32° = 60 ÷ 0.6248… = 96.0…, which is 96 m.
    33A skate ramp is 3 m long along its slope and makes an angle of 18° with the ground. How high is the top of the ramp? Give your answer in metres, to 2 decimal places. [Figure: Skate ramp as a triangle]0.93
    Working: sin 18° = h/3, so h = 3 × sin 18° = 3 × 0.3090… = 0.927…, which is 0.93 m to 2 decimal places.
    34Ali's eyes are 1.6 m above the ground. He stands 25 m from a building and looks at the top. The angle of elevation is 50°. How tall is the building, to 1 decimal place? [Figure: Angle of elevation to the top of a building from eye level]31.4
    Working: tan 50° = h/25, so h = 25 × tan 50° = 25 × 1.1917… = 29.79 m above his eyes. Add 1.6 m: 29.79 + 1.6 = 31.39, which is 31.4 m.
    35sin x = 0.8. Find x in degrees, to 1 decimal place.53.1
    Working: x = sin^−1(0.8) = 53.13…°, which is 53.1°.
    36Work out cos^−1(cos 40°) without a calculator. Give your answer in degrees.40
    Working: cos^−1 undoes cos, so cos^−1(cos 40°) = 40.
    37A 5 m ladder must reach 4.5 m up a wall. What angle must it make with the ground? Give your answer in degrees, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Ladder that must reach 4.5 metres up a wall]64.2
    Working: sin x = 4.5/5 = 0.9, so x = sin^−1(0.9) = 64.16…°, which is 64.2°.
    38A ramp is 6 m long along its slope and makes an angle of 5° with the ground. How high does it rise? Give your answer in metres, to 2 decimal places.0.52
    Working: sin 5° = h/6, so h = 6 × sin 5° = 6 × 0.0871… = 0.5229…, which is 0.52 m.
    39A ramp must rise 0.3 m at an angle of 4° to the ground. How long is the sloping surface? Give your answer in metres, to 1 decimal place.4.3
    Working: sin 4° = 0.3/L, so L = 0.3 ÷ sin 4° = 0.3 ÷ 0.0697… = 4.30…, which is 4.3 m.
    40A right-angled triangle has shorter sides of 5 cm and 12 cm. Find the smallest angle, in degrees, to 1 decimal place. Then check it using the hypotenuse from Pythagoras. [Figure: Triangle with shorter sides 5 and 12]22.6
    Working: tan x = 5/12 = 0.4166…, so x = tan^−1(0.4166…) = 22.6°. Check: the hypotenuse is √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13, and sin^−1(5/13) = 22.6° ✓.
    41Write down the exact value of sin 30°. Give your answer as a fraction, like 2/3.1/2
    Working: sin 30° = opposite ÷ hypotenuse = 1/2.
    42Write down the exact value of tan 45°.1
    Working: In the 45° triangle the opposite and adjacent sides are both 1, so tan 45° = 1/1 = 1.
    43What is the exact value of tan 60°?√3
    Working: In the triangle with sides 1, √3 and 2, the side opposite 60° is √3 and the side adjacent is 1. So tan 60° = √3 ÷ 1 = √3.
    44A right-angled triangle has an angle of 60°. The adjacent side is 6 cm. Find the hypotenuse exactly, without a calculator. Give your answer in centimetres.12
    Working: cos 60° = 6/h and cos 60° = 1/2, so 1/2 = 6/h, which gives h = 12 cm.
    45A ladder 8 m long leans at 60° to the ground. What is the exact height it reaches up the wall?4√3 m
    Working: h = 8 × sin 60° = 8 × (√3)/2 = 4√3 m. (That is about 6.9 m.)
    46Show that sin 45° × cos 45° = 1/2. Type the value of the product, as a fraction like 2/3.1/2
    Working: sin 45° × cos 45° = (√2)/2 × (√2)/2 = 2/4 = 1/2.
    47Eli says: The hypotenuse is 10 cm and the angle is 40°. The opposite side is x = 10 ÷ sin 40° = 15.6 cm. What went wrong? [Figure: Eli's triangle]sin 40° = x ÷ 10, so he should multiply by 10, not divide.
    Working: sin 40° = x/10, so x = 10 × sin 40° = 6.4 cm (1 d.p.). His answer, 15.6, is bigger than the hypotenuse, which cannot happen.
    48Ria says: sin x = 0.6, so x = 0.6435. She typed sin^−1(0.6). The right answer is 36.9°. What went wrong?Her calculator was in radian mode.
    Working: In radian mode sin^−1(0.6) = 0.6435 (radians). In degree mode it is 36.9°.
    49Tom says: The opposite side is 5 and the hypotenuse is 13, so tan x = 5/13. What went wrong?tan needs the adjacent side. He should use sin.
    Working: Opposite and hypotenuse use sin: sin x = 5/13, so x = 22.6°. Tan would need the adjacent side.
    50A right-angled triangle has shorter sides of 9 cm and 12 cm. How long is the hypotenuse, in centimetres?15
    Working: 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225, and √225 = 15 cm.
    51Work out sin 25° to 3 decimal places.0.423
    Working: sin 25° = 0.42261…, which is 0.423 to 3 decimal places.
    52Find the length x to 1 decimal place. The hypotenuse is 20 cm, the angle is 36° and x is the side adjacent to the angle.16.2
    Working: cos 36° = x/20, so x = 20 × cos 36° = 20 × 0.8090… = 16.18…, which is 16.2 cm.
    53Find the angle x to the nearest degree. The side opposite x is 8 m and the side adjacent to x is 5 m.58
    Working: tan x = 8/5 = 1.6, so x = tan^−1(1.6) = 57.99…°, which is 58° to the nearest degree.
    54A triangle has a right angle and its sides are in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5. Find its smallest angle to 1 decimal place.36.9
    Working: The sides are 3, 4 and 5 (the hypotenuse is the longest). The smallest angle x is opposite 3: sin x = 3/5 = 0.6, so x = sin^−1(0.6) = 36.87…°, which is 36.9°.
    55A 6 m ladder should lean at about 75° to the ground. How far should its foot be from the wall? Give your answer in metres, to 2 decimal places. [Figure: Ladder at 75 degrees]1.55
    Working: cos 75° = d/6, so d = 6 × cos 75° = 6 × 0.2588… = 1.55…, which is 1.55 m.
    56In a right-angled triangle, tan x = 0.75 and the side adjacent to x is 12 cm. How long is the side opposite x, in centimetres?9
    Working: 0.75 = opposite/12, so opposite = 12 × 0.75 = 9 cm.
    57You know the adjacent side and the hypotenuse and want the angle. Which do you use?cos^−1
    Working: Adjacent and hypotenuse are CAH, so cos x = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse, and you find the angle with cos^−1.
    58A ramp up to a loading bay is 5.2 m long. It makes an angle of 12° with the ground. Work out the height of the loading bay. Give your answer in metres, to 3 significant figures. [Figure: Loading bay ramp]1.08
    1. M1 for sin 12° = h/5.2
    2. M1 for h = 5.2 × sin 12°
    3. A1 for h = 1.08 m to 3 significant figures
    Working: sin 12° = h/5.2. h = 5.2 × sin 12° = 5.2 × 0.2079… = 1.081…. So h = 1.08 m to 3 significant figures.

    59A roof is 8.4 m wide. Each rafter slopes up from the wall at 35° to the horizontal, and the two rafters meet in the middle. Work out the length of one rafter. Give your answer in metres, to 2 decimal places. [Figure: Roof truss as a triangle]5.13
    1. M1 for half the width = 8.4 ÷ 2 = 4.2 m
    2. M1 for cos 35° = 4.2/r
    3. M1 for r = 4.2 ÷ cos 35°
    4. A1 for r = 5.13 m to 2 decimal places
    Working: Half the width = 8.4 ÷ 2 = 4.2 m. cos 35° = 4.2/r. r = 4.2 ÷ cos 35° = 4.2 ÷ 0.8191…. So r = 5.13 m to 2 decimal places.
    		4
    60From the top of a 24 m cliff, a boat is 70 m out to sea, measured along the sea level from the foot of the cliff. Work out the angle of depression of the boat from the top of the cliff. Give your answer in degrees, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Cliff and boat]18.9
    1. M1 for tan x = 24/70, using the angle at the boat (alternate angles)
    2. M1 for x = tan^−1(24/70)
    3. A1 for 18.9° to 1 decimal place
    Working: The angle at the boat is x (alternate angles), so tan x = 24/70. x = tan^−1(24/70) = 18.92…°, which is 18.9° to 1 decimal place.
    		3
    61Higher A right-angled triangle has an angle of 60° and an adjacent side of 6 cm. Work out the length of the hypotenuse without a calculator. Give your answer in centimetres.12
    1. M1 for cos 60° = 6/h
    2. M1 for cos 60° = 1/2, so 1/2 = 6/h
    3. A1 for h = 12 cm
    Working: cos 60° = 6/h. Also cos 60° = 1/2, so 1/2 = 6/h. So h = 12 cm.
    		3
    62A ramp makes an angle of 30° with the ground. A second, longer ramp is built at the same angle. Which is true?The ratio of height to sloping length is the same for both ramps.
    Working: Both ramps are right-angled triangles with a 30° angle, so they are similar. Height ÷ sloping length = sin 30° = 0.5 for both. The angle (the steepness) does not change.
    63A road has a gradient of 1 in 8: for every 8 m along (horizontally), it rises 1 m. What angle does the road make with the horizontal? Give your answer in degrees, to 1 decimal place.7.1
    Working: tan x = 1/8 = 0.125, so x = tan^−1(0.125) = 7.125…°, which is 7.1°.
    64An isosceles triangle has two equal sides of 10 cm and a base of 12 cm. Find one of the base angles, in degrees to 1 decimal place. (Hint: cut the triangle in half.) [Figure: Half of an isosceles triangle]53.1
    Working: The half-triangle has base 6 cm and hypotenuse 10 cm. cos x = 6/10 = 0.6, so x = cos^−1(0.6) = 53.13…°, which is 53.1°.
    65Higher For an acute angle x, sin x = 5/13. Use sin²x + cos²x = 1 to find cos x. Give your answer as a fraction, like 2/3.12/13
    Working: sin²x = 25/169, so cos²x = 1 − 25/169 = 144/169. cos x = 12/13. (Why does the rule work? sin²x + cos²x = (o²)/(h²) + (a²)/(h²) = (o² + a²)/(h²) = (h²)/(h²) = 1, by Pythagoras.)
    66Higher From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is 30°. From a point on the ground 20 m closer to the tower, the angle of elevation of the top is 45°. How tall is the tower? Give your answer in metres, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: Tower seen from two points]27.3
    Working: tan 45° = 1, so d = h. tan 30° = 1/(√3) = h/(d + 20) = h/(h + 20). So h + 20 = h√3, which gives 20 = h(√3 − 1) and h = 20/(√3 − 1) = 27.32…, which is 27.3 m.
    67A waste pipe must fall 1 m for every 40 m it runs along, a fall of 1 in 40. What angle does the pipe make with the horizontal? Give your answer in degrees, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: A waste pipe with a 1 in 40 fall]1.4
    Working: tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent = 1 ÷ 40 = 0.025. θ = tan^−1(0.025) = 1.43..., so the angle is 1.4° (1 d.p.).
    68The rafters of a garage roof rise 1.35 m over a horizontal run of 3 m. What is the pitch of the roof, the angle to the horizontal? Give your answer in degrees, to 1 decimal place. [Figure: A pitched roof]24.2
    Working: tan θ = 1.35 ÷ 3 = 0.45. θ = tan^−1(0.45) = 24.22..., so the pitch is 24.2° (1 d.p.).
    69A ramp is 3 m long along its sloping surface and rises 0.6 m. What angle does it make with the ground? Give your answer in degrees, to 1 decimal place.11.5
    Working: sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse = 0.6 ÷ 3 = 0.2. θ = sin^−1(0.2) = 11.53..., so the angle is 11.5° (1 d.p.).
    70A waste pipe runs 5.2 m along the horizontal and slopes down at 1.5° to the horizontal. How far does it drop over the 5.2 m? Give your answer in millimetres, to the nearest millimetre.136
    1. M1 for changing 5.2 m to 5200 mm
    2. M1 for drop = 5200 × tan 1.5°
    3. A1 for 136 mm to the nearest mm
    Working: Run = 5.2 m = 5200 mm. tan 1.5° = drop ÷ 5200, so drop = 5200 × tan 1.5° = 136.17... mm. The drop is 136 mm to the nearest mm.
    		3
    71A flue pipe leaves a boiler at 40° to the horizontal and rises 0.9 m. How long is the sloping pipe, in metres? Give your answer to 2 decimal places.1.40
    Working: sin 40° = 0.9 ÷ pipe, so pipe = 0.9 ÷ sin 40° = 0.9 ÷ 0.6428 = 1.40 m (2 d.p.). (Sense check: the pipe is longer than the 0.9 m rise ✓.)
    72A length of conduit is 2.4 m long and is fixed at 35° to the horizontal. How high does it rise? Give your answer in metres, to 2 decimal places.1.38
    Working: sin 35° = height ÷ 2.4, so height = 2.4 × sin 35° = 2.4 × 0.5736 = 1.38 m (2 d.p.). (Sense check: the height is shorter than the 2.4 m conduit ✓.)
    73A service cable runs from the top of a 6 m pole to a point on the ground 8 m from the foot of the pole. What angle does the cable make with the ground? Give your answer in degrees, to 1 decimal place.36.9
    Working: tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent = 6 ÷ 8 = 0.75. θ = tan^−1(0.75) = 36.87..., so the angle is 36.9° (1 d.p.).
