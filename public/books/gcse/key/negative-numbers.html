    31What is the balance in pounds? [Figure: Vertical scale for a bank balance]75

    32A shipwreck is 30 m below sea level (−30 m). A cliff is 150 m above sea level. How many metres higher is the cliff than the wreck? [Figure: Heights above and below sea level]180

    36Ben says: “−3 − 4 = 1.” What went wrong?He did 4 − 3. He ignored the start at −3.

    37Cara says: “(−2) × (−5) = −10.” What went wrong?Negative times negative is positive.
    Working: Negative times negative is positive: (−2) × (−5) = 10.
    38Dan says: “5 − (−2) = 3.” What went wrong?He did 5 − 2, not 5 + 2.
    Working: 5 − (−2) = 5 + 2 = 7. He did 5 − 2, not 5 + 2: subtracting a negative adds.
    39A football team has a goal difference of −8. After a win, it goes up by 3. What is the goal difference now?-5
    Working: −8 + 3 = −5.
    40Work out 6 − (−6).12
    Working: 12.
    41Work out (−2) × (−2) × (−2).-8
    Working: 4 × (−2) = −8. Three negatives make a negative.
    42Which is the biggest: −0.5, −0.05 or −5?−0.05
    Working: −0.05 is closest to zero, so it is the biggest.
    43Work out −12 ÷ (−3) × (−2).-8
    Working: −12 ÷ (−3) = 4, then 4 × (−2) = −8.

    Working: 4 − (−3) = 4 + 3 = 7 degrees.

    47Work out (−2)^3.-8
    Working: 4 × (−2) = −8.
    48Two numbers multiply to give −12 and add to give 1. What is the larger of the two?4
    Working: −3 and 4: −3 × 4 = −12 and −3 + 4 = 1. The larger is 4. (This is the idea behind factorising quadratics.)
    49In this magic square every row, every column and both long diagonals add up to the same total (you are not told it). Find the number x in the bottom-left corner. [Figure: A three by three magic square with gaps]-1
    Working: The diagonal −3 + 0 + 3 = 0 shows the magic total is 0. The other diagonal is 1 + 0 + x = 0, so x = −1. Check the bottom row: −1 + (−2) + 3 = 0.
    50Take any 5 whole numbers, positive or negative. Two of them must have a difference that is a multiple of 4. (For example, −3 and 5 have difference 8.) Which is the key idea in the proof?There are only 4 possible remainders on dividing by 4, so two of the 5 numbers share one.
    Working: There are only 4 possible remainders on dividing by 4, so two of the 5 numbers share one. Write them as 4p + r and 4q + r. Their difference is 4p − 4q = 4(p − q), a multiple of 4. Negative numbers have remainders too: −3 = 4 × (−1) + 1, so −3 and 5 = 4 × 1 + 1 both have remainder 1, and 5 − (−3) = 8.
    51A student says: “−x is always negative. Check x = 3: −x = −3. Check x = 5: −x = −5. Both negative, so it is always true.” Which sentence names the flaw?x could be negative: if x = −2 then −x = 2.
    Working: x could be negative: if x = −2 then −x = −(−2) = 2, which is positive. So the claim is false, and two examples that happen to work do not prove it. A letter can stand for a negative number as well as a positive one.
