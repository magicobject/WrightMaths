    Area and perimeter: answers

    89 questions, 10 of them marked (31 marks in all). Question numbers match the page. Use your browser's Print for a copy.

    Answers for Area and perimeter
    No.QuestionAnswerMarks
    1Work out 24 × 15.360
    Working: 24 × 10 = 240 and 24 × 5 = 120. 240 + 120 = 360.
    2Two angles in a triangle are 50° and 60°. What is the third angle? Answer in degrees.70
    Working: 50 + 60 = 110, and 180 − 110 = 70. The third angle is 70°.
    3Which shape has four equal sides and four right angles? [Figure: Walking round a rectangle]Square
    Working: A square has four equal sides and four right angles. A rectangle needs only opposite sides equal, and a rhombus needs only equal sides.
    4What is the perimeter of this rectangle? Answer in cm. [Figure: A rectangle]22
    Working: 8 + 3 + 8 + 3 = 22, so the perimeter is 22 cm.
    5A square patio has sides of 7 m. What is its perimeter? Answer in m. [Figure: A square]28
    Working: 4 × 7 = 28, so the perimeter is 28 m.
    6What is the perimeter of this triangle? Answer in cm. [Figure: A triangle with three sides labelled]17
    Working: 7 + 5.5 + 4.5 = 17, so the perimeter is 17 cm.
    7A garden is a rectangle 15 m long and 9 m wide. Fencing goes all the way round the edge except for a gate gap 2 m wide. How many metres of fencing are needed?46
    Working: All the way round: 15 + 9 + 15 + 9 = 48 m. Take off the gate gap: 48 − 2 = 46 m of fencing.
    8A room is 4.2 m by 3.5 m. A carpenter fits skirting board round the walls except across a 0.9 m door gap. How many metres of skirting board does she need?14.5
    Working: All the way round: 4.2 + 3.5 + 4.2 + 3.5 = 15.4 m. Take off the door: 15.4 − 0.9 = 14.5 m.
    9A rectangle has a perimeter of 30 cm. Which pair could be its length and width? [Figure: A grid of squares]9 cm and 6 cm
    Working: 9 + 6 + 9 + 6 = 30. The other two pairs give 40 and 60.
    10Each small square is 1 cm². What is the area of the shaded shape? Answer in cm². [Figure: A grid of squares]12
    Working: 4 squares in each row and 3 rows: 4 × 3 = 12 cm².
    11A rectangle is 9 cm long and 5 cm wide. What is its area? Answer in cm². [Figure: A rectangle]45
    Working: 9 × 5 = 45, so the area is 45 cm².
    12A square has sides of 6 cm. What is its area? Answer in cm².36
    Working: 6 × 6 = 36, so the area is 36 cm².
    13A rectangle is 7 m by 4 m. What is its area?28 m²
    Working: 7 × 4 = 28 and the unit is m², so the area is 28 m².
    14A lawn is 12.5 m long and 8 m wide. What is its area? Answer in m².100
    Working: 12.5 × 8 = 100, so the area is 100 m². (12 × 8 = 96 and 0.5 × 8 = 4.)
    15A rectangle measures 10 cm by 2 cm. A square has a side of 6 cm. Both have a perimeter of 24 cm. Which has the bigger area?The square
    Working: Rectangle: 10 × 2 = 20 cm². Square: 6 × 6 = 36 cm². The square has the bigger area, even though the perimeters are equal.
    16A tabletop is 1.2 m long and 0.8 m wide. What is its area? Answer in m². [Figure: A triangle and its rectangle]0.96
    Working: 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, so the area is 0.96 m². (12 × 8 = 96, and there are two decimal places in total.)
    17What is the area of this triangle? Answer in cm². [Figure: A triangle with a dashed height]84
    Working: A = 1/2 × 14 × 12 = 1/2 × 168 = 84. The area is 84 cm².
    18A triangle has a base of 7 m and a perpendicular height of 6 m. What is its area? Answer in m².21
    Working: 7 × 6 = 42, and half of 42 is 21. The area is 21 m².
    19A boat sail is a triangle with a base of 3.2 m and a height of 4.5 m (measured at right angles to the base). What is its area? Answer in m².7.2
    Working: 3.2 × 4.5 = 14.4, and half of 14.4 is 7.2. The area is 7.2 m².
    20What is the area of this triangle? Answer in cm². [Figure: An isosceles triangle with a dashed height]60
    Working: The height is the dashed line, 12 cm. A = 1/2 × 10 × 12 = 60. The area is 60 cm².
    21This triangle has a right angle. What is its area? Answer in cm². [Figure: A right-angled triangle]27
    Working: 1/2 × 9 × 6 = 27. The area is 27 cm².
    22What is the area of this parallelogram? Answer in cm². [Figure: A parallelogram with a dashed height]66
    Working: A = 11 × 6 = 66. The area is 66 cm².
    23What is the area of this trapezium? Answer in cm². [Figure: A trapezium with a dashed height]30
    Working: 1/2 × (4 + 8) × 5 = 1/2 × 12 × 5 = 30. The area is 30 cm².
    24The side of a shed is a trapezium. The two parallel sides are 2.0 m and 2.4 m, and they are 3 m apart. What is the area of the side? Answer in m².6.6
    Working: 2.0 + 2.4 = 4.4. Half of 4.4 is 2.2, and 2.2 × 3 = 6.6. The area is 6.6 m².
    25A trapezium has parallel sides of 9 cm and 15 cm. They are 7 cm apart. What is its area? Answer in cm².84
    Working: 9 + 15 = 24, half of 24 is 12, and 12 × 7 = 84. The area is 84 cm².
    26Which is the formula for the area of a trapezium? [Figure: An L-shaped figure]1/2(a + b)h
    Working: Area of a trapezium = 1/2 × (a + b) × h, where a and b are the parallel sides and h is the distance between them.
    27All the corners are right angles. What is the area of this shape? Answer in cm². [Figure: An L-shape]33
    Working: Bottom part: 8 × 3 = 24. Top part: 3 × 3 = 9. 24 + 9 = 33. The area is 33 cm². (Check: 8 × 6 = 48, take away the missing 5 × 3 = 15, which also gives 33.)
    28This is the same shape. What is its perimeter? Answer in cm. [Figure: The same L-shape]28
    Working: Step across: 8 − 3 = 5. Step up: 6 − 3 = 3. Perimeter: 8 + 3 + 5 + 3 + 3 + 6 = 28 cm. Check: 2 × (8 + 6) = 28.
    29A lawn is 12 m by 9 m. In it there is a square pond with sides of 3 m. How many square metres of grass are there? Answer in m².99
    Working: Whole lawn: 12 × 9 = 108 m². Pond: 3 × 3 = 9 m². Grass: 108 − 9 = 99 m².
    30A picture frame is 30 cm by 20 cm on the outside. The picture inside is 24 cm by 14 cm. What is the area of the wooden frame itself? Answer in cm². [Figure: A picture frame]264
    Working: Outer: 30 × 20 = 600 cm². Picture: 24 × 14 = 336 cm². Frame: 600 − 336 = 264 cm².
    31The end wall of a house is a rectangle with a triangle on top. How many square metres of wall will be painted? [Figure: The end wall of a house]30
    Working: Rectangle: 6 × 4 = 24 m². Triangle: 1/2 × 6 × 2 = 6 m². Total: 24 + 6 = 30 m².
    32How do you find the area of a T-shape? [Figure: One square metre in centimetres]Split it into rectangles
    Working: Split the T into two rectangles, find each area and add them. Adding the sides gives the perimeter.
    33How many square centimetres are in 1 square metre? Type it like 5000.10000 (also accept: 10 000, 10,000)
    Working: 1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m² = 100 × 100 = 10000 cm² (ten thousand).
    34Change 3 m² to cm². Type it like 5000.30000
    Working: 3 × 10 000 = 30000, so 3 m² = 30000 cm².
    35Change 45 000 cm² to m².4.5
    Working: 45 000 ÷ 10 000 = 4.5, so 45 000 cm² = 4.5 m².
    36A rectangle is 3 m long and 40 cm wide. What is its area in m²?1.2
    Working: 40 cm = 0.4 m. 3 × 0.4 = 1.2, so the area is 1.2 m².
    37A rectangle is 80 cm long and 1.5 m wide. What is its area in cm²? Type it like 5000.12000 (also accept: 12 000, 12,000)
    Working: 1.5 m = 150 cm. 80 × 150 = 12000, so the area is 12000 cm².
    38Which is bigger, 2 m² or 15 000 cm²?2 m²
    Working: 15 000 cm² = 1.5 m², which is less than 2 m². So 2 m² is bigger.
    39A lawn is 9 m by 6 m. Turf costs £5.50 per m². How much does the turf for the lawn cost? Type it like 12.50.297 (also accept: 297.00)
    Working: Area = 9 × 6 = 54 m². Cost = 54 × 5.50 = 297, so £297.00.
    40A room is 4.5 m by 3.6 m. Laminate flooring costs £14.50 per m². What does it cost to cover the whole floor? Type it like 12.50.234.9 (also accept: 234.90)
    Working: Area = 4.5 × 3.6 = 16.2 m². Cost = 16.2 × 14.50 = 234.90, so £234.90.
    41A wall to be tiled is 5.4 m². Each box of tiles covers 2 m². How many boxes must be bought?3
    Working: 5.4 ÷ 2 = 2.7. You cannot buy 0.7 of a box, so round up to 3 boxes.
    42A wall is 6 m long and 2.5 m high. It needs 2 coats of paint. One litre covers 10 m² in one coat. How many litres are needed?3
    Working: Area = 6 × 2.5 = 15 m². Two coats: 15 × 2 = 30 m². Litres: 30 ÷ 10 = 3.
    43A kitchen wall 3.6 m wide and 2.5 m high is tiled. Boxes of tiles cover 1.5 m² and cost £29.50 a box. How much do the tiles cost? Type it like 12.50.177 (also accept: 177.00)
    Working: Area = 3.6 × 2.5 = 9 m². Boxes = 9 ÷ 1.5 = 6. Cost = 6 × 29.50 = 177, so £177.00.
    44A patio is 4 m by 3.5 m. It is covered with square slabs, each 50 cm by 50 cm. How many slabs are needed? [Figure: A cuboid]56
    Working: Patio: 4 × 3.5 = 14 m². One slab: 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 m². Slabs: 14 ÷ 0.25 = 56.
    45What is the volume of this cuboid? Answer in cm³. [Figure: A cuboid]72
    Working: 6 × 4 × 3 = 72, so the volume is 72 cm³.
    46A cuboid is 5 cm by 4 cm by 2 cm. What is its surface area? Answer in cm².76
    Working: lw = 5 × 4 = 20, lh = 5 × 2 = 10, wh = 4 × 2 = 8. 20 + 10 + 8 = 38, and doubling gives 76. The surface area is 76 cm².
    47This prism has a right-angled triangle for its cross-section. The triangle has a base of 5 cm and a height of 4 cm. The prism is 8 cm long. What is its volume? Answer in cm³. [Figure: A triangular prism]80
    Working: Triangle area = 1/2 × 5 × 4 = 10 cm². Volume = 10 × 8 = 80 cm³.
    48A fish tank is 60 cm long, 30 cm wide and 40 cm high. What is its volume? Answer in cm³. Type it like 5000.72000 (also accept: 72 000, 72,000)
    Working: 60 × 30 × 40 = 72000, so the volume is 72000 cm³.
    491000 cm³ is the same as 1 litre. The fish tank has a volume of 72 000 cm³. How many litres of water can it hold when it is full?72
    Working: 72 000 ÷ 1000 = 72, so the tank holds 72 litres.
    50A path is 12 m long, 1.5 m wide and 0.1 m thick, made of concrete. Concrete costs £125 per m³. How much does the concrete cost? Type it like 12.50.225 (also accept: 225.00)
    Working: Volume = 12 × 1.5 × 0.1 = 1.8 m³. Cost = 1.8 × 125 = 225, so £225.00.
    51Which unit is used for volume? [Figure: Forwards: length and width to area]m³
    Working: Volume is length × width × height, three lengths multiplied, so it is in cubic units such as m³.
    52A rectangle has an area of 54 cm² and a width of 6 cm. What is its length? Answer in cm.9
    Working: 54 = l × 6, so l = 54 ÷ 6 = 9. The length is 9 cm.
    53A square lawn has an area of 64 m². How many metres of edging go all the way round it?32
    Working: 8 × 8 = 64, so the side is 8 m. Perimeter = 4 × 8 = 32 m.
    54A rectangle has a perimeter of 36 cm and a length of 11 cm. What is its width? Answer in cm.7
    Working: Half of 36 is 18, so length + width = 18. Width = 18 − 11 = 7 cm. Check: 11 + 7 + 11 + 7 = 36.
    55A triangle has an area of 24 cm² and a base of 8 cm. What is its perpendicular height? Answer in cm.6
    Working: 1/2 × 8 = 4, so 24 = 4 × h. h = 24 ÷ 4 = 6. The height is 6 cm.
    56A parallelogram has an area of 72 cm² and a perpendicular height of 8 cm. What is its base? Answer in cm.9
    Working: 72 = b × 8, so b = 72 ÷ 8 = 9. The base is 9 cm.
    57A trapezium has an area of 45 cm². Its parallel sides are 4 cm and 11 cm. How far apart are they? Answer in cm.6
    Working: 1/2 × (4 + 11) = 7.5, so 45 = 7.5 × h. h = 45 ÷ 7.5 = 6. The height is 6 cm.
    58A cuboid has a volume of 240 cm³. It is 10 cm long and 6 cm wide. How high is it? Answer in cm.4
    Working: 10 × 6 = 60, so 240 = 60 × h. h = 240 ÷ 60 = 4. The height is 4 cm.
    59Laminate flooring costs £16 per m². A customer has £480 to spend on it. What is the largest area it can cover? Answer in m².30
    Working: 480 = area × 16, so area = 480 ÷ 16 = 30. The flooring can cover 30 m².
    60Zara says: the area of a triangle with a base of 10 cm and a height of 6 cm is 10 × 6 = 60 cm². What went wrong?She left out the half.
    Working: The triangle is half of the 10 by 6 rectangle: 1/2 × 10 × 6 = 30 cm².
    61Ben says: 1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m² = 100 cm². What is wrong?1 m² is 10 000 cm²
    Working: A square 1 m by 1 m is 100 cm by 100 cm. Its area is 100 × 100 = 10 000 cm².
    62Mia says: the perimeter of a rectangle 5 cm by 3 cm is 5 × 3 = 15 cm. What is wrong?She found the area, not the perimeter
    Working: Multiplying the sides gives the area, 15 cm². The perimeter is the distance round: 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 cm.
    63You are buying paint to cover a wall. Which measurement do you need?Area
    Working: Paint covers a flat surface, so you need the area of the wall.
    64A rectangle has a perimeter of 26 cm and a width of 4 cm. What is its length? Answer in cm.9
    Working: 26 ÷ 2 = 13, and 13 − 4 = 9. The length is 9 cm.
    65A room is 5 m by 4 m. Carpet costs £18 per m². How much does the carpet cost? Type it like 12.50.360 (also accept: 360.00)
    Working: Area = 5 × 4 = 20 m². Cost = 20 × 18 = 360, so £360.00.
    66Change 0.6 m² to cm². Type it like 5000.6000 (also accept: 6 000, 6,000)
    Working: 0.6 × 10 000 = 6000, so 0.6 m² = 6000 cm².
    67A cube has edges of 4 cm. What is its volume? Answer in cm³.64
    Working: 4 × 4 × 4 = 64, so the volume is 64 cm³.
    68A right-angled triangle has two sides next to the right angle of 9 cm and 12 cm. What is its area? Answer in cm².54
    Working: 1/2 × 9 × 12 = 54, so the area is 54 cm².
    69A garden is a rectangle 12 m by 8 m. A rectangular pond 4 m by 3 m is in the garden. The rest of the garden is lawn. Turf costs £5.50 per m². Work out the cost of the turf for the lawn. Type it like 12.50.462 (also accept: 462.00)
    1. M1 for the lawn area: 96 − 12 = 84
    2. M1 for 84 × 5.50
    3. A1 for £462.00
    Working: Garden: 12 × 8 = 96 m². Pond: 4 × 3 = 12 m². Lawn: 96 − 12 = 84 m². Cost: 84 × 5.50 = 462, so £462.00.
    		3
    70(a) [3 marks] A wall is 3.6 m wide and 2.4 m high. It is covered with square tiles with sides of 30 cm. Work out how many tiles are needed.96
    1. M1 for 360 ÷ 30 = 12 (or 240 ÷ 30 = 8)
    2. M1 for 12 × 8
    3. A1 for 96
    Working: Across: 360 ÷ 30 = 12 tiles. Up: 240 ÷ 30 = 8 tiles. Total: 12 × 8 = 96 tiles.
    		3
    71(b) [2 marks] Tiles are sold in packs of 10 at £14.99 a pack. How much do the tiles for the wall cost? Type it like 12.50.149.90 (also accept: 149.9)
    1. M1 for 10 packs (9.6 rounded up)
    2. A1 for £149.90
    Working: 96 ÷ 10 = 9.6, so 10 packs are needed. 10 × 14.99 = 149.90, so £149.90.
    		2
    72A rectangular field has a perimeter of 140 m. The length of the field is 50 m. Work out the area of the field. Answer in m².1000
    1. M1 for 140 ÷ 2 = 70
    2. M1 for 70 − 50 = 20
    3. M1 for 50 × 20
    4. A1 for 1000
    Working: Length + width = 140 ÷ 2 = 70. Width = 70 − 50 = 20 m. Area = 50 × 20 = 1000 m².
    		4
    73A concrete ramp is a prism. Its cross-section is a trapezium with parallel sides of 3 m and 5 m, 2 m apart. The ramp is 6 m long. Concrete costs £120 per m³. Show that the concrete costs £5760.Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for the cross-section area 1/2 × (3 + 5) × 2 = 8
    2. M1 for the volume 8 × 6 = 48
    3. M1 for 48 × 120
    4. C1 for £5760 stated as the final answer
    Working: Cross-section = 1/2 × (3 + 5) × 2 = 8 m². Volume = 8 × 6 = 48 m³. Cost = 48 × 120 = 5760, so the concrete costs £5760 as required.
    		4
    74A water tank is a cuboid 80 cm long and 35 cm wide. It is filled to a depth of 30 cm. 1000 cm³ is 1 litre. How many litres of water are in the tank?84
    1. M1 for 80 × 35 × 30 (= 84000)
    2. M1 for dividing by 1000
    3. A1 for 84
    Working: Volume of water = 80 × 35 × 30 = 84000 cm³. Litres = 84000 ÷ 1000 = 84.
    		3
    75A rectangle has whole-number sides in cm and a perimeter of 20 cm. What is the largest area it could have? Answer in cm². (Try 1 by 9, 2 by 8 and so on.)25
    Working: Pairs that add to 10: 1 by 9 gives 9, 2 by 8 gives 16, 3 by 7 gives 21, 4 by 6 gives 24, 5 by 5 gives 25. The largest area is 25 cm², from the square. For a fixed perimeter, the nearer to a square, the bigger the area.
    76A square has sides of 5 cm. Each side is doubled to 10 cm. How many times bigger is the new area than the old area? (Then try a rectangle of your own. Does the same thing happen?)4
    Working: Old area: 5 × 5 = 25 cm². New area: 10 × 10 = 100 cm². 100 ÷ 25 = 4. Doubling both lengths makes the area 4 times bigger (2 × 2 = 4). A cube would be 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 times bigger in volume.
    77A square lawn has a path 1 m wide all the way round the outside. The path has an area of 36 m². What is the side of the lawn? Answer in m. (Try a few lawn sizes: try 5 m and see how big the path is.)8
    Working: Try a lawn of 8 m: the outer square is 10 m. 10 × 10 = 100 and 8 × 8 = 64, so the path is 100 − 64 = 36 m². So the lawn has a side of 8 m. (Check 5 m: 49 − 25 = 24, too small. 10 m: 144 − 100 = 44, too big.)
    78Two identical trapezia fit together to make a parallelogram with base (a + b) and height h. How does that explain the area formula for a trapezium?It is half of (a + b) × h
    Working: The parallelogram has base a + b and height h, so its area is (a + b) × h. It is made of 2 identical trapezia, so one trapezium has area 1/2 × (a + b) × h.
    79A plumber is fitting underfloor heating in a bathroom 3.2 m by 2.5 m. The heating mat costs £38 per m². How much does the mat cost for the whole floor? Type it like 12.50.304 (also accept: 304.00)
    Working: Area = 3.2 × 2.5 = 8 m². Cost = 8 × 38 = 304, so £304.00.
    80A carpenter cuts a triangular gable panel with a base of 2.4 m and a height of 1.1 m at right angles to the base. What is its area? Answer in m².1.32
    Working: 2.4 × 1.1 = 2.64, and half of 2.64 is 1.32. The panel is 1.32 m².
    81An electrician runs cable trunking along all four walls of a room 5 m by 3.6 m, except across a 1 m door gap. Trunking is sold in 3 m lengths. How many lengths must be bought?6
    Working: All round: 2 × (5 + 3.6) = 17.2 m. Take off the door: 17.2 − 1 = 16.2 m. 16.2 ÷ 3 = 5.4, so round up to 6 lengths.
    82A concrete base for a boiler store is 3 m by 2.5 m and 0.1 m thick. Ready-mixed concrete costs £130 per m³. How much does the concrete cost? Type it like 12.50.97.5 (also accept: 97.50)
    Working: Volume = 3 × 2.5 × 0.1 = 0.75 m³. Cost = 0.75 × 130 = 97.50, so £97.50.
    83A plasterer boards one side of a stud wall 4.8 m long and 2.4 m high. Plasterboard sheets are 2.4 m by 1.2 m and cost £9.80 each. Work out the cost of the sheets. Type it like 12.50.39.2 (also accept: 39.20)
    1. M1 for the number of sheets: 11.52 ÷ 2.88 = 4 (or 2 × 2)
    2. M1 for 4 × 9.80
    3. A1 for £39.20
    Working: Wall: 4.8 × 2.4 = 11.52 m². Sheet: 2.4 × 1.2 = 2.88 m². Sheets: 11.52 ÷ 2.88 = 4. Cost: 4 × 9.80 = 39.20, so £39.20.
    		3
    84A plumber fits a cold water tank that is a cuboid 1.2 m long, 0.6 m wide and 0.5 m high. 1 m³ holds 1000 litres. How many litres does the tank hold when it is full?360
    1. M1 for 1.2 × 0.6 × 0.5 (= 0.36)
    2. M1 for × 1000
    3. A1 for 360
    Working: Volume = 1.2 × 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.36 m³. Litres = 0.36 × 1000 = 360.
    		3
    85A 5-a-side pitch is a rectangle 25 m long and 15 m wide. What is the perimeter of the pitch, in metres? [Figure: A 5-a-side pitch]80
    Working: 25 + 15 + 25 + 15 = 80 m. (Or 2 × (25 + 15) = 2 × 40 = 80 m.)
    86The same pitch is 25 m by 15 m. What is its area, in square metres?375
    Working: 25 × 15 = 250 + 125 = 375 m².
    87Artificial turf costs £6.40 per square metre. The pitch is 25 m by 15 m. How many pounds does it cost to cover the whole pitch? Type it like 12.50.2400.00 (also accept: 2400)
    Working: Area = 25 × 15 = 375 m². Cost = 375 × 6.40 = £2400.00.
    88The pitch is 25 m by 15 m. Each end has a rectangular goal area 10 m wide and 4 m deep, painted a different colour. What is the area of the pitch outside the two goal areas, in square metres? [Figure: A 5-a-side pitch with two goal areas]295
    1. M1 for 25 × 15 = 375
    2. M1 for 2 × (10 × 4) = 80
    3. A1 for 375 − 80 = 295
    Working: Whole pitch: 25 × 15 = 375 m². One goal area: 10 × 4 = 40 m², so two are 80 m². Outside the goal areas: 375 − 80 = 295 m².
    		3
    89A club puts fencing round the 25 m by 15 m pitch, but leaves a 3 m gap for a gate on one side. Fencing costs £12.50 per metre. How many pounds does the fencing cost? Type it like 12.50.962.50 (also accept: 962.5)
    1. M1 for the perimeter 80 m
    2. M1 for 80 − 3 = 77 m
    3. A1 for 77 × 12.50 = 962.50
    Working: Perimeter = 2 × (25 + 15) = 80 m. Fencing needed: 80 − 3 = 77 m. Cost: 77 × 12.50 = £962.50.
    		3
