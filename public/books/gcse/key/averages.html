Answers for Averages and spread
No.QuestionAnswerMarks
1Work out 84 ÷ 7.12
Working: 7 × 12 = 84, so 84 ÷ 7 = 12.
2Work out 23 + 17 + 35 + 25.100
Working: 23 + 17 = 40 and 35 + 25 = 60, so the total is 100.
3Work out 45 ÷ 4. Give your answer as a decimal.11.25
Working: 45 ÷ 4 = 11 remainder 1, and 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25, so the answer is 11.25.
4Work out 1/2 of 9.4.5
Working: 9 ÷ 2 = 4.5.
5Find the mean of 4, 7, 9 and 12.8
Working: 4 + 7 + 9 + 12 = 32, and 32 ÷ 4 = 8.
6A plumber’s weekly materials bills were £42, £56, £38 and £64. What was the mean weekly bill? Give your answer in pounds.50
Working: 42 + 56 + 38 + 64 = 200, and 200 ÷ 4 = £50.
7A team scored 3, 1, 4, 2, 0 and 3 goals in six games. What is the mean number of goals? Give your answer to 1 decimal place.2.2
Working: 3 + 1 + 4 + 2 + 0 + 3 = 13, and 13 ÷ 6 = 2.1666… ≈ 2.2.
8Kai scored 120, 95, 140, 105 and 110 points in five rounds of a game. What was his mean score?114
Working: 120 + 95 + 140 + 105 + 110 = 570, and 570 ÷ 5 = 114.
9Which calculation finds the mean of 6, 10, 8 and 12?(6 + 10 + 8 + 12) ÷ 4
Working: The total is 6 + 10 + 8 + 12 = 36 and there are 4 numbers, so the mean is 36 ÷ 4 = 9. The brackets make the adding happen first.
10Find the median of 9, 3, 7, 1, 5.5
Working: In order: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. The middle value is 5.
11Find the median of 12, 5, 9, 14, 7, 11.10
Working: In order: 5, 7, 9, 11, 12, 14. The middle two are 9 and 11, so the median is (9 + 11) ÷ 2 = 10.
12A shop sold these shoe sizes in one hour: 6, 8, 7, 6, 9, 6, 8, 7. What is the mode?6
Working: 6 appears 3 times, 7 and 8 twice each and 9 once. The mode is 6.
13Noon temperatures over five days were 4, 9, −2, 6 and 3 degrees Celsius. What is the range?11
Working: Largest 9, smallest −2. Range = 9 − (−2) = 11 degrees.
14The times, in seconds, for six runs of a game level were 62, 48, 55, 48, 70 and 48. What is the median time?51.5
Working: In order: 48, 48, 48, 55, 62, 70. The 3rd and 4th are 48 and 55, so the median is (48 + 55) ÷ 2 = 51.5 seconds.
15Which sentence about the range is true?It tells you how spread out the data are
Working: The range is the largest value minus the smallest, so it measures spread.
16The table shows how many brothers and sisters 20 students have. What is the mode?1
Working: The biggest frequency is 9, for 1 brother or sister. The mode is 1.
17Use the same table to find the mean number of brothers and sisters. Give your answer as a decimal.1.05
Working: 0 × 6 + 1 × 9 + 2 × 3 + 3 × 2 = 0 + 9 + 6 + 6 = 21. The mean is 21 ÷ 20 = 1.05.
18The table shows the texts sent by 20 students in one hour. What is the median number of texts?3
Working: The first 7 values are 1, the next 2 are 2 (reaching 9), the next 3 are 3 (reaching 12). The 10th and 11th values are both 3, so the median is 3.
19Use the same table to find the mean number of texts. Give your answer as a decimal.2.8
Working: 1 × 7 + 2 × 2 + 3 × 3 + 4 × 4 + 5 × 4 = 7 + 4 + 9 + 16 + 20 = 56. The mean is 56 ÷ 20 = 2.8.
20In a frequency table, the biggest frequency is 12 and it belongs to the value 3. What is the mode?3, because it has the biggest frequency
Working: The value 3 happens 12 times, more than any other value. The mode is 3.
21Five workers earn £400, £420, £410, £430 and £1740 a week. Which average best shows the typical pay?The median
Working: In order: 400, 410, 420, 430, 1740. The median is £420, which is close to what four of the five earn. The mean (£680) is dragged up by the £1740.
22Work out the mean of the five weekly pays in the last question: £400, £420, £410, £430 and £1740. Give your answer in pounds.680
Working: 400 + 420 + 410 + 430 + 1740 = 3400, and 3400 ÷ 5 = £680. This is much higher than most of the pays.
23A clothes shop wants to know which T-shirt size to order the most of. Which average should it use?The mode
Working: The shop needs the size that is bought most often, which is the mode.
24Which average can you find for the colours of cars in a car park (red, blue, white, black…)?The mode only
Working: Colours are not numbers, so you cannot find a mean or a median. You can count them and find the most common one, which is the mode.
25The mean of five numbers is 20. A sixth number, 50, is added. What is the new mean?25
Working: The first five numbers add to 5 × 20 = 100. The new total is 100 + 50 = 150. The new mean is 150 ÷ 6 = 25.
26Ali scored 5, 8, 7, 6, 4 in five games. Bea scored 6, 6, 7, 5, 6. Both have a mean of 6. Who is more consistent? Type Ali or Bea.bea
Working: Ali: range 8 − 4 = 4. Bea: range 7 − 5 = 2. Bea has the smaller range, so Bea is more consistent. The answer is Bea.
27Daily takings (in pounds) at two cafes. Cafe A: 210, 250, 190, 260, 240. Cafe B: 200, 320, 150, 310, 220. What is the difference between the two ranges? Give your answer in pounds.100
Working: Cafe A range: 260 − 190 = 70. Cafe B range: 320 − 150 = 170. Difference: 170 − 70 = £100.
28Team A scores have a mean of 24 and a range of 8. Team B scores have a mean of 21 and a range of 3. Which statement is correct?A scored more on average, but B was more consistent
Working: A’s mean (24) is bigger than B’s (21), so A scored more on average. B’s range (3) is smaller than A’s (8), so B was more consistent.
29Pippa ran 100 m in 12.1, 12.4, 11.9, 12.6 and 12.0 seconds. What is her mean time, in seconds? [Figure: Bar chart of favourite sports]12.2
Working: 12.1 + 12.4 + 11.9 + 12.6 + 12.0 = 61.0, and 61.0 ÷ 5 = 12.2 seconds.
30How many students chose swimming? [Figure: Bar chart of favourite sports]11
Working: The swimming bar reaches 11 on the scale, so 11 students chose swimming.
31How many more students chose football than tennis? [Figure: Bar chart of favourite sports]8
Working: Football 14, tennis 6. The difference is 14 − 6 = 8 students.
32How many cups of tea were sold on Tuesday? [Figure: Pictogram of cups of tea sold]10
Working: One symbol is 4 cups. Tuesday has 2 full symbols and a half: 2 × 4 + 2 = 10 cups.
33How many cups of tea were sold on the four days altogether? [Figure: Pictogram of cups of tea sold]44
Working: Monday 3 × 4 = 12, Tuesday 10, Wednesday 4 × 4 = 16, Thursday 1.5 × 4 = 6. Total 12 + 10 + 16 + 6 = 44 cups.
34The pie chart shows how 36 students travel to school. How many students come by bus? [Figure: Pie chart of how 36 students travel to school]12
Working: 360 ÷ 36 = 10° for each student. Bus: 120 ÷ 10 = 12 students.
35In a survey of 40 students, 7 chose cycling. In a pie chart of the 40 students, what angle in degrees is the cycling sector? [Figure: Scatter graph of revision and test score with a line of best fit]63
Working: 360 ÷ 40 = 9° for each student. 7 × 9 = 63°.
36What type of correlation does the graph show? [Figure: Scatter graph of revision and test score with a line of best fit]Positive
Working: The points slope up to the right: more revision goes with a higher score. This is positive correlation.
37Use the line of best fit to estimate the test score for a student who revised for 6 hours. (Accept a small reading error.) [Figure: Scatter graph of revision and test score with a line of best fit]66 (also accept: 64, 65, 67, 68)
Working: Going up from 6 hours to the line, then across, gives about 66 marks.
38Use the line of best fit to estimate how many hours of revision go with a test score of 60. (Accept a small reading error.) [Figure: Scatter graph of revision and test score with a line of best fit]5 (also accept: 4.5, 5.5)
Working: From 60 on the score axis, go across to the line, then down: about 5 hours.
39Alex revised for 15 hours, which is off the right-hand end of the graph. Why is it risky to use the line to predict his score? [Figure: Scatter graph of revision and test score with a line of best fit]15 hours is outside the data, so the trend may stop
Working: The graph only shows 0 to 10 hours. Beyond that we do not know if the pattern continues (a score cannot go above 100, for one thing). Using the line there is extrapolation and is unreliable.
40The graph shows the age and value of used cars. Use the line of best fit to estimate the value, in thousands of pounds, of a car that is 5 years old. (Accept a small reading error.) [Figure: Scatter graph of car age and value with a line of best fit]7 (also accept: 6.5, 7.5)
Working: From 5 years go up to the line, then across: about 7, so about £7000.
41Shoe size and maths test score are measured for 30 students. What correlation would you expect? [Figure: Bar chart of sales at two shops]No correlation
Working: There is no sensible link between shoe size and a maths score, so the points would be scattered: no correlation.
42What makes this graph misleading? [Figure: Bar chart of sales at two shops]The vertical axis starts at 44, not 0
Working: The scale starts at 44, so the bars are cut off and the small difference between 48 and 52 looks huge. Starting at 0 would show the two bars almost the same height.
43In the graph, Shop A sold 48 hundred and Shop B sold 52 hundred. Shop B sold what percentage more than Shop A? Give your answer to the nearest whole number, like 12%.8% (also accept: 8)
Working: 52 − 48 = 4. 4 ÷ 48 = 0.0833…, and 0.0833… × 100 = 8.33… ≈ 8%.
44Which of these does not make a graph misleading?A title and labelled axes
Working: A title and labelled axes help the reader. The other two distort the picture.
45Two pie charts are the same size. In both, half the students chose pizza. Ravi says the same number chose pizza in each class. Why can he not say that?The two classes may be different sizes, and the pie charts show no totals
Working: Half of 20 is 10 but half of 30 is 15. Without the total number of students in each class, the pie charts cannot be compared.
46What is the midpoint of the class 20 < x ≤ 30?25
Working: (20 + 30) ÷ 2 = 25.
47Estimate the mean length of these journeys, in km.17
Working: 5 × 8 = 40, 15 × 12 = 180, 25 × 6 = 150, 35 × 4 = 140. Total = 510. The estimated mean is 510 ÷ 30 = 17 km.
48Which is the modal class in the table of 30 journeys?10 ≤ d < 20
Working: The biggest frequency is 12, which belongs to the class 10 ≤ d < 20. That is the modal class.
49Why is the mean from a grouped table only an estimate?The exact values are not known, only the class
Working: We only know which class each value is in, not its exact size. Using the midpoint is a sensible guess, but it is still a guess.
50A grouped table has these classes and frequencies: 0 < t ≤ 20 (3), 20 < t ≤ 40 (7), 40 < t ≤ 60 (6), 60 < t ≤ 80 (4). Estimate the mean.41
Working: 10 × 3 = 30, 30 × 7 = 210, 50 × 6 = 300, 70 × 4 = 280. Total = 820. The estimated mean is 820 ÷ 20 = 41.
51The mean of 4 numbers is 12. What is their total?48
Working: 4 × 12 = 48.
52The mean of 5 numbers is 8. Four of the numbers are 6, 9, 7 and 10. What is the fifth number?8
Working: Total of five numbers = 5 × 8 = 40. The four known add to 6 + 9 + 7 + 10 = 32. The fifth is 40 − 32 = 8.
53Over four weeks a gamer’s mean spend was £15 a week. In the first three weeks he spent £12, £18 and £9. How much did he spend in week 4? Give your answer in pounds.21
Working: 4 × 15 = 60 for four weeks. The first three weeks were 12 + 18 + 9 = 39. Week 4 = 60 − 39 = £21.
54Jo’s mean mark after 4 tests is 62. What must she score in her fifth test to raise her mean to 65?77
Working: 5 × 65 = 325 marks needed in total. So far 4 × 62 = 248. She needs 325 − 248 = 77.
55The mean of 3 numbers is 10. Two of them are 4 and 8. Which calculation finds the third?30 − (4 + 8)
Working: The total is 3 × 10 = 30, so the third number is 30 − (4 + 8) = 18.
56The mean of 8 numbers is 5. A ninth number is added, and the mean of all 9 numbers becomes 6. What is the ninth number?14
Working: First 8: 8 × 5 = 40. All 9: 9 × 6 = 54. The ninth number is 54 − 40 = 14.
57Sam says: the median of 7, 2, 9, 4, 5 is 9, because it is the middle number in the list. What went wrong?He did not put the numbers in order first
Working: In order: 2, 4, 5, 7, 9. The middle value is 5, not 9.
58Dee says: in this frequency table, the mode is 9. The values are 1, 2, 3 with frequencies 4, 9, 2. What went wrong?She gave the frequency. The mode is 2
Working: The value 2 has frequency 9, the biggest. So the mode is 2. The 9 is how many times it occurred.
59Pat says: the range of 3, 8 and 12 is “3 to 12”. What went wrong?The range is one number: 12 − 3 = 9
Working: The range is the largest minus the smallest: 12 − 3 = 9.
60Find the mean of 12.5, 7.5 and 10.10
Working: 12.5 + 7.5 + 10 = 30, and 30 ÷ 3 = 10.
61Find the median of 1.2, 0.8, 1.5 and 0.9.1.05
Working: In order: 0.8, 0.9, 1.2, 1.5. The middle two are 0.9 and 1.2, so the median is (0.9 + 1.2) ÷ 2 = 1.05.
62Find the range of 15, −3, 8 and 0.18
Working: Largest 15, smallest −3. Range = 15 − (−3) = 18.
63Four overnight temperatures were −2, 4, −6 and 8 degrees. What is the mean temperature?1
Working: −2 + 4 − 6 + 8 = 4, and 4 ÷ 4 = 1 degree.
64In a survey of 30 students, 12 chose swimming. In a pie chart, what angle in degrees is the swimming sector?144
Working: 360 ÷ 30 = 12° for each student. 12 × 12 = 144°.
65A pie chart sector has an angle of 90°. What percentage of the whole is the sector? Give your answer like 12%.25% (also accept: 25)
Working: 90/360 = 1/4 = 25%.
66An electrician records the cable used on five jobs: 12 m, 18 m, 9 m, 15 m and 21 m. What is the mean length of cable used, in metres?15
Working: 12 + 18 + 9 + 15 + 21 = 75, and 75 ÷ 5 = 15 m.
67A plumber’s call-out costs in one week were £65, £80, £65, £120 and £95. What is the mean call-out cost, in pounds? Type it like 42.75.85.00 (also accept: 85)
  1. M1 for 65 + 80 + 65 + 120 + 95 = 425
  2. A1 for £85.00
Working: 65 + 80 + 65 + 120 + 95 = 425, and 425 ÷ 5 = £85.00.

68A carpenter cuts six shelves. Their lengths in cm are 80, 82, 79, 81, 80 and 78. What is the range of the lengths, in cm?4
Working: Longest 82 cm, shortest 78 cm. Range = 82 − 78 = 4 cm.
69An electrician’s mean time on four jobs was 2.5 hours. The first three jobs took 2, 3 and 1.5 hours. How long did the fourth job take, in hours?3.5
Working: Total for four jobs = 4 × 2.5 = 10 hours. The first three add to 2 + 3 + 1.5 = 6.5. The fourth took 10 − 6.5 = 3.5 hours.
70A builders’ merchant sold 38, 41, 40, 39 and 142 bags of cement on five days. The 142 was one huge order. Which average best shows a typical day?The median
Working: In order: 38, 39, 40, 41, 142. The median is 40 bags, a typical day. The mean (60) is dragged up by the huge order.
71A £720 bathroom budget is shown on a pie chart. The sector for tiles and materials is 150°. How many pounds is that?300
Working: 150/360 of 720: 720 ÷ 360 = 2 pounds for each degree, so 150 × 2 = £300.
72Work out the mean number of bedrooms.2.6
  1. M1 for the products 4, 18, 36, 20 (or at least three correct)
  2. M1 for 78 ÷ 30
  3. A1 for 2.6
Working: 1 × 4 + 2 × 9 + 3 × 12 + 4 × 5 = 4 + 18 + 36 + 20 = 78. The mean is 78 ÷ 30 = 2.6.

73The mean of five numbers is 12. Four of the numbers are 9, 15, 11 and 14. Work out the fifth number.11
  1. M1 for 5 × 12 = 60
  2. M1 for 60 − (9 + 15 + 11 + 14)
  3. A1 for 11
Working: Total of five numbers = 5 × 12 = 60. The four known add to 9 + 15 + 11 + 14 = 49. The fifth number is 60 − 49 = 11.

74Team A scored 12, 15, 9, 14 and 10 points in five games. Team B scored 8, 20, 6, 18 and 8. Show that the two teams have the same mean score, but that Team A is more consistent.Written answer: see the mark scheme
  1. M1 for finding a total and dividing by 5 for either team
  2. M1 for finding the range of either team (largest minus smallest)
  3. A1 for both means 12, and both ranges 6 and 14
  4. C1 for a conclusion: same mean, but A has the smaller range so is more consistent
Working: Team A: 12 + 15 + 9 + 14 + 10 = 60, mean = 60 ÷ 5 = 12. Team B: 8 + 20 + 6 + 18 + 8 = 60, mean = 60 ÷ 5 = 12. The means are the same. Team A range = 15 − 9 = 6. Team B range = 20 − 6 = 14. The means are equal, and Team A has the smaller range (6 < 14), so Team A is more consistent.

75Higher [4 marks] The grouped table shows the times taken by 30 people to complete a task. Work out an estimate for the mean time.19
  1. M1 for midpoints 5, 15, 25 and 35
  2. M1 for f × midpoint (at least three correct)
  3. M1 for 570 ÷ 30
  4. A1 for 19
Working: Midpoints: 5, 15, 25, 35. f × midpoint: 25, 180, 225, 140. Total = 570. Estimated mean = 570 ÷ 30 = 19 minutes.

76A pie chart shows how 45 club members choose an activity. 18 members chose swimming. Work out the angle for swimming in the pie chart, in degrees.144
  1. M1 for 360 ÷ 45 = 8
  2. M1 for 18 × 8
  3. A1 for 144
Working: 360 ÷ 45 = 8° for each member. Swimming: 18 × 8 = 144°.
3
7710 students in class A have a mean score of 12. 15 students in class B have a mean score of 16. What is the mean score of all 25 students together? (The answer is not 14, the mean of 12 and 16. Why?)14.4
Working: Class A total: 10 × 12 = 120. Class B total: 15 × 16 = 240. All together: 360 for 25 students. Mean = 360 ÷ 25 = 14.4. It is closer to 16 than to 12 because there are more students in class B.
78Five whole numbers, in order, have mode 4, median 5, mean 6 and range 6. What is the largest number?10
Working: The median (3rd number) is 5, and the mode 4 needs two 4s, so the first two numbers are 4 and 4. The range is 6, so the largest is 4 + 6 = 10. The total is 30, so the 4th number is 30 − (4 + 4 + 5 + 10) = 7. The numbers are 4, 4, 5, 7, 10.
79Five numbers have a mean of 8. Every number is multiplied by 3 and then 2 is added to each. What is the new mean? (Does the range change in the same way?)26
Working: Total = 5 × 8 = 40. After × 3 the total is 120. Adding 2 to each of 5 numbers adds 10, so the new total is 130 and the new mean is 130 ÷ 5 = 26. This is 3 × 8 + 2 = 26: the same operations as on each number. The range is not changed the same way: adding 2 to every value leaves the range alone, and multiplying by 3 triples it.
