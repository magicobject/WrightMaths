    1Non-calculator Grade 4 Write 0.35 as a fraction in its simplest form. Type your answer like 3/8. Uses: Place value Uses: Fractions7/20
    1. M1 for 35/100
    2. A1 for 7/20
    Working: 0.35 = 35/100. Divide the top and bottom by 5: 35 ÷ 5 = 7 and 100 ÷ 5 = 20. Answer: 7/20.

    2Non-calculator Grade 4 Work out 2 1/3 + 1 3/4. Give your answer as an improper fraction, like 11/6. Uses: Fractions49/12
    1. M1 for both mixed numbers written as improper fractions, 7/3 and 7/4
    2. M1 for a common denominator of 12, such as 28/12 and 21/12
    3. A1 for 49/12
    Working: 2 1/3 = 7/3 and 1 3/4 = 7/4. The common denominator is 12: 7/3 = 28/12 and 7/4 = 21/12. 28/12 + 21/12 = 49/12. Answer: 49/12.

    3Calculator Grade 5 In a sale, a set of spanners is reduced by 30%. The sale price is £84. Show that the original price was £120. Uses: PercentagesWritten answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for 70% (or a multiplier of 0.7) linked to £84
    2. M1 for 84 ÷ 0.7 (or 84 ÷ 70 × 100)
    3. C1 for 120, with the check or a clear conclusion
    Working: The price is reduced by 30%, so the sale price is 100% − 30% = 70% of the original. 70% = £84, so 1% = 84 ÷ 70 = £1.20, and 100% = £120. Check: 30% of 120 = 36 and 120 − 36 = 84, so the original price was £120, as required.
    		3
    4Non-calculator Grade 5 By rounding each number to 1 significant figure, work out an estimate for (4.87 × 21.2)/0.52. You must show your working. Uses: Place value Uses: Four operations200
    1. M1 for 5, 20 and 0.5
    2. M1 for 5 × 20 ÷ 0.5, or 100 ÷ 0.5
    3. A1 for 200
    Working: 4.87 ≈ 5, 21.2 ≈ 20 and 0.52 ≈ 0.5. (5 × 20)/0.5 = 100 ÷ 0.5 = 200. The estimate is 200.

    5Calculator Grade 6 A carpenter puts £2500 into a savings account. The account pays compound interest of 4% a year. How much is in the account after 3 years? Type your answer in pounds, like 1234.56. Uses: Percentage change2812.16
    1. M1 for the multiplier 1.04
    2. M1 for 2500 × 1.04³
    3. A1 for 2812.16
    Working: Multiplier for +4% = 1.04. After 3 years: 2500 × 1.04³ = 2500 × 1.124864 = 2812.16. The account holds £2812.16.

    6Non-calculator Grade 4 A plumber and her apprentice share £360 in the ratio 5 : 4. Work out how much the apprentice gets. Type the number of pounds. Uses: Ratio160
    1. M1 for 360 ÷ (5 + 4)
    2. M1 for 4 × (their 40)
    3. A1 for 160
    Working: 5 + 4 = 9 parts, so one part = 360 ÷ 9 = £40. The apprentice has 4 parts: 4 × 40 = £160. Check: the plumber gets 5 × 40 = £200, and 200 + 160 = 360.
    		3
    7Calculator Grade 4 A shop sells paint in two tins. A 2.5 litre tin costs £14.50. A 4 litre tin costs £22.00. Which tin is the better value for money? You must show your working. Uses: Ratio Uses: Four operationsWritten answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for 14.50 ÷ 2.5 (= 5.80)
    2. M1 for 22 ÷ 4 (= 5.50)
    3. C1 for choosing the 4 litre tin, with a comparison of the two prices per litre
    Working: 2.5 litre tin: 14.50 ÷ 2.5 = £5.80 per litre. 4 litre tin: 22.00 ÷ 4 = £5.50 per litre. The 4 litre tin costs less per litre (£5.50 is less than £5.80), so it is the better value.
    		3
    8Calculator Grade 5 A recipe for 15 pancakes uses 240 g of flour. Work out how much flour is needed for 25 pancakes. Type the number of grams. Uses: Ratio400
    1. M1 for 240 ÷ 15 (= 16)
    2. M1 for (their 16) × 25
    3. A1 for 400
    Working: 240 ÷ 15 = 16 g of flour for 1 pancake. 16 × 25 = 400 g. Answer: 400 g.
    		3
    9Calculator Grade 5 A builder mixes cement, sand and gravel in the ratio 1 : 2 : 5 by mass. He makes 2400 kg of the mix. Cement costs £0.22 per kg, sand costs £0.04 per kg and gravel costs £0.03 per kg. Work out the total cost of the cement, sand and gravel. Type the number of pounds. Uses: Ratio135
    1. M1 for 2400 ÷ 8 (= 300)
    2. A1 for 300, 600 and 1500 kg
    3. M1 for multiplying each mass by its cost per kg
    4. A1 for 135
    Working: 1 + 2 + 5 = 8 parts, so one part = 2400 ÷ 8 = 300 kg. Cement 300 kg, sand 600 kg, gravel 1500 kg. Cost = 300 × 0.22 + 600 × 0.04 + 1500 × 0.03 = 66 + 24 + 45 = £135.
    		4
    10Calculator Grade 6 A paint store has red tins and blue tins in the ratio 3 : 7. There are 12 more blue tins than red tins. Work out the total number of tins in the store. Uses: Ratio30
    1. M1 for 7 − 3 = 4 parts, equal to 12 tins
    2. M1 for 12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 10 parts in total
    3. A1 for 30
    Working: The difference is 7 − 3 = 4 parts, and 4 parts = 12 tins, so one part = 3 tins. There are 3 + 7 = 10 parts altogether: 10 × 3 = 30 tins. Check: red is 9, blue is 21, and 21 − 9 = 12.
    		3
    11Non-calculator Grade 4 Work out the value of 5x² − 3x when x = −2. Uses: Algebra basics Uses: Negative numbers26
    1. M1 for 5 × 4 (or 20), or for −3 × −2 = +6
    2. A1 for 26
    Working: 5 × (−2)² − 3 × (−2) = 5 × 4 + 6 = 20 + 6 = 26. Answer: 26.

    12Non-calculator Grade 4 Solve 5(x − 2) = 3x + 8. Uses: Solving equations Uses: Algebra basics9
    1. M1 for 5x − 10
    2. M1 for collecting terms, such as 2x = 18
    3. A1 for 9
    Working: 5x − 10 = 3x + 8 2x − 10 = 8 2x = 18, so x = 9. Check: 5 × (9 − 2) = 35 and 3 × 9 + 8 = 35.

    13Non-calculator Grade 5 Here are the first four terms of a sequence: 5, 9, 13, 17. Show that 101 is a term in this sequence. Uses: SequencesWritten answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for a rule of 4n + 1 (or 4n + c with a correct method for c)
    2. M1 for setting 4n + 1 = 101 and solving, to get n = 25
    3. C1 for stating that n = 25 is a whole number, so 101 is the 25th term
    Working: The terms go up by 4 each time, so the nth term is 4n + something. When n = 1, 4n = 4, so we add 1: the nth term is 4n + 1. Set 4n + 1 = 101. Then 4n = 100 and n = 25. 25 is a whole number, so 101 is a term in the sequence. It is the 25th term.
    		3
    14Calculator Grade 5 A plumber charges a call-out fee of £40 plus £36 for each hour of work. A job costs £220. For how many hours did the plumber work? Uses: Solving equations5
    1. M1 for 220 − 40 (= 180), or for 40 + 36h = 220
    2. M1 for 180 ÷ 36
    3. A1 for 5
    Working: Let h be the number of hours. Then 40 + 36h = 220. 36h = 220 − 40 = 180. h = 180 ÷ 36 = 5 hours.
    		3
    15Calculator Grade 6 A rectangular worktop has length (x + 5) cm and width (x − 1) cm. Its perimeter is 40 cm. Work out the area of the worktop. Type the number only. Uses: Solving equations Uses: Area and perimeter91
    1. M1 for an expression for the perimeter, such as 2(x + 5) + 2(x − 1) = 40
    2. M1 for solving to get x = 8
    3. M1 for length 13 and width 7, multiplied together
    4. A1 for 91
    Working: Perimeter = 2(x + 5) + 2(x − 1) = 4x + 8. 4x + 8 = 40, so 4x = 32 and x = 8. Length = 8 + 5 = 13 cm and width = 8 − 1 = 7 cm. Area = 13 × 7 = 91 cm².
    		4
    16Non-calculator Grade 4 Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC. Angle A = 48°. Work out the size of angle B. You must show your working. Type the number of degrees. Uses: Shapes and angles66
    1. M1 for 180 − 48 (= 132)
    2. M1 for dividing by 2 because the base angles are equal
    3. A1 for 66
    Working: The angles in a triangle add up to 180°. Angle B + angle C = 180 − 48 = 132°. The triangle is isosceles with AB = AC, so angle B = angle C = 132 ÷ 2 = 66°.
    		3
    17Calculator Grade 5 A 6.5 m ladder leans against a vertical wall. The top of the ladder is 6 m above the ground. Show that the foot of the ladder is 2.5 m from the wall. Uses: PythagorasWritten answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for 6.5² − 6² (subtracting the squares)
    2. M1 for 42.25 − 36 = 6.25
    3. C1 for √6.25 = 2.5, stated as the conclusion
    Working: The ladder is the longest side (the hypotenuse), so the distance d from the wall satisfies d² + 6² = 6.5². d² = 42.25 − 36 = 6.25. d = √6.25 = 2.5 m, as required.
    		3
    18Calculator Grade 4 A circular pond has a diameter of 4.2 m. A gardener wants to put an edging strip all the way round the pond. Work out the length of edging needed. Give your answer in metres, correct to 1 decimal place. Uses: Circles13.2
    1. M1 for π × 4.2
    2. A1 for 13.2
    Working: Circumference = π × diameter = π × 4.2 = 13.194… ≈ 13.2 m (to 1 d.p.). The edging needed is 13.2 m.

    19Calculator Grade 6 A water trough is a prism. Its cross-section is a triangle with base 40 cm and height 30 cm. The trough is 150 cm long. Work out how many litres of water it holds when it is full. 1000 cm³ = 1 litre. Type the number of litres. Uses: Area and perimeter90
    1. M1 for ½ × 40 × 30
    2. A1 for 600
    3. M1 for 600 × 150 (= 90 000)
    4. A1 for 90
    Working: Area of the triangle = ½ × 40 × 30 = 600 cm². Volume = 600 × 150 = 90 000 cm³. In litres: 90 000 ÷ 1000 = 90 litres.
    		4
    20Non-calculator Grade 5 An L-shaped room is made from a rectangle 8 m long and 5 m wide, with a square corner measuring 3 m by 3 m cut out of it. Show that the floor area of the room is 31 m². Uses: Area and perimeterWritten answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for 8 × 5 = 40
    2. M1 for 3 × 3 = 9, subtracted from 40
    3. C1 for 31, stated as the conclusion
    Working: Whole rectangle: 8 × 5 = 40 m². Corner removed: 3 × 3 = 9 m². Floor area = 40 − 9 = 31 m², as required.

    21Non-calculator Grade 4 A bag has 4 red counters, 7 blue counters and 9 green counters. One counter is taken from the bag at random. Work out the probability that it is not blue. Give your answer as a decimal, like 0.4. Uses: Probability basics0.65
    1. M1 for 13/20, or for 1 − 7/20
    2. A1 for 0.65
    Working: 4 + 7 + 9 = 20 counters. P(blue) = 7/20 = 0.35. P(not blue) = 1 − 0.35 = 0.65. (Check: 13 counters are not blue, and 13/20 = 0.65.) Answer: 0.65.
    		2
    22Non-calculator Grade 5 The mean of six numbers is 7. Five of the numbers are 4, 9, 6, 10 and 5. Work out the sixth number. Uses: Averages8
    1. M1 for 6 × 7 (= 42)
    2. M1 for 4 + 9 + 6 + 10 + 5 (= 34) and subtracting from the total
    3. A1 for 8
    Working: Total of six numbers = 6 × 7 = 42. The five known numbers: 4 + 9 + 6 + 10 + 5 = 34. Sixth number = 42 − 34 = 8.
    		3
    23Calculator Grade 5 An electrician checks 20 houses and records how many faults she finds in each. 5 houses had 0 faults, 8 houses had 1 fault, 4 houses had 2 faults and 3 houses had 3 faults. Work out the mean number of faults per house. Uses: Averages1.25
    1. M1 for multiplying each number of faults by its frequency
    2. M1 for 25 (the total) divided by 20
    3. A1 for 1.25
    Working: Total faults = 0 × 5 + 1 × 8 + 2 × 4 + 3 × 3 = 0 + 8 + 8 + 9 = 25. Number of houses = 5 + 8 + 4 + 3 = 20. Mean = 25 ÷ 20 = 1.25 faults per house.
    		3
    24Non-calculator Grade 4 A spinner has four colours. The probability of red is 0.25 and the probability of blue is 0.4. The probabilities of green and yellow are the same. Show that the probability of green is 0.175. Uses: Probability basicsWritten answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for 0.25 + 0.4 = 0.65
    2. M1 for 1 − 0.65 = 0.35, then dividing by 2
    3. C1 for 0.175, stated as the conclusion
    Working: The probabilities add up to 1. Red + blue = 0.25 + 0.4 = 0.65. Green + yellow = 1 − 0.65 = 0.35. They are equal, so green = 0.35 ÷ 2 = 0.175, as required.
    		3
    25Calculator Grade 5 The bar chart shows how many jobs a plumber did on each day of one week. Work out the mean number of jobs per day. Uses: Averages [Figure: Bar chart of jobs per day]5.8
    1. M1 for reading the five bars correctly (6, 9, 4, 7, 3)
    2. M1 for 29 ÷ 5
    3. A1 for 5.8
    Working: Reading the bars: Monday 6, Tuesday 9, Wednesday 4, Thursday 7, Friday 3. Total = 6 + 9 + 4 + 7 + 3 = 29 jobs. Mean = 29 ÷ 5 = 5.8 jobs per day.
    		3
    26Calculator Grade 4 An electrician quotes for a job. Parts cost £85. Labour is 4 hours at £45 per hour. VAT at 20% is added to the whole of the quote. Work out the total cost including VAT. Type the number of pounds. Uses: Percentages Uses: Four operations318
    1. M1 for 4 × 45 + 85 (= 265)
    2. M1 for adding 20% VAT, or multiplying by 1.2
    3. A1 for 318
    Working: Labour = 4 × 45 = £180. Before VAT: 180 + 85 = £265. VAT = 20% of 265 = £53, so the total is 265 + 53 = £318. (Or 265 × 1.2 = 318.)
    		3
    27Non-calculator Grade 4 A tiler is tiling a rectangular floor 5 m long and 4 m wide. Each tile is a square with sides of 50 cm. Show that 80 tiles are needed to cover the floor. Uses: Area and perimeterWritten answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for 5 × 4 = 20 m²
    2. M1 for 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 m² (same units as the floor)
    3. C1 for 20 ÷ 0.25 = 80, stated as the conclusion
    Working: Floor area = 5 × 4 = 20 m². One tile = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 m². Number of tiles = 20 ÷ 0.25 = 80, as required.
    		3
    28Calculator Grade 6 An electrician wires a new socket circuit. The ring needs 24 m of cable and a spur needs 7.5 m. He buys 10% extra to allow for waste. The cable costs £1.85 per metre before VAT. VAT at 20% is then added. Work out the total cost of the cable including VAT. Give your answer in pounds to the nearest penny, like 12.50. Uses: Percentages Uses: Four operations76.92
    1. M1 for 24 + 7.5 (= 31.5)
    2. M1 for 31.5 × 1.1 (= 34.65)
    3. M1 for (their 34.65) × 1.85
    4. M1 for adding 20% VAT, or multiplying by 1.2
    5. A1 for 76.92
    Working: Length needed = 24 + 7.5 = 31.5 m. With 10% extra: 31.5 × 1.1 = 34.65 m. Cost before VAT = 34.65 × 1.85 = £64.1025. With VAT: 64.1025 × 1.2 = 76.923 ≈ £76.92.
    		5
    29Non-calculator Grade 5 A plumber needs 7 pieces of copper pipe, each 85 cm long. The pipe is sold in 3 m lengths, and each 3 m length costs £8.40. Offcuts that are too short cannot be used. Work out the least cost of the pipe. Give your answer in pounds, like 12.50. Uses: Four operations Uses: Factors and multiples25.20
    1. M1 for 300 ÷ 85 and 3 whole pieces per length
    2. M1 for 7 ÷ 3 and rounding up to 3 lengths
    3. M1 for 3 × 8.40
    4. A1 for 25.20
    Working: 300 ÷ 85 = 3.5…, so only 3 whole pieces come out of each 3 m length (3 × 85 = 255 cm, leaving 45 cm, which is too short). 7 ÷ 3 = 2.33…, so he needs 3 lengths (2 lengths give only 6 pieces). Cost = 3 × 8.40 = £25.20.
    		4
    30Calculator Grade 6 A homeowner gets two quotes to retile a bathroom. Quote A is £1250 including VAT. Quote B is £400 plus £38 for each hour of work, and then 20% VAT is added to the total. Quote B says the job will take 22 hours. Which quote is cheaper? You must show your working, and explain your answer. Uses: Percentages Uses: Solving equationsWritten answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for 22 × 38 (= 836)
    2. M1 for adding 400 and adding 20% VAT (or × 1.2) to get 1483.20
    3. M1 for comparing like with like, both including VAT
    4. C1 for stating that Quote A is cheaper (by £233.20), with the figures
    Working: Labour for B = 22 × 38 = £836. Before VAT: 400 + 836 = £1236. With VAT: 1236 × 1.2 = £1483.20. Quote A is £1250 including VAT. Since £1250 is less than £1483.20, Quote A is cheaper, by £233.20.
    		4
    31Non-calculator Grade 4 A cut and colour takes 1 hour 45 minutes and starts at 10:20. What time does it finish? Type it in 24-hour time, like 12:05. Uses: Four operations12:05 (also accept: 1205, 12.05)
    Working: 10:20 + 1 hour = 11:20. 11:20 + 40 minutes = 12:00, and 5 more minutes makes 12:05. So it finishes at 12:05.

    36Calculator Grade 5 A stylist is paid £11.40 per hour for the first 35 hours in a week, and time and a half (1.5 times the rate) for every hour after that. Show that she earns £501.60 in a week when she works 41 hours. You must show your working. Uses: Percentages Uses: Four operationsWritten answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for 35 × 11.40 (= 399)
    2. M1 for 6 × 17.10 (= 102.60), or 6 × 11.40 × 1.5
    3. C1 for 399 + 102.60 = 501.60, stated as the answer
    Working: Normal pay: 35 × 11.40 = £399. Overtime rate: 11.40 × 1.5 = £17.10 per hour. Overtime hours: 41 − 35 = 6, so 6 × 17.10 = £102.60. Total: 399 + 102.60 = £501.60, as the question says.
    		3
