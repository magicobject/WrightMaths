    1Two numbers add up to 11. The bigger number is 3 more than the smaller number. What are the two numbers? Type the bigger first, then the smaller, with a comma and no brackets, like 9,2. [Figure: Two numbers: a bigger and a smaller]7,4
    Working: 11 − 3 = 8, and 8 ÷ 2 = 4 is the smaller number. The bigger is 4 + 3 = 7. Answer: 7,4. Check: 7 + 4 = 11 and 7 − 4 = 3.
    2Solve 2x + 5 = 17.6
    Working: 2x = 17 − 5 = 12, so x = 6. Check: 2 × 6 + 5 = 17.
    3Solve x − 7 = −2.5
    Working: x = −2 + 7 = 5. Check: 5 − 7 = −2.
    4Work out 3x + 2y when x = 4 and y = −1.10
    Working: 3 × 4 = 12 and 2 × (−1) = −2, so 12 − 2 = 10.
    5Which pair makes both x + y = 8 and x − y = 2 true?x = 5, y = 3
    Working: 5 + 3 = 8 and 5 − 3 = 2, so x = 5, y = 3 works in both. The others each fail one equation.
    6How many pairs of positive whole numbers make x + y = 5 true? (1, 4) and (4, 1) count as different pairs.4
    Working: The pairs are (1, 4), (2, 3), (3, 2) and (4, 1). That is 4 pairs, so one equation alone is not enough to pin down x and y.
    7x + y = 12 and x − y = 4. Find x and y by trying pairs that add to 12. Type x first, then y, like 9,3.8,4
    Working: 8 + 4 = 12 and 8 − 4 = 4. Answer: 8,4.
    8Why is one equation, such as x + y = 5, not enough to find x and y?Many pairs fit it.
    Working: 1 + 4 = 5 and 2 + 3 = 5, so many pairs fit. A second equation is the extra clue that picks out one pair.
    9Solve x + y = 7 and x − y = 1.4,3
    Working: Add: 2x = 8, so x = 4. Then 4 + y = 7, so y = 3. Check: 4 − 3 = 1. Answer: 4,3.
    10Solve (1) 2x + y = 12 and (2) x + y = 7.5,2
    Working: (1) − (2): x = 5. Then 5 + y = 7, so y = 2. Check (1): 10 + 2 = 12. Answer: 5,2.
    11Solve (1) 2x + 3y = 15 and (2) 2x − 3y = 9.6,1
    Working: Add: 4x = 24, so x = 6. Then 12 + 3y = 15, so 3y = 3 and y = 1. Check (2): 12 − 3 = 9. Answer: 6,1.
    12Solve (1) x + 5y = 16 and (2) 3x + 5y = 18.1,3
    Working: (2) − (1): 2x = 2, so x = 1. Then 1 + 5y = 16, so y = 3. Check (2): 3 + 15 = 18. Answer: 1,3.
    13To remove y from 4x + y = 9 and 2x + y = 5, what should you do?Subtract them
    Working: Both y terms are +y, so subtract: 4x − 2x = 2x, y − y = 0 and 9 − 5 = 4. That leaves 2x = 4.
    14Solve (1) 3x + y = 5 and (2) x + y = 9. One of the answers is negative: type it like -2,5.-2,11
    Working: (1) − (2): 2x = −4, so x = −2. Then −2 + y = 9, so y = 11. Check (1): −6 + 11 = 5. Answer: -2,11.
    15Solve (1) x + 2y = 9 and (2) 3x + y = 7.1,4
    Working: (2) × 2: 6x + 2y = 14. Subtract (1): 5x = 5, so x = 1. Then 1 + 2y = 9, so y = 4. Check (2): 3 + 4 = 7. Answer: 1,4.
    16Solve (1) 2x + y = 7 and (2) 3x + 2y = 11.3,1
    Working: (1) × 2: 4x + 2y = 14. Subtract (2): x = 3. Then 6 + y = 7, so y = 1. Check (2): 9 + 2 = 11. Answer: 3,1.
    17Solve (1) 2x + 3y = 13 and (2) 3x + 2y = 12.2,3
    Working: (1) × 3: 6x + 9y = 39. (2) × 2: 6x + 4y = 24. Subtract: 5y = 15, so y = 3. Then 2x + 9 = 13, so x = 2. Check (2): 6 + 6 = 12. Answer: 2,3.
    18Solve (1) 3x + 2y = 4 and (2) x − y = 3. Type your answer like 5,-2.2,-1
    Working: (2) × 2: 2x − 2y = 6. Add (1): 5x = 10, so x = 2. Then 2 − y = 3, so y = −1. Check (1): 6 − 2 = 4. Answer: 2,-1.
    19You have (1) 4x + 3y = 22 and (2) 2x + y = 10. What should you do first to remove x?Multiply (2) by 2
    Working: (2) × 2 gives 4x + 2y = 20. Now both equations have 4x, so you can subtract. That leaves y = 2, and then x = 4.
    20(1) 4x + 3y = 17 and (2) 4x + y = 11. Find y only.3
    Working: (1) − (2): 2y = 6, so y = 3. Then 4x + 3 = 11, so x = 2. Check (1): 8 + 9 = 17.
    21Solve (1) y = x + 4 and (2) 2x + y = 22.6,10
    Working: 3x + 4 = 22, so 3x = 18 and x = 6. Then y = 6 + 4 = 10. Check (2): 12 + 10 = 22. Answer: 6,10.
    22Solve (1) x = 3y and (2) x + y = 20.15,5
    Working: 4y = 20, so y = 5. Then x = 3 × 5 = 15. Check (2): 15 + 5 = 20. Answer: 15,5.
    23Solve (1) y = 2x − 3 and (2) 5x + y = 25.4,5
    Working: 7x = 28, so x = 4. Then y = 2 × 4 − 3 = 5. Check (2): 20 + 5 = 25. Answer: 4,5.
    24Solve (1) x + y = 10 and (2) 3x − y = 6. Rearrange (1) to get y on its own first.4,6
    Working: 3x − 10 + x = 6, so 4x = 16 and x = 4. Then y = 10 − 4 = 6. Check (2): 12 − 6 = 6. Answer: 4,6.
    25You have y = 3x − 2 and 2x + y = 13. Which method is quickest to start?Substitution: y is already on its own.
    Working: y = 3x − 2 is on its own, so replace y in the second equation: 2x + 3x − 2 = 13.
    26Use y = 3x − 2 and 2x + y = 13 to find x.3
    Working: 5x = 15, so x = 3. Then y = 3 × 3 − 2 = 7. Check: 6 + 7 = 13.
    27A band sells T-shirts and posters. 2 T-shirts and 1 poster cost £35. 1 T-shirt and 1 poster cost £22. How much is one T-shirt, in pounds? [Figure: Bars for T-shirts and posters]13
    Working: (2t + p) − (t + p): t = 35 − 22 = 13. A T-shirt is £13. Then p = 22 − 13 = 9. Check: 26 + 9 = 35.
    28At a caf&eacute;, 2 cakes and 3 teas cost £17. 4 cakes and 1 tea cost £19. How much is one tea, in pounds?3
    Working: (2) × 3: 12c + 3t = 57. Subtract (1): 10c = 40, so c = 4. Then 8 + 3t = 17, so t = 3. A tea is £3. Check (2): 16 + 3 = 19.
    29In a game, 4 coins and 2 gems score 26 points. 3 coins and 5 gems score 37 points. How many points is each coin and each gem worth? Type the coin first, then the gem, like 2,9.4,5
    Working: (1) × 5: 20c + 10g = 130. (2) × 2: 6c + 10g = 74. Subtract: 14c = 56, so c = 4. Then 16 + 2g = 26, so g = 5. Answer: 4,5. Check (2): 12 + 25 = 37.
    30A builder pays £47 for 3 bags of sand and 2 bags of cement. She pays £48 for 5 bags of sand and 1 bag of cement. How much is one bag of cement, in pounds? (The prices are made up for practice.)13
    Working: (2) × 2: 10s + 2c = 96. Subtract (1): 7s = 49, so s = 7. Then 35 + c = 48, so c = 13. A bag of cement is £13. Check (1): 21 + 26 = 47.
    31A minibus carries 16 people. Adults pay £4 each and children pay £2 each. The total paid is £44. How many children are on the minibus?10
    Working: (2) − (1) × 2: 2a = 12, so a = 6. Then c = 16 − 6 = 10 children. Check: 4 × 6 + 2 × 10 = 44.
    32The sum of two numbers is 30. The larger number is 6 more than twice the smaller number. What is the larger number?22
    Working: 3y + 6 = 30, so 3y = 24 and y = 8. The larger number is x = 2 × 8 + 6 = 22. Check: 22 + 8 = 30.
    332 pens and 1 notebook cost £7. 1 pen and 3 notebooks cost £11. Let p be the price of a pen and n the price of a notebook, in pounds. Which pair of equations matches the story? [Figure: Two lines crossing]2p + n = 7 and p + 3n = 11
    Working: 2 pens cost 2p and 1 notebook costs n, so 2p + n = 7. 1 pen costs p and 3 notebooks cost 3n, so p + 3n = 11.
    34The diagram shows the lines x + y = 5 and x − y = 1. Read off where they cross. Type x first, then y, like 1,4. [Figure: Two lines on a grid]3,2
    Working: The lines meet at x = 3, y = 2. Check: 3 + 2 = 5 and 3 − 2 = 1. Answer: 3,2.
    35The point (1, 4) is on the line x + y = 5. Work out the value of x − y at (1, 4). Is (1, 4) also on the line x − y = 1?-3
    Working: 1 − 4 = −3, which is not 1. So (1, 4) is on the first line but not the second, so it is not where they cross.
    36Two straight lines cross at one point. What does that point tell you about their two equations?The values that make both true.
    Working: A point on a line fits that line's equation. A point on both lines fits both equations, so its x and y are the solution.
    37Solve (1) 3x = 2y + 8 and (2) x + y = 6.4,2
    Working: Add: 5x = 20, so x = 4. Then 4 + y = 6, so y = 2. Check (1): 12 = 4 + 8. Answer: 4,2.
    38Solve (1) 2x − 3y = 13 and (2) 5x + 2y = 4. Type your answer like 5,-1.2,-3
    Working: Add: 19x = 38, so x = 2. Then 10 + 2y = 4, so y = −3. Check (1): 4 + 9 = 13. Answer: 2,-3.
    39Solve (1) 4x + y = 4 and (2) 2x + 3y = 7.0.5,2 (also accept: 1/2,2)
    Working: (1) × 3: 12x + 3y = 12. Subtract (2): 10x = 5, so x = 0.5. Then 2 + y = 4, so y = 2. Check (2): 1 + 6 = 7. Answer: 0.5,2.
    40A rectangle has a perimeter of 34 cm. Its length is 3 cm more than its width. How long is the rectangle, in cm? [Figure: A rectangle with length l and width w]10
    Working: 2w + 6 + 2w = 34, so 4w = 28 and w = 7. The length is l = 7 + 3 = 10 cm. Check: 2 × 10 + 2 × 7 = 34.
    41A student says x = 4, y = 1 solves both 3x − y = 11 and x + 2y = 7. Is the student right?No: it fails the second equation.
    Working: 3 × 4 − 1 = 11 works, but 4 + 2 × 1 = 6, not 7. A solution must work in both, so the student is wrong.
    42The pair x = 4, y = 1 solves x + y = 5 and also ax − y = 7. Find the number a.2
    Working: 4a − 1 = 7, so 4a = 8 and a = 2. Check: 2 × 4 − 1 = 7.
    43Which pair of equations has x = 2, y = 3 as its solution?x + y = 5 and 2x − y = 1
    Working: 2 + 3 = 5 and 2 × 2 − 3 = 1, so this pair works in both. The others each fail one.
    44x + y = 10 and x − y = 4. Add the two equations. You get 2x = a number. What is the number?14
    Working: x + x = 2x and y − y = 0, so 2x = 10 + 4 = 14. That gives x = 7, and then y = 3.
    45Solve x + y = 12 and x − y = 2, then check your answer in both equations. Type x first, then y.7,5
    Working: Add: 2x = 14, so x = 7. Then 7 + y = 12, so y = 5. Check: 7 + 5 = 12 and 7 − 5 = 2. Answer: 7,5.
    46Tia says x = 5, y = 1 solves 3x + 2y = 17 and 2x + y = 9. Work out 2x + y for her numbers. Is she right?11
    Working: 2 × 5 + 1 = 11, not 9, so Tia is wrong. Her pair fits the first equation (15 + 2 = 17) but not the second.
    47The pair x = 2, y = 3 solves x + y = 5. It also solves 2x + y = c. Find c.7
    Working: 2 × 2 + 3 = 7, so c = 7.
    48Leo says: for 3x + 2y = 11 and x + 2y = 5, add the equations to get 4x + 4y = 16. What went wrong?Adding keeps y. He should subtract.
    Working: Both y terms are +2y, so subtract: 2x = 6, so x = 3. Then 3 + 2y = 5, so y = 1. Check: 9 + 2 = 11.
    49Zara says: for 2x + y = 9 and x − y = 3, adding gives 3x = 12, so x = 4. Then she writes y = 3 + 4 = 7. What went wrong?y is 4 − 3 = 1, not 3 + 4.
    Working: 4 − y = 3 gives y = 1. Check: 2 × 4 + 1 = 9 and 4 − 1 = 3.
    50Ravi says: x = 4, y = 1 solves x + y = 5, so it must solve x + y = 5 and 2x − y = 6. What went wrong?He did not check equation two.
    Working: 2 × 4 − 1 = 7, which is not 6. So the pair fails the second equation and is not a solution of both.
    51Solve 4x − 5 = 19.6
    Working: 4x = 19 + 5 = 24, so x = 6. Check: 4 × 6 − 5 = 19.
    52Solve x + y = 15 and x − y = 5.10,5
    Working: Add: 2x = 20, so x = 10. Then y = 15 − 10 = 5. Check: 10 − 5 = 5. Answer: 10,5.
    53Solve (1) 6x + y = 20 and (2) 2x + y = 8.3,2
    Working: (1) − (2): 4x = 12, so x = 3. Then 6 + y = 8, so y = 2. Check (1): 18 + 2 = 20. Answer: 3,2.
    54A plumber buys 3 lengths of 15 mm pipe and 2 lengths of 22 mm pipe for £37. She buys 2 lengths of 15 mm pipe and 1 length of 22 mm pipe for £21. How much is one length of 22 mm pipe, in pounds? (The prices are made up for practice.)11
    Working: (2) × 2: 4a + 2b = 42. Subtract (1): a = 5. Then 10 + b = 21, so b = 11. A 22 mm length is £11. Check (1): 15 + 22 = 37.
    55Solve y = 3x and x + y = 20.5,15
    Working: x + 3x = 20, so 4x = 20 and x = 5. Then y = 3 × 5 = 15. Check: 5 + 15 = 20. Answer: 5,15.
    56A shop hires out bikes for a day. 2 mountain bikes and 3 road bikes cost £96. 1 mountain bike and 2 road bikes cost £58. How much is one mountain bike for the day, in pounds? (The prices are made up for practice.)18
    Working: (2) × 2: 2m + 4r = 116. Subtract (1): r = 20. Then m + 40 = 58, so m = 18. A mountain bike is £18. Check (1): 36 + 60 = 96.
    57Work out 5x − 2y when x = 3 and y = −2.19
    Working: 15 − (−4) = 15 + 4 = 19.
    58Solve the simultaneous equations 3x + 2y = 19 and x + 4y = 23. Type x first, then y, like 2,5.3,5
    1. M1 for eliminating y: 6x + 4y = 38, then subtracting (2) to get 5x = 15
    2. A1 for x = 3
    3. M1 for substituting x = 3: 3 + 4y = 23
    4. A1 for y = 5
    Working: Multiply (1) by 2: 6x + 4y = 38. Subtract (2): 5x = 15, so x = 3. Then 3 + 4y = 23, so y = 5. Check (1): 9 + 10 = 19. Answer: 3,5.

    59A caf&eacute; sells pizzas and salads. 5 pizzas and 3 salads cost £67. 2 pizzas and 5 salads cost £61. Work out the cost of one salad, in pounds.9
    1. B1 for 5p + 3s = 67
    2. B1 for 2p + 5s = 61
    3. M1 for making the p terms match and subtracting: 19s = 171
    4. A1 for s = 9
    Working: Write 5p + 3s = 67 and 2p + 5s = 61. Multiply (1) by 2 to get 10p + 6s = 134 and (2) by 5 to get 10p + 25s = 305. Subtract: 19s = 171, so s = 9, and a salad costs £9. Check: p = 8, and 16 + 45 = 61.
    		4
    60(a) [2 marks] Hannah says the solution of x + y = 7 and 3x − y = 5 is x = 2, y = 5. Show that Hannah is wrong. Type the value of 3x − y for her numbers.1
    1. M1 for 3 × 2 − 5 = 1
    2. C1 for saying 1 is not 5, so the pair fails the second equation
    Working: 3 × 2 − 5 = 1. 1 is not 5, so the pair fails the second equation, even though 2 + 5 = 7 fits the first. Hannah is wrong.
    		2
    61(b) [2 marks] Work out the correct solution of x + y = 7 and 3x − y = 5. Type x first, then y.3,4
    1. M1 for a valid method to eliminate or substitute, such as adding the equations: 4x = 12
    2. A1 for x = 3 and y = 4 both correct
    Working: Add the equations: 4x = 12, so x = 3. Then 3 + y = 7, so y = 4. Check: 9 − 4 = 5. Answer: 3,4.
    		2
    62Look at the two lines x + y = 5 and x + y = 7. How many pairs of numbers make both equations true? [Figure: Two parallel lines]None: the lines never meet.
    Working: The same x + y cannot equal both 5 and 7, so no pair works. On the graph the lines are parallel and never meet, so there is no solution.
    63Look at x + y = 5 and 2x + 2y = 10. How many pairs of numbers make both equations true?Infinitely many: they are the same line.
    Working: Dividing 2x + 2y = 10 by 2 gives x + y = 5. They are the same equation, so every pair that fits one fits the other. The lines lie on top of each other.
    64Two numbers x and y have sum S and difference D, so x + y = S and x − y = D. Make x the subject of a formula using S and D. Use brackets where you need them.(s+d)/2 (also accept: s/2+d/2)
    Working: Add: 2x = S + D, so x = (S + D)/2, typed (s+d)/2. For example, a sum of 20 and a difference of 6 gives x = 13 and y = 7.
    65x + y = 10 and x − y = 4. Without finding x or y, work out x² − y². Higher40
    Working: x² − y² = (x − y)(x + y) = 4 × 10 = 40. Check with x = 7 and y = 3: 49 − 9 = 40.
    66Two numbers x and y have x + y = 7 and xy = 10. Without finding x and y first, work out x² + y².29
    Working: (x + y)² = x² + 2xy + y², so 7² = x² + y² + 2 × 10. That gives 49 = x² + y² + 20, so x² + y² = 29. Check: the numbers are 2 and 5, and 4 + 25 = 29.
