    Answers for Coordinates and straight-line graphs
    No.QuestionAnswerMarks
    1Work out 2x + 1 when x = −3.-5
    Working: 2 × (−3) = −6, then −6 + 1 = −5.
    2Work out 7 − (−3).10
    Working: 7 − (−3) = 7 + 3 = 10.
    3Work out 3x − 2 when x = 4.10
    Working: 3 × 4 = 12, then 12 − 2 = 10.
    4Work out (−2 + 6) ÷ 2. [Figure: The four quadrants]2
    Working: −2 + 6 = 4, then 4 ÷ 2 = 2.
    5Write the coordinates of point P. Type them like (2, 5). [Figure: A grid with four points]3,4 (also accept: (3, 4))
    Working: P is 3 along to the right and 4 up, so P is (3, 4).
    6Write the coordinates of point Q. Type them like (2, 5). [Figure: A grid with four points]-2,3 (also accept: (-2, 3))
    Working: Q is 2 to the left, so x = −2. It is 3 up, so y = 3. Q is (−2, 3).
    7Write the coordinates of point S. Type them like (2, 5). [Figure: A grid with four points]1,-4 (also accept: (1, -4))
    Working: S is 1 to the right and 4 down, so S is (1, −4).
    8A point is 5 units to the left of the y-axis and 2 units below the x-axis. Write its coordinates, like (2, 5).-5,-2 (also accept: (-5, -2))
    Working: 5 to the left is x = −5. 2 below is y = −2. The point is (−5, −2).
    9In which quadrant is the point (−3, 6)? [Figure: A line segment and its midpoint]Quadrant 2
    Working: x = −3 is to the left and y = 6 is above, so the point is top left: quadrant 2.
    10Find the midpoint of (2, 4) and (8, 10). Type it like (2, 5).5,7 (also accept: (5, 7))
    Working: (2 + 8) ÷ 2 = 5 and (4 + 10) ÷ 2 = 7, so the midpoint is (5, 7).
    11Find the midpoint of (−6, 3) and (2, −5). Type it like (2, 5).-2,-1 (also accept: (-2, -1))
    Working: (−6 + 2) ÷ 2 = −4 ÷ 2 = −2. (3 + (−5)) ÷ 2 = −2 ÷ 2 = −1. The midpoint is (−2, −1).
    12M is the midpoint of AB. A is (1, −2) and M is (4, 1). Find B. Type it like (2, 5).7,4 (also accept: (7, 4))
    Working: From A to M: 4 − 1 = 3 along and 1 − (−2) = 3 up. Do the same step again from M: (4 + 3, 1 + 3) = (7, 4). Check: the midpoint of (1, −2) and (7, 4) is (4, 1). ✓
    13On a plan (in metres) a joist runs from (1, 2) to (9, 6). A support goes at its midpoint. Write the coordinates of the support, like (2, 5). [Figure: The graph of y = 2x - 1]5,4 (also accept: (5, 4))
    Working: (1 + 9) ÷ 2 = 5 and (2 + 6) ÷ 2 = 4. The support is at (5, 4).
    14A table of values is made for y = 2x + 3. What is y when x = −2?-1
    Working: 2 × (−2) = −4, then −4 + 3 = −1.
    15A table of values is made for y = 4 − 2x. What is y when x = −3?10
    Working: 2x = 2 × (−3) = −6. Then y = 4 − (−6) = 4 + 6 = 10.
    16Which point lies on the line y = 3x − 1?(2, 5)
    Working: For (2, 5): 3 × 2 − 1 = 5, which matches. The other two points do not fit the rule.
    17The line y = 2x + 1 passes through the point (4, k). Find k. [Figure: Rise and run on an upward line]9
    Working: Put x = 4 into y = 2x + 1: y = 2 × 4 + 1 = 9. So k = 9.
    18The line goes through A and B, marked on the graph. What is its gradient? [Figure: A line through two marked points]3
    Working: From A to B the line rises 7 − 1 = 6 and runs 3 − 1 = 2. Gradient = 6 ÷ 2 = 3.
    19The line goes through C and D, marked on the graph. What is its gradient? Give it as a decimal or fraction, like −2 or −3/4. [Figure: A line sloping downwards]-1.5 (also accept: -3/2)
    Working: From C to D the height changes by −1 − 5 = −6 (a fall of 6) and the run is 2 − (−2) = 4. Gradient = −6 ÷ 4 = −1.5.
    20The line is drawn on the grid. At what value of y does it cross the y-axis? [Figure: A line crossing the vertical axis]3
    Working: The line meets the y-axis where x = 0. On the graph that is at y = 3.
    21A ramp rises 0.4 m over a horizontal distance of 4.8 m. What is its gradient? Give it as a fraction in its simplest form, like 1/20.1/12
    Working: Gradient = rise ÷ run = 0.4 ÷ 4.8. Multiply top and bottom by 10: 4/48. Divide top and bottom by 4: 1/12.
    22Find the gradient of the line through (1, 9) and (4, 0). [Figure: Two parallel lines]-3
    Working: (0 − 9) ÷ (4 − 1) = −9 ÷ 3 = −3.
    23What is the gradient of the line y = 4x − 7?4
    Working: Compare with y = mx + c: m = 4, so the gradient is 4. (The y-intercept is −7.)
    24What is the y-intercept of the line y = 6 − 2x?6
    Working: Put x = 0: y = 6 − 0 = 6. The line crosses the y-axis at 6.
    25The line passes through the two points marked. Write its equation in the form y = mx + c. Type it like y = 2x + 5. [Figure: A line with two marked points]3x-2 (also accept: -2+3x)
    Working: The line crosses the y-axis at (0, −2), so c = −2. From (0, −2) to (2, 4) the rise is 6 and the run is 2, so m = 6 ÷ 2 = 3. The equation is y = 3x − 2.
    26Which line is parallel to y = 5x − 1?y = 5x + 4
    Working: y = 5x − 1 has gradient 5, and so does y = 5x + 4. Equal gradients mean the lines are parallel.
    27A line has the equation 2y = 8x + 6. What is its gradient?4
    Working: Divide every term by 2: y = 4x + 3. The number multiplying x is 4, so the gradient is 4.
    28A line has gradient 4. What is the gradient of a line perpendicular to it? Type it like 3/5 or −2.-1/4 (also accept: -0.25)
    Working: 4 = 4/1. Flip it to get 1/4 and change the sign: −1/4. Check: 4 × (−1/4) = −1.
    29Which line is perpendicular to y = 2x + 3? [Figure: Two lines that cross]y = −x/2
    Working: The gradient of y = −x/2 is −1/2, and 2 × (−1/2) = −1, so the lines are perpendicular.
    30The graph shows two straight lines, L and M. Write the coordinates of the point where they cross. Type it like (2, 5). [Figure: Two lines crossing]2,3 (also accept: (2, 3))
    Working: The lines meet at 2 along and 3 up, so the point is (2, 3). Check with L: y = 2x − 1 gives 2 × 2 − 1 = 3 and with M: y = 5 − x gives 5 − 2 = 3. ✓
    31Does the point (3, 7) lie on both of the lines y = 2x + 1 and y = 10 − x? Type yes or no.yes
    Working: y = 2x + 1: 2 × 3 + 1 = 7. y = 10 − x: 10 − 3 = 7. Both give 7, so the answer is yes.
    32The lines y = x + 2 and y = 3x − 4 cross at one point. What is the x-coordinate of that point? [Figure: Cost of a plumber against hours]3
    Working: x + 2 = 3x − 4. Take x from both sides: 2 = 2x − 4. Add 4: 6 = 2x, so x = 3. (Then y = 3 + 2 = 5, and 3 × 3 − 4 = 5 too.)
    33The graph shows a plumber’s cost against hours worked. Two points are marked. How much does the plumber charge for each extra hour? Answer in pounds. [Figure: Cost of a plumber against hours]35
    Working: From (2, 120) to (4, 190) the cost rises by 70 over 2 hours. 70 ÷ 2 = 35, so the plumber charges £35 for each extra hour.
    34A plumber charges a £55 call-out fee plus £30 per hour, so the cost is C = 30h + 55. What does a job of 4.5 hours cost? Answer in pounds.190
    Working: 30 × 4.5 = 135, then 135 + 55 = 190. The job costs £190.
    35The graph shows how far a delivery van is from the depot. What is the van’s speed in the first hour, in km/h? [Figure: A distance-time graph for a van]60
    Working: In the first hour the line goes from 0 km up to 60 km. Speed = distance ÷ time = 60 ÷ 1 = 60 km/h.
    36A plumber’s quote is C = 45h + 60, where C is the cost in pounds and h is the hours. For how many hours is the bill £375?7
    Working: 45h + 60 = 375, so 45h = 315 and h = 7. The bill is £375 for 7 hours.
    37A tank starts full. A graph of the litres left against the minutes passed is a straight line sloping down. What does its gradient tell you?How fast it empties
    Working: The gradient is the change in litres for each minute. A line sloping down has a negative gradient, and its size is the rate at which the tank empties.
    38Write the equation of the line, in the form y = mx + c. For a gradient of 1 you can write x. Type it like y = 2x + 5. [Figure: A line to write the equation of]-x+2 (also accept: 2-x)
    Working: The line crosses the y-axis at (0, 2), so c = 2. It goes along 2 and down 2 to reach (2, 0), so the gradient is −2 ÷ 2 = −1. The equation is y = −x + 2.
    39Write the equation of the line with gradient 3 that crosses the y-axis at (0, 4). Type it like y = 2x + 5.3x+4 (also accept: 4+3x)
    Working: y = mx + c with m = 3 and c = 4 gives y = 3x + 4.
    40A line goes through (1, 5) and (3, 11). Write its equation in the form y = mx + c. Type it like y = 2x + 5.3x+2 (also accept: 2+3x)
    Working: Gradient = (11 − 5) ÷ (3 − 1) = 6 ÷ 2 = 3, so y = 3x + c. Put in (1, 5): 5 = 3 × 1 + c, so c = 2. The equation is y = 3x + 2. Check (3, 11): 3 × 3 + 2 = 11. ✓
    41The line y = 2x + c goes through the point (3, 4). Find c.-2
    Working: Put x = 3 and y = 4 into y = 2x + c: 4 = 6 + c, so c = −2. The line is y = 2x − 2.
    42Sam says: the gradient of the line through (0, 1) and (2, 5) is 2 ÷ 4 = 0.5. What went wrong?He used run ÷ rise instead of rise ÷ run.
    Working: The rise is 5 − 1 = 4 and the run is 2 − 0 = 2. Gradient = 4 ÷ 2 = 2. Sam turned it upside down.
    43Mia says: the line y = 3 − 2x has gradient 3 and y-intercept −2, because she read the numbers in the order they are written. What went wrong?The gradient multiplies x, so it is −2, and c is 3.
    Working: y = 3 − 2x is the same as y = −2x + 3. The gradient m is −2 and the y-intercept c is 3.
    44Find the midpoint of (−1, −1) and (5, 3). Type it like (2, 5).2,1 (also accept: (2, 1))
    Working: (−1 + 5) ÷ 2 = 2 and (−1 + 3) ÷ 2 = 1. The midpoint is (2, 1).
    45What is the gradient of the line y = 7 − 3x?-3
    Working: y = 7 − 3x is the same as y = −3x + 7, so the gradient is −3.
    46What is y when x = −3 on the line y = 2x + 5?-1
    Working: 2 × (−3) = −6, then −6 + 5 = −1.
    47A line is parallel to y = 4x + 1 and crosses the y-axis at (0, 6). Write its equation. Type it like y = 2x + 5.4x+6 (also accept: 6+4x)
    Working: Parallel lines have the same gradient, 4. The new line crosses the y-axis at 6, so c = 6. The equation is y = 4x + 6.
    48(a) [2 marks] Find the midpoint of AB. Type it like (2, 5).0,5 (also accept: (0, 5))
    1. M1 for (−2 + 2) ÷ 2 or (1 + 9) ÷ 2
    2. A1 for (0, 5)
    Working: (−2 + 2) ÷ 2 = 0 and (1 + 9) ÷ 2 = 5. The midpoint is (0, 5).
    		2
    49(b) [2 marks] Find the gradient of the line AB.2
    1. M1 for (9 − 1) ÷ (2 − (−2))
    2. A1 for 2
    Working: (9 − 1) ÷ (2 − (−2)) = 8 ÷ 4 = 2.
    		2
    50(c) [3 marks] The line L is parallel to AB and crosses the y-axis at (0, −4). Write the equation of L. Type it like y = 3x + 1.2x-4 (also accept: -4+2x)
    1. M1 for gradient 2 (parallel lines have equal gradients)
    2. M1 for c = −4 from the y-axis crossing
    3. A1 for y = 2x − 4
    Working: L is parallel to AB, so its gradient is 2. It crosses the y-axis at −4, so c = −4. The equation is y = 2x − 4.
    		3
    51Find the coordinates of P. Type them like (2, 5).3,7 (also accept: (3, 7))
    1. M1 for 3x − 2 = x + 4
    2. M1 for solving to get x = 3
    3. A1 for (3, 7)
    Working: 3x − 2 = x + 4, so 2x = 6 and x = 3. Then y = 3 + 4 = 7. Check: 3 × 3 − 2 = 7. P is (3, 7).
    		3
    52For how many hours of work do the two firms charge the same? Answer in hours.3.5
    1. M1 for the two costs 45h + 60 and 55h + 25
    2. M1 for setting them equal
    3. M1 for 35 = 10h
    4. A1 for 3.5
    Working: Firm A: 45h + 60. Firm B: 55h + 25. Set them equal: 45h + 60 = 55h + 25. Take 45h and 25 from both sides: 35 = 10h, so h = 3.5. Check: A costs 45 × 3.5 + 60 = £217.50 and B costs 55 × 3.5 + 25 = £217.50. ✓
    		4
    53The line y = 2x + 3 crosses the x-axis. What is the x-coordinate of the point where it crosses? Type it as a decimal or a fraction.-1.5 (also accept: -3/2)
    Working: On the x-axis y = 0. So 0 = 2x + 3, which gives 2x = −3 and x = −1.5.
    54Higher The points A(1, 2) and B(7, 6) are joined by a straight line. What is the gradient of a line perpendicular to AB? Type it like 3/4 or −2.-3/2 (also accept: -1.5)
    Working: Gradient of AB = (6 − 2) ÷ (7 − 1) = 4/6 = 2/3. Flip it and change the sign: −3/2, which is −1.5. Check: 2/3 × (−3/2) = −1.
    55Show that the lines y = 3x + 1 and y = 3x − 5 never meet.Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for setting 3x + 1 = 3x − 5
    2. M1 for simplifying to 1 = −5, which is impossible
    3. C1 for concluding that the lines never meet (they are parallel)
    Working: Where they meet, 3x + 1 = 3x − 5. Take 3x from both sides: 1 = −5, which is never true. So there is no point where they meet. (Both lines have gradient 3, so they are parallel.)
    		3
    56A waste pipe must fall 1 cm for every 40 cm of its length (a gradient of 1 in 40). A run of pipe is 3.2 m long. How many cm must it fall over that run?8
    Working: 3.2 m = 320 cm. 320 ÷ 40 = 8, so the pipe must fall 8 cm.
    57A wheelchair ramp must not be steeper than a gradient of 1/12 (1 cm of rise for every 12 cm along). A doorstep is 18 cm high. What is the shortest horizontal length of ramp, in cm?216
    Working: Each 1 cm of rise needs 12 cm along. 18 × 12 = 216 cm. Check: 18 ÷ 216 = 1/12. ✓
    58An electrician’s bill in pounds is C = 42h + 70, where h is the hours worked. A bill comes to £238. How many hours were worked?4
    Working: 42h + 70 = 238, so 42h = 168 and h = 4. The electrician worked 4 hours.
    59On a plan (in metres) a beam runs from (1, 2) to (9, 12). A support goes under its midpoint. Write the coordinates of the support, like (2, 5).5,7 (also accept: (5, 7))
    Working: (1 + 9) ÷ 2 = 5 and (2 + 12) ÷ 2 = 7. The support is at (5, 7).
    60Firm A hires out scaffolding at a cost of C = 14d + 85 pounds for d days. Firm B charges C = 20d + 25. For how many days is the cost the same?10
    1. M1 for setting 14d + 85 = 20d + 25
    2. M1 for 60 = 6d
    3. A1 for 10
    Working: Set the costs equal: 14d + 85 = 20d + 25. Take 14d and 25 from both sides: 60 = 6d, so d = 10. Check: A costs 14 × 10 + 85 = £225 and B costs 20 × 10 + 25 = £225. ✓
    		3
    61On a plan, a pipe runs along the line y = 0.5x + 2. A second pipe must run parallel to it and pass through (0, 7). Write the equation of the second pipe. Type it like y = 0.5x + 7.0.5x+7 (also accept: x/2+7, 1/2x+7, 7+0.5x, (1/2)x+7)
    Working: Parallel lines have the same gradient, 0.5. The second pipe crosses the y-axis at 7, so c = 7. The equation is y = 0.5x + 7.
