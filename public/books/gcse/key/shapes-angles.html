    Shapes and angles: answers

    54 questions, 4 of them marked (10 marks in all). Question numbers match the page. Use your browser's Print for a copy.

    Answers for Shapes and angles
    No.QuestionAnswerMarks
    1Two angles sit side by side on a straight line. One is 110°. How big is the other, in degrees? [Figure: Two angles on a straight line]70
    Working: 180 − 110 = 70°.
    2Work out 180 − 74.106
    Working: 180 − 74 = 106.
    3Work out 360 ÷ 5.72
    Working: 360 ÷ 5 = 72.
    4Work out 3 × 60. [Figure: The four types of angle]180
    Working: 3 × 60 = 180.
    5An angle of 135° is…obtuse
    Working: 135° is between 90° and 180°, so it is obtuse.
    6Which of these is a reflex angle?270°
    Working: 270° is between 180° and 360°.
    7How many degrees are there in a full turn?360
    Working: A full turn is 360°.
    8How many degrees are there in a right angle? [Figure: Angles on a straight line]90
    Working: 360 ÷ 4 = 90°.
    9Two angles on a straight line are 65° and x. Find x. [Figure: Angles on a straight line: 65 degrees and x]115
    Working: 180 − 65 = 115°.
    10Four angles around a point are 90°, 120°, 100° and x. Find x. [Figure: Angles around a point]50
    Working: 360 − 310 = 50°.
    11Two straight lines cross. One of the angles is 48°. How big is the angle directly opposite it? [Figure: Two straight lines crossing]48
    Working: Vertically opposite angles are equal, so 48°.
    12Two angles on a straight line are x and 2x. Find x. [Figure: Angles on a straight line: x and 2x]60
    Working: 3x = 180, so x = 60° (and the other angle is 120°).
    13A right angle is split into two angles. One is 35°. Find the other angle. [Figure: A right angle split into two angles]55
    Working: 90 − 35 = 55°.
    14Two angles of a triangle are 50° and 70°. Find the third angle. [Figure: A triangle with two angles known]60
    Working: 50 + 70 = 120, so 180 − 120 = 60°.
    15An isosceles triangle has a 40° angle between its two equal sides. Find each base angle. [Figure: An isosceles triangle with a 40 degree top angle]70
    Working: (180 − 40) ÷ 2 = 70°.
    16A right-angled triangle has a 90° angle and an angle of 35°. Find the third angle. [Figure: A right-angled triangle with a 35 degree angle]55
    Working: 180 − 90 − 35 = 55°.
    17What is each angle of an equilateral triangle? [Figure: An equilateral triangle]60
    Working: 180 ÷ 3 = 60°.
    18The angles of a triangle are x, 2x and 3x. Find x. [Figure: A triangle with angles x, 2x and 3x]30
    Working: 6x = 180, so x = 30°. (The angles are 30°, 60° and 90°.)
    19Three angles of a quadrilateral are 90°, 110° and 70°. Find the fourth angle. [Figure: A quadrilateral with three angles known]90
    Working: 360 − 270 = 90°.
    20Work out the sum of the interior angles of a pentagon. [Figure: A pentagon]540
    Working: 3 × 180 = 540°.
    21Work out the sum of the interior angles of a hexagon. [Figure: A hexagon]720
    Working: 4 × 180 = 720°.
    22Find each exterior angle (turn) of a regular octagon. [Figure: Exterior angle of a regular octagon]45
    Working: 360 ÷ 8 = 45°.
    23Find each interior angle of a regular hexagon. [Figure: Interior angle of a regular hexagon]120
    Working: (6 − 2) × 180 ÷ 6 = 120°.
    24Two parallel lines are crossed by a third. One angle is 70°. What is the alternate angle? [Figure: Parallel lines: alternate angles]70°
    Working: 70°. Alternate angles are equal.
    25Two parallel lines are crossed by a third line. One angle is 70°. Find the co-interior angle to it. [Figure: Parallel lines: co-interior angles]110
    Working: 180 − 70 = 110°.
    26Two parallel lines are crossed by a third line. One angle is 70°. Find the corresponding angle to it. [Figure: Parallel lines: corresponding angles]70
    Working: 70°. Corresponding angles are equal.
    27Which pair of angles adds up to 180° when the lines are parallel?Co-interior
    Working: Co-interior angles add to 180°.
    28Parallel lines are crossed by a third line. The angle marked x is 70°, the same as the angle marked 70°. Which reason earns the mark? [Figure: Parallel lines: angle x and 70 degrees]Alternate angles are equal.
    Working: x = 70° because alternate angles are equal.
    29Parallel lines are crossed by a third line. One angle is 70°. The angle marked x is 110°. Which reason earns the mark? [Figure: Parallel lines: angle x and 70 degrees]Co-interior angles add up to 180°.
    Working: x = 180 − 70 = 110° because co-interior angles add up to 180°.
    30Parallel lines are crossed by a third line. The angle marked x is 70°, the same as the angle marked 70°. Which reason earns the mark? [Figure: Parallel lines: angle x and 70 degrees]Corresponding angles are equal.
    Working: x = 70° because corresponding angles are equal.
    31A regular polygon has exterior angles of 36°. How many sides does it have? [Figure: Exterior angle of a regular polygon]10
    Working: 360 ÷ 36 = 10 sides.
    32Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 150°. How many sides does it have? [Figure: Interior angle of a regular polygon]12
    Working: Exterior angle = 30°. 360 ÷ 30 = 12 sides.
    33Could a triangle have angles 50°, 60° and 80°?No: they add up to 190°
    Working: 50 + 60 + 80 = 190, not 180, so no triangle has these angles.
    34Could a quadrilateral have angles 100°, 80°, 90° and 90°?Yes: they add up to 360°
    Working: 100 + 80 + 90 + 90 = 360, so it could be.
    35A triangle has angles 3x, 4x and 5x. Find the largest angle. [Figure: A triangle with angles 3x, 4x and 5x]75
    Working: x = 15°, so the angles are 45°, 60° and 75°. The largest is 75°.
    36Ravi says: “The angles in a triangle are 60°, 70° and 40°, which add up to 170°, so it must be fine.” What went wrong?Triangle angles must add up to exactly 180°.
    Working: 60 + 70 + 40 = 170°. That is 10° short of 180°, so it is not a triangle.
    37Tia says: “A regular pentagon has interior angles of 5 × 180° = 900°, so each is 180°.” What went wrong?She used n, not n − 2. 5 × 180 is too big.
    Working: The total is (5 − 2) × 180 = 540°. Each angle is 540 ÷ 5 = 108°.
    38Uma says: “Co-interior angles are equal.” What went wrong?Co-interior angles add up to 180°. They are not equal.
    Working: Equal angles are alternate or corresponding. Co-interior angles add up to 180°.
    39An isosceles triangle has a top angle of 48°. Find a base angle. [Figure: An isosceles triangle with a 48 degree top angle]66
    Working: 132 ÷ 2 = 66°.
    40Find each interior angle of a regular pentagon. [Figure: Interior angle of a regular pentagon]108
    Working: (5 − 2) × 180 ÷ 5 = 108°.
    41Two angles on a straight line are 2x and x + 30, in degrees. Find x. (Uses solving equations.) [Figure: Angles on a straight line: 2x and x + 30]50
    Working: 3x + 30 = 180, so 3x = 150 and x = 50.
    42Two parallel lines are crossed by a third line. Two co-interior angles are x and 2x. Find x. [Figure: Parallel lines: co-interior angles x and 2x]60
    Working: 3x = 180, so x = 60°.
    43What is the sum of the interior angles of a 15-sided polygon?2340
    Working: 13 × 180 = 2,340°.
    44(a) [3 marks] Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC. Angle A = 48°. Work out angle B. [Figure: Isosceles triangle ABC with AB = AC]66
    1. M1 for 180 − 48 = 132 (angles in a triangle add to 180°)
    2. M1 for dividing by 2, because the base angles are equal
    3. A1 for 66
    Working: Angles in a triangle add to 180°: 180 − 48 = 132. Base angles are equal: 132 ÷ 2 = 66°.
    		3
    45(b) [2 marks] Work out the size of one interior angle of a regular pentagon. [Figure: Interior angle of a regular pentagon]108
    1. M1 for 3 × 180 = 540, the sum of the interior angles
    2. A1 for 540 ÷ 5 = 108
    Working: 3 × 180 = 540 and 540 ÷ 5 = 108°.
    		2
    46(c) [2 marks] Two parallel lines are crossed by a third. One angle is 65°. Find the co-interior angle to it. [Figure: Parallel lines with a 65 degree angle]115
    1. M1 for 180 − 65 (co-interior angles add up to 180°)
    2. A1 for 115
    Working: 180 − 65 = 115° (co-interior angles add up to 180°).
    		2
    47Work out the sum of the interior angles of a 12-sided polygon.1800
    Working: 10 × 180 = 1,800°.
    48Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 140°. How many sides does it have? [Figure: Interior angle of a regular polygon]9
    Working: 360 ÷ 40 = 9 sides (a nonagon).
    49Five people sit round a circular table with five identical chairs. Two seatings count as the same if everyone has the same neighbours on their left and right, so turning everyone one place round the table makes no difference. A seating and its mirror image count as different. In how many different ways can they sit?24
    Working: Fix one person in a chair, since turning the table gives the same seating. The other 4 people fill the other 4 chairs in 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24 ways. So there are 24 different seatings. Check: with numbered chairs there are 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120 ways, and each seating appears 5 times (once for each turn), so 120 ÷ 5 = 24.
    50A pipe offset uses two 45° bends. At the first bend the pipe turns through 45°. Angle x is the angle inside the bend, between the straight piece before it and the sloping piece. Find angle x, in degrees. [Figure: A pipe offset made with two 45 degree bends]135
    Working: The turn (45°) and angle x are on a straight line, and angles on a straight line add to 180°. So x = 180 − 45 = 135°.
    51Four brackets are fixed round a flue pipe where it passes through a wall. Looking at the flue end-on, the angles between neighbouring brackets are 82°, 104°, 97° and x. Find x, in degrees.77
    Working: Angles round a point add up to 360°. 82 + 104 + 97 = 283, so x = 360 − 283 = 77°.
    52A roof truss is an isosceles triangle. The angle at the top is 110°. Find one of the two equal base angles, x, in degrees. [Figure: A roof truss]35
    Working: Angles in a triangle add to 180°, so the two base angles total 180 − 110 = 70°. They are equal, so each is 70 ÷ 2 = 35°.
    53A pipe loops round a pillar and ends up pointing the way it started. It turns the same way at every bend, through 90°, 45°, 75°, 60°, 50° and x. A full loop is a turn of 360°. Find x, in degrees.40
    1. M1 for adding the known turns: 320
    2. M1 for using 360 for a full turn
    3. A1 for x = 40
    Working: The turns add up to 360°. Known turns: 90 + 45 + 75 + 60 + 50 = 320. x = 360 − 320 = 40°.
    		3
    54A symmetrical roof truss has two base angles of 38°. A vertical post goes straight down from the top to the middle of the tie beam. What angle does the post make with each sloping rafter, in degrees?52
    Working: The post meets the tie beam at 90°, so each half is a right-angled triangle. Its angles add to 180°: 90 + 38 + angle = 180, so the angle is 52°. (The top angle of the whole truss is 104°, and half of it is 52° ✓.)
