    Bearings: answers

    55 questions, 5 of them marked (18 marks in all). Question numbers match the page. Use your browser's Print for a copy.

    Open the questions Back to the teacher page
    Answers for Bearings
    No.QuestionAnswerMarks
    1How many degrees are there in a full turn?360
    Working: A full turn is 4 quarter turns: 4 × 90 = 360 degrees.
    2Angles on a straight line add up to 180°. One angle is 65°. What is the other angle, in degrees?115
    Working: The two angles add up to 180°. 180 − 65 = 115°.
    3Two lines are parallel. A third line crosses both. One angle is 70°, and the alternate angle is in the Z shape. What is the alternate angle, in degrees?70
    Working: Alternate angles on parallel lines are equal, so the angle is 70°.
    4A map has a scale of 1 : 50 000. Two paths are 3 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they in real life, in km? [Figure: A compass with eight points]1.5
    Working: 3 × 50 000 = 150 000 cm = 1500 m = 1.5 km.
    5What is the three-figure bearing of due west?270
    Working: West is three quarter turns clockwise from north: 3 × 90 = 270°.
    6Which three-figure bearing is exactly half way between north (000°) and east (090°)?045
    Working: (0 + 90) / 2 = 45. As a three-figure bearing that is 045°. This is north-east.
    7Which compass point is on a bearing of 225°?South-west
    Working: 225 is half way between 180 (south) and 270 (west), so the direction is south-west.
    8A ferry sails due south-east. What is its three-figure bearing? [Figure: Finding the bearing of B from A]135
    Working: (90 + 180) / 2 = 135. The bearing is 135°.
    9A bearing is always written with…three figures, like 045
    Working: A bearing is written with three figures: 005°, 045°, 145°. That makes every bearing the same length, so none can be misread.
    10Find the bearing of B from A, in degrees. The angle is marked. [Figure: Finding the bearing of B from A]128
    Working: The angle is measured clockwise from north at A, so the bearing is 128°.
    11Find the bearing of B from A, in degrees. The angle shown is measured anticlockwise from north. [Figure: A bearing measured anticlockwise]320
    Working: 360 − 40 = 320. The bearing of B from A is 320°.
    12A walker on a hill heads off on a path that is 7° clockwise from north. What is her three-figure bearing? Type it like 007.007
    Working: The angle is 7° clockwise from north. As three figures, that is 007°.
    13A boat is 20° south of due west . West is 270° and south is 180°. What is the three-figure bearing of the boat? (Moving from west towards south means the angle gets smaller.) [Figure: A bearing and its back bearing]250
    Working: Turning from west (270°) towards south (180°) makes the bearing smaller. 270 − 20 = 250°.
    14The bearing of B from A is 040°. What is the bearing of A from B, in degrees?220
    Working: 40 is less than 180, so add 180: 40 + 180 = 220. The bearing of A from B is 220°.
    15The bearing of B from A is 285°. What is the bearing of A from B, in degrees?105
    Working: 285 is more than 180, so subtract 180: 285 − 180 = 105. The bearing of A from B is 105°.
    16A ship sails from a port on a bearing of 135°. On what bearing must it sail to return to the port?315
    Working: 135 + 180 = 315. The ship must sail on a bearing of 315°.
    17The bearing of a lighthouse from a boat is 072°. What is the bearing of the boat from the lighthouse? [Figure: A two-leg journey drawn to scale]252°
    Working: 72 is less than 180, so add 180: 72 + 180 = 252. The bearing of the boat from the lighthouse is 252°.
    18A scale drawing uses 1 cm to 2 km. A path is 7 km long in real life. How long is the path on the drawing, in cm?3.5
    Working: 7 ÷ 2 = 3.5 cm.
    19A scale drawing uses 1 cm to 5 km. A ship's path measures 6.4 cm on the drawing. How long is the real path, in km?32
    Working: 6.4 × 5 = 32 km.
    20The scale drawing shows a walk from A to B and then B to C. The scale is 1 cm to 2 km. The path from B to C is 4 cm on the drawing. How long is the real path from B to C, in km? [Figure: A two-leg journey drawn to scale]8
    Working: The leg from B to C is 4 cm on the drawing. 4 × 2 = 8 km.
    21In the same scale drawing, the bearing of C from B is 130° and the bearing of B from A is 040°. What is the bearing of A from B, in degrees? [Figure: A two-leg journey drawn to scale]220
    Working: The back bearing of 040° is 040 + 180 = 220°. So the bearing of A from B is 220°.
    22You are drawing a journey with a protractor. What do you do first at the start of each leg? [Figure: A journey split into north and east]Draw a north line at the starting point
    Working: A bearing is measured clockwise from north. So you first draw a north line through the start point, then measure the angle from it.
    23A ship sails 50 km on a bearing of 030°. How far north does it go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in km.43.3
    Working: North = 50 × cos 30° = 43.30… ≈ 43.3 km.
    24A hiker walks 80 m on a bearing of 025°. How far east does she go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m.33.8
    Working: East = 80 × sin 25° = 33.809… ≈ 33.8 m.
    25A drone flies 120 m on a bearing of 065°. How far east does it go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m. [Figure: A drone flight split into north and east]108.8
    Working: East = 120 × sin 65° = 108.757… ≈ 108.8 m.
    26A cyclist rides 5 km due north and then 12 km due east. How far is she from her start, in a straight line, in km?13
    Working: Distance² = 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169. Distance = the square root of 169 = 13 km.
    27A ship sails 20 km on a bearing of 040°. Which calculation gives how far north it goes? [Figure: Forwards: a bearing and a distance to north and east]20 × cos 40
    Working: The north side is next to the 40° angle, so it is the adjacent side. adjacent = hypotenuse × cos(angle) = 20 × cos 40° = 15.3 km.
    28A drone flies 7 m north and then 24 m east. How far is it from where it started, in m?25
    Working: Distance² = 7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625. Distance = the square root of 625 = 25 m.
    29The same drone ends 7 m north and 24 m east of its start. Find the bearing of the drone from its start. Give your answer to 1 decimal place, as a three-figure bearing, like 053.1.073.7
    Working: tan(bearing) = 24 ÷ 7 = 3.428…. Bearing = tan^−1(3.428…) = 73.74…° ≈ 73.7°. As a three-figure bearing, 073.7°.
    30A cyclist rides 10 km north and then 10 km east. What is the three-figure bearing of her finish from her start?045
    Working: tan(bearing) = 10 ÷ 10 = 1. tan^−1(1) = 45°. As a three-figure bearing, 045°.
    31A walker finishes on a bearing of 045° from her start. She went 12 km east. How far north did she go, in km? (Hint: what is tan 45°?) [Figure: A journey on a bearing of 130 degrees]12
    Working: tan 45° = 1, so east ÷ north = 1. The north distance equals the east distance, so north = 12 km.
    32Higher A plane flies 100 km on a bearing of 150°. How far south does it go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in km.86.6
    Working: The angle from the south line is 180 − 150 = 30°. South = 100 × cos 30° = 86.60… ≈ 86.6 km.
    33Higher A ship sails 40 km on a bearing of 250°. How far west does it go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in km.37.6
    Working: The angle from the south line is 250 − 180 = 70°. West = 40 × sin 70° = 37.58… ≈ 37.6 km.
    34Higher A walker goes 25 km on a bearing of 310°. How far north does she go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in km.16.1
    Working: The angle from the north line is 360 − 310 = 50°. North = 25 × cos 50° = 16.06… ≈ 16.1 km.
    35Higher A walker goes 3 km north and then 4 km west. Find the bearing of her finish from her start. Give your answer to 1 decimal place, as a three-figure bearing.306.9
    Working: The angle west of north = tan^−1(4 ÷ 3) = 53.13…° ≈ 53.1°. Bearing = 360 − 53.1 = 306.9°.
    36Fay says: the angle from north to the path is 40°, measured anticlockwise, so the bearing is 040°. What went wrong?She measured anticlockwise. It is 360 − 40 = 320°
    Working: Bearings are measured clockwise. An angle of 40° anticlockwise from north is 360 − 40 = 320° clockwise, so the bearing is 320°.
    37Gus says: the back bearing of 130° is 130° − 180° = −50°. What went wrong?130 is below 180, so add 180: 310°
    Working: 130 is less than 180, so add 180: 130 + 180 = 310°. Subtracting would give a negative number, which cannot be a bearing.
    38Hal says: a ship sailing 20 km on a bearing of 040° goes 20 × sin 40° = 12.9 km north. What went wrong?North is next to the angle, so use cos: 15.3 km
    Working: The north line is next to the 40° angle (adjacent). adjacent = hypotenuse × cos(angle) = 20 × cos 40° = 15.3 km. The 12.9 km is the east distance.
    39A walker goes 9 km due east and then 12 km due north. How far is she from her start, in km?15
    Working: Distance² = 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225. Distance = the square root of 225 = 15 km.
    40A map has a scale of 1 : 20 000. A path is 8 cm long on the map. How long is the real path, in km?1.6
    Working: 8 × 20 000 = 160 000 cm = 1600 m = 1.6 km.
    41From a hilltop A, the bearing of a church B is 070° and the bearing of a farm C is 130°. What is the size of angle BAC, in degrees?60
    Working: Angle BAC = 130 − 70 = 60°.
    42A ship has sailed 3/5 of a 250 km trip. How many km are left?100
    Working: 2/5 is left. 2/5 of 250 = 250 ÷ 5 × 2 = 100 km.
    43Surveying. A surveyor stands at point A. A corner of a plot, B, is 45 m away on a bearing of 060°. How far east of A is B? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m.39.0
    Working: East = 45 × sin 60° = 38.97… ≈ 39.0 m.
    44Electrical. A cable runs from a junction box 18 m north and 24 m east to a new socket, in a straight line across a large floor space. Twin and earth cable costs £1.80 per metre. How much does the cable cost? Give your answer in pounds, like 12.50.54.00
    Working: Length² = 18² + 24² = 324 + 576 = 900. Length = 30 m. Cost = 30 × 1.80 = £54.00.
    45Plumbing. On a site plan, north is up the page. A pipe run is 12 m long and goes on a bearing of 070°. How far north does the pipe go? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in m.4.1
    Working: North = 12 × cos 70° = 4.104… ≈ 4.1 m.
    46Setting out. From peg A, peg B is on a bearing of 112°. An engineer at peg B wants to look back at peg A. What bearing should she look on, in degrees?292
    Working: 112 is less than 180, so add 180: 112 + 180 = 292°.
    47Navigation. A boat leaves a harbour and sails 15 km north and then 20 km east. What is the bearing of the boat from the harbour? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, as a three-figure bearing, like 053.1.053.1
    Working: tan(bearing) = 20 ÷ 15 = 1.333…. Bearing = tan^−1(1.333…) = 53.13…° ≈ 53.1°. As a three-figure bearing, 053.1°.
    48A ship sails 24 km north and then 7 km east. Work out the straight-line distance from its start to its finish, in km. [Figure: A ship sails north then east]25
    1. M1 for 24² + 7² (576 + 49)
    2. M1 for the square root of 625
    3. A1 for 25
    Working: Distance² = 24² + 7² = 576 + 49 = 625. Distance = the square root of 625 = 25 km.
    		3
    49The same ship sails 24 km north and then 7 km east. Work out the bearing of the ship from its start. Give your answer to 1 decimal place, as a three-figure bearing, like 053.1.016.3
    1. M1 for tan(bearing) = 7 ÷ 24 (east over north)
    2. M1 for tan^−1(7 ÷ 24)
    3. A1 for 16.3
    4. B1 for writing the bearing with three figures, 016.3
    Working: tan(bearing) = 7 ÷ 24 = 0.2916…. Bearing = tan^−1(0.2916…) = 16.26…° ≈ 16.3°. As a three-figure bearing, 016.3°.
    		4
    50A, B and C are three points. The bearing of B from A is 072°. The bearing of C from B is 162°. Work out the size of angle ABC, in degrees. [Figure: A journey from A to B to C]90
    1. M1 for the bearing of A from B: 072 + 180 = 252
    2. M1 for 252 − 162
    3. A1 for 90
    Working: The bearing of A from B = 072 + 180 = 252°. Angle ABC = 252 − 162 = 90°.
    		3
    51Higher [4 marks] A boat sails 6 km on a bearing of 040° from A to B. It then sails 8 km on a bearing of 130° from B to C. Show that C is 10 km from A. [Figure: A two-leg journey with a right angle at B]Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. C1 for the bearing of A from B: 040 + 180 = 220, so angle ABC = 220 − 130
    2. M1 for angle ABC = 90°, so AC² = 6² + 8²
    3. M1 for AC² = 36 + 64 = 100
    4. A1 for AC = 10 km, shown
    Working: The bearing of A from B is 040 + 180 = 220°. So angle ABC = 220 − 130 = 90°. Triangle ABC has a right angle at B. AC² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100. AC = the square root of 100 = 10 km.
    		4
    52Higher [4 marks] The boat in the last question sails 6 km on a bearing of 040° from A to B, then 8 km on a bearing of 130° from B to C. There is a right angle at B. Work out the bearing of C from A. Give your answer to 1 decimal place, as a three-figure bearing. [Figure: A two-leg journey with a right angle at B]093.1
    1. M1 for tan(angle BAC) = 8 ÷ 6
    2. M1 for angle BAC = 53.1°
    3. M1 for 040 + 53.1
    4. A1 for 093.1
    Working: Angle BAC = tan^−1(8 ÷ 6) = 53.13…° ≈ 53.1°. The bearing of C from A = 040 + 53.1 = 093.1°.
    		4
    53A walker leaves camp A and walks 5 km on a bearing of 090°. She turns right through 120° (so her bearing goes up by 120°) and walks 5 km. She turns right through 120° again and walks 5 km. What is the bearing of her third leg, in degrees? (Where does she end up?) [Figure: A walk with three equal legs]330
    Working: Leg 1: 090°. After the first turn, leg 2: 090 + 120 = 210°. After the second turn, leg 3: 210 + 120 = 330°. The three legs make an equilateral triangle, so she ends up back at camp.
    54Two lighthouses are 5 km apart, with L2 due east of L1. A ship is seen from L1 on a bearing of 045° and from L2 on a bearing of 315°. How far is the ship from L1? Give your answer to 1 decimal place, in km. [Figure: Two lighthouses and a ship]3.5
    Working: At L1 the angle between L1 to L2 (east, 090°) and L1 to the ship (045°) is 45°. At L2 the angle between west (270°) and 315° is 45°. The angle at the ship is 180 − 45 − 45 = 90°. L1 to L2 (5 km) is the hypotenuse. L1 to the ship = 5 × cos 45° = 3.535… ≈ 3.5 km.
    55Higher A boat leaves a port P and sails 12 km on a bearing of 060° to B. It then sails 5 km on a bearing of 150° to C. Work out the bearing of the port P from C. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [Figure: A dog-leg journey from a port]262.6
    Working: The bearing of P from B is 060 + 180 = 240°. Angle PBC = 240 − 150 = 90°. Angle BPC = tan^−1(5 ÷ 12) = 22.62…°. The bearing of C from P = 060 + 22.62 = 82.62°. The bearing of P from C is the back bearing: 82.62 + 180 = 262.62…° ≈ 262.6°.
