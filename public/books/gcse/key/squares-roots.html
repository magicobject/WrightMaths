    1A square tile has sides of 7 cm. What is its area, in cm²? [Figure: A square tile 7 cm by 7 cm]49
    Working: 7 × 7 = 49 cm².
    2Last time: how many factors does 36 have?9
    Working: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36: nine. (6 is its own partner, so it is counted once.)
    3What is the HCF of 12 and 18?6
    Working: Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 6. HCF = 6.
    4Work out 49 ÷ 7.7
    Working: 49 ÷ 7 = 7, exactly. So 49 is a multiple of 7.

    Working: 225 + 64 = 289 and √289 = 17.
    15Between which two whole numbers does √50 lie?7 and 8
    Working: 7² = 49 and 8² = 64. 50 is between them, so √50 is between 7 and 8.

    Working: √30 = 5.477… ≈ 5.5 (1 d.p.).

    Working: 6.3² = 39.69, very close to 40.
    18Use a calculator to find √70 to 1 decimal place.8.4
    Working: √70 = 8.366… ≈ 8.4 (1 d.p.).
    19Write √45 in the form a √ b , with b as small as possible. (Type it like 3√5 or 3sqrt5.)3√5 (also accept: 3sqrt5, 3sqrt(5))
    Working: √45 = √(9 × 5) = √9 × √5 = 3√5.
    20Write √75 in its simplest form. (Type it like 5√3 or 5sqrt3.)5√3 (also accept: 5sqrt3, 5sqrt(3))
    Working: √75 = √(25 × 3) = 5√3.
    21Write √72 in its simplest form. (Type it like 6√2 or 6sqrt2.)6√2 (also accept: 6sqrt2, 6sqrt(2))
    Working: √72 = √(36 × 2) = 6√2. (Or √72 = 2√18 = 2 × 3√2 = 6√2 in two steps.)
    22Work out 3^3. [Figure: A cube with edge 3]27
    Working: 9 × 3 = 27.
    23Work out 4^3. [Figure: A cube with edge 4]64
    Working: 16 × 4 = 64.
    24Work out 10^3.1000
    Working: 100 × 10 = 1,000.
    25Find the cube root of 125. (That is, which number cubed gives 125?) [Figure: A cube with volume 125]5
    Working: 5 × 5 × 5 = 125.
    26Work out (−2)^3. [Figure: Forwards: a side to an area]-8
    Working: 4 × (−2) = −8. An odd number of negatives gives a negative.
    27A square has an area of 81 cm². What is the length of one side, in cm? [Figure: A square with area 81 square centimetres]9
    Working: √81 = 9 cm.
    28A square has a perimeter of 20 cm. What is its area, in cm²? [Figure: A square with perimeter 20 centimetres]25
    Working: Side = 20 ÷ 4 = 5 cm. Area = 5² = 25 cm².
    29√x = 7. What is x?49
    Working: 7² = 49. Check: √49 = 7.
    30x² = 169. Write down the positive solution for x.13
    Working: x = 13 or x = −13. The positive solution is 13.
    31How many different numbers satisfy x² = 49?2
    Working: x = 7 and x = −7: two solutions.
    32Ian says: “6² = 12.” What went wrong? [Figure: A square of side 6]He doubled it: 6² = 36.
    Working: He doubled it. 6² = 6 × 6 = 36.
    33Jen says: “√(9 + 16) = √9 + √16 = 7.” What went wrong?Add first: √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5.
    Working: In general a root does not split over an addition. Add first: √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5.
    34Kit says: “√16 = 8.” What went wrong?He halved it: 4 × 4 = 16.
    Working: He halved it. √16 = 4 because 4 × 4 = 16.
    35Work out 11² + 5².146
    Working: 11² = 121 and 5² = 25, so 146.
    36Work out √(100 − 36).8
    Working: 100 − 36 = 64 and √64 = 8.
    37Work out √(13² − 5²).12
    Working: 169 − 25 = 144 and √144 = 12.
    38Which of these is NOT a square number: 36, 48, 100?48
    Working: 48 is between 36 and 49, so it is not a square.
    39Work out 2^3 + 3².17
    Working: 2^3 = 8 and 3² = 9: 17.
    40(a) [2 marks] A square garden has an area of 144 m². Work out the perimeter of the garden, in m. [Figure: A square garden with area 144 square metres]48
    1. M1 for √144 = 12
    2. A1 for 4 × 12 = 48
    Working: √144 = 12 m, so the perimeter is 4 × 12 = 48 m.

    41(b) [2 marks] Work out √(5² − 3²).4
    1. M1 for 25 − 9 = 16
    2. A1 for √16 = 4
    Working: 25 − 9 = 16 and √16 = 4. (This is a 3, 4, 5 right-angled triangle.)

    42(c) [2 marks] Use a calculator to work out √90. Give your answer to 1 decimal place.9.5
    1. M1 for √90 = 9.486… from the calculator
    2. A1 for 9.5, rounded to 1 decimal place
    Working: √90 = 9.486… ≈ 9.5 (1 d.p.).

    43What is the smallest whole number you can multiply 12 by to get a square number?3
    Working: 12 × 3 = 36 = 6².
    44How many square numbers are there between 100 and 200?4
    Working: 121, 144, 169, 196: four square numbers (10² = 100 and 15² = 225 are outside).
    45A rational number can be written as a fraction of two whole numbers. Which statement about √2 is correct?It is irrational: no fraction of whole numbers equals it.
    Working: No fraction of two whole numbers equals √2, so it is irrational. The two descriptions go together: a number that is not a fraction of whole numbers has a decimal that never ends and never repeats, and the other way round. So its decimal, 1.41421…, never ends and never repeats. (Ending and repeating decimals can always be written as fractions.)
    46Work out 31² − 30².61
    Working: 961 − 900 = 61. In general, n ² and ( n + 1)² differ by 2 n + 1. Here n = 30, so 2 × 30 + 1 = 61.
    47The difference between two consecutive square numbers is always odd. Use the pattern to work out 25² − 24² without squaring either number.49
    Working: 2 × 24 + 1 = 49. Check: 625 − 576 = 49. ✓
    48Here are four lines from a proof that √2 is irrational. Which line is the contradiction?So p and q are both even, which contradicts “no common factor”.
    Working: The proof runs in this order: assume √2 = p/q in lowest terms; p² = 2q² so p² is even, so p is even (if p were odd, p² would be odd too); p = 2k gives q even; so p and q are both even. That disagrees with the assumption that they have no common factor, so no such fraction exists.
    49The expression n ² + n + 41 gives a prime number for every whole number n from 0 to 39. Find the smallest whole number n that shows it does not always give a prime.40
    Working: When n = 40: 1600 + 40 + 41 = 1681 = 41 × 41, which is not prime. One counter-example is enough to show a claim is false. (Forty examples in a row were all primes, which is why examples alone are not a proof.)
    50Which line is a correct algebraic proof that the sum of three consecutive whole numbers is always a multiple of 3?n + ( n + 1) + ( n + 2) = 3 n + 3 = 3( n + 1), a multiple of 3.
    Working: n + ( n + 1) + ( n + 2) = 3 n + 3 = 3( n + 1). This has a factor 3 whatever whole number n is, so it is a multiple of 3.
    51Work out √(10 × 11 × 12 × 13 + 1). (The number under the root is a perfect square, so the answer is a whole number. Look for a pattern rather than using a calculator.)131
    Working: The pattern: √(1 × 2 × 3 × 4 + 1) = √25 = 5, √(2 × 3 × 4 × 5 + 1) = √121 = 11, √(3 × 4 × 5 × 6 + 1) = √361 = 19, and 5, 11, 19 are n ² + 3 n + 1. Why: let k = n ( n + 3) = n ² + 3 n . Then ( n + 1)( n + 2) = k + 2, so the product plus 1 is k ( k + 2) + 1 = ( k + 1)². For n = 10, k = 130, so the root is 131. Check: 131² = 17,161 = 17,160 + 1.
    52Non-calculator Grade 8 a and b are any two numbers. Show that a² + b² is never less than 2ab. (Try a = 3 and b = 5 first.)Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. B1 for (a − b)² ≥ 0, because a square cannot be negative
    2. M1 for expanding to a² − 2ab + b² ≥ 0
    3. C1 for adding 2ab to both sides and stating a² + b² ≥ 2ab
    Working: A square is never negative, so (a − b)² ≥ 0 for all a and b. Expanding: a² − 2ab + b² ≥ 0. Add 2ab to both sides: a² + b² ≥ 2ab. So a² + b² is never less than 2ab. (Check with a = 3 and b = 5: 9 + 25 = 34 and 2 × 15 = 30, and 34 ≥ 30.)
    		3
    53A student tries to show that (n + 1)² − n² = 2n + 1. Line 1: (n + 1)² − n² = n² + 1 − n². Line 2: = 1. Line 3: So the difference is 1, not 2n + 1. Where is the flaw?Line 1: (n + 1)² is not n² + 1, because the middle term 2n is missing.
    Working: Line 1: (n + 1)² is not n² + 1, because the middle term 2n is missing. (n + 1)² = n² + 2n + 1, so the difference is n² + 2n + 1 − n² = 2n + 1, as it should be. Check with n = 3: 16 − 9 = 7 = 2 × 3 + 1.
