    1A point at (2, 3) is translated by 4 −1 . Write the new point. Type it like (2, 5).6,2 (also accept: (6, 2))
    Working: x: 2 + 4 = 6. y: 3 − 1 = 2. The new point is (6, 2).
    2Work out 2 × (−3).-6
    Working: 2 × (−3) = −6.
    3A right-angled triangle has two short sides of 3 cm and 4 cm. How long is the longest side? Answer in cm.5
    Working: 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25. √25 = 5 cm.
    4The point (4, 1) is reflected in the y-axis, then the image is moved 1 unit up. Write the final point. Type it like (2, 5). [Figure: Two equal vectors]-4,2 (also accept: (-4, 2))
    Working: Reflect in the y-axis: (4, 1) goes to (−4, 1). Move 1 up: (−4, 2).
    5Write the vector PQ, top number first. Type it like (3, -2), with a comma between the two numbers. [Figure: An arrow from P to Q]4,-2 (also accept: (4, -2))
    Working: From P(1, 4) to Q(5, 2): across 5 − 1 = 4, up 2 − 4 = −2. PQ = 4 −2 , typed (4, -2).
    6On a plan in metres, a pipe runs from A(1, 2) to B(7, 5). Write the vector AB. Type it like (3, -2).6,3 (also accept: (6, 3))
    Working: Across: 7 − 1 = 6. Up: 5 − 2 = 3. AB = 6 3 , typed (6, 3).
    7The vector AB is (6, 3). Write the vector BA. Type it like (3, -2).-6,-3 (also accept: (-6, -3))
    Working: BA = −AB = −6 −3 , typed (-6, -3).
    8The point A is (2, 1) and the vector AB is 3 4 . Write the coordinates of B. Type them like (2, 5).5,5 (also accept: (5, 5))
    Working: B = A + AB = (2 + 3, 1 + 4) = (5, 5).
    9Which statement is true about two arrows that have the same length and point the same way?They are the same vector
    Working: A vector is defined by its size and direction only. Arrows with the same length and direction are the same vector, wherever they are drawn.
    10A game character is at (4, 1) after moving by 2 −2 . Where did it start? Type it like (2, 5). [Figure: Adding two vectors head to tail]2,3 (also accept: (2, 3))
    Working: Start = finish − vector: x = 4 − 2 = 2. y = 1 − (−2) = 3. The start was (2, 3).
    11a = 3 4 and b = −1 2 . Work out a + b . Type it like (3, -2).2,6 (also accept: (2, 6))
    Working: Tops: 3 + (−1) = 2. Bottoms: 4 + 2 = 6. a + b = 2 6 , typed (2, 6).
    12a = 3 4 and b = −1 2 . Work out a − b . Type it like (3, -2).4,2 (also accept: (4, 2))
    Working: Tops: 3 − (−1) = 4. Bottoms: 4 − 2 = 2. a − b = 4 2 , typed (4, 2).
    13AB = 2 3 and BC = 4 −1 . Find the vector AC. Type it like (3, -2).6,2 (also accept: (6, 2))
    Working: AC = AB + BC = 2 + 4 3 + (−1) = 6 2 , typed (6, 2).
    14AC = 7 1 and AB = 3 4 . Find the vector BC. Type it like (3, -2).4,-3 (also accept: (4, -3))
    Working: BC = AC − AB = 7 − 3 1 − 4 = 4 −3 , typed (4, -3).
    15Which of these is equal to AB + BC?AC
    Working: A to B and then B to C finishes at C. That is the same as going from A straight to C: AB + BC = AC.
    16A cable goes from the consumer unit to a junction box, a journey of 5 2 , and then on to a socket, a journey of −3 −2 . What is the single vector from the consumer unit to the socket? Type it like (3, -2).2,0 (also accept: (2, 0))
    Working: 5 + (−3) 2 + (−2) = 2 0 , typed (2, 0).
    17p = 2 3 and q = 6 4 . Work out p − q . Type it like (3, -2). [Figure: A vector, double the vector and minus the vector]-4,-1 (also accept: (-4, -1))
    Working: Tops: 2 − 6 = −4. Bottoms: 3 − 4 = −1. p − q = −4 −1 , typed (-4, -1).
    18Work out 3 2 −1 . Type it like (3, -2).6,-3 (also accept: (6, -3))
    Working: 3 × 2 = 6 and 3 × (−1) = −3. The answer is 6 −3 , typed (6, -3).
    196 −9 = k 2 −3 . What is k?3
    Working: 6 ÷ 2 = 3 and (−9) ÷ (−3) = 3. So k = 3.
    20Which vector is parallel to 4 6 ?Top 2 and bottom 3
    Working: (4, 6) = 2 × (2, 3), so the vector with top 2 and bottom 3 is parallel to (4, 6). The others do not give the same multiple for the top and the bottom.
    21a = 1 2 and b = 3 −1 . Work out 2 a + 3 b . Type it like (3, -2).11,1 (also accept: (11, 1))
    Working: 2 a = 2 4 and 3 b = 9 −3 . Add: 11 1 , typed (11, 1).
    22Work out −2 3 1 . Type it like (3, -2).-6,-2 (also accept: (-6, -2))
    Working: −2 × 3 = −6 and −2 × 1 = −2. The answer is −6 −2 , typed (-6, -2).
    23Find k so that k 15 is parallel to 1 3 .5
    Working: 15 ÷ 3 = 5, so the big vector is 5 times the small one. The top is 5 × 1 = 5, so k = 5.
    24a = 3 1 and b = −1 4 . Work out 2 a − b . Type it like (3, -2). [Figure: A vector and a right-angled triangle]7,-2 (also accept: (7, -2))
    Working: 2 a = 6 2 . Subtract b : 6 − (−1) 2 − 4 = 7 −2 , typed (7, -2).
    25Find the magnitude of 3 4 .5
    Working: 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25. √25 = 5.
    26Find the magnitude of −5 12 .13
    Working: (−5)² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169. √169 = 13.
    27A is the point (1, 2) and B is the point (7, 10). Find the length AB.10
    Working: AB = (7 − 1, 10 − 2) = (6, 8). 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100. √100 = 10.
    28A drone flies 20 m east and then 21 m north. How far is it, in a straight line, from where it started? Answer in m.29
    Working: 20² + 21² = 400 + 441 = 841. √841 = 29 m.
    29What is the magnitude of −3 4 ?5
    Working: (−3)² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25. √25 = 5.
    30a = 6 8 . What is the magnitude of 2 a ? [Figure: Triangle OAB]20
    Working: | a | = √(36 + 64) = 10. So |2 a | = 2 × 10 = 20. (Check: 2 a = (12, 16), and √(144 + 256) = 20.)
    31In triangle OAB, OA = a and OB = b . Find the vector AB in terms of a and b . Type it in letters, like 2a + b. [Figure: Triangle OAB with two vectors]-a+b (also accept: b-a)
    Working: AB = AO + OB = − a + b = b − a .
    32In triangle OAB, OA = a and OB = b . Find the vector BA in terms of a and b . Type it in letters, like 2a + b. [Figure: Triangle OAB with two vectors]a-b (also accept: -b+a)
    Working: BA = BO + OA = − b + a = a − b .
    33OACB is a parallelogram with OA = a and OB = b . Find the vector OC. Type it in letters, like 2a + b. [Figure: Parallelogram OACB]a+b (also accept: b+a)
    Working: OC = OA + AC = a + b , because AC is equal and parallel to OB.
    34OACB is a parallelogram with OA = a and OB = b . Find the vector AC. Type it in letters, like 2a + b. [Figure: Parallelogram OACB]b
    Working: AC is opposite OB, so AC = OB = b .
    35OACB is a parallelogram with OA = a and OB = b . Find the vector CB. Type it in letters, like 2a + b. [Figure: Parallelogram OACB]-a
    Working: BC = OA = a . CB goes the other way, so CB = − a .
    36In triangle OAB, OA = a = 4 1 and OB = b = 1 3 . Write AB as a column vector. Type it like (3, -2).-3,2 (also accept: (-3, 2))
    Working: AB = b − a = 1 − 4 3 − 1 = −3 2 , typed (-3, 2).
    37In triangle OAB, OA = a and OB = b . P is the point with OP = 2 a + b . Find the vector AP. Type it in letters, like 2a + b. [Figure: A line AB cut in the ratio 1 to 2]a+b (also accept: b+a)
    Working: AP = AO + OP = − a + (2 a + b ) = a + b .
    38A is (2, 1) and B is (8, 7). M is the midpoint of AB. Use vectors to find the coordinates of M. Type it like (2, 5).5,4 (also accept: (5, 4))
    Working: AB = (6, 6). Half of AB = (3, 3). M = A + (3, 3) = (5, 4).
    39OA = a and OB = b . M is the midpoint of AB. Find OM in terms of a and b . Type a half as 1/2, like 2a + 1/2b. [Figure: Triangle OAB with a midpoint](a+b)/2 (also accept: 1/2(a+b), (1/2)(a+b), 0.5(a+b), 1/2a+1/2b, 0.5a+0.5b, a/2+b/2, 1/2b+1/2a, 0.5b+0.5a, b/2+a/2, (b+a)/2, 1/2(b+a), 0.5(b+a))
    Working: AB = b − a . OM = OA + 1/2AB = a + 1/2( b − a ) = 1/2 a + 1/2 b = 1/2( a + b ).
    40A is (2, 1) and B is (8, 10). P is on AB with AP : PB = 1 : 2. Find the coordinates of P. Type it like (2, 5).4,4 (also accept: (4, 4))
    Working: AB = (6, 9). AP = 1/3 of (6, 9) = (2, 3). P = (2 + 2, 1 + 3) = (4, 4).
    41A is (1, 2) and B is (10, 5). P is on AB with AP : PB = 2 : 1. Find the coordinates of P. Type it like (2, 5).7,4 (also accept: (7, 4))
    Working: AB = (9, 3). AP = 2/3 of (9, 3) = (6, 2). P = (1 + 6, 2 + 2) = (7, 4).
    42A is (0, 1), B is (2, 4) and C is (6, 10). Which statement is correct? [Figure: Three points on a grid]A, B and C are collinear, because BC = 2AB
    Working: AB = (2, 3) and BC = (4, 6) = 2AB. They are parallel and share B, so A, B and C are collinear.
    43A is (1, 1), B is (3, 2) and C is (7, 4). BC = k AB. What is k?2
    Working: AB = (2, 1) and BC = (4, 2). 4 ÷ 2 = 2 and 2 ÷ 1 = 2, so BC = 2AB and k = 2.
    44A is (1, 2), B is (4, 3) and C is (k, 5). A, B and C are in a straight line. Find k.10
    Working: AB = (3, 1). BC = (k − 4, 2). For BC to be parallel to AB, BC = 2AB = (6, 2). So k − 4 = 6, and k = 10.
    45OA = a and OB = b . M is the midpoint of OA. Find MB in terms of a and b . Type a half as 1/2, like 2a + 1/2b. [Figure: Triangle OAB with a midpoint of OA]-1/2a+b (also accept: b-1/2a, -0.5a+b, b-0.5a, -a/2+b, b-a/2, (2b-a)/2, (-a+2b)/2, 1/2(2b-a), (1/2)(2b-a))
    Working: OM = 1/2 a , so MO = −1/2 a . MB = MO + OB = −1/2 a + b .
    46PQ = a + 2 b and QR = 3 a + 6 b . Show that P, Q and R are in a straight line.Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for QR = 3( a + 2 b ) or comparing both parts
    2. C1 for QR = 3PQ, so they are parallel
    3. C1 for the common point Q, so collinear
    Working: QR = 3 a + 6 b = 3( a + 2 b ) = 3PQ. So QR is parallel to PQ. They share the point Q, so P, Q and R are in a straight line.

    47A is (0, 0), B is (4, 1), C is (6, 4) and D is (2, 3). Show that ABCD is a parallelogram.Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. B1 for AB = (4, 1)
    2. B1 for DC = (4, 1)
    3. C1 for AB = DC, so equal and parallel, so a parallelogram
    Working: AB = (4, 1). DC = C − D = (6 − 2, 4 − 3) = (4, 1). So AB = DC: the two sides are equal in length and parallel. A quadrilateral with a pair of equal parallel opposite sides is a parallelogram.
    		3
    48OA = a and OB = b . Show that AB = b − a . [Figure: Forwards: two points to the vector between them]Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for AB = AO + OB, with AO = − a
    2. C1 for − a + b = b − a stated
    Working: AB = AO + OB = − a + b = b − a .
    		2
    49A is (−1, 4) and AB = 5 −6 . Write the coordinates of B. Type them like (2, 5).4,-2 (also accept: (4, -2))
    Working: B = A + AB = (−1 + 5, 4 − 6) = (4, −2).
    50B is (3, 2) and AB = −2 5 . Write the coordinates of A. Type them like (2, 5).5,-3 (also accept: (5, -3))
    Working: A = B − AB = (3 − (−2), 2 − 5) = (5, −3).
    51A journey goes 4 to the left and 7 down. Write it as a column vector. Type it like (3, -2).-4,-7 (also accept: (-4, -7))
    Working: Left 4 is −4 across. Down 7 is −7 up. The vector is −4 −7 , typed (-4, -7).
    52Which journey is the vector −3 5 ?3 left and 5 up
    Working: The top number −3 is 3 to the left. The bottom number 5 is 5 up.
    53The vector (k, 12) has magnitude 13, and k is positive. Find k.5
    Working: k² + 144 = 169, so k² = 25 and k = 5.
    54AB = 2 a . Find the vector BA in terms of a . Type it like -3a.-2a
    Working: BA = −AB = −2 a .
    55AB = (3, 4) and AC = (9, 12). AC = k AB. Find k.3
    Working: 9 ÷ 3 = 3 and 12 ÷ 4 = 3, so AC = 3AB and k = 3. (So A, B and C are in a straight line.)
    56Dan says: A is (2, 5) and B is (6, 1), so AB = −4 4 . What went wrong?He subtracted the wrong way round: start minus end, instead of end minus start
    Working: AB = B − A = (6 − 2, 1 − 5) = 4 −4 . Dan worked out A − B, which is BA.
    57Eve says: the magnitude of 6 8 is 6 + 8 = 14. What went wrong?She added instead of squaring
    Working: 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100, and √100 = 10. The magnitude is 10.
    58Ida says: AB = 2 3 and BC = 4 5 , so AC = 8 15 . What went wrong?She multiplied instead of adding
    Working: AC = AB + BC = 2 + 4 3 + 5 = 6 8 .
    59Fay says: 2 3 and 4 5 are parallel because they both go up and to the right. What went wrong?Parallel vectors must be multiples of each other
    Working: 4 ÷ 2 = 2, but 5 ÷ 3 = 1.67… The multiples are not the same, so the vectors are not parallel.
    60Work out 3 2 −1 + 1 4 . Type it like (3, -2).7,1 (also accept: (7, 1))
    Working: 3 2 −1 = 6 −3 . Add 1 4 : 7 1 , typed (7, 1).
    61Find the magnitude of 8 15 .17
    Working: 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289. √289 = 17.
    62A is (3, −1) and B is (−2, 4). Write the vector AB. Type it like (3, -2).-5,5 (also accept: (-5, 5))
    Working: Across: −2 − 3 = −5. Up: 4 − (−1) = 5. AB = −5 5 , typed (-5, 5).
    63In triangle OAB, OA = a and OB = b . Find BA in terms of a and b . Type it like 2a + b.a-b (also accept: -b+a)
    Working: BA = BO + OA = − b + a = a − b .
    64a = 1 2 and b = −3 0 . Work out a − 2 b . Type it like (3, -2).7,2 (also accept: (7, 2))
    Working: 2 b = −6 0 . a − 2 b = 1 − (−6) 2 − 0 = 7 2 , typed (7, 2).
    65Find k so that k 8 is parallel to 3 2 .12
    Working: 8 ÷ 2 = 4, so multiply the top by 4: k = 3 × 4 = 12.
    66A is (−2, 3) and B is (6, −1). M is the midpoint of AB. Find the coordinates of M. Type it like (2, 5).2,1 (also accept: (2, 1))
    Working: AB = (8, −4). Half of AB = (4, −2). M = (−2 + 4, 3 − 2) = (2, 1).
    67An electrician runs conduit along a wall plan. The first length is 4 3 (in metres) and the second is 8 6 . The two lengths are in the same direction, so the conduit is a straight line. How long is the whole run? Answer in m.15
    Working: Total = (4 + 8, 3 + 6) = (12, 9). 12² + 9² = 144 + 81 = 225. √225 = 15 m. (Check: 5 + 10 = 15.)
    68An electrician runs cable in a straight line from the consumer unit at (1, 2) to a socket at (7, 10) on a plan in metres. The cable costs £1.35 per metre. How much does the cable cost? Give your answer in pounds to 2 decimal places, like 12.50.13.50 (also accept: 13.5)
    1. M1 for the vector (6, 8)
    2. M1 for the length 10 and × 1.35
    3. A1 for 13.50
    Working: Vector = (7 − 1, 10 − 2) = (6, 8). Length = √(36 + 64) = 10 m. Cost = 10 × 1.35 = £13.50.
    		3
    69A plumber puts pipe clips at A(2, 1), B(5, 3) and C(14, 9) on a wall plan. BC = k AB. What is k? (It shows the clips are in a straight line.)3
    Working: AB = (3, 2) and BC = (9, 6). 9 ÷ 3 = 3 and 6 ÷ 2 = 3. So BC = 3AB and k = 3. The clips are in a straight line.
    70On a plan in metres the boiler is at (0, 0) and a radiator is at (9, 12). Copper pipe goes straight between them and costs £4.20 per metre. How much does the pipe cost? Give your answer in pounds to 2 decimal places, like 12.50.63.00 (also accept: 63)
    1. M1 for 9² + 12² = 225
    2. M1 for 15 × 4.20
    3. A1 for 63.00
    Working: Length = √(9² + 12²) = √(81 + 144) = √225 = 15 m. Cost = 15 × 4.20 = £63.00.
    		3
    71A carpenter cuts 6 rafters. Each rafter runs 2.4 m across and 1 m up along its slope. Timber costs £6.80 per metre. How much does the timber for the 6 rafters cost? Give your answer in pounds to 2 decimal places, like 12.50.106.08
    1. M1 for 2.4² + 1² = 6.76
    2. M1 for the length 2.6 m
    3. M1 for 6 × 2.6 = 15.6 and × 6.80
    4. A1 for 106.08
    Working: One rafter: √(2.4² + 1²) = √(5.76 + 1) = √6.76 = 2.6 m. 6 rafters = 15.6 m. Cost = 15.6 × 6.80 = £106.08.
    		4
    72A carpenter fits a straight brace from the foot of a post to a point 1.5 m along the wall and 2 m up. How long is the brace? Answer in m.2.5
    Working: 1.5² + 2² = 2.25 + 4 = 6.25. √6.25 = 2.5 m.
    73A is the point (1, 2) and B is the point (6, 5). Write the vector AB. Type it like (3, -2).5,3 (also accept: (5, 3))
    1. M1 for 6 − 1 or 5 − 2
    2. A1 for (5, 3)
    Working: AB = (6 − 1, 5 − 2) = 5 3 , typed (5, 3).

    74a = 2 3 and b = −1 4 . Work out 3 a − 2 b . Type it like (3, -2).8,1 (also accept: (8, 1))
    1. M1 for 3 a = (6, 9) or 2 b = (−2, 8)
    2. M1 for subtracting the two vectors
    3. A1 for (8, 1)
    Working: 3 a = 6 9 and 2 b = −2 8 . 3 a − 2 b = 6 − (−2) 9 − 8 = 8 1 , typed (8, 1).

    75OA = a and OB = b . M is the midpoint of AB. Show that OM = 1/2( a + b ).Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. B1 for AB = b − a
    2. M1 for OM = a + 1/2( b − a )
    3. C1 for simplifying to 1/2( a + b )
    Working: AB = AO + OB = b − a . AM = 1/2AB = 1/2( b − a ). OM = OA + AM = a + 1/2( b − a ) = 1/2 a + 1/2 b = 1/2( a + b ), as required.

    76PQ = 2 a − b and PR = 6 a − 3 b . Show that P, Q and R are in a straight line.Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. M1 for factorising PR = 3(2 a − b )
    2. C1 for PR = 3PQ, so they are parallel
    3. C1 for the common point P, so collinear
    Working: PR = 6 a − 3 b = 3(2 a − b ) = 3PQ. So PR is parallel to PQ. They both start at P, so P, Q and R are in a straight line.
    		3
    77OA = a and OB = b . P is on AB with AP : PB = 2 : 1. Find OP in terms of a and b . Give your answer in its simplest form.Written answer: see the mark scheme
    1. B1 for AB = b − a
    2. M1 for AP = 2/3AB
    3. M1 for OP = a + AP
    4. A1 for 1/3 a + 2/3 b
    Working: AB = b − a . AP = 2/3AB = 2/3( b − a ). OP = OA + AP = a + 2/3( b − a ) = 1/3 a + 2/3 b .

    78A is the point (3, 1) and B is the point (−2, 13). Work out the length of AB.13
    1. M1 for the vector (−5, 12)
    2. M1 for 25 + 144 = 169
    3. A1 for 13
    Working: AB = (−2 − 3, 13 − 1) = (−5, 12). Length = √((−5)² + 12²) = √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13.
    		3
    79A is the point (−1, 2) and B is the point (8, 8). P is on AB with AP : PB = 2 : 1. Find the coordinates of P. Type them like (2, 5).5,6 (also accept: (5, 6))
    1. M1 for AB = (9, 6)
    2. M1 for AP = 2/3AB = (6, 4)
    3. M1 for adding to A
    4. A1 for (5, 6)
    Working: AB = (9, 6). AP = 2/3 of (9, 6) = (6, 4). P = (−1 + 6, 2 + 4) = (5, 6).
    		4
    80O is (0, 0), A is (6, 2) and B is (2, 8). M is the midpoint of OA and N is the midpoint of OB. MN = k AB. Find k. (Work out M, N, MN and AB as vectors. Then think about why the answer would be the same for any triangle.)1/2 (also accept: 0.5)
    Working: M = (3, 1) and N = (1, 4). MN = (−2, 3). AB = (2 − 6, 8 − 2) = (−4, 6). MN = 1/2AB, so k = 1/2. This is true for any triangle: MN = 1/2 b − 1/2 a = 1/2AB. A line joining the midpoints of two sides is parallel to the third side and half as long.
    81OACB is a parallelogram. O is (0, 0), A is (6, 1) and B is (2, 5), so C is (8, 6). Find the midpoint of AB. Then find the midpoint of OC, and say what you notice. Type the midpoint of AB like (2, 5).4,3 (also accept: (4, 3))
    Working: AB = (−4, 4). Half = (−2, 2). Midpoint of AB = (6 − 2, 1 + 2) = (4, 3). The midpoint of OC is half of (8, 6) = (4, 3). It is the same point, so the diagonals of a parallelogram cut each other in half. With vectors: the midpoint of AB is 1/2( a + b ), and so is the midpoint of OC.
    82Find numbers m and n so that m 2 1 + n 1 3 = 7 11 . Type your answer as m, n, like (4, 1) if m = 4 and n = 1.2,3 (also accept: (2, 3))
    Working: Tops: 2m + n = 7. Bottoms: m + 3n = 11. From the first, n = 7 − 2m. Put it in the second: m + 3(7 − 2m) = 11, so m + 21 − 6m = 11, so −5m = −10 and m = 2. Then n = 7 − 4 = 3. The answer is (2, 3). Check: 2(2, 1) + 3(1, 3) = (4 + 3, 2 + 9) = (7, 11).
