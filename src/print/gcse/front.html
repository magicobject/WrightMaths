How to use this book

The short version: work through a chapter in order. Do the questions on paper. Check each answer at the back. If you are stuck, use the hints. Nothing in this book needs a computer.

How a chapter is laid out

  • Before you start says what the topic stands on. Maths is built like a pyramid, and each chapter rests on the ones below it. If the “Last time” questions feel shaky, go back to that chapter first.
  • Words you need lists the new words. Each is explained again in the Glossary at the back.
  • A short explanation and a worked example come next, then an example you finish yourself (Now you finish it).
  • After every chunk there is a Check your knowledge set.
  • Flip it runs the topic backwards. Exam style questions show their marks.
  • The chapter ends with an I can… list. Tick a box when you can do it without looking, then come back in a week and tick again.

Question numbers

Question 7.12 is question 12 in Chapter 7. Its answer is in the Answers section at the back under 7.12, and its hints are in the Hints section under 7.12. The mixed practice papers at the end of the chapters are numbered in the same way.

When you are stuck: the hints

  1. Read the question again and say what it is asking.
  2. Turn to the Hints section and read Hint 1 only. Try again.
  3. Still stuck? Read Hint 2, and so on. Each hint gives away a little more.
  4. Last of all, read the worked answer. Then cover it up and try the question again on a fresh line.

Using a hint is not cheating. It is how you learn.

Checking your answers

The Answers section gives the answer and a short worked solution for every question. Many answers also have lines that begin “If you got…”. These explain the slip that gives a wrong answer, so you can see what went wrong.

For questions with marks, write your working first, then mark yourself against the mark scheme. M marks are for a correct method, A marks for an accurate answer, B marks are independent marks (no method is needed), and C marks are for explaining clearly.

For “Now you finish it”, the Answers section gives the missing numbers in the order the gaps appear, then a short check.

Calculators

Each question says whether a calculator is allowed. For trigonometry, set your calculator to degrees: look for D or DEG on its display and check that sin 30 gives 0.5.

Words in the questions

Some questions use everyday words that you may not know. Here are the common ones. The Glossary at the back has the maths words, and the Index tells you which pages to read about each one.

UK money

  • Pence (p): 100p is £1.
  • Wage: pay for each hour or week worked. Salary: a fixed amount of pay for a year.
  • Profit: the money you take in, minus your costs. Loss: when your costs are more than the money you take in.
  • Discount: money taken off a price. Deposit: part of the price, paid at the start. Commission: extra pay worked out as a percentage of the sales you make. Tip: a thank-you amount added to a bill.
  • VAT: a tax added to many prices. Time and a half: pay at 1.5 times the normal rate.
  • Call-out fee: a fixed charge for a worker to come to your home.

Building and trade words

  • Skirting board: the wooden strip along the bottom of a wall.
  • Joist: a beam under a floor. Stud wall: a wall with a wooden frame inside.
  • Turf: rolls of ready-grown grass for a lawn.
  • Ring and spur: kinds of electrical cable circuit in a house.

Games, sport and shops

  • Raffle: a prize draw with numbered tickets.
  • 5-a-side: football with five players on each team.
  • Squash (the drink): a fruit drink you mix with water.
  • Sweets: small sugary things to eat (candy, in American English).
