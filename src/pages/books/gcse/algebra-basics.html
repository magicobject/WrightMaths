Warm-up: what do you already know?

Here is one picture to keep in mind for the whole page. A traditional kitchen scale only balances when both pans weigh the same. A letter can stand for a mystery weight on a pan. Have a go at the question, then open the recap if you want a reminder of the earlier layers.

A traditional kitchen scale balances. On the left pan there is a mystery weight and a 3 g weight. On the right pan there is a 10 g weight. How heavy is the mystery weight, in grams?

  1. Both pans must weigh the same.
  2. What must the mystery weight add to 3 to make 10?
  • That adds 3 and 10. The scale balances, so the mystery weight and the 3 g together make 10.
  • That is the weight you can see. The mystery weight is the one that makes the two sides equal.

1. Letters stand for numbers

In algebra a letter stands for a number. If we do not know the number, the letter is an {term:unknown|unknown}. If it can change, it is a variable. Putting letters and numbers together with operations gives an {term:expression}.

n + 5
a number plus 5
n − 5
5 less than the number
3n
3 × n (3 lots of n)
{frac:n/2}
n ÷ 2 (half of n)

Key idea: we leave out the × sign: 3n means 3 × n, and ab means a × b. Write the number first: 3n, not n3. This is a convention, an agreed habit, so that everyone reads algebra the same way. And a × a is written a². The letter is just a placeholder for a number, so everything you know about numbers still works.

Worked example

Write an expression for “double a number, then add 3”.

  1. Call the number n.
  2. Double it: 2n.
  3. Add 3: 2n + 3.

Check your knowledge: letters and expressions

If n = 4, what is n + 7?

  1. Replace n with 4.
  • n + 7 means add 7. It does not join the digits.

4 + 7 = 11.

If n = 5, what is 3n?

  1. 3n means 3 × n.
  • 3n does not mean “3 and 5”. It means 3 times n.
  • That is 3 + 5. 3n means 3 times n.

3 × 5 = 15.

If n = 10, what is {frac:n/2}?

  1. {frac:n/2} means n ÷ 2.
  • That multiplies by 2. {frac:n/2} is half of n.

10 ÷ 2 = 5.

Which expression means “5 less than n”?

  • 5 − n
  • n − 5
  • n + 5
  1. Start with n and take 5 away.

n − 5. (If n = 8 then 5 less than 8 is 3: 8 − 5.)

Which expression means “double n, then add 3”?

  • 2n + 3
  • 2(n + 3)
  • n + 6
  1. Double first: 2n. Then add.

2n + 3.

A tube of blue paint costs £b. Zara buys 3 tubes and a £2 brush. Write an expression for the total cost in pounds. (Type it like 3b+2.)

  1. 3 tubes cost 3 × b = 3b.
  2. Then add the brush.
  • That treats the brush as another tube. The brush costs a fixed £2, so it stays as a number.
  • That adds a £2 brush to every tube. Only one brush is bought: 3b + 2.

3 tubes cost 3b pounds and the brush costs 2 pounds, so the total is 3b + 2.

2. Putting numbers in (substitution)

To {term:substitute} is to replace each letter with a number and work out the answer. Because a letter stands for a number, you must follow the order of operations (BIDMAS): powers before multiplying, brackets first.

Key idea: write the letter’s value in brackets when you substitute, especially for negatives: for x² with x = −3, write (−3)² = 9. Without brackets you might compute −3² = −9, which is not the same.

Worked example

Work out 2x² when x = 3.

  1. Substitute: 2 × 3².
  2. Powers first: 3² = 9.
  3. Then 2 × 9 = 18.

Not (2 × 3)² = 36: the square belongs only to the x.

Check your knowledge: substitution

Work out 2x + 3 when x = 4.

  1. 2 × 4 + 3.
  • That puts 4 next to the 2 to make 24. 2x means 2 × 4.

8 + 3 = 11.

Work out 5a − 2 when a = 3.

  1. 5 × 3 − 2.
  • 5a means 5 × a. It does not join the digits.

15 − 2 = 13.

Work out x² when x = −3.

  1. (−3) × (−3).
  • Squaring a negative gives a positive: (−3) × (−3) = 9.
  • That is −3 × 2. Squaring multiplies the number by itself.

A negative times a negative is positive: (−3)² = 9.

Work out 3(y + 2) when y = 1.

  1. Brackets first: 1 + 2.
  • You may have done 3 × 1 + 2. The brackets come first.

3 × 3 = 9.

Work out a + b when a = 2 and b = −5.

  1. 2 + (−5).
  • Adding −5 is the same as subtracting 5.
  • The answer is negative: 2 is smaller than 5.

2 + (−5) = 2 − 5 = −3.

Work out 2x² when x = 3.

  1. Power first: 3² = 9.
  • That is (2 × 3)². The square belongs only to the x.

9 × 2 = 18.

A tube of blue paint costs £b. Zara buys 3 tubes and a £2 brush, so the cost is 3b + 2 pounds. Work out the cost when b = 4.

  1. Put 4 in for b: 3 × 4 + 2.
  2. Multiply before you add.
  • That is (3 + 2) × 4. The brush is not multiplied by b: work out 3 × 4 + 2.
  • That leaves out the £2 brush: 3 × 4 = 12, then add 2.
  • That adds 3 + 2 + 4. 3b means 3 × b.

3 × 4 + 2 = 12 + 2 = 14, so the cost is £14.

A playlist has a 3-minute intro and then 4 songs of s minutes each. It lasts 4s + 3 minutes. Work out how long it lasts when s = 3.5.

  1. Put 3.5 in for s: 4 × 3.5 + 3.
  2. 4 × 3.5 = 14.
  • That leaves out the 3-minute intro. Add 3.
  • That adds 4 + 3.5. 4s means 4 × s.
  • That multiplies the intro by 4 as well: 4 × (3.5 + 3). Only the songs are multiplied.

4 × 3.5 + 3 = 14 + 3 = 17 minutes.

3. Collecting like terms

The {term:term|terms} of an expression are the parts joined by + and −. {term:like-terms|Like terms} have exactly the same letter part, so you can add or subtract their {term:coefficient|coefficients}: 3a + 2a = 5a. You cannot combine a with b, or a with a².

Key idea: 3a + 2a = 5a is like “3 apples + 2 apples = 5 apples”. It works because a is the same number in both terms: 3a + 2a = (3 + 2)a. A letter is a number, not an object. But 3 apples + 2 bananas stays as 3 apples + 2 bananas, and 3a + 2b stays as it is, because a and b can be different numbers. Each term keeps the sign in front of it, so in 5x + 2 − x + 4 the x terms are 5x and −x (which is −1x).

Worked example

Simplify 5x + 2 − x + 4.

  1. Group the x terms: 5x − x = 4x.
  2. Group the numbers: 2 + 4 = 6.
  3. Answer: 4x + 6.

Check your knowledge: simplifying

Simplify 3a + 2a.

  1. 3 apples + 2 apples.
  • Adding like terms does not square the letter: 3a + 2a = 5a.
  • That multiplies 3 × 2. Add the coefficients.

3a + 2a = 5a.

Simplify 7x − 3x.

  1. 7 − 3 = 4.
  • That multiplies. Subtract the coefficients.
  • The letter stays: 4x.

7x − 3x = 4x.

Simplify 4a + 3b + 2a. (Type it like 6a+3b.)

  1. Collect the a terms: 4a + 2a.
  2. The b term cannot join.
  • a and b are different letters, so you cannot add them together.
  • Only like terms combine. 3b stays as it is.

4a + 2a = 6a, and 3b stays: 6a + 3b.

Simplify 5x + 2 − x + 4. (Type it like 4x+6.)

  1. x terms: 5x − x.
  2. Number terms: 2 + 4.
  • The −x also needs to be taken away: 5x − x = 4x, not 6x.
  • You lost the +4. Group the numbers: 2 + 4 = 6.

5x − x = 4x and 2 + 4 = 6: 4x + 6.

Write a × a as simply as possible. (Type it like a^2.)

  1. a × a is a squared.
  • That is a + a. a × a is a squared.

a × a = a².

Write 3 × a × b as simply as possible. (Type it like 3ab.)

  1. Number first, then the letters in order.
  • Write the number first: 3ab.
  • Write the number first: 3ab.

3 × a × b = 3ab.

Tiles are laid in a row: 3 pink tiles and 2 blue tiles, each a cm long, and 4 gold tiles, each 1 cm long. Write the total length in cm in its simplest form. (Type it like 5a+4.)

  1. Pink and blue tiles are the same length, so 3a + 2a.
  2. The gold tiles are 4 on their own.
  • The gold tiles are 1 cm long, not a cm. Only the pink and blue tiles are a.
  • Pink and blue make 3 + 2 = 5 lots of a. The 4 gold tiles are the number 4.

3a + 2a = 5a, and the gold tiles add 4, so the length is 5a + 4 cm.

4. Brackets: expanding

To {term:expand} a bracket, multiply every term inside by what is outside. 3(x + 4) means three lots of (x + 4), which is 3x + 12.

For a minus inside the bracket, the same idea holds: 2(a − 5) = 2 × a + 2 × (−5). A positive times a negative is negative, so 2 × (−5) = −10 and 2(a − 5) = 2a − 10.

Key idea: the number outside multiplies both terms. 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12, not 3x + 4. If there is a minus inside, keep the sign: 2(a − 5) = 2a − 10, because 2 × (−5) = −10.

Worked example

Expand 5(2y + 1).

  1. 5 × 2y = 10y.
  2. 5 × 1 = 5.
  3. 5(2y + 1) = 10y + 5.

Check your knowledge: expanding

A sketchbook cover is 3 cm tall and (x + 4) cm wide. Expand 3(x + 4) to write its area. (Type it like 3x+12.)

  1. 3 × x and 3 × 4.
  • The 3 multiplies the 4 as well: 3 × 4 = 12.
  • Expanding is multiplying, not adding: 3x + 12.

3x + 12.

The sketchbook cover's area is 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12 cm². Work out the area when x = 5, in cm².

  1. Put 5 in for x: 3 × 5 + 12, or 3 × (5 + 4).
  • That is 3x + 4. The 3 multiplies the 4 too: 3x + 12.
  • That is the width, x + 4, not the area. The height 3 multiplies it.

3 × 5 + 12 = 27 cm². Check: 3 × (5 + 4) = 27.

Expand 2(a − 5). (Type it like 2a-10.)

  1. 2 × a and 2 × (−5).
  2. A positive times a negative is negative, so 2 × (−5) = −10.
  • The 2 multiplies the 5 too: 2 × 5 = 10.
  • Keep the minus: 2 × (−5) = −10.

2a − 10.

Expand 5(2y + 1). (Type it like 10y+5.)

  1. 5 × 2y and 5 × 1.
  • The 5 multiplies the 1 as well: 5 × 1 = 5.
  • Expanding is multiplying, not adding.

10y + 5.

Expand 4(m + 3) and then work out its value when m = 2.

  1. 4m + 12, then substitute m = 2.
  • That expands as 4m + 3. The 4 multiplies the 3 too: 4m + 12.

4(m + 3) = 4m + 12. When m = 2: 8 + 12 = 20. (Check: 4 × (2 + 3) = 20.)

5. The equals sign is a balance

The = sign says both sides have the same value. Think of a traditional kitchen scale with a pan on each side. The scale only balances when both pans weigh the same.

Key idea: putting the right value for the unknown is what makes both sides of the = the same. That value is the solution. For x + 3 = 10 the right value is x = 7, because 7 + 3 = 10 and the scale balances.

If you add something to one pan, the scale tips, so to keep it balanced you must do the same amount to the other pan as well. For equations the rule is: do the same amount to both sides. That is the whole idea behind solving equations, and you will use it in Solving equations.

Worked example

Two equal mystery weights balance a 14 g weight. How heavy is each?

  1. The two mystery weights together make 14 g: 2x = 14.
  2. Divide both sides by 2: 14 ÷ 2 = 7, so x = 7.
  3. Each is 7 g. Check: 7 + 7 = 14. ✓

Check your knowledge: the balance

x + 3 = 10. What value of x makes both sides equal?

  1. What must you add to 3 to make 10?
  • That adds 3 and 10. The scale needs x + 3 to equal 10.
  • x is the mystery weight, not the 3 g weight.

x = 7, because 7 + 3 = 10 and the scale balances.

x + 7 = 12. Does x = 5 make the scale balance?

  • No: 5 + 7 = 13, so the left side is bigger
  • No: 5 + 7 = 11, so the left side is smaller
  • Yes: 5 + 7 = 12, so both sides are equal
  1. Put 5 in for x and work out the left side.

5 + 7 = 12 matches the right-hand side, so x = 5 balances it.

2x = 14. What value of x makes both sides equal?

  1. Two equal weights make 14 g. Divide both sides by 2.
  • That is 2 × 14. Share the 14 between two weights.
  • That is 14 − 2. 2x means 2 lots of x.

14 ÷ 2 = 7. Check: 2 × 7 = 14.

x − 4 = 9. What value of x makes both sides equal?

  1. The mystery weight, with 4 taken off, is 9. So how heavy is x?
  • That subtracts 4 from 9. To undo a subtraction, add.

x = 9 + 4 = 13. Check: 13 − 4 = 9.

You put 5 g more in the left pan. What keeps the scale balanced?

  • Take 5 g away from the right pan
  • Put 5 g more in the right pan
  • Leave the right pan as it is
  1. Do the same amount to both sides.

Add 5 g to the right pan as well: the same change to both sides keeps the balance.

Flip it: put numbers in, or find the number that fits

So far you have gone one way. Now run it backwards: this is where much of the real understanding comes from.

Forwards

Given x = 4, work out 2x + 3: 2 × 4 + 3 = 11.

Substitute: from the letter to the answer.

Backwards

Given 2x + 3 = 11, find the x that makes the scale balance: x = 4.

Solve: from the answer back to the letter.

Key idea: substituting and solving are two directions of the same relationship. In the same way, expanding and factorising are a pair. They are the same statement read in two directions: 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12.

Forwards

Expand: 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12

Multiply the bracket out.

Backwards

Factorise: 3x + 12 = 3(x + 4)

Take the common factor out.

Higher tier: because these two sides are equal for every x, mathematicians write 3(x + 4) {term:identity|≡} 3x + 12. See Identity in the glossary.

Check your knowledge: flip it

2x + 3 = 11. Find the value of x that makes both sides equal.

  1. Undo the + 3 first, then the × 2.
  • That is 11 − 3. Now undo the × 2 as well by dividing by 2.
  • That is 11 + 3. Undo a + 3 by taking away 3.

11 − 3 = 8 and 8 ÷ 2 = 4. Check: 2 × 4 + 3 = 11.

3x + 12 = 3( ? ). Fill in the bracket. (Type it like x+4.)

  1. Each term has a factor of 3. What is left when you take it out?
  • The 12 also divides by 3: 12 ÷ 3 = 4.
  • Take the factor 3 out of both terms: x and 4.

3x ÷ 3 = x and 12 ÷ 3 = 4, so 3(x + 4). Check by expanding: 3x + 12.

6x + 9 = 3( ? ). Fill in the bracket. (Type it like 2x+3.)

  1. 6x ÷ 3 and 9 ÷ 3.
  • The 9 also divides by 3.
  • Divide 6x by 3: it is 2x, not x.

6x ÷ 3 = 2x and 9 ÷ 3 = 3, so 3(2x + 3). Check: 6x + 9. ✓

A rule: multiply n by 3, then add 1. When n = 1, 2, 3 the answers are 4, 7, 10. What is the answer when n = 10?

  1. The rule is 3n + 1.
  • That is 10 + 3. The rule is 3 × n + 1: multiply first.
  • You forgot to add 1.

3 × 10 + 1 = 31.

A different rule is 3n + 2. When the answer is 17, what was n?

  1. Undo the + 2, then undo the × 3.
  • That is 17 − 2. Undo the × 3 as well: 15 ÷ 3.
  • That is 17 + 2. Undo a + 2 by taking away 2.

Spot the mistake

Pat says: “3a + 2b = 5ab.” What went wrong?

  • Nothing is wrong: 3 + 2 = 5, and the letters a and b multiply together.
  • It should be 5a, because you add the numbers and keep the first letter.
  • a and b are different letters, so they are not like terms. 3a + 2b cannot be simplified.
  1. Apples and bananas do not combine.

3a + 2b stays as it is.

Quin says: “2(x + 3) = 2x + 3.” What went wrong?

  • Nothing is wrong: the 2 multiplies the x and the 3 stays as it is.
  • He multiplied only the x. The 2 multiplies everything in the bracket: 2x + 6.
  • It should be 5x, because you add the 2 and the 3 first.
  1. Try x = 1 in both: do they give the same answer?

2(x + 3) = 2x + 6.

Rae says: “When x = 4, 3x = 34.” What went wrong?

  • Nothing is wrong: the 3 and the 4 are written side by side.
  • It should be 7, because 3x means add the 3 and the x.
  • 3x means 3 × x, not “3 and x”. 3 × 4 = 12.
  1. 3x is 3 lots of x.

3 × 4 = 12.

Going further: double brackets

With two brackets, every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second. Draw a box for each pair. Two terms times two terms gives four boxes.

2 × x = 2x
second × first
2 × 3 = 6

Why it works

  • A letter is a number. So the rules you know for numbers (order of operations, negatives) still apply.
  • Like terms combine like apples. 3 apples + 2 apples = 5 apples, but apples and bananas stay separate. With letters, 3a + 2a = (3 + 2)a, because a is the same number in both terms.
  • The equals sign is a balance. Putting the right value for the unknown is what makes both sides the same, like a traditional kitchen scale.

Common traps

  • Joining the digits when you substitute (3x with x = 4 is 12, not 34).
  • Combining unlike terms (3a + 2b is not 5ab).
  • Multiplying only the first term in a bracket.
  • Losing a negative sign when substituting a negative number.
  • Squaring the coefficient (2x² is 2 × x², not (2x)²).

Real jobs from the building and plumbing trades. Prices are typical, so check them with your own supplier.
