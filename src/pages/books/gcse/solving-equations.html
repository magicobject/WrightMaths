Solving equations

An equation is a puzzle with a missing number. Learn to undo it step by step, then flip it round and build your own puzzles.

Where you will use this: working backwards from a quote to find the hours or the price per item, comparing two tradesmen to see when one becomes cheaper, and checking a bill.

I think of a number, double it, then add 3. My answer is 17. What was my number?

  1. Work backwards from 17. What was the last thing I did?
  2. Undo it: take 3 away. Then undo the doubling.
  • That is 17 − 3. I also doubled my number at the start, so there is one more step to undo.
  • You added 3 instead of taking it away. Working backwards means doing the opposite.

17 − 3 = 14, then 14 ÷ 2 = 7. You just solved 2x + 3 = 17.

1. An equation is a balance

An {term:equation} says two things are equal, like a set of balanced scales. The {term:unknown} is a letter, usually x, standing for the number you want to find. To {term:solve} the equation is to find the value that keeps it true.

x + 7 = 12

The best picture is a traditional kitchen scale: a pan on each side that only balances when both hold the same amount. The unknown is the mystery weight. Putting the right value for the unknown in is what makes both sides of the = the same.

Key idea: do the same amount to both sides, so the kitchen scale stays balanced. For example, take 7 from both sides, or divide both sides by 2. To find x, use the {term:inverse-operation|inverse operation}: the one that undoes what was done to x.

Where the scale stops: weights cannot be negative, so for negative answers and minus terms use the same rule: do the same amount to both sides. The idea that both sides must stay equal never changes.

+ undoes −
− undoes +
× undoes ÷
÷ undoes ×

Worked example

Solve x + 7 = 12.

  1. 7 has been added to x. The inverse is to take away 7.
  2. Do it to both sides: x + 7 − 7 = 12 − 7.
  3. x = 5.
  4. Check: 5 + 7 = 12 ✓

2. Two steps

Most equations have two things done to x. Undo them in the reverse order to the way they were done. Think of getting dressed: socks, then shoes. To undo it you take off the shoes first.

Key idea: in 3x + 5 = 20, x was first multiplied by 3, then 5 was added. So undo the "+ 5" first, then the "× 3".

Worked example

Solve 3x + 5 = 20.

  1. Take 5 from both sides: 3x = 15.
  2. Divide both sides by 3: x = 5.
  3. Check: 3 × 5 + 5 = 20 ✓

Now you finish it

Solve {frac:x/2} + 3 = 8.

  1. Take 3 from both sides: {frac:x/2} = 5.
  2. Multiply both sides by 2: x = ___.
Check my answer

x = 10. Check: 10 ÷ 2 + 3 = 8 ✓

3. Brackets and letters on both sides

With brackets you have two options: divide both sides by the number outside, or {term:expand} the bracket first. If x appears on both sides, collect the x terms on one side, then the numbers on the other.

Worked example: brackets

Solve 3(x + 2) = 21.

  1. Divide both sides by 3: x + 2 = 7.
  2. Take 2 from both sides: x = 5.
  3. Check: 3 × (5 + 2) = 21 ✓

Worked example: x on both sides

Solve 5x + 3 = 2x + 15.

  1. Take 2x from both sides, so the x terms are on the left: 3x + 3 = 15.
  2. Take 3 from both sides: 3x = 12.
  3. Divide by 3: x = 4.
  4. Check: left 5 × 4 + 3 = 23; right 2 × 4 + 15 = 23 ✓

Key idea: take away the smaller x-term from both sides, so you are left with a positive number of x. The other way also works, but then you divide by a negative.

x + 3 = 7, so x = 4.

4. Equations with fractions

A fraction bar means divide. To remove it, multiply both sides by the denominator. With two fractions, multiply by a number that both denominators go into.

Key idea: multiply every term on both sides. Put brackets around a top line such as x + 1.

Worked example

Solve {frac:x + 1/3} = {frac:x − 3/2}.

  1. The denominators are 3 and 2. Both go into 6, so multiply both sides by 6.
  2. This gives 2(x + 1) = 3(x − 3).
  3. Expand: 2x + 2 = 3x − 9.
  4. Take 2x from both sides: 2 = x − 9. Add 9: x = 11.
  5. Check: {frac:12/3} = 4 and {frac:8/2} = 4 ✓

Check your knowledge: equations with fractions

Solve {frac:x + 2/3} = 5.

  1. Multiply both sides by 3.
  2. You get x + 2 = 15. Now undo the "+ 2".
  • That is x + 2. Take away 2 to finish.
  • That adds 2. Undo a "+ 2" by taking away 2.

x + 2 = 15, so x = 13. Check: (13 + 2) ÷ 3 = 5 ✓

Solve {frac:2x/5} = 6.

  1. Multiply both sides by 5 to get 2x = 30.
  • That is 2x. Divide by 2 to finish.
  • That is 6 × 2 ÷ 5. Undo ÷ 5 by × 5, then undo × 2 by ÷ 2.

2x = 30, so x = 15. Check: 2 × 15 ÷ 5 = 6 ✓

Solve {frac:x − 1/4} = 3.

  1. Multiply both sides by 4.
  2. You get x − 1 = 12. Now undo the "− 1".
  • That is x − 1. Add 1 to finish.
  • That subtracts 1 again. Undo "− 1" by adding 1.

x − 1 = 12, so x = 13. Check: (13 − 1) ÷ 4 = 3 ✓

Solve {frac:x + 1/2} = {frac:x + 4/5}.

  1. Multiply both sides by 10: 5(x + 1) = 2(x + 4).
  2. Expand: 5x + 5 = 2x + 8. Collect the x terms.
  • That is 3x. Divide by 3 to finish.
  • You added 5 instead of taking it away: 3x = 8 − 5.

5x + 5 = 2x + 8 gives 3x = 3, so x = 1. Check: {frac:2/2} = 1 and {frac:5/5} = 1 ✓

Flip it: build the equation

Solving goes from an equation to a number. Flip it: go from a number puzzle or a real situation to an equation. Being able to do both is what turns algebra from symbols into a tool.

Forwards

Solve 3x − 4 = 11

From the equation to x.

Backwards

"I think of a number, multiply by 3, take away 4, and get 11."

From the words to the equation.

Key idea: a number puzzle is an equation told in words. Call the number x and write each step in order: 3x − 4 = 11. To solve it, undo the steps in reverse: 11 + 4 = 15, then 15 ÷ 3 = 5.

Another reversal: checking. Put your answer back into the equation. If both sides match, you are right. If they do not, you will find the slip.

Check your knowledge: flip it

Is x = 3 a solution of 4x − 3 = 10?

  • Yes: the left-hand side works out as 10
  • No: the left-hand side works out as 9
  • No: the left-hand side works out as 12
  1. Replace x with 3 and work out the left-hand side.

4 × 3 − 3 = 9, which is not 10, so x = 3 is not a solution.

Which equation has the solution x = 6?

  • 2x + 3 = 9
  • 3x − 6 = 9
  • 2x − 3 = 9
  1. Put x = 6 into each one and see which gives the right-hand side.

2 × 6 − 3 = 9 ✓. The other two give 15 and 12 on the left.

I think of a number, multiply by 4, then subtract 6. The answer is 22. What is my number?

  1. Call the number x and write the equation: 4x − 6 = 22.
  2. Undo in reverse: add 6, then divide by 4.
  • You subtracted 6, but the puzzle subtracted 6 from 4x, so undo it by adding: 22 + 6 = 28, then divide by 4.

4x − 6 = 22 ⇒ 4x = 28 ⇒ x = 7. Check: 4 × 7 − 6 = 22 ✓

A pen costs £x. Four pens and a £2 ruler cost £10 altogether. Form an equation and find x (in pounds).

  1. Four pens cost 4x. Add the ruler: 4x + 2 = 10.
  • That adds 2: 10 + 2 = 12. Undo "+ 2" by taking 2 away.
  • That is 4x. The question asks for the price of one pen.

4x + 2 = 10, so 4x = 8 and x = 2. A pen costs £2.

Tom is x years old. His sister is 3 years older. Their ages add up to 25. How old is Tom?

  1. Sister is x + 3. The sum is x + (x + 3) = 25.
  • That is 2x. Divide by 2 to finish.
  • You split 25 equally, but the ages are not equal: sister is 3 years older.
  • That is the sister's age. The question asks for Tom.

2x + 3 = 25, so 2x = 22 and x = 11. Tom is 11, his sister is 14 and 11 + 14 = 25 ✓

Spot the mistake

Ali says: "3x + 5 = 20, so 3x = 25." What went wrong?

  • He should have multiplied both sides by 3 to remove the 3 from 3x.
  • He should have divided both sides by 3 before dealing with the 5.
  • He added 5 to both sides. To undo "+ 5" he should have subtracted 5.
  1. Check Ali's line by putting x = 25 ÷ 3 back in: it will not give 20.

The correct step is 3x = 20 − 5 = 15, so x = 5.

Bea says: "2(x + 3) = 10, so 2x + 3 = 10." What went wrong?

  • Nothing went wrong. Multiplying out the bracket gives 2x + 3 on this line.
  • She multiplied only the x by 2. The 2 multiplies everything in the bracket, giving 2x + 6.
  • She should have taken 3 from both sides first, then divided by 2.
  1. Expand 2(x + 3). Does every term inside get multiplied by 2?

2x + 6 = 10, so 2x = 4 and x = 2.

The sum of three consecutive whole numbers is 48. What is the smallest of them?

  1. Call the smallest n. The others are n + 1 and n + 2.
  2. n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 48.

Exam style

Write your working on paper, then type the answer.

There are four questions, 15 marks in all. On a real paper that is about 15 minutes: roughly one mark a minute.

Question 1 (5 marks): perimeter

A triangle has sides of length x cm, (2x + 1) cm and (3x − 2) cm. Its perimeter is 29 cm.

(a) [2 marks] {term:write-down|Write down} a simplified expression for the perimeter in terms of x. (Type it like 6x-1.)

  1. Add the three sides: x + (2x + 1) + (3x − 2).
  2. Collect the x terms (x + 2x + 3x) and then the numbers (+1 − 2).
  • Check the numbers: +1 and −2 make −1.
  • +1 and −2 make −1, not −3.
  • Collect the x terms: x + 2x + 3x = 6x.

x + 2x + 3x = 6x and 1 − 2 = −1, so the perimeter is 6x − 1.

(b) [2 marks] Use the perimeter to form an equation and find x.

  1. 6x − 1 = 29.
  • That is 6x. One more step: divide both sides by 6.
  • That is 28 ÷ 6. Check: 6x = 30, not 28.

6x = 30, so x = 5.

(c) [1 mark] What is the length of the longest side, in cm?

  1. Put x = 5 into each side to find all three.
  • That is the shortest side. Work out all three first.
  • That is the middle side, 2x + 1. The longest is 3x − 2.

The sides are 5, 11 and 13 cm. Check: 5 + 11 + 13 = 29 ✓

Question 2 (3 marks): show that

{term:show-that|Show that} x = 4 is the solution of 3(x + 2) = 5x − 2. Do it by working out each side when x = 4.

(a) [1 mark] When x = 4, what is the value of the left side, 3(x + 2)?

  1. Brackets first: 4 + 2.
  • That is 3 × 4 + 2. The 3 multiplies the whole bracket.

3 × (4 + 2) = 3 × 6 = 18.

(b) [1 mark] When x = 4, what is the value of the right side, 5x − 2?

  1. 5 × 4 first, then take away 2.
  • That adds 2. The sum is 20 − 2.
  • That is 5 × (4 − 2). The 5 multiplies only the x.

5 × 4 − 2 = 20 − 2 = 18.

(c) [1 mark] What does this show?

  • The two sides are different, so x = 4 is not the solution
  • Both sides are the same, 18, so x = 4 is the solution
  • Both sides are the same, 18, so x = 18 is the solution
  1. Compare your two answers.

Left side 18 and right side 18 are equal, so x = 4 makes the equation true.

Question 3 (4 marks): brackets

A rectangle has length (2x + 3) cm and width (x − 1) cm. Its perimeter is 34 cm.

(a) [3 marks] Form an equation using the perimeter, then {term:work-out|work out} x.

  1. The perimeter is 2 lengths and 2 widths: 2(2x + 3) + 2(x − 1) = 34.
  2. Expand both brackets, collect like terms, then solve.
  • You counted each side once. A rectangle has two lengths and two widths.
  • 2(x − 1) is 2x − 2, not 2x − 1. Multiply both terms.
  • 2(2x + 3) is 4x + 6, not 4x + 3. Multiply both terms.
  • That is 6x. Divide by 6 to finish.

4x + 6 + 2x − 2 = 34, so 6x + 4 = 34 and 6x = 30. Then x = 5.

(b) [1 mark] What is the width of the rectangle, in cm?

  1. The width is x − 1. Put in x = 5.
  • That is the length, 2x + 3. The width is x − 1.

Width = 5 − 1 = 4 cm. The length is 13 cm. Check: 2 × 13 + 2 × 4 = 34 ✓

Question 4 (3 marks): form and solve

A plumber charges £25 for a call-out and then £18 for each hour of work. Jo’s bill is £97.

[3 marks] Form an equation, with h for the number of hours, and solve it. How many hours did the plumber work?

  1. The bill is 25 + 18h, so 25 + 18h = 97.
  2. Take 25 from both sides, then divide by 18.
  • That is 18h. Divide by 18 to finish.
  • That ignores the £25 call-out. Take 25 from 97 first.
  • That adds 25. Undo "+ 25" by taking 25 away.

25 + 18h = 97, so 18h = 72 and h = 4. Check: 25 + 18 × 4 = 97 ✓

Why it works

  • Same on both sides. If A = B, then A + 5 = B + 5 and 3A = 3B. The equals sign is a promise, and doing the same to both sides keeps it.
  • Reverse order. The steps were done in one order, so the undoing happens in the opposite order. Shoes off before socks.
  • Checking is a reverse. Substituting your answer runs the equation forwards and tests the solution.

Common traps

  • Doing the operation to only one side.
  • Using the same operation instead of the inverse (adding when you should take away).
  • Dividing only one term instead of every term.
  • Forgetting that a bracket multiplies everything inside.
  • Losing a minus sign, especially with "12 − 2x".

Going further

Solve {frac:2x + 1/3} = {frac:x + 5/2}.

  1. Multiply both sides by 6 to clear the fractions: 2(2x + 1) = 3(x + 5).
  2. Expand: 4x + 2 = 3x + 15.
  • That adds 2. Take 2 from both sides: x = 15 − 2.

4x + 2 = 3x + 15 gives x = 13. Check: {frac:27/3} = 9 and {frac:18/2} = 9 ✓

Solve 5 − 2x = 3x − 10.

  1. Add 2x to both sides so x is positive: 5 = 5x − 10.
  • Check the signs: 5 + 10 = 15, so 5x = 15 and x is positive.

5 = 5x − 10 ⇒ 15 = 5x ⇒ x = 3. Check: 5 − 6 = −1 and 9 − 10 = −1 ✓

Check your knowledge: on the job

Real jobs from the building and plumbing trades. Prices are typical, so check them with your own supplier.

A plumber charges a £45 call-out fee plus £38 for every hour of work. A job costs £159. How many hours did the job take?

  1. What does the £159 include apart from the hours?
  2. Write an equation: 45 + 38h = 159. Undo the +45 first.
  3. Subtract 45 from both sides, then divide by 38.
  • You divided 159 by 38 without taking off the call-out fee first.
  • That is the cost of the hours. Divide by 38 to get the number of hours.

45 + 38h = 159. Subtract 45: 38h = 114. Divide by 38: h = 3. The job took 3 hours. (Check: 45 + 38 × 3 = 45 + 114 = 159 ✓.)

Plumber A charges a £30 call-out fee plus £44 an hour. Plumber B has no call-out fee and charges £52 an hour. After how many hours do they cost the same? Type it like 2.5.

  1. Write the cost of each plumber for h hours.
  2. The costs are the same, so set the two expressions equal: 30 + 44h = 52h.
  3. Take 44h from both sides, then divide.
  • You added 44h and 52h. Subtract 44h from both sides to get the h terms on one side.
  • You divided 30 by 44 and ignored the 52h. Put the h terms together first.

Plumber A: 30 + 44h. Plumber B: 52h. Equal when 30 + 44h = 52h. Subtract 44h: 30 = 8h. Divide by 8: h = 3.75 hours. (Check: A is 30 + 165 = £195 and B is 52 × 3.75 = £195 ✓.)

[3 marks] A quote is £60 call-out, plus 4.5 hours of labour at £x an hour, plus £86.40 of parts. The total is £362.40. Form an equation and find x, in pounds per hour.

  1. List the three parts of the quote and what each costs.
  2. Form the equation 60 + 4.5x + 86.40 = 362.40.
  3. Take off the call-out and the parts from 362.40, then divide by 4.5.
  • You forgot to take off the £86.40 of parts as well as the call-out fee.
  • That is 4.5x, the total labour. Divide by 4.5 to get the hourly rate.

Equation: 60 + 4.5x + 86.40 = 362.40 (1 mark). So 4.5x = 362.40 − 60 − 86.40 = 216 (1 mark). x = 216 ÷ 4.5 = 48 (1 mark). The rate is £48 an hour. (Check: 60 + 216 + 86.40 = 362.40 ✓.)

A fitter buys 8 lengths of pipe at £x each and pays £15 for delivery. The total is £65. Form an equation and find x, in pounds. Type it like 4.50.

  1. What is the cost of the 8 lengths on their own?
  2. Form the equation 8x + 15 = 65.
  3. Subtract 15 from both sides, then divide by 8.
  • You forgot to take off the £15 delivery before dividing by 8.
  • That is 8x, the cost of all 8 lengths. Divide by 8 to find one.

8x + 15 = 65. Subtract 15: 8x = 50. Divide by 8: x = 6.25. Each length costs £6.25. (Check: 8 × 6.25 + 15 = 50 + 15 = 65 ✓.)

A plumber cuts one third off a length of pipe, then cuts a further 25 cm off. 85 cm is left. How long was the pipe to start with, in centimetres? Call the starting length x.

  1. Write what is left in terms of x: x take away a third of x, take away 25.
  2. Form x − x/3 − 25 = 85. Then x − x/3 is two thirds of x.
  3. Add 25 to both sides, then multiply by 3 and divide by 2.
  • That is two thirds of the pipe. Multiply by 3 and divide by 2 to find the whole pipe.
  • You multiplied by 3 only. Two thirds of x is 110, so x is 110 × 3 ÷ 2.

x − x/3 − 25 = 85, so 2x/3 = 110. Multiply by 3: 2x = 330. Divide by 2: x = 165. The pipe was 165 cm. (Check: a third is 55, and 165 − 55 − 25 = 85 ✓.)
