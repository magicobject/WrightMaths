1. Measuring turns

An {term:angle} measures a turn. We measure it in degrees: a full turn is 360°. There are four names to know:

{term:acute-angle|acute}: less than 90°
{term:right-angle|right angle}: exactly 90°
{term:obtuse-angle|obtuse}: between 90° and 180°
{term:reflex-angle|reflex}: between 180° and 360°

Key idea: exactly 180° is a straight line and exactly 360° is a full turn. When you see an angle in a diagram, say its type first: that gives you an estimate to check your answer against.

Check your knowledge: types of angle

An angle of 135° is…

  • acute
  • right
  • obtuse
  • reflex
  1. Is it bigger than 90° and smaller than 180°?

135° is between 90° and 180°, so it is obtuse.

Which of these is a reflex angle?

  • 45°
  • 120°
  • 270°
  1. Reflex means more than 180°.

270° is between 180° and 360°.

How many degrees are there in a full turn?

  1. A whole turn all the way round.
  • That is a half turn (a straight line).

A full turn is 360°.

How many degrees are there in a right angle?

  1. A quarter of a full turn.
  • That is a straight line (two right angles).

360 ÷ 4 = 90°.

2. Angle facts

Four facts handle most simple angle questions:

  • Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.
  • Angles around a point add up to 360°.
  • Vertically opposite angles (the “X” shape) are equal.
  • A right angle is 90°.

Key idea: to find a missing angle: spot which fact applies, write the total (180 or 360), and subtract the angles you know. Say why in your answer, for example “angles on a straight line add up to 180°”. That reason earns marks in exams.

Worked example

Angles around a point are 90°, 120° and 100°, and x. Find x.

  1. Angles around a point add up to 360°.
  2. 90 + 120 + 100 = 310.
  3. x = 360 − 310 = 50°.

Check: 90 + 120 + 100 + 50 = 360. ✓

Check your knowledge: angle facts

Angles on a straight line are 65° and x. Find x.

  1. They add up to 180°.
  • You added to 360. A straight line is 180°.
  • You subtracted from 90. A straight line is 180°.

180 − 65 = 115°.

Angles around a point are 90°, 120°, 100° and x. Find x.

  1. They add up to 360°.
  • That is 310 − 180. Around a point the total is 360°, not 180°.
  • That is the sum of the angles you know.

360 − 310 = 50°.

Two straight lines cross. One of the angles is 48°. How big is the angle directly opposite it?

  1. Vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • That is the angle next to it (180 − 48). The one directly opposite is equal.

Vertically opposite angles are equal, so 48°.

Two angles on a straight line are x and 2x. Find x.

  1. Together: x + 2x = 3x = 180.
  • That would make the two angles x and x. Here the second is double the first.
  • That is 2x. The question asks for x.

3x = 180, so x = 60° (and the other angle is 120°).

A right angle is split into two angles. One is 35°. Find the other.

  1. They add to 90°.
  • That uses 180. A right angle is 90°.

90 − 35 = 55°.

3. Triangles

The three angles inside any triangle add up to 180°. An {term:isosceles-triangle|isosceles triangle} has two equal sides, and the two angles opposite them (the base angles) are equal. An equilateral triangle has all sides equal and all angles 60°.

Key idea: 180 is the total for triangles, 360 for quadrilaterals and for a full turn. Pick the total that matches the shape before you subtract.

Worked example: isosceles

An isosceles triangle has an angle of 40° at the top between the two equal sides. Find a base angle.

  1. The angles add up to 180°.
  2. The two base angles are equal, so share what is left: 180 − 40 = 140.
  3. 140 ÷ 2 = 70° each.

Check your knowledge: triangles

Two angles of a triangle are 50° and 70°. Find the third angle.

  1. The three add to 180°.
  • That is the sum of the two you know.
  • That is 180 − 50. Subtract both angles.

50 + 70 = 120, so 180 − 120 = 60°.

An isosceles triangle has a 40° angle between its two equal sides. Find each base angle.

  1. Share 180 − 40 between the two equal angles.
  • That is the total of the two base angles. Share it between the two.

(180 − 40) ÷ 2 = 70°.

A right-angled triangle has one other angle of 35°. Find the third angle.

  1. The three angles add to 180°, and one is 90°.
  • That is 180 − 35. Remember the right angle too.

180 − 90 − 35 = 55°.

What is each angle of an equilateral triangle?

  1. The three angles are equal and add to 180°.
  • That is a right angle. 3 × 90 = 270, not 180.

180 ÷ 3 = 60°.

The angles of a triangle are x, 2x and 3x. Find x.

  1. x + 2x + 3x = 6x = 180.
  • That is 2x. The question asks for x.
  • That is the total, not x.

6x = 180, so x = 30°. (The angles are 30°, 60° and 90°.)

4. Quadrilaterals and polygons

The four angles inside any quadrilateral add up to 360°. For other {term:polygon|polygons} the total depends on the number of sides, n: it is (n − 2) × 180°. A {term:regular-polygon|regular polygon} has all its sides and angles equal, so each interior angle is the total divided by n. The exterior angles of any convex polygon (one with no dents) always add up to 360°, so each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 360 ÷ n.

Key idea: (n − 2) × 180 works because any polygon can be cut into n − 2 triangles, each with 180°. Interior + exterior angle at a corner = 180° (they sit on a straight line).

Worked example

Find each interior angle of a regular hexagon.

  1. A hexagon has 6 sides: (6 − 2) × 180 = 720°.
  2. Regular, so each angle is 720 ÷ 6 = 120°.

Check with exterior angles: 360 ÷ 6 = 60, and 180 − 60 = 120. ✓

Check your knowledge: polygons

Three angles of a quadrilateral are 90°, 110° and 70°. Find the fourth.

  1. The four angles add to 360°.
  • That leaves out one of the angles: 360 − 90 − 70 = 200. Subtract all three.
  • That is the sum of the three you know.

360 − 270 = 90°.

Work out the sum of the interior angles of a pentagon.

  1. A pentagon has 5 sides: (5 − 2) × 180.
  • That is 5 × 180. Use (n − 2) × 180: a pentagon has n = 5, so n − 2 = 3.

3 × 180 = 540°.

Work out the sum of the interior angles of a hexagon.

  1. (6 − 2) × 180.
  • That is 6 × 180. Use (n − 2).

4 × 180 = 720°.

Find each exterior angle of a regular octagon.

  1. An octagon has 8 sides; exterior angles add to 360°.
  • That is the interior angle. The question asks for the exterior angle.

360 ÷ 8 = 45°.

Find each interior angle of a regular hexagon.

  1. 720 ÷ 6, or 180 − 60.
  • That is the exterior angle. The interior angle is 180 − 60.

(6 − 2) × 180 ÷ 6 = 120°.

5. Parallel lines

Lines that never meet, and stay the same distance apart, are {term:parallel|parallel}. Mark them with arrows. When a third line crosses a pair of parallel lines, it makes angles that are linked:

  • Corresponding angles (an F shape) are equal.
  • Alternate angles (a Z shape) are equal.
  • Co-interior angles (a C shape) add up to 180°.

Key idea: whenever you use one of these facts, name it: “alternate angles are equal”. The name is the reason, and the reason earns the mark. Vertically opposite and angles on a straight line often help you get from the angle you know to the one you need.

Check your knowledge: parallel lines

Two parallel lines are crossed by a third. One angle is 70°. What is the alternate angle?

  • 70°
  • 110°
  • 20°
  1. Alternate angles make a Z shape and are equal.

70°. Alternate angles are equal.

Parallel lines: one angle is 70°. Find the co-interior angle.

  1. Co-interior angles add to 180°.
  • That is the alternate or corresponding angle. Co-interior angles add to 180°.
  • That is 360 − 70. Co-interior angles add to 180°, not 360°.

180 − 70 = 110°.

Parallel lines: one angle is 70°. Find the corresponding angle.

  1. Corresponding angles make an F shape and are equal.
  • That is the co-interior angle. Corresponding angles are equal.

70°. Corresponding angles are equal.

Which pair of angles adds up to 180° when the lines are parallel?

  • Alternate
  • Corresponding
  • Co-interior
  1. Think of the C shape.

Co-interior angles add to 180°.

Flip it: find the angle, or build the shape

So far you have gone one way. Now run it backwards: this is where much of the real understanding comes from.

Forwards

A regular polygon has 10 sides. Find the exterior angle: 360 ÷ 10 = 36°.

Sides to angle.

Backwards

A regular polygon has exterior angles of 36°. How many sides? 360 ÷ 36 = 10.

Angle to sides.

Key idea: angle facts run both ways. You can use the angle facts to find a missing angle, and you can use angles to decide whether a shape can exist or what shape it is.

Another reversal is the test: three angles make a triangle only if they add up to exactly 180°.

Check your knowledge: flip it

A regular polygon has exterior angles of 36°. How many sides does it have?

  1. Exterior angles add to 360°. How many 36s fit?
  • That is the exterior angle. Divide 360 by it to count the sides.
  • That is 360 − 36. Divide 360 by the exterior angle.

360 ÷ 36 = 10 sides.

Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 150°. How many sides does it have?

  1. Find the exterior angle first: 180 − 150.
  2. Then 360 ÷ that.
  • That is the exterior angle. Divide 360 by it to find the number of sides.

Exterior angle = 30°. 360 ÷ 30 = 12 sides.

Could a triangle have angles 50°, 60° and 80°?

  • Yes
  • No: they add up to 190°
  1. Add the three angles. Do they make 180°?

50 + 60 + 80 = 190, not 180, so no triangle has these angles.

Could a quadrilateral have angles 100°, 80°, 90° and 90°?

  • Yes: they add up to 360°
  • No
  1. Add the four angles. A quadrilateral needs 360°.

100 + 80 + 90 + 90 = 360, so it could be.

A triangle has angles 3x, 4x and 5x. Find the largest angle.

  1. 12x = 180, so x = 15.
  • That is x, not the largest angle (5x).
  • That is 4x. The largest is 5x.

x = 15°, so the angles are 45°, 60° and 75°. The largest is 75°.

Spot the mistake

Ravi says: “The angles in a triangle are 60°, 70° and 40°, which add up to 170°, so it must be fine.” What went wrong?

  • The angles in a triangle must add up to exactly 180°, so these cannot be the angles of a triangle.
  • Nothing: it is fine
  • They should add to 360°
  1. Add them: 60 + 70 + 40.

They add to 170°, which is 10° short of 180°.

Tia says: “A regular pentagon has interior angles of 5 × 180 = 900°, so each is 180°.” What went wrong?

  • She used n instead of n − 2. The total is (5 − 2) × 180 = 540°, so each angle is 108°.
  • Nothing: 180° is right
  • She should divide by 4
  1. A pentagon splits into 3 triangles.

540 ÷ 5 = 108°.

Uma says: “Co-interior angles are equal.” What went wrong?

  • That is alternate and corresponding angles. Co-interior angles add up to 180°.
  • Nothing: all angles between parallel lines are equal
  • They add to 360°
  1. Which shape is co-interior? C: they add to 180°.

Co-interior angles add up to 180°.

  1. (180 − 48) ÷ 2.
  • That is both base angles together.

132 ÷ 2 = 66°.

  • That is the exterior angle (360 ÷ 5). The interior angle is 180 − 72.

(5 − 2) × 180 ÷ 5 = 108°.

  1. 2x + x + 30 = 180.
  2. 3x = 150.
  • You may have left out the 30: 3x = 150.

3x + 30 = 180, so 3x = 150 and x = 50.

Parallel lines: two co-interior angles are x and 2x. Find x.

  1. Co-interior angles add to 180°.
  • That would make the two angles equal. The second is double the first.

3x = 180, so x = 60°.

Exam style

Give a reason for each step: the reason earns marks.

(a) [3 marks] Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC. Angle A = 48°. Work out angle B.

  1. Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.
  2. 180 − 48 = 132, then halve.
  • That is angle B plus angle C. They are equal, so halve it.
  • That is angle A.

Angles in a triangle add to 180°: 180 − 48 = 132. Base angles are equal: 132 ÷ 2 = 66°.

(b) [2 marks] Work out the size of one interior angle of a regular pentagon.

  1. Sum = (5 − 2) × 180.
  • That is the exterior angle.

3 × 180 = 540 and 540 ÷ 5 = 108°.

(c) [2 marks] Two parallel lines are crossed by a third. One angle is 65°. Find the co-interior angle to it.

  1. Co-interior angles add to 180°.
  • That is the alternate or corresponding angle. Co-interior angles add to 180.

180 − 65 = 115° (co-interior angles add up to 180°).

Going further

Work out the sum of the interior angles of a 12-sided polygon.

  1. (n − 2) × 180 with n = 12.
  • Use n − 2, not n.

10 × 180 = 1,800°.

Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 140°. How many sides does it have?

  1. Exterior = 180 − 140 = 40.
  2. 360 ÷ 40.
  • That is the exterior angle. Divide 360 by it.

360 ÷ 40 = 9 sides (a nonagon).

Why it works

  • Every angle fact is a total. Straight line 180°, point 360°, triangle 180°, quadrilateral 360°. Missing angle = total minus the angles you know.
  • Polygons split into triangles. That is why the interior angle sum is (n − 2) × 180°.
  • Parallel lines copy angles. Corresponding and alternate angles match; co-interior angles make a straight line together.

Common traps

  • Using 180 when the total is 360 (or the other way round).
  • Using n instead of n − 2 for polygon angle sums.
  • Mixing up alternate (equal) and co-interior (sum to 180°).
  • Forgetting that the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.
  • Giving an angle with no reason in an exam answer.
