1. Adding and subtracting

Sum = the answer to an add. Difference = how far apart. Product = the answer to a times. The {term:sum|sum} of 4 and 9 is 13. The {term:difference|difference} between them is 5.

Both written methods use columns. Line up the {term:place-value|place values}: units under units, tens under tens. Put the decimal points under each other.

Key idea: start at the right-hand column. Adding: 10 or more in a column? Carry 1 into the next column. Subtracting: top {term:digit|digit} too small? Take 1 from the next column. It turns into 10 here. This is called an exchange. Some people say {term:carry-borrow|borrow}. Keep digits in line. Untidy columns cause most mistakes.

Try it: in 38 + 27, what do you write in the units column, and what do you carry?

Worked example: add

Work out 4,578 + 3,964.

  1. Units: 8 + 4 = 12. Write 2, carry 1.
  2. Tens: 7 + 6 + 1 = 14. Write 4, carry 1.
  3. Hundreds: 5 + 9 + 1 = 15. Write 5, carry 1.
  4. Thousands: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8.
  5. 4,578 + 3,964 = 8,542.

Now you finish it: subtract

Work out 6,003 − 2,458.

  1. Zeros in the way make an exchange messy. Count up instead.
  2. Start at 2,458. Go up to 2,500. That is 42.
  3. Go from 2,500 up to 6,000. That is 3,500.
  4. Go from 6,000 up to 6,003. That is 3.
  5. Add the jumps: 42 + 3,500 + 3 = ___.
Check my answer

3,545. Check by adding: 3,545 + 2,458 = 6,003.

2. Multiplying

The {term:product|product} is the answer when you multiply. For bigger numbers, split one number into easy pieces. This is {term:partitioning|partitioning}. Multiply each piece, then add.

Key idea: 3.2 × 4. Ignore the point: 32 × 4 = 128. 3.2 has one digit after the point. So the answer gets one: 12.8.

Try it: work out 24 × 3 by splitting 24 into 20 + 4.

Worked example

Work out 36 × 24.

  1. Split 24 into 20 + 4.
  2. 36 × 20 = 720 and 36 × 4 = 144.
  3. 720 + 144 = 864.

Worked example: decimals

Work out 0.3 × 0.4.

  1. Ignore the points: 3 × 4 = 12.
  2. 0.3 has one {term:decimal-place|decimal place}. 0.4 has one. So the answer has two.
  3. 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12.

Sense check: multiplying by a number less than 1 makes things smaller. So the answer must be less than 0.3.

3. Dividing

In 12 ÷ 4, the {term:dividend|dividend} is 12 (the number being divided). The {term:divisor|divisor} is 4 (the number you divide by). If a number does not divide exactly, what is left over is the {term:remainder|remainder}. Use short division and set out each step.

Key idea: division asks “how many groups fit in?” Or it asks you to share equally. To divide by a decimal, make the divisor whole. Multiply both numbers by 10, 100, 1,000 ... until the number you divide by is whole. Example: 3.6 ÷ 0.4 becomes 36 ÷ 4.

Try it: how many groups of 5 fit into 35?

Worked example

Work out 1,456 ÷ 7.

  1. 7 into 14 goes 2 times. Write 2 above the 4.
  2. 7 into 5? It does not go. Write 0.
  3. Carry the 5 to make 56.
  4. 7 into 56 goes 8 times. Write 8.
  5. 1,456 ÷ 7 = 208.

Check by multiplying: 7 × 208 = 1,456. ✓

Check your knowledge: divide

A school raises £252 for its football club. The money is shared equally between 6 teams. How many pounds does each team get?

  1. 25 ÷ 6 = 4 remainder 1.
  • You missed the carry. 25 ÷ 6 = 4 remainder 1, then 12 ÷ 6.

252 ÷ 6 = 42. Check: 6 × 42 = 252.

Work out 1,456 ÷ 7.

  1. 14 ÷ 7 = 2, then 5, then 56.
  • 5 ÷ 7 does not go, so write a 0 in the answer.

208. Check: 7 × 208 = 1,456.

What is the {term:remainder|remainder} when 95 is divided by 4?

  1. 4 × 23 = 92.
  • That is how many whole 4s fit. The remainder is what is left over.

4 × 23 = 92 and 95 − 92 = 3.

Write 95 ÷ 4 as a decimal.

  1. 23 remainder 3: the 3 is three quarters.
  • A remainder of 3 out of 4 is 0.75, not 0.3.

95 ÷ 4 = 23.75.

Work out 3.6 ÷ 0.4.

  1. Multiply both numbers by 10: 36 ÷ 4.
  • You moved one number only. Multiply both by 10: 36 ÷ 4.

36 ÷ 4 = 9.

Flip it: every operation has a partner that undoes it

So far you have gone one way. Now run it backwards. Same idea, other direction.

Forwards

342 + 289 = 631

Add to build up.

Backwards

631 − 289 = 342

Subtract to undo it.

Key idea: add and subtract are {term:inverse-operation|inverse operations}, and so are multiply and divide. Use this to check an answer by reversing it. Use it to find a missing number by undoing the steps.

Forwards

24 × 6 = 144

Six groups of 24.

Backwards

144 ÷ 6 = 24, and 144 ÷ 24 = 6

One multiplication gives you two divisions.

Check your knowledge: flip it

You know 342 + 289 = 631. What is 631 − 289?

  1. Subtracting undoes adding.
  • That is 631 + 289. Subtract to undo the addition.

631 − 289 = 342: you get back to where you started.

I think of a number, multiply it by 7 and get 91. What is my number?

  1. Undo × 7 with ÷ 7.
  • That is 91 × 7. Undo a multiplication by dividing.

91 ÷ 7 = 13. Check: 13 × 7 = 91.

46 × ? = 1,196. Find the missing number.

  1. Undo with division: 1,196 ÷ 46.
  • That is 46 × 1,196. Divide to find the missing factor.

1,196 ÷ 46 = 26. Check: 46 × 26 = 1,196.

? − 138 = 267. Find the missing number.

  1. The missing number is bigger. Undo − 138 with + 138.
  • That is 267 − 138. To undo a subtraction, add.

267 + 138 = 405. Check: 405 − 138 = 267.

? ÷ 8 = 15. Find the missing number.

  1. Undo ÷ 8 with × 8.
  • That is 15 ÷ 8. Undo a division by multiplying.

15 × 8 = 120.

You know 24 × 6 = 144. Write the answer to 144 ÷ 24.

  1. One multiplication gives two divisions.
  • You divided and got 24 back. Which factor is left?

144 ÷ 24 = 6, because 24 × 6 = 144.

4. Using it in real situations

First decide which {term:operation|operation} to use. Words give clues:

  • “Altogether” often means add.
  • “Change” (money you get back) means subtract.
  • “Shared equally” means divide.
  • “Each” can mean multiply or divide.

Clues can mislead, so picture what is happening. Then make an {term:estimate|estimate} to check the size of your answer.

Key idea: counting whole things? A remainder usually means one more. 70 students, 16 in each minibus (a small bus). You need 5 minibuses, not 4.375.

Try it: 25 people, 4 in each car. How many cars?

Check your knowledge: problems

  • 4 tickets.
  • Each ticket costs £8.50. (£ means pounds, the UK money.)
  • There is one £3 booking fee for the whole order.

What is the total cost in pounds?

  1. Four tickets first: 4 × 8.50.
  2. Then add the booking fee once.
  • That adds. There are four tickets at £8.50 each.
  • You charged £3 on every ticket. It is charged once.
  • You left out the booking fee. Add £3 once.

4 × 8.50 = 34, and 34 + 3 = £37.

156 pencils are shared equally between 12 children. How many pencils does each child get?

  1. Shared equally means divide.
  • That is 156 + 12. Sharing means dividing.
  • That is 156 − 12. Sharing equally means dividing.

156 ÷ 12 = 13. Check: 12 × 13 = 156.

70 students go on a trip. Each minibus (a small bus) holds 16 students. How many minibuses are needed?

  1. 70 ÷ 16 is between 4 and 5.
  2. You cannot have part of a minibus.
  • 4 minibuses hold 64. Six students would be left behind.
  • That is the exact answer. You cannot hire part of a minibus.

70 ÷ 16 = 4.375, so 4 minibuses would leave some students behind. You need 5.

A bag holds 1.5 kg of flour. How many 250 g portions can you make? (1 kg = 1,000 g.)

  1. Look at the bar. What is each part? What is the whole? Are the units the same?
  2. Use grams first: 1.5 kg = 1,500 g.
  • That divides 1.5 by 250. Use the same unit: 1,500 ÷ 250.
  • That multiplies. How many 250 g portions fit? Divide.

1,500 ÷ 250 = 6.

Jo saves £12.50 each week. How many weeks does it take her to save at least £100?

  1. 100 ÷ 12.50 = 8 exactly.
  • 7 weeks gives only £87.50.

12.50 × 8 = 100, so 8 weeks.

Spot the mistake

Mia says: “6,003 − 2,458 = 4,455.” (She did each column by taking the smaller digit from the bigger.) What went wrong?

  • The smaller digit was taken from the bigger every time.
  • Nothing is wrong: 4,455 is the right answer.
  • She should have added the two numbers.
  1. Check by adding back: 4,455 + 2,458.

The correct answer is 3,545. Check: 3,545 + 2,458 = 6,003.

Tom says: “3.5 × 0.2 = 7.” What went wrong?

  • The decimal point was lost from the answer.
  • Nothing is wrong: 35 × 2 = 70, so 7 fits.
  • He should have divided instead of multiplying.
  1. Estimate: 3.5 is near 4. 0.2 is one fifth. A fifth of 4 is less than 1.

35 × 2 = 70. 3.5 and 0.2 have one digit after the point each, so the answer has two: 0.70 = 0.7. A fifth of 3.5 is 0.7.

Going further

Use the digits 2, 3 and 4 once each to make a two-digit number multiplied by a one-digit number. What is the largest product you can make?

  1. There are only six ways to arrange 2, 3 and 4. Try them all and compare.
  • Putting the biggest digits together does not give the biggest product. Try all six.

43 × 2 = 86, 42 × 3 = 126, 32 × 4 = 128, 34 × 2 = 68, 24 × 3 = 72, 23 × 4 = 92. The largest is 32 × 4 = 128. Putting the biggest digit (4) on its own as the multiplier wins.

You know 4,872 ÷ 24 = 203. What is 203 × 24?

  1. Multiplying undoes dividing.
  • That is 203 + 24. Multiplying by 24 undoes dividing by 24.

203 × 24 = 4,872, the number you started with.

Why it works

  • Carrying and exchanging are just place value: ten units become one ten, and one ten becomes ten units.
  • Partitioning works because 24 is 20 + 4. So 24 lots of 36 is 20 lots plus 4 lots: 36 × 24 = 36 × 20 + 36 × 4.
  • Inverse operations undo each other, so every answer can be checked by running it backwards.

Common traps

  • Not lining up place values or decimal points.
  • Taking the smaller digit from the bigger in every column.
  • Losing the decimal point after multiplying.
  • Forgetting the zero in an answer such as 208.
  • Giving 4.375 minibuses when a whole number was needed.
