How it works

Five habits built into every page

Flip it

Maths comes in pairs: multiplying and dividing, expanding and factorising, finding the long side and finding a short side. You learn each idea, then you flip it and use it backwards. Each side makes the other make sense.

Words in teal

Every mathematical word is highlighted. Tap or click one to see what it means, or open the glossary for a full explanation, an example, a warning about common traps and the reverse side of the idea.

Help, I'm stuck!

Every question has a ladder of hints. Reveal one at a time and try again after each. You always do the final step yourself, and getting help is never a failure.

Check your knowledge

Quick questions after every chunk, not just at the end. Getting an idea out of your head is what makes it stick, far more than reading it again.

Build the base

Each topic tells you what it stands on and what it unlocks. If a topic feels hard, the cause is nearly always a gap underneath it, and the "Before you start" box shows you where to look.

Private by design

No accounts, no tracking. Your ticks are saved only in this browser, on this device. Nothing about you is sent anywhere.

The method

How to get the most from each topic

  1. Try it first. Spend a few minutes before you look at any help. Being stuck is normal; it is where the learning happens.
  2. Use one hint at a time. After each hint, go back and try again.
  3. Read the key idea, then try once more before you read the full solution.
  4. Compare line by line with the worked solution and mark anything you missed.
  5. Read "Why it works" and the common traps. They help you understand the method, not just copy it.
  6. Come back in a week and do the Check your knowledge questions again. Returning after a gap is one of the best ways to make an idea last.
  7. Explain it. If you can explain the key idea to a friend in two sentences, you have understood it.

Calculators: unless a question says otherwise, work without one first and then check with one.
